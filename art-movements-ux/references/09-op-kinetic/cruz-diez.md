# Carlos Cruz-Diez — Kinetic / Op art (Venezuelan)

## Essence
Cruz-Diez treated color as an event, not a surface property. It happens between thin parallel bands, in the eye, and changes as the body moves, the light shifts, or the distance closes. His works produce colors that aren't physically there (red + green bands read as yellow), and each is completed only by someone walking past.

## Key works examined
- **Couleur Additive / *Additive Yellow*, 1959**: two lines, red and green, produce a yellow that isn't painted anywhere. Teaches that adjacency generates color. *(1959 original not obtained. Related works checked on the Cruz-Diez atelier site: **Amarillo Aditivo**: orange-red and green line grids crossing on a black ground (54% black); where they overlap a yellow-olive appears that is painted nowhere. **Couleur Additive 76**: horizontal bands of orange #FC5D15 (29%), green #3AB620 (26%) and violet #522F92 (16%), each band ~0.4–0.7% of the frame height, a three-band repeat ≈1.1%; no black separators; diagonal seams shift the band phase. **Couleur Additive n°1 (Denise René edition)**: red #F81F02, green #62DB02, blue #0015B9 and white #EFF4EC bands in equal shares (~21% each), fanning into slanted panels.)*
- ***Physichromie 1*, 1959**: painted edges of red, green and white cardboard strips set in relief inside a frame. Color sits *on the edges* and blooms in the gaps between them. *(1959 original not obtained; later Physichromies checked on the atelier site: **Physichromie 1778** reads as amber #BF7103, yellow #D7B303, green #66B124 and blue #0479B2 blades with nested rectangles shifting hue; **Physichromie 948** photographed from front, left and right turns from orange to red/blue as the viewpoint moves, confirming colour as a function of position.)*
- ***Physichromie 113*, 1963/1976**: adds reflective lumaline and one circle that cuts across the strips, so the change in color becomes legible. Teaches that one counter-form makes a field readable. *(visually checked: Tate T02094, frontal photo. 86 blades across the width, pitch ≈1.16% of the width, dark blade edges ~25% of each pitch. Frontally the field is low-chroma: slate #4D5C65 and greys #A9A59D/#31393C outside the circle, olive #494A33/#716D50 inside (chroma ≤17). The single circle spans ~53% of the width, centred horizontally and slightly below the middle. Saturated colour is reserved for the blade sides and appears only off-axis.)*
- ***Physichromie No. 123*, 1964** *(added, visually checked: Tate T03715)*: the striped relief fills only the top ~45% of a black #1B150E box; the lower half is blank black. Colours are aged ochre #AE9164, rust #8D5535, olive — a void is used as a counterweight.
- ***Induction Chromatique n° 54. Bleu + Noir = Jaune*, 1973** (120 × 120 cm, Pompidou): an afterimage made permanent. Complementary "virtual" colors appear instantly instead of after you stare. *(n° 54 visually checked in the retry pass: Centre Pompidou AM 2020-256 photo. 67 horizontal bands run the full height (each band ≈1.5% of the height, 2–3× coarser than the Couleur Additive bands), with blue or black bands as wide as the white ones. Band colour swaps by zone (blue/white vs black/white blocks, roughly a 3 × 3 field), and two axonometric cubes, upper right and lower left, are drawn only by these swaps, with side faces in slanted bands. - ***Chromosaturation*, 1965/1968**: three walk-through chambers flooded with red, green and blue light that blend at the thresholds. Teaches that color can be architecture you enter. *(visually checked: atelier photo, GAM Turin 2017: chambers of blue #012EC0, orange-red #FE642A and green #01DE01 light; at the thresholds the lights mix into magenta and peach #FEAD62. Lighting-dependent.)*
- ***Ambientación de Color Aditivo*, Maiquetía airport, 1974–78**: about 2,925 m² of thin parallel floor bands whose combinations shift as travelers walk. *(visually checked: atelier archive photo, 1974: a long runway of thin multicolour bands, read along its length.)*
- ***Mural de Color Aditivo*, Guri hydroelectric plant, 1977–86**: 17 × 245 m. Shows that a stripe system can scale from 30 cm to 245 m without changing its rules. *(not visually checked; the atelier's monumental page shows an unidentified Color Aditivo mural of wide vertical bands — blue, yellow, orange, green, black — which I did not count as Guri.)*

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Carlos Cruz-Diez.** *Do not paint the colour you want: set narrow bands side by side so that it arises between them, and make which colour arises depend on where the viewer is.*
Process:
1. Fix one band direction and one pitch for the whole field: a band is 0.4–0.7% of the field's length where neighbours must fuse, and up to 1.5% where one must induce a colour in the other; a repeat is 2–3 bands. All bands are equal in width, the darkest and the lightest included. There are no separators.
2. Run the bands edge to edge, across the direction of travel. Any emptiness is one solid block (up to 55% of the format), never scattered.
3. Choose 2–3 band colours per zone as generators. The colour meant to be seen is the one produced between them, by fusion or by induction; it is not painted (if it appears at all it is ≤15%, as a band among bands).
4. Make every figure and every level by zone: inside a region, swap one generator for another, shift the phase by half a pitch, or slant the bands. Keep to about 3 × 3 zones and draw no outline. At most one counter-form crosses the bands, about half the field wide.
5. Add a second layer whose alignment with the first depends on the viewer's position (the sides of a relief, a slower layer, a tilt). Seen frontally the field is at its quietest; intensity appears off-axis. When the viewer stops, the colour stops.
6. Mode for interface use: the band field is decoration and carries no text and no control; reading and acting happen on the solid block of step 2. The induced colour never carries meaning by itself.
Why it works:
- Below the eye's colour resolution, neighbouring bands fuse into a mixture; a little above it, each band pushes its neighbour toward its own opposite. Either way the seen colour exists only in the viewer, so it changes with distance, angle and light. Widen the bands past about 1.5% of the field and they read as two paints in stripes; paint the target and it becomes a surface.
- An inducer needs area: a dark band as wide as the light one shifts it, while a hairline only draws an edge. That is why the dark is a full band or the whole ground.
- With one pitch everywhere, a change of sequence or phase is the only event, so a zone reads as a figure with no contour and the field keeps a single skin.
Colour relations: generators near-equal in share (20–30% each); dark and light inducers as wide as the hues; the target colour absent, or ≤15%; frontal state low in chroma (≤17 in the one relief measured), off-axis state saturated; a void is the darkest value on the surface; no meaning is assigned to an induced colour.
Space and line: one direction, one pitch, straight parallel bands only; formats made to be walked along (up to 1:14); zones ≈3 × 3; one counter-form ≈50% of the width; void ≤55%, in one block; relief depth tiny against the format.
Type requirement: never on the bands. Tall, narrow letters with even vertical stems at a regular advance, so a line of type repeats the band rhythm at a larger pitch; one large size and one small; set on the solid block.
Motion and interaction: colour is a function of position input (scroll, pointer, tilt) and never of a clock: two layers at 1.0× and 0.85×, or a phase offset of up to half a pitch. Where scroll-linked animation is unsupported, or reduced motion is asked for, the field rests at one composed phase: declare that static state first, then add the scroll timeline inside a support check (`@supports (animation-timeline: scroll())`). Thresholds between zones blend over 20–30% of a screen, and are travelled, not timed.
Fails when: a colour must be exact or coded (brand swatches, charts, status); the output is a screenshot, a print or a low-density display (the induced colour vanishes, or the bands alias into moiré); the page is mostly text; full-contrast inducer bands fill the viewport.
Signature (do not carry together): red, green and blue blades · a circle cutting across vertical stripes · rooms flooded with one colour of light.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Cruz-Diez's procedure → an hourly weather screen in soft pastels (phone, 390 × 844). Pale, low-contrast, read once a day.
1. *Pitch.* Horizontal bands 4px high (0.47% of the height), a two-band repeat of 8px (0.95%), equal widths, no rules between them.
2. *Field and void.* The band field fills the top 45% (380px). The lower 55% is one solid block in the palest tone: all text and both controls sit there.
3. *Generators.* A pale coral and a pale aqua, at near-equal lightness (L 82 and 84), 50 : 50. What is seen is a warm lilac-grey that is in neither band. The temperature tint is never painted.
4. *Zones.* The field is 3 hour-columns × 3 rows. In a column where rain is due, aqua is swapped for a pale slate; where it warms, coral is swapped for a pale straw. The current hour's zone is shifted by half a pitch (4px), which is its only marker: no outline, no counter-form.
5. *Position.* A second band layer moves at 0.85× of the horizontal hour-scroll, so the tint drifts as the user drags through the day and holds when the finger stops. Without scroll-timeline support, or with reduced motion, the layers rest at a fixed 2px offset.
6. *Interface mode.* The field is `aria-hidden`. The block below states the hour, temperature and rain as text; "now" is also written out, because a phase shift cannot carry meaning alone.
- *Type.* One tall narrow face: the temperature at 96px and labels at 14px, both on the block.
- *Contrast.* Neighbouring bands differ by under 1.2:1, so the 8px period is not a glare pattern.
Result: a pastel sky made of threads that changes tint as the day is dragged past. No saturated red, green or blue, no black, no circle. What survived is one pitch, colour produced between bands, figure by zone and colour tied to position.

## Space & composition
- **The field is the composition.** Works are near-uniform fields of vertical (or floor-parallel) bands that run edge to edge with no focal centerpiece. Hierarchy comes from *modules*: zones where the band sequence changes, not from objects placed on a background. Even a figure is made this way: n° 54's two cubes are only zones where blue bands replace black ones, with no outline.
- **Extreme aspect ratios.** Guri is about 1:14, and Maiquetía is a runway. He designed for traversal, not a frontal viewpoint.
- **One intruder.** A single circle or diagonal across the parallel lines (*Physichromie 113*: one circle ~53% of the width over 86 blades) is the only "figure" allowed.
- **Depth through thin relief.** Physichromie blades project at right angles from the ground, so the surface has a z-axis only a few centimeters deep. That shallow depth does all the work.
- **Density over void, with one exception.** Most fields run edge to edge, but *Physichromie 123* leaves the lower ~55% of its box blank black: when there is a void, it is one solid block, never scattered padding.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

Additive, reflective, subtractive: narrow band pairs mix optically, transparent strips filter, reflection spills color onto neighbors. Measured band widths are coarser than hairlines: ~0.4–0.7% of the frame per band, ~1.1–1.2% per repeat (Couleur Additive 76, Physichromie 113), i.e. about 6–10 px bands on a 1440 px screen; Induction Chromatique n° 54 is 2–3× coarser, ≈1.5% of the height per band (67 bands); go down to 2–4 px only for the layer that must mix optically at arm's length. Black is an inducer, not a neutral: in Induction Chromatique and Amarillo Aditivo it is a full-width band or the whole ground, not a hairline.

| Hex | Role |
|---|---|
| `#F81F02` | Light-primary red (Couleur Additive n°1, ~21%; Couleur Additive 76 uses orange `#FC5D15` in the red slot) |
| `#3AB620` | Light-primary green (red's partner; together they induce yellow; Couleur Additive 76, ~26%) |
| `#0015B9` | Light-primary blue (Couleur Additive n°1, ~23%; Chromosaturation blue light `#012EC0`) |
| `#000000` | Separator/inducer black (Amarillo Aditivo ground, 54%; Induction Chromatique bands, 31%; digital files, so true black) |
| `#EFF4EC` | White blade edge / reflective rest state (paper of Couleur Additive n°1) |
| `#522F92` | Violet band, replacing the old magenta: no magenta was found in the painted works checked (only in Chromosaturation's light thresholds); violet is the third band of Couleur Additive 76 (~16%) |
| `#D7B303` | "Virtual" yellow: induced, never painted, in Couleur Additive and Amarillo Aditivo, but painted on Physichromie 1778 blades (~14%) |

## Form, line & typography
The vocabulary is the straight parallel line at a strict pitch, with no curves except the rare intruding circle. Use tall, condensed, vertical-stressed type whose stems read as more stripes. Recommended fonts: **Big Shoulders Display** for headlines (narrow, stem-driven), **Archivo** (variable width; use the condensed axis for UI labels), and **IBM Plex Mono** for data, since its fixed pitch echoes his fixed band spacing.

## Rhythm, motion & time
The work moves only because *you* move. Drive animation by user input (scroll, pointer x, device tilt), not autoplay timers. Color is a continuous function of position, not a transition. Moving parallel to the field shifts hue, and moving toward it changes intensity, as the Tate notes for *Additive Yellow*. Thresholds between sections should blend, as at Chromosaturation's doorways, rather than cut.

## UI translations
1. **Layout:** Build hero and section backgrounds from `repeating-linear-gradient` bands at a fixed pitch (bands ≈0.5% of the viewport, a full colour repeat ≈1.1%, e.g. 6–8 px bands on desktop; up to ≈1.5% per band as in n° 54) running the full width of the viewport. Text never sits on the bands: put all readable content on flat, solid panels (one value, ≥4.5:1 text contrast) set into the field, with no shadow.
2. **Color/parallax:** Put two band layers at different `translateY` speeds (e.g. 1.0× and 0.85× scroll), so their alignment, and the color that emerges between them, changes as the user scrolls.
3. **Color:** Never fill a large area with the target accent. Interleave thin red-orange and green bands (2–4 px for the mixing layer, no separators) and let the yellow appear optically. Reserve real `#D7B303` for one critical action.
4. **Interaction:** Map `pointermove` x to a CSS custom property `--phase` that offsets one band set by up to half a pitch. The hover color of the whole surface drifts with the cursor.
5. **Motion:** Use scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline: scroll()`) for color, with zero duration-based loops. If the user stops, the color stops.
6. **Navigation:** Treat sections as Chromosaturation chambers. Each route floods the viewport with one light-primary tint, and the boundary is a 20–30 vh gradient blend you scroll *through*.
7. **Type:** Set headlines in Big Shoulders Display at 10–14 vw with `letter-spacing: -0.02em`, so the stems echo the stripe field. Set them in one solid colour on a flat panel, never clipped to or laid over stripes (vibrating bands break reading); stripes may fill only non-text shapes.
8. **One intruder:** Allow exactly one non-orthogonal element per screen (a circle avatar or a single diagonal CTA) cutting across the band field.
9. **Relief:** Render list rows as "blades": a 1 px light top edge plus a colored right edge that only shows as rows parallax at slightly different rates.
10. **Floor-as-path:** In long flows (checkout, onboarding), run a narrow band strip along the bottom edge whose sequence advances with progress, like the Maiquetía corridor.

## What this artist would reject
- Static flat brand color applied as a 100% fill on large surfaces, which is color as paint rather than event.
- Time-based autoplay carousels and looping ambient gradients that move while the user is still.
- Shadowed cards on neutral gray; hierarchy should come from field modules.
- Gradient "glows" that fake mixing instead of producing it through adjacency.
- A frontal, fold-centered composition that assumes a single fixed viewpoint.
- Color that carries symbolic meaning ("green = success") instead of pure perceptual behavior.

## Signature prompt seed
Design the screen as a full-bleed field of thin vertical color bands at a strict pitch (bands ≈0.5% of the viewport), with red-orange, green and violet interleaved so yellow appears optically, and black used as whole bands or a full ground to induce colour. Two band layers parallax at different scroll speeds, so the field's color is a function of scroll position and pointer x, never a timer. Sections are chromatic chambers flooded with red, green or blue, blending across tall thresholds. Condensed stem-heavy headlines and all text sit in solid colour on flat panels, never on the stripes. One circle interrupts the field. No shadowed cards, no shadows; flat fills only behind text.
