# Jesús Rafael Soto — Kinetic art (Venezuelan)

## Essence
Soto's images exist in the gap between a foreground of thin lines and a ground of stripes or air, and they only switch on when the viewer moves. Form comes from the density of many identical strokes, never from outlines, so a solid shape seen from far away turns into air up close.

## Key works examined
- **Première vibration, 1957**: wire in front of black-and-white stripes. The moiré comes from layering. *(not obtained)*
- **Vibration, 1960 (MoMA)**: oil and wire on wood, 99.7 × 99.7 × 2.5 cm. Only 2.5 cm deep, yet it reads as deep. *(not obtained: moma.org blocked)*
- **Light Trap, 1965 (Tate)**: wood, nylon and printed paper. Cheap, thin materials turned into shimmer. *(visually checked: Tate T03770. A black box #2C2A2F/#3F3D46 (~52% of the face) frames a central panel of vertical stripes, beige paper #BCAC98 with dark lines at ~28% duty, ~57 lines across, pitch ≈1.7% of the panel width; the panel is ~36% of the face; one thin vertical rod hangs in front at the centre line. No saturated colour.)*
- **Volume suspendu, 1968 (Pompidou)**: blue and black volumes of 63 rods each (rows of 11 alternating with 10) in front of a striped panel. *(visually checked in the retry pass: Centre Pompidou installation photo. Two rod volumes hang side by side from painted top plates, the left one blue #1B69A4, the right one black, in front of a white panel of fine vertical lines that is narrower and much lower than the volumes (≈47% of the frame width, ≈30% of its height). Each volume hangs half over the panel and half against the dark wall, so the rods only vibrate where they cross the stripes. The rod count and the 11/10 row offset cannot be verified from one photo.)*
- **Penetrables, 1967 onward**: thousands of single-colour tubes hung like rain. BBL Jaune (1999) fills more than 150 m³. *(BBL Jaune not obtained; **Pénétrable de Chicago, 1971**, AIC 38094, checked from the museum photo: white/clear vertical strands hung from a white ceiling grid over the whole room; colourless, the strands read as a pale haze #C1B0AC–#CABEBE against the gallery wall, and the visitors' bodies become the only dense, coloured mass.)*
- **Houston Penetrable, 2004–14**: 24,000 PVC tubes, 28 ft tall. A yellow ellipse inked across clear tubes reads as a floating orb. *(not obtained: mfah.org 403; Commons rate-limited)*
- **Sphère Lutétia, 1996**: a Virtual Volume. Coloured beams make a sphere from afar and come apart as you approach. *(not obtained)*
- **Horizontal Movement, 1963** *(added, visually checked: Tate T00649)*: a white board #E5E5E0 whose top ~40% is left empty; the lower ~55% carries ~126 horizontal black lines at ~50% duty (pitch ≈0.36% of the height); one black rod crosses in front at ~10° and casts a shadow.
- **Cardinal, 1965** *(added, visually checked: Tate T00793)*: horizontal grey hairline ground (#5D5D5E) in a black frame; ~40 black, violet and pale-lilac rods zigzag across it at 2–20°, most under 10°. The violet (#352B47) is the only hue, and it is near-black.
- **Colour volumes, Centre Pompidou (added in the retry pass, substitutes for the colour Penetrables)** *(visually checked: Pompidou photos)*: **Vibration jaune, 1965** (106 × 106 cm): the top ≈50% of the board is flat lemon yellow #DCCA16, the bottom ≈50% a grey hairline ground crossed by loose yellow wires, split by one vertical nylon thread on the centre line. **Cube bleu interne, 1976**: a cube of black rods whose middle segments are painted blue (#16548B in the photo), so a smaller blue cube appears inside with no outline. **Grand carré cadmio, 1984**: white rods on a white hairline ground with a faint cadmium square in the centre, barely above the ground (chroma ≤24). **Courbes immatérielles jaunes, 1979**: thin yellow strands over a pale grey ground, with the hue only in the strands (a few % of the area). **Cube pénétrable, 1996** (450 × 500 × 400 cm): grey strands with a muted crimson inner volume (#4C2E39 through the strands).
- **Twelve Blacks and Four Silvers, 1966** *(added, visually checked: Tate T03769)*: a 4 × 4 grid of squares (each ~1/7 of the width) floating in front of fine vertical lines (~347 across, pitch ≈0.29% of the width, ~58% dark); the top three rows are matte black, the bottom row brushed silver, i.e. one row in four is the accent, by material not hue.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Jesús Rafael Soto.** *Hang a sparse layer of identical strokes a small gap in front of a dense one, draw no shape, and let form exist only as density, as one hue, and as the interference the viewer's own movement makes.*
Process:
1. Lay the ground: identical parallel lines at a pitch of 0.3–0.4% of the field (1.7% in the light variant), with a dark share of 50–58% (≈28% in the light variant). Confine it to a zone of 36–60% of the face. The rest is flat: a dark frame, or blank board.
2. Hang the second plane a small gap in front: 1–40 identical strokes, as thin as the ground lines, parallel to them or crossing at under 10° (a few up to 20°). Keep it sparse, so that the ground shows through everywhere.
3. Draw no outline. A body is the region where the strokes change one property (hue along a segment, finish, density), and its edge is only where that property stops.
4. Use one hue per work; everything else is white, grey or black. The hue is carried by the strokes of the body (a few % of the area) or by one flat half of the board (≈50%), never by trim.
5. Leave it still. The only motion is the viewer's: moving shifts the planes against each other, and a touch displaces the strokes, which settle back by themselves.
6. Allow two readings by distance: from far away the strokes fuse into one mass; close up the mass comes apart into strands that can be counted or entered.
7. Mode for interface use: the two-plane zone is decoration, with no text and no control on it; they sit on the flat part left by step 1.
Why it works:
- Two near-identical line systems a small offset apart beat against each other: a shift of a fraction of a pitch moves the interference by many pitches, so a tiny movement of the viewer gives a large and exactly proportional change. Thicken or multiply the front strokes and they occlude the ground and become objects before a backdrop.
- Strokes that share one property group by likeness, and the eye closes the group into a volume. An outline would make it a flat shape; several hues would make the strokes a pattern and not a body.
- Fine lines fuse into tone with distance, so the same marks are solid from afar and air up close. Presence becomes a function of how far away the viewer stands.
Colour relations: achromatic ground and frame; one hue, either ≤5% as strokes or ≈50% as one flat half; no second hue (a second emphasis is a change of finish, on one row in four); inactive strokes are a lighter neutral, not a tint of the hue.
Space and line: ground pitch 0.3–0.4%, 50–58% dark; zone 36–60% of the face and the rest flat; 1–40 front strokes at under 10°; gap about 1–3% of the width (from catalogue dimensions, not seen); no outlines; depth from interference, not from shadow.
Type requirement: monolinear, low-contrast letters in light weights, evenly tracked so that a label is a row of equal strokes; the heaviest mark on the surface is the hue field, never a word; all text on the flat part, at full reading contrast.
Motion and interaction: user-driven only. Pointer, scroll or tilt moves the front plane 2–6px (or at 0.9× of scroll); a touch parts the nearby strokes, which return on a damped spring in 800–1200ms. No autoplay. Under reduced motion the planes sit at one composed offset. A selection changes the body (which strokes carry the hue) and also a label or mark on the flat part, never the hue alone.
Fails when: text or controls must cover most of the surface (no flat part is left); several categories each need a colour (status, charts); a low-density or scaled display aliases the ground; the device gives no continuous input (no pointer, scroll or tilt).
Signature (do not carry together): lemon yellow · thin rods or tubes hanging before hairline stripes · a sphere that dissolves as you approach.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Soto's procedure → a backup and storage dashboard in warm dark browns (desktop, 1440 × 900). Dark, dense, utility.
1. *Ground.* A zone on the right, 58% of the face (835 × 900px), holds vertical lines at a 4px pitch (0.28% of the width), 2px line and 2px gap: about 208 lines in a brown two steps lighter than the ground (contrast 1.6:1, so it is tone, not glare). The left 42% is flat and holds every label, number and button.
2. *Second plane.* 24 thin strokes, one per top-level folder, 2px wide, drawn 12px "in front" (a separate layer) and leaning 3–8° off vertical.
3. *No outline.* The selected folder's share of the disk is a region of ground lines whose middle segments change: 31% of the lines, over the central 60% of their height. Its edge is where the changed segments stop.
4. *One hue.* A muted sea-green, only on those segments (about 4% of the screen). Everything else is brown, cream or near-black. A folder that failed its last backup is not a second colour: its stroke is dashed, and the flat list says "failed" in words.
5. *Stillness.* Nothing plays. The pointer's x moves the front strokes by up to 5px against the ground, so the zone shimmers as the hand moves and stops with it. Reduced motion: a fixed 2px offset.
6. *Two distances.* At overview the green segments read as one block of "used". Opening the folder (in the flat list) splits it: each line becomes one file type, named in the list.
7. *Interface mode.* The zone is `aria-hidden` and not focusable; selection happens in the list, which also shows a mark and the percentage beside the chosen row.
- *Type.* A light monolinear face, labels in tracked capitals at 12px, figures at 28px; the green block outweighs every word.
Result: a brown engineering panel with a quiet body of green lines beside a plain list. No yellow, no rods on a white wall, no sphere. What survived is two sparse and dense planes, form as a property change with no contour, and one hue.

## Space & composition
- **Two planes, small gap.** Every Vibration stacks a striped ground, a gap of about 1–3 cm, then sparse lines. The effect is parallax between planes, not anything drawn. In the works checked the foreground is very sparse: one rod (Light Trap, Horizontal Movement) up to ~40 rods (Cardinal).
- **Framed or half-empty fields.** Light Trap puts its stripe panel (~36% of the face) inside a black box, and Horizontal Movement leaves the top ~40% of the board blank. The stripe field is a zone, not always edge to edge.
- **Volume as density.** A Virtual Volume is a grid of identical strokes, and the coloured ones fill a sphere, ellipse or cube. No outline is ever drawn.
- **Phase shift.** Rows of 11/10/11/10 offset the grid by half a pitch, like brickwork, so it never looks static.
- **Whole field, edge to edge.** Penetrables hang from a full ceiling grid. Composition means picking which region of a uniform field gets saturated.
- **Scale jumps.** The same idea works at 55 cm (the Lutétia model) and at 28 ft (Houston).

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

One saturated hue sits in a neutral field of stripes, clear plastic or white. Colour marks where the virtual body is and is never decoration.

| Hex | Role |
|---|---|
| `#E5E5E0` | Ground: off-white wall or gallery air (Horizontal Movement board) |
| `#2C2A2F` | Stripe/rod black: the vibration lines and box frames (Light Trap; scans read as soft black, never pure) |
| `#DCCA16` | Signature yellow: the virtual volume (Vibration jaune 1965, flat painted field ≈50% of the board; Houston and BBL Jaune still not obtained) |
| `#1B69A4` | Soto blue: a second volume or alternate state (Volume suspendu rods, photo; Cube bleu interne reads darker, #16548B) |
| `#CABEBE` | Translucent "clear tube": inactive strands (Pénétrable de Chicago strands against the wall, warm-lit; lighting-dependent) |
| `#352B47` | Rare accent, replacing the old red: Cardinal's violet rods, near-black. The only red seen is muted (Cube pénétrable's inner volume, #4C2E39 through grey strands) |
| `#BCAC98` | Paper beige (added): Light Trap's striped panel |

Measured: the six first-pass works are achromatic or nearly so (chroma ≤5 except Light Trap's beige paper). The colour works checked in the retry pass confirm the rule: one hue per work (yellow, blue or crimson), carried by rods or strands, or by one flat half of the board; the rest stays white, grey or black. Hairline grounds run at ~50–58% dark duty (Horizontal Movement, Twelve Blacks) or ~28% (Light Trap), not 1-in-4.

## Form, line & typography
Soto's whole vocabulary is the thin line repeated: hairline stripes, straight rods, and small T-shaped pieces he called "Tes." For type, that means monolinear, low-contrast geometric letters. Track uppercase labels at +0.08em so they read as evenly spaced rods. Body text should be light and evenly textured, like a striped ground.
- **Space Grotesk** or **Jost**: geometric, monolinear headings.
- **IBM Plex Mono**: even pitch for data, which echoes the rod grid.
- Avoid heavy weights. The heaviest mark on the page should be a coloured field, not a word.

## Rhythm, motion & time
The work changes only when the viewer moves, so motion should come from the user (cursor, scroll, tilt), never from autoplay. Moiré turns a small offset into a large visual change. Penetrables sway when touched, then settle: a damped spring with a slow return over about 800–1200 ms. From far away you see the shape, and up close you see the strands.

## UI translations
1. **Layout:** Put a ground of fine black lines (`repeating-linear-gradient`, pitch ≈0.3–0.4% of the field, e.g. 2px line + 2px gap, ~50% dark; or 1px in 4px for a lighter ~28% field) behind one to a few dozen foreground rules, and move the foreground 2–6px with the cursor so moiré shimmers.
2. **Layout:** Build hero shapes from a grid of 2px vertical bars. Colour the bars inside an ellipse and leave the rest at 15% grey, with no outline.
3. **Layout:** Offset alternate rows of repeated items (cards, icons, list markers) by half a column, the way the 11/10 rows do in Volume suspendu.
4. **Color:** Keep 90% of the screen achromatic. Use one saturated hue (`#DCCA16`) only for the "virtual volume", meaning the current selection or primary state. Never use it on chrome.
5. **Color:** Show inactive items as translucent strands at 30–40% opacity on the ground, and saturate them when active, the way the Houston yellow sits among clear tubes.
6. **Type:** Set nav and labels in tracked uppercase monolinear type at small size, spaced evenly, so the header reads as a row of rods.
7. **Motion:** Tie the colour field to scroll. As the user scrolls, the coloured bars move across the striped grid with a slight lag (0.9× scroll speed) so the shape seems to float in a separate plane.
8. **Motion:** On hover, push nearby bars aside with a damped spring (stiffness about 120, damping about 8) and let them sway back. This is the curtain of tubes parting around a hand.
9. **Interaction:** Use semantic zoom on approach. At overview zoom a dataset reads as one coloured mass. Zooming or opening it splits the mass into its individual strands (rows or items), the way Sphère Lutétia comes apart as you walk toward it.
10. **Texture:** Swap drop shadows for depth through interference. Separate layers with rods that cross the ground at small angles (Cardinal: most under 10°, a few up to ~20°) instead of using blur or elevation.

## What this artist would reject
- Elevation shadows and frosted-glass blur as fake depth. Real depth here comes from parallax between planes.
- Shapes defined by outlines and borders. Soto's volumes have no edges.
- Autoplay carousels and looping hero animations that move whether or not anyone is there.
- Rainbow palettes and gradient fills. He uses one hue at a time, placed against black and white.
- Heavy display type that fights the field. The image, not the word, carries the weight.
- A lone object centred in empty space.

## Signature prompt seed
Design the screen as a two-plane vibration field. Behind everything, lay a ground of fine black lines on off-white at a ~4px pitch and ~50% dark duty. In front, use sparse vertical bars that shift a few pixels with the cursor so moiré shimmers. Imply the main element as a Soto virtual volume: an ellipse made only of coloured bars in saturated yellow among translucent grey ones, with no outline. Tracked monolinear uppercase labels act as rods. Motion only responds to the user, with spring-damped sway. Zooming in breaks the mass into individual strands.
