# Bridget Riley — Op Art

## Essence
Riley builds motion from a repeated unit with one variable changed at a steady rate: the width of a square, the curve of a line, the step of a grey. The surface is flat, still and made without visible touch, yet the eye produces the movement. Her subject is perception, set off by a strict system of tempo.

## Key works examined
- **Movement in Squares, 1961**: a checkerboard whose columns squeeze into thin slivers right of centre. It shows that progressive compression of one axis reads as a fold in space. *(visually checked: Arts Council Collection image. 12 rows of equal height; only the column widths change. Full squares (≈8.3% of the width) hold for the left quarter, then narrow steadily to 4 px slivers (≈0.5%, an 18:1 squeeze) at ≈65% of the width, and reopen to only ≈75% of full width at the right edge. Black and white are exactly 50/50; no grey, no colour.)*
- **Fall, 1963** (Tate, 141 × 140 cm, PVA emulsion on hardboard): black waves over white primer at a roughly constant 5–6 mm pitch while the radius tightens. Keep the spacing fixed and vary the curvature. *(visually checked: Tate scan T00616. 107 black stripes across the width at every height, so the pitch is constant at ~0.93% of the width per black+white pair, black share 46%. The waves are long and slow over the top ~70% and tighten to several short ripples per 10% of height in the bottom quarter.)*
- **Hesitate, 1964**: circles flatten to ellipses, narrowest about one third from the top. The grey sequence steps 0→24 in twos, speeds up, then slows. Tempo is a designed curve. *(visually checked: Tate scan T04132. Ellipse height falls from 48 px (full circle) to 13 px at 30% from the top, a 3.7:1 squash, over ~30% of the height, then reopens over ~25%; the bottom 45% are undistorted circles. Greys run diagonally from near-white to near-black corner to corner, and the order flips inside the squashed band. Ground #EEF0EB covers 57%.)*
- **Cataract 3, 1967** (222 × 223 cm): vermilion and turquoise twisted along curves. Grey at the edges cools the hues and saturation peaks in the centre. Color pairs change wherever lines meet. *(visually checked: secondary source, artchive.com, Cataract 3, 400 px; colour unreliable, the turquoise reads blue-violet there.)* Seen: square; ≈40 parallel wavy lines on white run as a rising diagonal (≈20° up to the right), each line swelling and thinning along its wave so the field seems to twist; each line is a pair of two colours. The warm red component is strongest in a horizontal band through the middle and fades to dark blue-grey toward the top and bottom edges, so the gradient runs across the lines, not along them. *(Earlier, Substitute checked: **Turquoise and Red Greys, Sequence Study, 1967**, AIC 158410: full vermilion #D6483E and turquoise #0790A8 chevrons at the top edge decay over ~80% of the height into greys #7E7B7D/#72838A of the SAME lightness (CIE L 51–56 throughout). Saturation peaks at one edge in this study, not in a central band.)*
- **Late Morning, 1967–68 / Rise I, 1968**: vertical stripe sequences. A narrow stripe changes the color of its neighbours, so you design the adjacencies, not the swatches. *(Late Morning visually checked: Tate scan T01032. Hundreds of equal-width vertical stripes in red #C1523F, green #659F67, blue #636E97 and pale tints, separated by white #E6E2D3 (36% of the area); red is ~16%. No black. Rise I not obtained.)*
- **Evoë 3, 2003, and Arcadia (wall painting, 2008)**: later curves placed freely, with large planes of soft color locked together. They borrow the "processional rhythms" of Mantegna and Raphael. *(not obtained; substitute checked: **Nataraja, 1993**, Tate T06859: about 12 hues in vertical columns cut into parallelograms by one fixed diagonal; no single hue covers more than 16%; blue #3867AA, ochre #DBB241, violet #7B76BA, red #B5544C, greens #7EB458/#336C53, pink #C77CAB.)*
- **Deny II, 1967** *(added, visually checked: Tate T02030)*: 19 × 20 grid of small ellipses on mid-grey #535355 (97% of the surface dark). The ellipses tilt progressively and lighten toward an inverted V near the top centre, then fade into the ground below. The dots are only ~2% of the area: the whole effect comes from tone and angle steps.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Cataract 3): one repeated unit (a wavy line) at a constant pitch over the whole square, on a diagonal; no figure, no edge treatment.
- **Colour relations** (Cataract 3): two hues per line on a white ground; the warm one is strongest in the middle band and recedes toward the edges, a vertical gradient laid across a diagonal pattern; white stays ≈half the surface.
- **Line and edge:** line width modulates along each wave (thick crest, thin flank); edges hard.
- **The procedure:** repeat one curved line at a fixed pitch across the field; modulate its width along its length; colour each line as a pair; shift the pair's balance gradually from the centre band to the edges.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Bridget Riley.** *Repeat one unit over the whole field, lock every property but one, and move that one along a single axis on a composed tempo: long calm, geometric build, off-centre climax, shorter release.*
Process:
1. Fix the unit and the lattice. One shape, tiled edge to edge with a constant count per row (12–20 rows, or 40–110 lines), in a format with no direction of its own (near 1:1). Margin, colour pair and weight are locked from here on.
2. Choose ONE variable and ONE axis: the unit's width, height, curvature, tilt, value step or chroma. A second variable is allowed only on the perpendicular axis, on its own curve. Counts never change, so every difference the eye finds is that variable.
3. Place the climax (the extreme value of the variable) off-centre on that axis: at 30–35% or 65–70% of it, or at the far edge. Never at 50%.
4. Compose the tempo around the climax: hold the resting value over 25–45% of the axis; build in geometric steps (each about 0.8 of the last) over 30–40% of it, to a total ratio between 3.7:1 and 18:1; release over a shorter run (about 0.85 of the build) and stop short of rest (≈75%), or omit the release when the climax is at the edge.
5. If the variable is colour, change chroma only: hold lightness within about 5 L from full strength down to neutral, alternate two opposed hues, and keep full chroma inside the climax band (≤15–20% of the axis). In a two-value field keep the dark share at 46–50%.
6. Mode for interface use: where the units are content (rows, columns, items), every unit is a flat surface and only the intervals follow the curve. Where the field is a pattern, it is decoration: it carries no text and no control, covers a bounded zone, and has a flat substitute the user can switch to.
Why it works:
- A regular gradient of size is the cue the eye uses for a surface turning away, so a smooth change in one dimension reads as a fold or as speed. Because the count per row is constant, the change is read as one surface bending, not as different objects. Vary two things on one axis and it reads as noise.
- Adaptation: the long calm stretch sets the rate the eye expects, so the build is felt as acceleration and the climax as a place. Centred, the climax becomes a symmetric target and the field its frame; with build and release equal, it becomes a mirrored ornament and stops being an event.
- Chroma changed at constant lightness alters intensity without altering depth or weight, so the colour peak stays in the same surface as the neutral and does not punch a hole in it.
Colour relations: two values at 46–50% dark and nothing else; or one opposed pair plus a neutral of the same lightness, with full chroma on ≤15–20% of the axis; or many hues (≈12), none above 16%, with a light interval of about a third of the area between them. Every colour is judged beside its real neighbour, never as a swatch.
Space and line: full bleed, margin under 1%; constant count; calm 25–45% : build 30–40% : release shorter than the build; climax at 30/70 or at the edge; extreme ratio 3.7:1–18:1; hard edges, no outline, no shadow, no trace of the hand.
Type requirement: one weight; rhythm from width or tracking, stepped on the same curve (a family with a continuous width range of at least 1.6:1); anything the user must read stays at the wide end, at full size, on a flat panel.
Motion and interaction: the tempo is already in the layout, so nothing has to move. Entrances stagger on the curve (intervals shrinking 40, 30, 20, 10, 5ms). A pattern field may shift phase by up to one pitch with scroll or pointer, in cycles of 2s or longer, never passing from light to dark more than 3 times a second; under reduced motion it is still. A compressed row may be DRAWN 16–20px high, but its pointer target stays ≥44px: rows drawn smaller share one target that reopens them, and keyboard focus always opens a row to full height.
Fails when: fewer than about 12 repeats lie on the axis (no curve can be read); the units are unequal (mixed cards, media); every row must be read or compared at the same size (tables, forms); text or controls would sit on the pattern; a full-contrast periodic field with a 6–16px period fills the viewport (pattern glare).
Signature (do not carry together): black and white only · a checkerboard or waves squeezed to slivers · a vermilion and turquoise pair dying into grey.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Riley's procedure → a bank-statement screen in warm paper tones (phone, 390 × 844). No stripe field anywhere.
1. *Unit and lattice.* The unit is the transaction row: full width, one weight, the same two paper tones alternating (1.5 L apart). 15 rows on the screen under an 84px flat header.
2. *Variable and axis.* Row height only, along the vertical axis (time). Type size, tones and the 16px side margin never change.
3. *Climax.* The month boundary, at y = 599 (71% of the height).
4. *Tempo.* Calm: the five newest rows at 64px (320px, 42% of the list). Build: six rows of 52, 42, 34, 27, 22, 18px (195px; each ≈0.81 of the last; 64:18 = 3.6:1). Release: four rows of 26, 38, 48, 48px (160px = 0.82 of the build), stopping at 75% of rest. These are the earlier months' summaries.
5. *Colour.* Not the variable, so it is not touched: no accent at the climax. The fold alone marks it.
6. *Interface mode.* Rows of 44px or more are tappable. The six rows drawn below 44px (34, 27, 22, 18, 26, 38) form one 165px target; a tap or focus reopens them to 64px, 40ms apart at first, then 30, 20, 10, 5. They show the amount only, at 12px.
- *Type.* One weight. The payee name narrows in width from 100% to 62% across the build; amounts stay at normal width in tabular figures.
- *Reduced motion.* The reopening is instant. Nothing else moves.
Result: a quiet cream statement that seems to fold away at last month. No black and white, no waves, no optical buzz. What survived is one variable on one axis, the off-centre climax and the asymmetric tempo.

## Space & composition
- **One unit, one variable.** In *Fall* only the curvature changes, never the pitch. That is why the motion reads as smooth, not noisy.
- **Off-centre climax.** The crush in *Movement in Squares* falls at ≈65% of the width (measured), after a ramp over ≈40% of the width and a shorter, incomplete release; the tightest ellipse in *Hesitate* sits at 30% from the top (measured), and in *Fall* the tightest ripples sit in the bottom quarter. Put the peak density at a 30/70 split (or at the very edge), never in the middle.
- **Asymmetric ramp.** In *Hesitate* the squash builds over ~30% of the height, releases over ~25%, and leaves the remaining ~45% calm. Compression is a local event, not a whole-screen gradient.
- **Edge-to-edge field, tiny margin.** A thin primer border is the only frame. In UI: full bleed with a 4–8px quiet gutter, not a card floating in padding.
- **Density gradient instead of hierarchy boxes.** Things get closer together as they approach what matters.
- **Square formats.** *Fall* and *Cataract 3* are about 1:1, so all direction comes from the pattern.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

Color arrived in 1967 as relationships: complementary pairs in a regular beat, grey to quiet the edges, and saturation pushed to a central peak ("a sudden flood of bright colour"). Later stripes drop the black and white separators so hues touch directly.

| Hex | Role |
|---|---|
| `#EEF0EB` | Primer white: ground and negative stripe (Hesitate ground, 57% of the area; Fall's lightest white reads #FDFDF6) |
| `#35352F` | Mars-black: line and positive stripe (Fall darkest 5%; black is ~46% of Fall) |
| `#7E7B7D` | Mid grey: edge damper, inactive state (end greys of the 1967 sequence study; Deny II uses a darker ground #535355) |
| `#D6483E` | Vermilion: climax accent, used only at the peak (1967 study; Late Morning red #C1523F) |
| `#0790A8` | Turquoise: vermilion's partner, in alternate bands (1967 study) |

Measured: when colour fades to grey it keeps its lightness (the 1967 study holds L 51–56 from full vermilion/turquoise to grey), so build the ramp by lowering chroma at constant OKLCH L, not by mixing toward white or black.


## Form, line & typography
Riley's line is impersonal: compass, T-square and template, painted by assistants "unchecked by any kind of touch." Type should feel as mechanical and be able to *compress progressively*, like her squares.
- **Archivo** (variable width axis 62–125): animate or step `font-stretch` across a line or list so letters narrow toward a focal point.
- **Instrument Sans** or **Inter Tight**: neutral, even-stroke text faces that won't add "touch."
Use one weight. Make rhythm with tracking and width, not bold.

## Rhythm, motion & time
Grey let her tempo be "accelerated … or delayed": *Hesitate* speeds up, then slows. In UI, that is easing written into layout. Stagger on a curve (0, 40, 70, 90, 100, 105ms), not in equal steps. Motion is slow and periodic (8–20s cycles). Interaction shifts a pattern's *phase*, not its objects. The flicker belongs to the eye, so the animation itself stays calm.

## UI translations
1. **Layout:** Build lists as a compression gradient: row height falls geometrically from 64px to as little as 16–20px (Hesitate squashes 3.7:1) toward the selected item, then reopens faster than it closed; leave the rest of the list at full height. Compress the drawn row, not the label or the target: text stays full size and every row keeps a hit area of at least 44px.
2. **Layout:** Place the hero's densest point about 30% (or 70%) along its axis, or at an edge, never at 50%.
3. **Color:** Ramp saturation at constant lightness: grey (`#7E7B7D`) where things are quiet, full vermilion/turquoise only in one band at the peak (the 1967 study keeps full colour to the top ~15–20%).
4. **Color:** Choose colors as adjacent pairs. Test each swatch beside its real neighbour; a 2px turquoise rule changes a vermilion block.
5. **Type:** Use Archivo across a nav row and compress only the visual row (spacing, underline bars or stripe rhythm going from 125% to 70% toward the active item); every label, the active one included, stays full size and full width, and each item keeps a hit area of at least 44 × 44px.
6. **Motion:** Use curved stagger timing (fast then slow, or slow-fast-slow) for entrances. Never use linear 50ms steps.
7. **Motion:** Loading states: a slow wave through 1px vertical lines (≥2s per cycle), not a spinner.
8. **Interaction:** Scroll or pointer shifts the phase of a `repeating-linear-gradient` field, so the page seems to bend as you move.
9. **Texture:** Draw separators as SVG sine waves with constant pitch and amplitude rising toward content.
10. **Accessibility:** High-contrast stripes near 3 cycles/degree with a 50% duty cycle are the peak pattern-glare zone. On a laptop at about 50cm, keep black/white stripes about 3–8px wide off large areas. Use unequal widths, lower contrast or color instead.
11. **Accessibility:** Put pattern motion behind `prefers-reduced-motion`. Never exceed 3 light/dark changes per second over 25% of a 10° field (WCAG 2.3.1). Offer a "calm surface" toggle.
12. **Accessibility:** Never put body text on a vibrating field. Give it a flat primer-white panel.

## What this artist would reject
- Decorative gradients with no system: her every change is stepped and measured.
- Drop shadows and glassmorphism: her space comes from pattern, not faked light.
- Handmade quirk (brush textures, wobbly illustration) posing as personality.
- Even, centred spacing with no build-up or climax.
- Saturated accents scattered everywhere instead of peaking once.
- Moving objects around when surface rhythm could do the work.

## Signature prompt seed
Design the screen as one full-bleed square field with a single repeated unit (stripe, square or wave) where only one property changes at a time. Compress the spacing in a geometric progression toward a focal point about 30% (or 70%) along one axis. Neutral grey edges on primer white (#EEF0EB); complementary vermilion and turquoise touch only in the focal band. Set type in Archivo, narrowing its width toward the active item. Use curved stagger timing and slow phase-shifting waves. Keep text panels flat. Avoid high-contrast stripes at about 3 cycles/degree, and respect reduced motion.
