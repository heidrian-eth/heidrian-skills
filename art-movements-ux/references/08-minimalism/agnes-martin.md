# Agnes Martin — Minimalism / abstract

## Essence
Martin filled a six-foot square with a grid of slightly *rectangular* cells (in the canvases checked, tall and narrow), ruled by hand in pencil over a pale wash. The work is about near-sameness: a rigid system drawn by a hand that wavers, and a structure that, on paper, stops short of the edge so it floats (on the canvases checked it runs to the edge). Up close you see trembling graphite. From across the room you see tinted, breathing light that stands for innocence and calm without depicting anything.

## Key works examined
- **Friendship, 1963** (MoMA, 190.5 cm square): gold leaf over oil, scored by hand to reveal the ground. Teaches that a grid can be carved *out* of a surface. *(visually checked: secondary source, thecollector.com)* Seen: gold covers the entire square to within ≈1–2% of the edge, with a dark rim; the scored grid runs edge to edge as ≈24 columns × ≈40 rows of horizontal cells (≈1.8:1, wider than tall), the lines thin and darker than the gold where the ground shows through, slightly wavering. Tonal variation comes from the leaf itself (patches and creases, darker toward the bottom corners), not from the grid. The gold is a single field, not an accent: in this work it is 100% of the surface. Colour from a secondary reproduction.
- **The Tree, 1964** (72 × 72 in): planned as a postage-stamp-sized sketch with calculations, then laid out with tape, ruler and pencil. Teaches you to design the system tiny and render it huge. *(not obtained)*
- **The City, 1966** (Cleveland, acrylic and graphite): thin rectangular cells over a faintly modulated ground. Teaches that the ground should vary while the structure stays fixed. *(not obtained: CMA record is copyrighted, no image)*
- **Untitled #4 / Untitled #12, 1977** (NGA Australia / Art Institute of Chicago, ink, graphite and gesso): an uneven ink wash with pencil lines that skip on the canvas tooth. Teaches that imperfection belongs inside the rule. *(#12 visually checked: AIC scan. The wash reads grey `#DEDCD1`, not tan; cells are tall, ≈18 × 95 px on an 843 px canvas (≈1:5), 48 columns × 9 rows, and the lines run to the canvas edge)*
- **With My Back to the World, 1997** (MoMA, six 60-inch panels): blue, peach and yellow bands that look identical until you look closely. Teaches variation across a series at the threshold of notice. *(visually checked: secondary source, thecollector.com, one panel)* Seen: 8 horizontal bands of about equal height (≈1/8 each), separated by faint graphite lines, in the sequence blue, peach, yellow, blue | blue, peach, yellow, blue: a four-band phrase stated twice, with the two blues meeting at the centre line. All three hues are pale and close in lightness, so the bands separate by hue and temperature, not value. Colour from a secondary reproduction.
- **Innocent Love series, 1999–2000** and **Untitled #1, 2003**: the palette warms toward apricot, salmon and buttercup. Teaches you to hold a restrained structure while the colour gets warmer. *(not obtained)*
- **Morning, 1965** (Tate): acrylic and graphite; cells ≈10 × 48 px on a 1531 px canvas (≈1:5, tall), every fourth vertical slightly stronger, lines running to the edge. *(visually checked: Tate image)*
- **Untitled, 1961** and **Untitled, 1964** (AIC, ink on tracing paper, 1964 with pink watercolour): on paper the grid is a centred square covering ≈65–69% of the sheet width with ≈15% margins and dotted guide marks outside it; the 1964 cells are ≈1:2 tall, and the pencil ticks overshoot the top line. *(visually checked: AIC scans)*
- **Untitled #8, 1990** (AIC, ink and graphite on paper): about 68 horizontal lines (pitch ≈1.4% of the square) inside a ruled square inset ≈11% from the sheet edge; no verticals. *(visually checked: AIC scan)*
- **Untitled #5, 1994; Faraway Love, 1999; Happy Holiday, 1999** (Tate): late horizontal bands, full width, edge to edge. Happy Holiday: 14 equal bands, peach and white alternating (peach 6/14 ≈ 43% of the area). Untitled #5: a narrow pink band (≈8% of the height) every third band, white bands ≈10%. Faraway Love: four grey-blue bands alternating ≈3:5 in height, split by white strips ≈2% of the height. *(visually checked: Tate images)*

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (With My Back to the World): the square is cut into equal horizontal bands; a short colour phrase repeats; the repetition point sits at the exact centre.
- **Space** (Friendship): one undivided field with an all-over fine grid of horizontal cells; no centre, no edge emphasis beyond a thin rim.
- **Colour relations:** either one material colour over everything (gold, 100%) or 3 pale hues at near-equal lightness in strict alternation; never a contrast accent.
- **Line and edge:** hand-ruled lines, thin, slightly wavering, low contrast (graphite on paint, scored gold showing the ground).
- **The procedure:** choose one division (equal bands or a fine grid of wide cells); fill with one colour or a short phrase of pale equal-value hues; repeat the phrase; draw the divisions by hand at low contrast.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Agnes Martin.** *Hold every difference just above the threshold of notice: one even division and near-equal fills, so the surface is a single tone from far away and a patient structure up close.*
Process:
1. Take one simple format and decide its status: a panel on a page (the division floats, inset 11–15% on every side) or the surface itself (the division runs to all four edges). Never a mixture.
2. Divide it by counting, not by content: either 8–14 full-width bands of equal height, or a fine all-over grid of cells that are never square (1:2 to 1:5; 40 or more divisions on the long count). Pitch comes from the container. No cell is larger, emptier or placed specially.
3. Fill within one narrow value band: every fill within about 9 L of every other and low in chroma, so fills differ by temperature only. Order them as a short phrase of 2–4 terms and repeat it strictly (every second, every third, or the phrase stated twice about the centre line).
4. Draw the division last, with one thin line weight only 8–10 L from the ground, and let it keep the small irregularity of being made by hand. The line must be weaker than any fill difference is wide.
5. Set two reading distances: at overview the division must fuse into tone; content becomes legible only at the near scale, sitting on the lines.
Why it works:
- Fine, low-contrast detail fuses into tone. The eye gets one calm value at a distance and a countable structure up close, so hierarchy is carried by viewing distance instead of size. Raise the line contrast and it becomes a table; make the cells square and it becomes tiling.
- At equal value nothing advances or recedes, so there is no figure, no depth and no rank; differences of temperature read as light, not as objects. One darker band at once becomes a stripe on a background.
- Near-threshold differences slow the viewer: the eye has to adapt before it can see them, and that waiting is the calm. Any strong accent resets adaptation and the rest goes blank.
Colour relations: one ground at a value extreme, 45–60%; 1–3 fills within about 9 L of it and of each other, chroma low (roughly a tenth of full), 40–55% in strict alternation; division line 8–10 L from the ground; no accent at all. Text is a separate ink at reading contrast (4.5:1 or more), and it is the only strong contrast present.
Space and line: one format, centred; equal bands (two heights near 3:5 with strips of about 2% as a variant); one line weight under 0.15% of the side; margins 11–15% or none; no focal point, no cluster.
Type requirement: light and even in colour so a paragraph reads as another ruled texture; hierarchy by a small size step (about 1.25×) and by position on the lines, never by weight; small plain labels on the lines; must hold reading contrast at light weight.
Motion and interaction: opacity only, 1s or longer, row by row; zoom is the main interaction (collapsed band = tone, opened band = ruled detail); selection deepens a fill by one step and adds a mark or label, since a near-equal-value change cannot carry a state alone.
Fails when: content has priorities, alerts or a primary action; categories must be told apart (equal-value hues vanish for colour-blind users and in greyscale, so each needs a label); small or sunlit screens and low-vision users, where the division is invisible, so nothing essential may depend on it.
Signature (do not carry together): pencil-ruled grid on a six-foot square canvas · pale pink, blue and peach washes on white · the trembling hand line.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Martin's procedure → a dark-mode warehouse shift roster in charcoal (tablet, 1024 × 768).
1. *Format.* The roster is the surface: bands run to all four edges, no outer margin, no card.
2. *Division.* 14 equal bands, one per day of a fortnight, each 7.1% of the height (≈55px, so every band is a 44px+ target). Within a band, 48 half-hour cells ≈21 × 55px (1:2.6).
3. *Fills.* Ground L 16. Three shift kinds at L 22, 23 and 24, chroma about a tenth of full: a cool, a warm and a green-leaning grey. Phrase: early, late, night, repeated by rota, so the fortnight is the phrase stated twice about the centre line. Each filled run also carries its letter (E, L, N) at reading contrast, because the three fills are identical in greyscale.
4. *Line.* Half-hour and day rules 1px at L 25 (9 L above ground), with ±0.3px vertical jitter per rule. Every fourth vertical (two hours) is 2 L stronger.
5. *Two distances.* At full view: one faintly banded charcoal tone showing only where the fortnight is heavy. Opening a day expands its band to 40% of the height and names, times and breaks appear at L 80 on the rules.
- *Type.* One light weight, 15px and 19px, names lowercase on the lines; no bold.
- *Motion.* Bands fade in over 1.1s, 70ms apart. Selecting a shift raises its fill 3 L and adds a 2px end mark and the worker's name.
Result: a dark, dense operations tool. No pastel, no square canvas, no pencil. What survived: one counted division, fills held inside one value band, a line weaker than the fills, and detail that exists only up close.

## Space & composition
- **The square holds a grid that is not square.** Martin said rectangles "a little bit off the square" lighten the square's weight. In the checked canvases the cells are *tall*: ≈1:5 (Morning 1965, Untitled #12 1977), ≈1:2 in a 1964 drawing, with horizontal lines spaced 4–5× wider than the verticals. In UI: use a square container with tall narrow cells (1:2 to 1:5) or full-width bands, never square cells.
- **The grid floats on paper, runs out on canvas.** On the works on paper the ruled square sits centred with ≈11–15% margins of bare sheet. On every canvas checked (1965, 1977, 1994, 1999) the lines and bands run to the canvas edge. Inset rules by 11–15% when the panel sits on a page; run them full-bleed when the panel *is* the surface.
- **All-over, no focal point.** No cell outranks another. Hierarchy comes from *distance*: up close it is detail, from far away it is tone.
- **Density as texture.** Cells a few millimetres tall on a 183 cm field read as a surface value, not as information.
- **Late bands.** From the 1990s, full-width horizontal bands split by single graphite lines: equal bands with every other or every third one tinted (Happy Holiday: 14 × 7.1%; Untitled #5: tinted bands slightly narrower, ≈8% vs ≈10%), or two alternating heights ≈3:5 separated by thin white strips ≈2% of the height (Faraway Love). The earlier "1:1:2:1:1" pattern was not seen.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

The method is translucent wash over bright white. When a colour felt too heavy she added white wash until it gave off light. Values sit within a few percent of each other, so contrast comes from hue, not value; and the hue is barely there: the pink of Untitled #5 measures C*3, the grey-blue of Faraway Love C*1, the peach of Happy Holiday C*8–12. The white grounds in the Tate and AIC scans are neutral to cool (C*0–1), not cream; only the works on paper are warm (aged sheet, 1961 tan ink wash `#DEBC9B`). At full-canvas view the graphite lines sit only ≈8–10 L* below the ground.

| Hex | Role |
|---|---|
| `#ECECEC` | Gesso ground: the default surface (Morning 1965, 43%) |
| `#E1DAD8` | Pale pink wash: section band (Untitled #5 1994) |
| `#D0D1D3` | Duck-egg blue wash: alternate band, selected state (Faraway Love 1999; reads grey-blue, almost neutral) |
| `#F7F1DE` | Pale lemon: highlight, success (nearest real: cream sheet of Untitled #8 1990; no lemon wash in the checked works) |
| `#D9D6CB` | Tan ink wash (1977): secondary panels (Untitled #12 1977 actually reads grey; warm tan only in the 1961 drawing, `#DEBC9B`) |
| `#B3B0A5` | Graphite: rules and text (line as seen at canvas scale, Untitled #12; the pencil itself is darker) |
| `#C2AB78` | Scored gold (Friendship): only as a whole-field material, never as an accent or action colour (Friendship seen in a secondary reproduction: there the gold is the whole field, scored) |
| `#E6D6CA` | Peach band (Happy Holiday 1999, ≈43% of the surface) |

Keep every band's L* (lightness) between 84 and 93 and chroma at or below 12. Text is graphite, never black.

## Form, line & typography
Her line is a ruled pencil stroke: straight in intent, uneven in pressure, about a hairline at screen scale. Type should be just as thin and even. Use 300 weights, wide tracking and tall leading, and never bold. Her titles are lowercase and plain ("the tree", "friendship").
- **Jost** 300: geometric, quiet, with even stroke width.
- **Cormorant Garamond** 300 italic: rare, gentle titles.
- **IBM Plex Mono** 300: tiny metadata, like her calculations in the margin.

## Rhythm, motion & time
The work changes with viewing distance and time, not with events. Bands fade in over 900–1400 ms (`cubic-bezier(.4,0,.2,1)`), staggered 60–80 ms per row from the top, like a wash spreading. No bounce, scale, or translation over 4 px. An optional ambient cycle moves between two adjacent tints over 20–40 s. Scrolling should feel like crossing an empty beach: long runs of near-identical rows and no parallax.

## UI translations
1. **Layout:** Make the main screen a centred square panel (`aspect-ratio: 1; width: 72vmin`). If it sits on a page, inset its ruled area 11–15% (the drawings); if it fills the view, run rules to the edge (the canvases).
2. **Layout:** Use tall narrow cells (1:2 to 1:5, horizontals 4–5× farther apart than verticals) or full-width bands (equal, or two heights at ≈3:5), with sizes derived from container width rather than from content.
3. **Line:** Draw dividers as 0.5–1 px `#9A9893` rules at 65% opacity (≈`#B3B0A5`, only 8–10 L* below the ground), with an SVG `feTurbulence` displacement of 0.3 px so each rule wobbles slightly, like a hand-ruled line (wobble not resolvable in the available scans).
4. **Color:** Give each section an `rgba` wash at 0.25–0.4 alpha over the gesso ground, with 2–3% grain noise showing through.
5. **Color:** For categories, rotate hue and hold lightness fixed, so bands are indistinguishable in greyscale.
6. **Type:** Use Jost 300 at 16 px, letter-spacing 0.04em, line-height 1.8. Headings get a 1.25× size step and no weight change.
7. **Type:** Set labels in lowercase mono, sitting on the ruled lines like notes written between pencil lines.
8. **Motion:** Make entrances opacity-only, 1 s or longer, row by row.
9. **Interaction:** On hover, deepen a row's wash by one step (alpha +0.1). No shadows, lift or colour inversion.
10. **Interaction:** Use distance as zoom. A collapsed view shows only tinted bands, and expanding a band reveals the fine ruled detail, like walking up to the canvas.
11. **Emphasis:** Mark the one primary action per screen with a graphite hairline outline and its label, never a filled button and never an accent colour (gold appears only as a whole field in Friendship, not as an accent).
12. **Empty states:** Show faint ruled lines, no illustration.

## What this artist would reject
- Elevated cards with drop shadows and 16 px radii.
- Saturated brand accents and pure black text.
- Bold-weight hierarchy and giant hero metrics.
- Hero images and focal-point layouts.
- Springy micro-animations and confetti.
- Pixel-identical, machined repetition with no trace of a hand.

## Signature prompt seed
Design this screen as a pale square field: cool gesso-white ground (#ECECEC), barely tinted washes of pink, grey-blue and peach, all at equal lightness (L* 84–93, chroma ≤12). Divide it into tall narrow cells or full-width bands, never squares, ruled with faint wavering graphite hairlines (only 8–10 L* darker than the ground) that stop 11–15% short of the panel edge when it sits on a page, so the structure floats. Use Jost Light, lowercase, widely spaced, with no bold anywhere. Remove all shadows, cards and icons. From a distance it should read as breathing light, and up close as trembling pencil. Use only slow opacity fades and one graphite hairline-outlined action. The mood is innocence and calm.
