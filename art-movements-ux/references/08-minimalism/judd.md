# Donald Judd — Minimalism

## Essence
Judd made boxes that are neither painting nor sculpture: factory-made units, identical or sized by a stated number series, set out in real space as "one thing after another" (*Specific Objects*). Nothing balances anything else. The order comes from interval, material and the whole run of units, never from a focal point.

## Key works examined
- **Untitled (Stack), 1967 (MoMA)**: twelve galvanized-iron boxes, each 9" high with 9" gaps. Solid and void are equal, and the unit count is cut down to fit the ceiling. It teaches gap = element, and that the count may change while the module stays fixed. *(MoMA image not obtainable; checked instead on two other stacks, see below)*
- **Untitled (stack), 1968 (AIC)**: ten stainless-steel units with amber Plexiglas floors, 6" units with 6" gaps. Steel on the outside, colour inside, and the amber throws a pink-orange glow onto the wall behind. *(visually checked: AIC scan)*
- **Untitled (stack), 1980 (Tate)**: ten units, blue Perspex front, aluminium sides and top. A vertical pixel scan reads front 64 px / gap 64 px all the way up. *(visually checked: Tate image)*
- **Untitled (progression), 1967 (Indianapolis)**: red boxes grow geometrically from right to left while the voids shrink. The largest box is ≈11× the length of the smallest. It teaches one rule that drives both the solids and the spaces between them. *(visually checked: Newfields (Indianapolis Museum of Art) collection image, object 40381)* Seen: a single pale brass bar runs the full length on top; five red enamel units hang under it. Measured left to right, the units are 379, 190, 95, 54, 34 px (≈11 : 5.6 : 2.8 : 1.6 : 1) and the gaps between them 19, 46, 88, 176 px (≈1 : 2.4 : 4.6 : 9.3). So the solids halve and the voids double, in opposite directions; the largest unit is ≈11× the smallest, not 16× (the two smallest steps fall short of exact halving). The bar is the constant that makes the series readable.
- **Untitled, 1969 (Van Abbe)**: polished aluminum blocks sized 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21. It teaches a spacing scale you can see, not one hidden in tokens. *(not obtained; Fibonacci claim unverified)*
- **Untitled, 1973 (Tate)**: a polished copper wall bar: a flat-faced block at the left, then half-cylinder segments cut by lens-shaped openings. Along the mid-line the solid segments stay about 150 px while the openings shrink 184 → 136 → 96 px left to right, roughly equal steps. It teaches arithmetic steps as an alternative to Fibonacci, and that the progression can live in the voids. *(visually checked: Tate image; the 1.5" figure itself cannot be measured from a photo)*
- **100 untitled works in mill aluminum, 1982–86 (Chinati, Marfa)**: every box is 41 × 51 × 72", set in a strict three-row grid. Each interior is different: halved vertically, horizontally or diagonally, with single or double walls 100 mm apart. It teaches a fixed outer shell with a different inside every time. *(visually checked: Chinati photos; the unit shown is split by one horizontal shelf at ≈50% height, and the "grey" aluminum reads sky-blue #ABC0E1 in daylight)*
- **Untitled, 1972 (Tate)**: an open copper box whose inside is lacquered deep red (#730020); the outside stays raw copper. *(visually checked: Tate image)*
- **Untitled, 1968 (AIC)**: stainless-steel ends with a window cut in them, yellow translucent acrylic sides; light passes through the colour. *(visually checked: AIC scan)*
- **Untitled, 1976 (AIC)** Douglas fir plywood, and **Untitled, c.1972/73 (AIC)** galvanized iron: two nested trays; the galvanized spangle reads as a coarse mottled texture, not a flat grey. *(visually checked: AIC scans)*
- **Furniture and "It's Hard to Find a Good Lamp," 1993**: rectangular volumes in pine, plywood and sheet metal. Judd held that a chair is judged as a chair, and that proportion is how its reasonableness shows. It teaches that function comes first and art is not added on top. *(not checked)*

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (progression 1967): one continuous constant element (the bar) carries a row of units whose lengths halve while the gaps double the other way; total length is fixed, so solid and void trade places along it.
- **Colour relations:** two colours only, one per role: the constant in a pale metal, the variables in one saturated enamel red; the red is ≈2/3 of the object's front area at the left and almost nothing at the right.
- **Line and edge:** no lines; edges are plane junctions, crisp.
- **The procedure:** fix a container length and one constant spine; choose a ratio (×2); scale the units down by it in one direction and the gaps up by it in the other; give the constant and the variables two different materials.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Donald Judd.** *Abolish ranking: one stated rule sets both the units and the gaps between them, so the interval counts as much as the thing and the run is read as a whole.*
Process:
1. Fix one unit before any content: one outer envelope and one orientation, or one constant spine spanning the whole run. The count may change to fit the container; the unit never rescales.
2. Derive the interval from the unit. Default: gap = unit (1:1), so empty and full alternate as equals. Nothing is clustered and nothing is centred.
3. If sizes must vary, state one ratio and apply it to units and gaps in opposite directions (e.g. units ×0.55 while gaps ×1.75 per step; over five units this gives the observed ≈11:1 for units and ≈1:9 for gaps), or hold the units constant and step only the gaps by a fixed decrement (each about 25% less). Keep one constant element beside the series so the rule can be read.
4. Put every difference inside the envelope: a split at half, a shelf, a diagonal, a doubled inner wall. Outer size and position never signal anything.
5. Assign colour by role, not by item: one saturated colour on one whole face of each unit (the front or the recessed inside, 20–40% of what is visible, the same face in every unit); the shell stays neutral and shows what it is. Or: constant element neutral, variable units saturated.
Why it works:
- When gap and unit are equal, figure and ground are balanced and the gap becomes countable as an element. Shrink the gap and the units fuse into one block (a list); widen it and each becomes an isolated object.
- With every envelope identical, grouping by likeness is total, so the eye stops ranking and reads the series; the only salient thing is a real difference inside a unit. Enlarge one unit and it becomes a hero with a supporting cast.
- Colour on a whole inner face reads as a property of the unit, and neutral outside with colour inside gives depth with no shadow. Put on small parts it turns into a signal and brings ranking back.
Colour relations: neutral ground and shell 60–80%; one saturated colour on whole faces only, 20–40% of each unit, identical placement in every unit; a second saturated colour only as a second role, never as a second rank; no gradients except the shell picking up its surroundings.
Space and line: gap:unit 1:1 (equal mode) or one ratio running opposite ways; one envelope; no drawn lines, every edge is two planes meeting; low, even density; no unit under 44px on its short side.
Type requirement: sizes taken from the same series as the layout (three steps, no in-betweens); labels read as specifications (plain, even, tabular numerals, at most two weights); text sits inside a unit and never bridges a gap.
Motion and interaction: serial and identical: a constant stagger (about 90ms per unit), near-linear, no overshoot; pointer or press changes the light on a face, never its size or height; on resize units are dropped or added, never scaled.
Fails when: one item truly must outrank the rest (an alert, a primary action among peers); content is dense or the screen short, because gap = unit halves capacity; items have very unequal content length, so one envelope truncates or pads.
Signature (do not carry together): cantilevered boxes stacked up a wall · raw metal shell with a coloured translucent face · factory-sharp right angles.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Judd's procedure → a children's bedtime-story app in dark plum and warm cream (phone, 390 × 844).
1. *Unit.* A story card 358 × 96px, corners rounded 20px (the procedure is indifferent). Four fit; on a 667px screen three fit at the same size.
2. *Interval.* 96px of bare ground between cards. No "featured tonight" card, no carousel.
3. *Progression.* The wind-down timeline under one constant 4px cream line, 358px long: sound segments 160 / 80 / 40 / 20 / 10px (16:1 over five, exact halving), silences between them 3 / 6 / 12 / 24px. Sound halves while silence doubles; the child sees quiet arriving.
4. *Interior.* Single story: split 30 / 70 (cover face / title). Two-part story: 50 / 50. Series: one horizontal shelf at half height. Outer size identical.
5. *Colour.* Ground plum at L 14, card shell the same plum at L 22. One warm apricot fills the whole cover face of every card (30% of the unit). On the timeline the constant line is cream and the sound segments apricot.
- *Type.* 13 / 21 / 34px only, one rounded face, two weights; durations in tabular numerals ("12 min").
- *Motion.* Cards arrive at a fixed 90ms stagger; pressing a card passes a lighter band across the apricot face; nothing lifts. Whole card is the hit area (96px).
Result: a soft, dark, rounded nursery app. No metal, no boxes on a wall, no right angles. What survived: gap equal to unit, one rule running through solids and voids, difference only inside the envelope.

## Space & composition
- **Non-relational.** No hero balanced by a sidebar. Every unit gets the same weight, and the series makes the order.
- **Gap equals module.** In the Stack the 9" box and the 9" void are equal; on the Tate stack the coloured fronts and the gaps both measure 64 px from top to bottom. A 72px card gets a 72px gutter.
- **Stated progressions.** Size elements 1:1:2:3:5:8 (unverified here) or in equal steps, and keep the series visible (stats bar, timeline, pricing ladder). As solids grow, voids shrink by the same rule; or, as in the 1973 copper bar, the solids stay fixed and only the openings step down (184 → 136 → 96 px, about −45 px each).
- **Fixed envelope, variable interior.** As at Marfa, all tiles share outer proportions (about 4:5 or 5:7). They differ only in how they are split inside (the photographed unit: one horizontal shelf at mid-height).
- **The wall is the ground.** Units hang in rows off a bare pale field. Density is low and even, with no clusters.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

Colour is treated as material: it simply exists and depicts nothing. It goes on whole faces, never in gradients: one saturated colour against bare metal or wood, with unpainted tops and edges showing what the object is made of. In the checked works the colour is mostly **translucent Plexiglas or Perspex** (amber, yellow, blue) or an enamel lining, and it sits on the **front or inside** face while the outer shell stays metal: about 20–40% of what you see (Tate stack blue 38%, AIC stack amber ≈25%, Tate 1972 red interior ≈20%). Translucent colour also tints the wall behind it (the AIC stack casts a pink-orange glow). No cadmium-red lacquer box and no purple lacquer were among the works obtained. Metal is never flat grey: galvanized iron is spangled, aluminum and steel pick up the colours around them.

## Form, line & typography
Right angles only. Edges are crisp because one plane meets another, not because a line is drawn. Type should feel fabricated: a neutral grotesk in sizes taken from the layout's progression, with tracked uppercase labels like a stencil on a crate. No italics, at most two weights. Fonts: **Inter Tight** or **Archivo** for UI and headings, **IBM Plex Mono** for numbers and measurements, which Judd would have listed plainly. His own working drawing (Tate, 1967/68) confirms the tone: one even hairline in isometric, hand-lettered capitals for materials ("INTERIOR ENDS STAINLESS STEEL"), dimensions written beside the edges (48, 68, 33), all on a single flat yellow sheet.

## Rhythm, motion & time
Meaning builds up in sequence as you walk past. Motion is serial and mechanical: a constant stagger (about 90ms), near-linear easing (`cubic-bezier(.2,0,.2,1)`), no bounce or overshoot. Hover changes the *light*, not the form. At Marfa the mill aluminum picks up sky and sunset, so let a faint sheen follow the cursor while the geometry stays still. Scrolling shows repetition with difference, never a narrative build-up.

## UI translations
1. Set the gutter equal to the module height. If list rows are 64px, the gaps between them are 64px.
2. Size a horizontal row of blocks by a visible progression (halving as in the 1967 progression, or equal steps as in the 1973 Tate bar; Fibonacci widths 1,1,2,3,5,8 × 16px are *(unverified)*: the Van Abbe work was not obtained) and shrink the gaps by the inverse.
3. Make every card the same outer size. Show the difference through internal splits only: a 50/50 divide, a diagonal hairline, or a recessed slot.
4. Use one accent colour, applied to a whole surface (a full card face, the recessed inside of a panel, or a full button fill), never as text links, icons or gradients. Keep it to roughly 20–40% of the component.
5. Leave edges and dividers in raw material grey (`#7B7B77`). The colour goes on the front or inner face, the metal shows on the outside and at the edges. A translucent accent may tint the surface behind it (a faint coloured glow on the page).
6. Take type sizes from the layout's progression (13/21/34) and stop there. No in-between sizes.
7. Build a dashboard as a single row or column of equal units. No hero metric, no featured tile.
8. Stagger list entry at a fixed 90ms with linear easing. Every item behaves identically.
9. On hover, move a faint reflective gradient across the surface. Never scale or lift with a shadow.
10. Let the item count change to fit the viewport, as the Stack drops units for a low ceiling, but never change the module size.
11. Build forms like Judd furniture: square-cornered fields, stencilled labels, no ornament.

## What this artist would reject
- Rounded corners, pill buttons and soft drop shadows that fake depth.
- A big hero with balancing side elements (relational composition).
- Gradients and illustrative colour. Colour should be flat and factual.
- Decorative icons and skeuomorphic texture added on top of function.
- Bento grids with mixed tile sizes and no stated rule.
- Bouncy, playful spring animations.

## Signature prompt seed
Design this app screen as a run of identical rectangular units on a whitewashed field (#F2F2F2), one thing after another. Fix a module and make every gap equal to it. Where sizes must vary, step them by a visible rule (halving or equal steps; Fibonacci is unverified) and shrink the voids inversely. Cards share one outer size and differ only by internal splits: halves, diagonals, a recessed slot. Use mill-aluminum greys, square corners, a single flat amber (#CE4901) or blue (#293F7E) on one whole inner face with metal outside, a plain grotesk at three sizes, linear staggered entry, and hover that shifts light rather than form.
