# James Turrell — Light and Space

## Essence
Turrell treats light as a physical material and perception itself as the medium. His work holds no objects or images. It is a field of color held by an edge so thin it seems to vanish. Over minutes, the viewer finds that the "flat" plane is actually depth, or that the depth is a flat plane. He has said he wants you to "sense yourself sensing" (Smithsonian).

## Key works examined
- **Afrum I (White), 1967**: projected light that reads as a solid cube in a corner. Light at a precise edge becomes an object. *(not obtained; the related 1967 plan drawing **Carn** (AIC) was checked instead: a graphite-toned room on blue perspective grid paper where the light is the only untoned shape, a crisp white rectangle straddling the back corner)*
- **Rayna, 1979 (AIC, installation photo, B&W)**: a flat rectangle ≈2.25:1 cut into a white wall, about half the photo's width, centred and slightly below mid-wall, with no frame, reveal or shadow line: it reads as a painted grey panel. *(visually checked: AIC image)*
- **Skyspaces, e.g. The Color Inside (UT Austin, 2013, his 84th)**: a sharp ceiling aperture flattens the sky into a plane. At sunrise and sunset, hidden LEDs recolor the room, and that shifts how the sky looks. *(visually checked: eight UT Austin Landmarks photos. The oculus is a razor-edged ellipse ≈25% of the photo width; the sky inside reads as the near-complement of the dome: violet dome `#9422FF` → olive-grey sky `#7E886F`; lavender `#867BA6` → yellow-green `#C7CA85`; cyan `#00BABE` → pink `#E1BAD1`; orange `#CB6511` → navy `#182947`; daylight grey `#97A2A3` → blue `#578BBA`. Below the dome the seating room is near-black `#090D1D`, divided from it by one thin bright LED cove line)*
- **Breathing Light (LACMA, 2013), a Ganzfeld**: an all-white room with rounded corners, so the eye finds no edge to measure depth by. It teaches that removing edges removes depth. *(visually checked: LACMA image. Lit, the white room is a saturated blue field `#2961E0` (L*45, C*75); the opening is a rounded rectangle ≈3:1, corner radius ≈15% of its height, only ≈7 L* lighter (`#2777F2`) and soft-edged)*
- **Aten Reign (Guggenheim, 2013)**: five elliptical rings of LED light and daylight stacked inside Wright's rotunda. The space reads as flat at one moment and as a deep funnel the next. *(visually checked: Guggenheim image, crimson state. Dark outer field L*20, then rings at L*28, 47, 56, 60, 66 and a pale pink core; each ring brightens toward its inner edge and ends in a crisp step)*
- **Roden Crater (Arizona, since 1977)**: a tunnel nearly three football fields long climbs to an elliptical portal that looks circular from one spot. It teaches reveal by stages. *(partly checked: rodencrater.com stills show the crater exterior and a dark ribbed tunnel; the portal view was not found)*

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**James Turrell.** *Hold one field and one opening still, and change what the opening looks like only by changing its surround, more slowly than the eye can follow.*
Process:
1. Clear the view to one continuous field of a single tone, 70–85% of the area, with nothing on it: no chrome, no second surface.
2. Cut one aperture and put all content inside it (a quarter to a half of the width, wider than tall, 2:1 to 3:1 when rectangular). There is only ever one.
3. Choose its edge once per view. Cut: two flat fills meeting with no thickness, no outline, no shadow. Or dissolved: a soft edge, corners rounded to about 15% of the height, and a step of only 4–8 L from the field.
4. Give the chroma to the field and keep the aperture's own fill fixed and near-neutral. The aperture will appear tinted toward the field's opposite; choose the field's hue to get the appearance wanted, and never repaint the aperture.
5. For depth with no object (entry and empty views): nest 3–5 concentric steps of 4–9 L each, with one larger jump (about 19 L) at the outermost boundary, each band a soft inward gradient ending in a crisp step.
6. Put time in the field only: it moves between states too slowly to be seen moving, while the content stays fixed. Any meaning is stated inside the aperture in words or an icon first; the field may then agree with it.
Why it works:
- Simultaneous contrast: a neutral patch takes on the opposite of its surround, so one fixed fill yields many apparent colours and the viewer catches their own perception changing. Colour the aperture directly and it is just a coloured panel.
- An edge with no thickness removes the cues that separate surface from depth, so the opening flips between a flat patch and a space, and attention stays on it. Add an outline or a shadow and it settles into a card on a background.
- Change below the threshold of notice is found only after it has happened (adaptation), which makes the viewer wait. With a single aperture nothing competes; a second one turns the field into a page.
Colour relations: field 70–85% with the highest chroma present; aperture 10–25%, fixed, near-neutral, 4–8 L from the field (dissolved) or at a clear value step (cut); field and apparent aperture colour are near-opposites; nested steps stay in one hue; content ink is checked at 4.5:1 or more against the aperture in every field state.
Space and line: no lines at all; one aperture, centred or just below centre; edge either cut or dissolved, never both; concentric nesting only; focus indicators remain visible (3:1) as the one permitted outline.
Type requirement: thin and quiet, large rather than heavy, held inside the aperture; soft in value but at reading contrast; no text on the field.
Motion and interaction: ambient drift is a named class (`ambient-field-drift`), 30–120s per state, linear, pausable, with a static reduced-motion state that holds one field/aperture pair. Interaction is 600–1200ms fades of value only; drill-down enlarges the aperture in stages while the field dims; nothing slides or scales with bounce.
Fails when: content needs several panels or side-by-side comparison; colour-coded data, photographs or product colours sit in the aperture (the surround shifts them); long reading against a saturated field (afterimages, fatigue); urgent states, which slow change cannot deliver.
Signature (do not carry together): a razor-edged oculus open to the sky · saturated violet or crimson emitted light · nested glowing ellipses.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Turrell's procedure → a bank-transfer confirmation flow in deep moss and bone (phone, 390 × 844).
1. *Field.* One moss green at L 30 fills the screen (85% after the aperture). No header bar, no tab bar, no logo on it.
2. *Aperture.* One rectangle 326 × 150px (2.2:1, 15% of the area; on a narrow phone the width range in step 2 is relaxed and the area share is kept), 46% from the top, holding payee, amount and the confirm control (56px high).
3. *Edge.* Cut: square corners, flat fill, no border or shadow. Focus ring 2px at 3:1 on the control only.
4. *Induction.* Aperture fill a fixed neutral bone at L 88. Against the moss it reads faintly rose. Ink at L 15 (over 12:1).
5. *Nesting.* The entry screen before any payee is chosen: four concentric rounded steps of moss at L 30 / 49 / 55 / 62 (one 19 L jump, then 6–7 L), each grading inward to a crisp step, the innermost holding "Who are you paying?".
6. *Time and state.* While idle the field carries `ambient-field-drift`: moss to a bluer green and back over 90s; reduced motion holds moss. On success the aperture shows a tick icon and "Sent: £240 to R. Okafor"; the field then eases to a lighter green over 900ms and stays there until dismissed. On failure: a warning icon and "Not sent: check the account number", field easing to a muted rust; bone now reads faintly cool. The words carry the state; the field only agrees.
- *Type.* One light weight; amount at 40px, labels at 15px, all inside the aperture.
- *Drill-down.* "Details" grows the aperture from 150px to 520px high in two stages over 1s while the field drops 6 L.
Result: a sober, earthy payment screen with square corners. No sky, no ellipse, no violet glow. What survived: one field, one borderless aperture, apparent colour set by the surround, time carried by the field alone.

## Space & composition
- **One field and one aperture.** A Skyspace is a large neutral surface with one cut-out where everything happens. Let roughly 70–85% of the screen be one continuous tone, with a single inset "aperture" at about 38% of the shorter side.
- **The knife edge.** Turrell bevels the aperture until its edge has no thickness, so the sky looks pasted onto the ceiling (confirmed in the Color Inside oculus and in Rayna, which photographs as a flat grey panel). In UI, a container should have no border or shadow, only a value step between two flat fills.
- **Edges either cut or dissolved.** Ganzfeld rooms curve corners away, and Skyspaces sharpen one edge to infinity. Pick one per screen: a razor-cut panel, or a soft-edged rounded rectangle (Breathing Light: radius ≈15% of its height) in a field whose edges fade (`mask-image: radial-gradient`).
- **Concentric and centered.** Aten Reign and the Crater portal are nested ellipses. Nest 3–5 rings, each 4–9 L* lighter than the one outside (one bigger jump, ≈19 L*, where the lit rings meet the dark outer field), each ring a soft gradient brightening inward and ending in a crisp step, and put content in the innermost one.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

The logic is simultaneous contrast. The same sky reads as navy against a warm wall and as grey-violet against a cool one, so the frame color controls the content color. In the Color Inside photographs the sky always reads as roughly the **complement of the dome**: violet → olive, lavender → yellow-green, cyan → pink, orange → navy. Color is emitted, not painted, and in the photographed states it is **saturated**, not pastel: violet C*117, crimson C*76, Ganzfeld blue C*75, cyan C*39, orange C*69. Only the daylight program is grey. The Aten Reign sequence ran from moon white and dusk pink through orange, lilac, violet and yellow to grey twilight (Domus).

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|
| Ganzfeld field / base surface | `#2961E0` (Breathing Light; the lit Ganzfeld is saturated blue, not pale) |
| Twilight grey (neutral frame, text-on-light) | `#97A2A3` (Color Inside dome, daylight program) |
| Aperture sky (primary accent) | `#578BBA` (daylight sky in the oculus) |
| Dusk pink (warm frame state) | `#D62038` (Aten Reign crimson ring; nearest real warm frame) |
| Lilac-violet (transition midpoint) | `#867BA6` (Color Inside lavender dome) |
| Sodium amber (rare alert, the "sunset" hit) | `#CB6511` (Color Inside orange dome) |
| Crater night (dark mode field) | `#090D1D` (Skyspace seating room at dusk) |
| Electric violet (peak frame state) | `#9422FF` (Color Inside) |
| Cyan (cool frame state) | `#00BABE` (Color Inside; makes the sky read pink `#E1BAD1`) |
| Ganzfeld aperture | `#2777F2` (only ≈7 L* above its field) |

Build states as frame/aperture pairs, frame and aperture near-complements (violet/olive, cyan/pink, orange/navy). Animate the frame and leave the content color fixed; its apparent color will change anyway.

## Form, line & typography
There is no lettering in Turrell's work, so type should behave like the knife edge: thin, quiet, light rather than ink. Use large sizes at weights 200–300, tracked +0.02em, in soft values that still pass contrast. Shapes are rectangles, circles, ellipses and soft rounded rectangles (Ganzfeld) with no outline. Fonts: **Jost** at weight 200 for display (its circular geometry echoes the oculus), **Manrope** at 300–400 for body, and optionally **IBM Plex Mono** at 300 for time readouts.

## Rhythm, motion & time
Turrell's time is slow. Skyspace programs last as long as a sunset, and Ganzfeld color changes too slowly to see as it happens. You notice only after it has changed.
- Run ambient color transitions over 30–120 s, linear, below the threshold of noticing.
- Make interaction transitions 600–1200 ms color and opacity fades, with no translate, scale or bounce. Light changes intensity; it does not slide.
- Procession: at Roden, the sky grows from a small disc as you walk. Drill-downs should open the aperture step by step.
- Tie the palette to the real sun (local sunrise and sunset times).

## UI translations
1. Make one flat field fill at least 75% of the viewport and place all primary content in a single borderless inset aperture.
2. Define every container only by a value step of 4–8% lightness. Use no `border`, no `box-shadow` and no `outline` except a focus ring.
3. Give hero and empty states an ellipse of 3–5 nested rings, each 4–9 L* lighter than the one outside it (a soft inward gradient per ring, a crisp step at its inner edge), with content in the core.
4. Slowly animate the frame's `background-color` over 60 s through saturated states (violet `#9422FF`, lavender, cyan, orange, crimson) and twilight grey, and keep the foreground static so contrast does the work: the content will read as the frame's near-complement.
5. Fade the canvas edges into the field with a radial mask so the screen has no visible edge, Ganzfeld-style.
6. Set headings in Jost 200 at 2.5–4rem and body in Manrope 300. Never use bold. Use size and luminance for hierarchy.
7. Animate hover only through luminance: raise the element's lightness by 6% over 400 ms, with no lift, shadow or scale.
8. Make drill-down navigation a procession: the selected item's aperture grows from 20% to 100% over 900 ms while the surrounding field dims.
9. Use colored light for state, but never colour alone: a text label and icon (e.g. "Saved ✓", "Error: …") carry every error or success, and a whole-field tint may accompany it as one monotone fade (≈2 s, none under reduced motion) instead of small red or green badges.
10. Ship a "dark adaptation" entry: for the first 1.2 s the bare field shows, then content resolves in.

## What this artist would reject
- Drop shadows and elevation layers. They are fake physical depth where perceptual depth should be.
- Card-grid dashboards with no dominant field.
- Snappy 150 ms spring animations and bouncing micro-interactions.
- Hairline borders and dividers. They give the eye the edges Turrell works to remove.
- Glassmorphism with visible highlights. In Turrell's work the light source is always hidden.

## Signature prompt seed
Design this screen as a Turrell Skyspace. One flat field of saturated emitted color (e.g. Ganzfeld blue #2961E0, or twilight grey #97A2A3 for a calm state) fills most of the viewport, with edges dissolving into a radial fade. Cut a single borderless elliptical aperture, defined only by a value step, and put the task inside it. No shadows, borders, bold or icons. Hierarchy comes from luminance and size alone (Jost 200 headings, Manrope 300 body). The frame drifts through violet, lavender, cyan and orange over sixty seconds while the content stays fixed and reads as the frame's complement. Every interaction is a slow change in light, never a movement.
