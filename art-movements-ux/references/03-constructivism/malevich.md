# Kazimir Malevich — Suprematism

## Essence
Malevich cut painting back to a "zero point" (he signalled it by putting "0" in the title of the 0,10 exhibition) and then rebuilt it from flat, unshaded planes floating in white space that has no edges. What makes his work recognisable is a few tilted, weightless shapes in a white field read as endless space instead of empty background.

## Key works examined
- **Black Square, 1915** (79.5 × 79.5 cm, Tretyakov). A single black plane inside a white border, hung high across a room corner where a household icon would go. Lesson: one element, placed with total authority, can carry a whole screen. *(painting not obtained; checked only the AIC drawing "Black Square, Second State", 1915/20: a hand-shaded square centred in a ruled frame with ≈13% margin on every side, edges not quite true.)* *(visually checked: secondary source, ideelart.com)* Seen in the 1915 painting: the square spans 72% of the canvas side (397 × 400 px on 552 × 549), centred within 1%, with a margin of ≈14% on each side, the bottom a hair narrower. So black covers ≈52% and the ground only ≈48%: here the figure outweighs the field. The black is a blue-black, and its craquelure lets the paler underlayer show through as a fine net.
- **Suprematist Composition: Airplane Flying, 1915** (58.1 × 48.3 cm, MoMA). Stretched, rotated, overlapping bars on white. Lesson: rotation and stacking create a sense of flight and direction without drawing any motion. *(visually checked: Commons reproduction of the MoMA work.)* Seen: the main cluster (three black slabs, six yellow-orange blocks) shares one axis at ≈35° (slab edges 33.5° / −53°); the two red bars are almost horizontal and the two blue bars take a counter-angle. Three black planes, not one (black 14.6% of the area); red only 0.6%, in thin bars of ≈20:1 and 9:1; yellow-orange 8%; ground ≈73%. Nothing touches or bleeds off the edges: every plane sits inside a margin of ≥5% and the content centroid is near the centre (45% / 53%).
- **Dynamic Suprematism (Supremus No. 57), 1915–16** (added, Tate T02319, museum scan). A huge pale grey triangle (22–25% of the area, L* 80 on an L* 88 ground) carries a cluster of ≈30 small planes: three black rectangles (3% of the area) tilted at −12°, +7° and steeper, a bright blue triangle, yellow, ochre and cream bars, maroon (not cadmium) bars, green and light-blue sticks, a navy circle, and hairline crosses and arcs as the smallest scale. Ground is a near-neutral off-white (`#E0DED9`); no plane bleeds off the canvas. *(visually checked: Tate)*
- **Red Square, 1915** (Russian Museum). The "square" is a slightly skewed quadrilateral. Lesson: being a little off true is what makes a shape look alive. *(visually checked: Russian Museum collection image, Ж-1643)* Seen: a red quadrilateral at ≈72% of the canvas width covering ≈53% of the surface; the bottom and sides are near-true but the top edge rises ≈2.7° to the right, so the top-left corner sits lower than the top-right (left side ≈73%, right side ≈76% of the height). Margins ≈14% left and right, ≈10–13% top, ≈13% bottom: centred like the Black Square, and the only departure from true is that one slanted edge. *(Also seen: the paired MoMA canvas **Black Square and Red Square, 1915** (71.4 × 44.4 cm): visually checked: secondary source, ideelart.com.)* Seen: on a tall 1:1.58 canvas, the black square (45% of the width, top edge at 19% of the height, 15% from the left) sits upper left, and a smaller red square (≈30% of the width) tilted ≈14° sits lower right, separated by a gap and touching nothing. Black 13%, red 5.6%, ground ≈81%.
- **Suprematist Composition: White on White, 1918** (79.4 × 79.4 cm, MoMA). A tilted, off-centre cool white square on a slightly warmer white ground, with loose edges. Lesson: hierarchy can come from temperature alone. *(visually checked: secondary source, ideelart.com)* Seen: the inner square is ≈68% of the canvas side, tilted ≈12.5°, pushed to the upper right so that its top corner touches the top edge and its right corner almost the right edge (≈2%), leaving ≈15% margins on the left and bottom. The two whites have about the same lightness; the inner one is cooler and greyer (reproduction samples: inner `#CDD0C2`, ground `#CECAB7`–`#D3D1BF`), and its edges are drawn only by a faint grey line, so the rule holds: temperature, not value, makes the hierarchy.
- **Black Square, later versions (c. 1923–1930s).** Repainted with students. Lesson: the sign is a reusable system mark, like a logo. *(not obtained: unverified; see the check's missing-works log (not shipped))*
- **Arkhitekton Alpha / Gota, 1923** (Gota: plaster, 85.3 × 56 × 52.5 cm). Bare white blocks gathered around one dominant central mass, spreading out horizontally (Alpha) or upward (Gota). Lesson: build a layout by adding smaller volumes to one main block. *(not obtained: unverified; see the check's missing-works log (not shipped))*

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Black Square 1915): one figure, centred, at 72% of the side; ground 48%. Figure outweighs field; the only tension is the slight untruth of the edges and the bottom margin being a hair narrower.
- **Space** (Black Square and Red Square 1915): two figures on a tall field, the larger high and left, the smaller low and right, with a clear gap; field ≈81%. The pair forms one diagonal from upper left to lower right.
- **Space** (White on White 1918): one figure (68% of the side) pushed into the upper-right corner until it touches the top edge; the open margins collect at the left and bottom (≈15% each).
- **Colour relations:** the number of colours drops by mode: 1 figure colour + ground (Black Square), 2 (Black + Red), 0 hues (White on White: two near-equal whites split by temperature). When there are two figures, the darker is larger (black ≈2.3× the red's area) and the saturated one is smaller and tilted. Figures never touch each other or the frame edge, except in White on White, where the figure touches the top edge.
- **Line and edge:** no outlines in the black works (edge = colour change); in White on White the edge is the only drawn line, faint and grey. Edges are slightly irregular by hand.
- **The procedure:** choose one figure; place it either dead centre at about 70% of the frame or, if there is a second figure, set the second smaller, more saturated, tilted 10–15°, diagonally opposite with a gap; for the quietest mode, keep value equal and separate figure from ground only by temperature, sliding the figure into a corner.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Kazimir Malevich.** *Make the empty field the subject: float a few unequal planes in it, clear of the frame, and rank them so that the largest is the quietest and the loudest is the thinnest.*
Process:
1. Choose the mode by how much has to be said, because the field share inverts between them. One thing: icon mode (step 6). Several things: field mode (steps 2–5).
2. Field mode: keep one unbroken ground at 65–75% of the area and hold every plane at least 5% of the short side inside the frame. Nothing bleeds off, nothing is boxed.
3. Seat one large quiet plane (20–25% of the area) a single value step from the ground (about 8 L), with no edge line. It carries the cluster.
4. Build the cluster on one shared axis 7–40° off the frame, its centroid within 5% of the centre and its length along a diagonal. Separate the main masses by slivers of ground; only thin bars may cross a mass.
5. Rank by inverse intensity: the value farthest from the ground takes 3–15% in 2–3 planes of clearly falling size; the most saturated colour takes ≤1% as one long thin bar (8:1–20:1) that leaves the cluster axis and lies near the frame's horizontal; other hues stay small (≤2% each, one secondary up to 8%) on one or two counter-angles. Sizes step about 1 : 8 : 40.
6. Icon mode: one figure centred within 1% and spanning 68–72% of the side, so the figure outweighs the ground (about 52 : 48), with one edge 1–3° off true. For two figures: the one farther in value from the ground is about 2.3× larger; the saturated one is smaller, tilted 10–15°, diagonally opposite across a gap. Quietest variant: figure and ground at equal value, split by temperature alone, the figure tilted about 12° and slid into a corner until it meets one edge.
Why it works:
- Figure and ground. A plane with ground on every side reads as an object in front of a space that continues behind and beyond it. Cut by the frame, it becomes a piece of a pattern and the ground becomes what was left over; boxed, the ground becomes a container.
- A shared tilt removes gravity: nothing rests on the frame's baseline or on a neighbour. The one near-horizontal bar is then odd in angle and in saturation at once, so it wins attention at under 1% of the area. Tilt it with the rest, or enlarge it, and it is one more plane.
- Visual weight is intensity × area. Large-and-quiet against small-and-loud equalises the weights, so no plane becomes a target and the eye keeps crossing the gaps. Equal sizes hand the screen to the saturated one.
Colour relations: ground 65–75% at the lowest chroma; one big plane a single value step from it; maximum-contrast mass 3–15% in 2–3 falling planes; peak saturation ≤1%; up to about ten small hues if none passes 2%; all flat. Icon mode 52 : 48 figure to ground. Equal-value mode: zero value difference, temperature only (non-informative surfaces only; anything meaningful still needs 3:1).
Space and line: no outlines, an edge is a colour change and may sit 1–3° off true; margins ≥5%; cluster axis 7–40° with 1–2 counter-angles; scale 1 : 8 : 40; in the equal-value mode a faint line is the only edge.
Type requirement: type behaves as a plane: a face blunt and heavy enough that a word reads as a bar from a distance, in two sizes far apart. A display word may ride the cluster axis as a decorative duplicate; reading text, labels and every control stay horizontal on the ground.
Motion and interaction: weightless and slow. Planes drift in along their own axis, heaviest first, 600–1200ms, about 80ms apart, no overshoot; reduced motion: they appear in place. A state change alters the medium, never the height or the angle: hover or focus steps the plane's value once and a small bar docks on its long side; the action bar lengthens 10–15% along its axis. The thin bar keeps a 44px hit band and a label beside it.
Fails when: the content is a dense table, a long form or n equal items (no field survives and the tilt costs legibility); the viewport is a narrow phone column where a 5% margin plus 65% ground leaves no room; the equal-value mode is asked to carry a control or a state.
Signature (do not carry together): a black square on white · tilted bars in primary colours · white on white.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Product: a deploy status board, dark mode, deep aubergine, desktop 1440 × 900.
1. *Mode.* Five services and one action, so field mode. The "no deploys" state uses icon mode.
2. *Ground.* Aubergine at L 12, 70% unbroken. Every plane sits ≥45px inside the frame. No cards, no top bar: three small horizontal text links sit on the ground at top left.
3. *Quiet plane.* A trapezoid of 22% at L 20 (8 L above the ground), no border. It stands for the production environment.
4. *Cluster.* Axis 24°, centroid at 48% / 52%, running lower left to upper right; 10px slivers of ground between the masses.
5. *Rank.* The value farthest from the ground is a pale lilac-grey (L 85): 11% in three planes of 6, 3.5 and 1.5% (the three services by traffic). Two idle services are muted teal planes of 1.5% each at −50°. Peak saturation is one acid-lime bar, 420 × 30px (1.0%), horizontal across the cluster: "Promote build 412", with a 44px hit band and its label in heavy capitals on the ground beside it. 1px ticks mark deploy times, completing 1 : 8 : 40.
6. *Icon mode.* No deploys running: one L 85 square, 630px (70% of the short side), centred, top edge 2° off true, one line of text below.
- *Type.* The environment name at 96px rides the 24° axis as decoration (hidden from assistive technology; the same word is horizontal in the header). Service names and statuses are 14px, horizontal, on the ground next to each plane.
- *Interaction.* Focus steps a plane from L 85 to L 92 and docks a 6px bar on its long side. A failed deploy applies the two-figure rule: a smaller coral plane tilted 12° appears diagonally opposite across a gap, with the word "Failed".
- *Motion.* Planes drift in along 24° over 900ms, heaviest first; with reduced motion they are simply there.
Result: a dark, purple status board. No white, no black square, no primaries. What survived: the field as subject, planes clear of the frame, and the action as the thinnest, loudest, only level thing.

## Space & composition
- **Make the ground mean infinity.** White is not padding. It is space that runs past the frame. Let 65–75% of the view stay as one uninterrupted field (measured: Airplane Flying ≈73%, Dynamic Suprematism ≈67% plus a 22–25% pale grey plane) and keep content away from the edges (≥5% margin), so the field reads as endless. The planes float inside the field; in the works checked nothing bleeds off the frame. Two exceptions, seen in secondary reproductions: the icon mode (Black Square: one figure centred at 72% of the side, ground only 48%) and White on White, whose square is pushed into the upper-right corner until it touches the top edge.
- **Rotate off the grid.** Tilt the main planes between 7° and 40° (Airplane Flying cluster ≈35°; Dynamic Suprematism black planes −12° and +7°). One axis is shared by the main cluster; thin bars take one or two counter-angles, and a long bar may sit nearly horizontal.
- **Weight off-centre.** The cluster runs along a diagonal through the field (upper left to lower right in Airplane Flying) while its centroid stays near the centre (45–47% across, 52–53% down in both works checked); the heaviest black sits in the lower half. Let thin bars drift toward the opposite corner.
- **Scale jumps of about 1:8:40.** Use one huge plane, a few medium bars, and hairlines or small dots (confirmed in Dynamic Suprematism: a triangle filling a quarter of the canvas, then bars, then hairline crosses and arcs).
- **Arkhitekton clustering.** Treat a dashboard as one main block with secondary panels stuck to its sides at varying depths and heights, never a uniform card grid.
- **Corner placement.** Like the icon-corner hang, set the key mark where two edges meet.

## Form, line & typography
Every shape is a plane, never an outlined box or a stroke-drawn icon. Edges are crisp but slightly off true: rectangles drift a degree out of square, and bars taper or skew. Type should behave like another plane. Set headings in a heavy geometric sans, rotated with the composition and sometimes running vertically along a bar. Body text stays horizontal and small. Faces with Cyrillic support (free on Google Fonts) suit his 1920s print work:
- **Unbounded**: wide and blunt, good for rotated display words.
- **Jost**: a Futura-like geometric sans for body and UI.
- **Russo One**: a single slab-heavy word or a numeral.

## Rhythm, motion & time
Motion should feel like drifting in zero gravity, not bouncing. Planes move slowly (600–1200 ms), with `cubic-bezier(.2,.0,.0,1)` as the default easing, so they glide in from off-screen and settle at their tilt. On scroll, layers can parallax at different rates (heavy black slowest, thin bars fastest) A "zero" state matters: empty states and loading should strip the screen down to one black or red plane on the field.

## UI translations
1. Make the page background `#E0DED9` and set one large panel in `#C8C7C8` (or cards in cream `#DED0BA`) with no border and no shadow, so a small lightness or temperature step alone separates them.
2. Rotate the hero cluster with `transform: rotate(-35deg)` (range 7–40°) and keep it fully inside the viewport with a ≥5% margin; the field, not a bleed, carries the sense of endless space.
3. Keep black to 3–15% of the screen, split into at most three planes of clearly descending size (Airplane Flying: one slab, two smaller ones).
4. Make the primary CTA a long red bar (8:1 to 20:1), under 1% of the screen, nearly horizontal across the tilted cluster, with a label in heavy uppercase Jost beside or on it.
5. Replace dividers with thin ultramarine or ochre bars 3–6 px thick, set at a counter-angle to the cluster and stopping short of the viewport edge.
6. Build dashboards arkhitekton-style: one dominant panel spanning 2/3 of the width, with 3–5 smaller panels joined to its edges at staggered offsets (CSS grid with deliberately uneven `grid-row` spans).
7. Run a section label vertically (`writing-mode: vertical-rl`) along the edge of a tall bar.
8. Animate entry so planes drift in from outside the viewport on their own axes, staggered 80 ms, heaviest first.
9. On hover, rotate the plane 3° and shift its z-order above its neighbour. No colour change, no glow.
10. Empty and onboarding states show only one off-centre black square on the field, with one line of small text below and left of it.

## What this artist would reject
- Drop shadows, gradients, and glassmorphism: illusionistic depth instead of overlapping flat planes.
- Uniform card grids with equal gutters, which give density without hierarchy.
- Centred, symmetric hero layouts with a headline, a subhead, and two buttons.
- Outlined icons and skeuomorphic illustration, which bring back the "object" he removed.
- Springy, bouncy micro-interactions: playfulness where there should be weightlessness.

One dominant black slab with two smaller ones sits in the lower half. A single long, thin red bar is the only call to action. Thin ultramarine and ochre bars cut across at counter-angles, stopping short of the edges (in the works checked nothing bleeds off the frame). Scale jumps sharply, from hugely heavy to hairline.
