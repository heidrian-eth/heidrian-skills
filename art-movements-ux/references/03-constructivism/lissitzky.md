# El Lissitzky — Constructivism

## Essence
Lissitzky treated a page, a wall or a room as a field of forces. He placed a few charged elements (a wedge, a bar, a block of type) on diagonals, so the viewer has to move: turn the sheet, flip to a tab, walk the room. Every element is either a vector or a counterweight, and the reader's eye or hand finishes the construction.

## Key works examined
- **Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge, 1919** (lithograph): a red triangle drives into a white circle. Meaning comes from collision and direction rather than illustration. Two shapes and one diagonal carry a whole narrative. *(visually checked: secondary source, worldhistory.org, reproduction of the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston impression)* Seen: a landscape sheet (1.25:1) split by one steep diagonal (≈64° from horizontal) into a paper-white left and a black right (paper ≈57%, black ≈28%). A large white circle (≈45% of the width across) sits inside the black zone, right of centre; the red wedge (≈10% of the surface with the small red shards) comes from the upper left and drives into the circle's left side, its tip reaching roughly the circle's centre: there is no gap, the wedge overlaps. Small red, black and grey shards scatter along the boundary like debris; two grey blocks sit near the impact. The words are placed along the diagonals: КЛИНОМ black on white, КРАСНЫМ in outline, БЕЙ red inside the circle, БЕЛЫХ white on a grey slab in the black.
- **Prouns, 1919–1923**: "a station" on the way to new form, built to be "surveyed from every direction." Axonometric volumes float with no horizon, so there is no single correct way up. *(visually checked: AIC Proun 1920, AIC Proun 1924–25, V&A Proun III 1923)* The 1920 Proun is 64% bare paper with a grey disc, tilted dark rectangles (≈30–35°), an axonometric cube and a sandpaper-textured block, and no red at all; Proun III is greys and black only; the 1924–25 Proun is 71% black ground with a horizontal red bar (8%), a translucent ochre band at ≈50° (11%) and concentric hairline squares that make a tunnel.
- **About Two Squares, 1922** (added; AIC, Russian and Dutch editions): a thin-ruled frame holds an orthogonal red square (≈42% of the frame width, 9% of the cover) docked against a huge black numeral "2"; only the small author name runs at 45°. Lesson: one red block plus one giant numeral is a complete cover. *(visually checked: AIC)*
- **Proun Room, Great Berlin Art Exhibition, 1923**: the Proun goes off the canvas and onto three walls of a small room, with reliefs that lead the visitor around corners. The surface becomes a route. *(retry pass, visually checked: Stedelijk 1923 photo postcard "Prounenraum. Große Berliner Kunstausstellung", object 83031; black and white, so colours not judged.)* Seen: white walls dominate (dark planes ≈15–20% of the visible wall); one tall dark slab stands on the left wall, a dark horizontal bar runs straight into and around the far corner, and long thin rods carrying small square reliefs cross whole walls and continue past corners. Nothing is framed or centred on a wall: every element either crosses a corner or points along the wall toward the next one, which confirms the route reading.
- **For the Voice (Dlia golosa), 1923**: 13 Mayakovsky poems. A die-cut thumb index marked with geometric signs, not letters, takes you straight to each poem. Every poem opens on a two-colour typographic title page. It is navigation as a designed object, a proto-tab bar. *(visually checked: AIC copy, "Svolochi" spread.)* Seen: 10 stepped tabs on the right fore-edge, and every tab carries BOTH the poem title in small black capitals AND a glyph (mostly red: squares, ring, cross, circles; some red + black), so the signs supplement words rather than replace them. On the title spread black bars and red text lines run at 45° (measured 44.8°), a 7-letter word stands vertically (rotated 90°) at ≈8% of the page height, and hairline black and red lines connect red circles to red bars. Red is only 3–4% of each page, black ≈11%.
- **"Topography of Typography," Merz 4, 1923**: eight theses. Printed words are taken in by sight, not sound. Optics should beat phonetics. Pages should run as a continuous, cinematic "bioscopic book." The printed surface itself would be surpassed by the "Electro-Library."
- **Soviet pavilion at Pressa, Cologne, 1928**: a 24 m × 3.5 m photomontage frieze, zig-zag conveyor belts, rotating models, running films, blinking letters. The visitor becomes a player on a stage. *(not obtained: unverified; see the check's missing-works log (not shipped))*

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Beat the Whites): one steep diagonal divides the field into light and dark halves; each half holds the opposite-valued figure (white circle in the black, red wedge on the white), so each figure reads at maximum contrast; the two meet at the boundary.
- **Colour relations:** paper ≈57%, black ≈28%, red ≈10%, grey a few %; the one saturated colour belongs to the aggressor only, plus the word БЕЙ (strike) and small shards; grey marks secondary blocks.
- **Line and edge:** all hard, flat; small shards and bars scatter along the boundary as broken pieces.
- **The procedure:** split the field on one strong diagonal into light and dark; set a large round passive form in the dark half and a pointed active form in the saturated colour on the light half; drive the point across the boundary into the round form; scatter fragments where they meet; place each word on the axis of the form it belongs to.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**El Lissitzky.** *Set one active form against one passive mass on a line of force and make everything else point along it, so that the eye, and then the hand, travels a route.*
Process:
1. Name the pair before drawing anything: the actor (what is done) and the target (what it is done to). One of each per view.
2. Give the frame one large flat field, 50–70% of the area. For a confrontation, split the frame instead on one steep diagonal (60–65°) into a light part and a dark part, about 2 : 1.
3. Place the target: large (about 45% of the width in the split mode), closed and static, orthogonal, in the zone of opposite value so that it reads at full contrast.
4. Place the actor: pointed or bar-like, at most 10% of the area, on an axis 30–50° off the frame (45° by default), aimed at the target and carrying the only saturated colour. Contact is a parameter with a meaning: stop short by less than the tip's own width for an act still pending; overlap as far as the target's centre for an act completed.
5. Keep one orthogonal counter-element (a bar, a square, or a numeral about 1/3 of the frame height) so that the diagonal has something to push against.
6. Put each word on the axis of the form it belongs to. Scatter 5–9 small fragments along the line of force and nowhere else. Join related elements with hairlines, used only as connectors.
7. Carry the line out of the view as a route: a permanent stepped index on one edge, each step a visible label plus a shape, and elements that cross a corner or point at the next surface. Nothing is centred on its own surface.
Why it works:
- A pointed form on an oblique axis is read as moving toward where it points, so the eye follows it to the target and the pair becomes an event. Two orthogonal forms are two objects and nothing happens; several actors cancel each other.
- Each form sits on the ground of opposite value, so both read at full contrast while saturation belongs to the actor alone: salience by oddness. Spread the saturated colour to a background or a second element and the actor disappears.
- The orthogonal counter-element supplies the frame of reference; with everything tilted the view reads as a rotated page. Forms that run past an edge or a corner continue in the mind, which is what turns a composition into a route.
Colour relations: two values and one agent: flat field 50–70%; mass at maximum contrast to it 11–28%; one saturated colour 3–10%, or none at all; a mid neutral for secondary blocks at a few percent; one translucent plane allowed. The saturated colour appears only on the actor, the word naming the act and a changed state, and is always paired with the pointed shape.
Space and line: main axis 30–50°; split diagonal 60–65°; on a route surface the dark elements total 15–20%; depth by parallel projection, hard offset slabs and one overlay, never by blur; thick rules (about 1% of the short side) as brackets; hairlines as connectors only; scale jumps of about 1 : 10 on one line.
Type requirement: heavy capitals that hold as beams when scaled tenfold, numerals heavy enough to act as masses, and a small-capital label legible at 12px for the index. A short display word (≤8 letters) may sit on an axis or stand vertical as a duplicate; reading text and control labels stay horizontal.
Motion and interaction: movement follows vectors and stops hard (220–320ms, no bounce, no fade). A route change cuts along the main axis. Hover pushes a block a few pixels along its own axis and focus adds a thick bracket. Idle motion exists only as a named kit class (`idle-turn`): at most one decorative element per view, one axis, ≥30s per revolution, no text on it; under reduced motion it rests in a fixed three-quarter pose and progress is carried by a text label. Pointed controls keep a 44px rectangular hit area.
Fails when: the view has no actor and target (reference pages, settings lists, n equal items); several actions are equally primary; the content is long reading text; the screen is too narrow for a 45° axis to leave a horizontal measure.
Signature (do not carry together): a red wedge driven into a white circle · red and black diagonal capitals on cream · a stepped thumb index with geometric glyphs.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Product: a person-to-person payment screen in soft peach and plum with rounded forms, phone 390 × 844.
1. *Pair.* Actor: the amount being sent. Target: the recipient.
2. *Split.* One diagonal at 63° divides the screen into pale peach (L 93, 60%, lower left) and deep plum (L 22, 34%, upper right).
3. *Target.* The recipient is a peach circle 176px across (45% of the width) inside the plum zone, right of centre, the name set horizontally in it.
4. *Actor.* A tapered, round-tipped band in the only saturated colour (coral), 8% of the area, lying on the peach on a 42° axis and aimed at the circle. Before confirmation its 16px tip stops 10px short. After confirmation it overlaps to the circle's centre and the label changes to "Sent".
5. *Counter.* The amount as an upright numeral 280px tall (1/3 of the height), plum on peach, lower left.
6. *Words and fragments.* The recipient's name on the target's horizontal; a decorative "send" along the 42° axis, with the real button label horizontal at the band's base (hit area 44px). Six fragments (fee, date, note, three recent amounts) lie along the axis; a hairline joins the fee to the amount.
7. *Route.* A stepped index on the right edge: Send, Request, Split, History, each step 48px tall with a 12px small-capital label and a shape, always visible.
- *Motion.* Confirming slides the band 60px along 42° in 260ms and stops dead. The waiting screen holds the one `idle-turn` block at 40s per revolution; with reduced motion it stands still beside the text "Sending…".
Result: a soft pastel payment screen with rounded shapes. No red, no black, no hard triangle. What survived: one actor aimed at one target across a split field, contact as state, and navigation as a visible stepped edge.

## Space & composition
- **The canvas has no fixed up.** Tilt the main axis between 30° and 50°; 45° is the most common value in the works checked (For the Voice bars 44.8°, Proun 1924–25 band ≈50°, Proun 1920 rectangles ≈30–35°). An orthogonal bar or square is often kept as the counter-axis (the horizontal red bar of Proun 1924–25, the upright red square of About Two Squares). Prouns were meant to work from every direction, so build layouts that still read when the dominant block is rotated.
- **Vector plus counterweight.** Each composition has one aggressor (wedge, bar, oversized numeral) and one heavy, static mass (circle, square, black slab). Put them on a shared diagonal running from lower-left to upper-right and leave a narrow gap at the point of contact (in Beat the Whites, by contrast, the wedge overlaps the circle up to its centre: collision rather than tension). The tension sits in that gap.
- **Big flat field, small cluster.** Let one flat colour field take 50–70% of the frame (measured: Proun 1920 paper 64%, Proun 1924–25 black ground 71%). Scatter 5–9 small fragments (rectangles, short bars, tiny type) as debris along the force line.
- **Floating axonometry.** Show depth with 30°/60° parallel projection, never vanishing-point perspective, and no ground plane. Depth in the Prouns also comes from hard, unblurred black extrusion slabs offset beside a plane, from one translucent plane laid over others (the ochre band of 1924–25), and from real texture (sandpaper block, 1920).

## Color

Binary opposition with one hot agent: when red appears it is the actor, never decoration; black and paper are the stage; grey is the in-between state. Red is optional: the 1920 Proun and Proun III have none and work in greys, black and paper only.

Rule: two inks plus paper per screen, as in For the Voice; red 3–10% of the area (For the Voice 3–4% per page, Proun 1924–25 8%, About Two Squares 9%), only where something happens, or none.

## Form, line & typography
Letters are building material: stacked, rotated 90°, scaled from tiny to giant on one line, boxed in by thick rules. Type carries load like beams. Use heavy grotesque capitals for key words, 4–12 px rules as brackets and arrows, and outsized numerals as section markers (the "2" of About Two Squares is about 1/3 of the frame height). Line is ruler-straight with occasional perfect arcs and circles, never freehand. Hairlines are used, but only as vectors: thin black and red lines that connect one charged element to another (For the Voice) or nest as concentric squares (Proun 1924–25), never as dividers.

Fonts: **Rubik Mono One** (display), **Oswald** (condensed, vertical/stacked labels), **IBM Plex Sans Condensed** (body).

## Rhythm, motion & time
The "bioscopic book" thesis makes each scroll step a film cut. Motion follows vectors: elements slide along the composition's −45° diagonal and stop hard, no bounce, with mechanical easing (`cubic-bezier(.7,0,.2,1)`, 220–320 ms). Pressa's blinking letters and conveyors allow at most one looping element per screen. Hover pushes a block along its own axis rather than lifting it with a shadow.

## UI translations
1. Rotate the hero composition's main bar to −45° (range 30–50°) and let it run off two viewport edges. Content sits along it, not below it.
2. Make the single primary CTA a red wedge (`clip-path: polygon(0 0,100% 50%,0 100%)`) pointing at the thing it affects.
3. Build section navigation as a fore-edge thumb index: a fixed right rail of stepped tabs that sit at different vertical offsets, each showing a short label in small caps above a red geometric glyph (■ ● ▲ ▬), labels always visible.
4. Give each destination its own title spread, a full-screen two-colour typographic page, before the content starts.
5. Cap every view at two inks plus paper. Use red only for state change, selection and action.
6. Set headings in mixed scale on one baseline: one word at 12vw, the next at 1.2rem, rotated 90° when space runs out.
7. Use thick black rules (8 px) as structural brackets that group content, never hairline dividers or blurred card shadows. 1px lines are allowed only as connectors that link a control to what it affects; a hard, unblurred offset black slab may stand in for depth.
8. Animate route changes as cuts along the diagonal: the outgoing screen leaves on −45° and the incoming one enters on the same vector, with a hard stop and no fade.
9. Draw empty states and illustrations as floating axonometric blocks (30°/60°) with no ground shadow.
10. On dashboards, run a horizontal photomontage strip (Pressa frieze) as a scrolling belt of image and data fragments that crop each other at odd angles.
11. Put one rotating 3D primitive per screen as a loading or idle state, rotating slowly on one axis.

## What this artist would reject
- Centered, symmetrical hero sections with nothing pushing against anything.
- Soft drop shadows and frosted glass that fake depth. Depth should be constructed, not lit.
- Colour used as brand wallpaper, such as gradient backgrounds where red, or any accent, carries no meaning.
- Hamburger menus that hide the structure. Navigation should be visible and physical, like the thumb index.
- Uniform type scales with no jumps, and body-sized headings.
- Decorative illustration that comments on content instead of driving it.
