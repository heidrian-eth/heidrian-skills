# Varvara Stepanova — Constructivism

## Essence
Stepanova designed systems, not pictures. She worked out one small unit (a chevron, a belted tunic, a slatted stool) under a budget of one or two dyes and four shapes, then let repetition and use produce the variety. Construction stays visible, and decoration has to earn its place by working.

## Key works examined
- **Gaust chaba, 1919**: a book of zaum (invented-language) poems, watercolor lettering painted over found newspaper pages, edition of 54. Teaches you to treat the substrate as a layer: a new, louder system printed straight over the old grid. *(not obtained: unverified; see the check's missing-works log (not shipped))*
- **Set for *The Death of Tarelkin* (Meyerhold), 1922**: separate wooden "apparatuses" (treadle and cage, stools, chairs, high and low tables, a folding screen) that actors could combine or use alone. Teaches modular components with mechanical, sometimes misbehaving, states. *(visually checked: State Central Theatre Museum (GCTM) collection photograph, object 36771, 1922)* Seen: about a dozen separate pieces stand in one loose row across a bare stage in front of a brick wall: two slatted cages, a folding screen, a tall lattice cabinet, a white half-disc on a board, ladder-like chairs, two tables, stools, and one chair collapsed at an angle. Every piece is built from the same thin pale slats with gaps equal to or wider than the slats, so the set reads as one family of parts. The spacing is uneven: the cages at the left stand apart, while in the middle the ladder, the half-disc board, the tables, stools and the cabinet touch and overlap in depth as one dense cluster, and the right end thins out again. No colour or ornament.
- **Sportswear designs for *LEF*, 1923**: boxy unisex tunics where emblems and contrasting color mark the team, not rank or gender. Differentiation must be functional. *(not obtained: unverified; see the check's missing-works log (not shipped))*
- **Fabric designs, First State Textile Printing Factory, 1924**: more than 150 designs in about a year, built only from triangles, circles, squares and lines in one or two dyes. Teaches a constrained token set with very large output. *(not obtained: unverified; see the check's missing-works log (not shipped))*
- **Chevron flannel, Tsindel factory, 1924 (V&A)**: wine-red and olive-brown chevrons on natural fawn. The unbleached ground counts as a color. *(visually checked: V&A photo.)* Seen: the "wine" is a faded rose-brick and the "olive-brown" a grey-green olive; the two dyes cover ≈38% and ≈36% and the fawn ground only ≈25%, reduced to narrow bands that read as lines between the chevrons. Arms run at ≈60–65° from horizontal; red and olive chevrons alternate column by column, each column shifted half a repeat (half-drop), about 2.6 repeats across the sample.
- **SA (Sovremennaia arkhitektura) no. 2 cover, 1926** (added; AIC, credited to Stepanova): red "СА" and black "2" fill the top third at full width; a black block takes ≈55% of the cover with paper-coloured type knocked out and one paper-coloured vertical bar. Red 10%, paper 31%. *(visually checked: AIC)*
- **Oktiabr' book catalog cover, 1927** (added; AIC): full-height vertical stripes alternating red and photo-grey, a 45° diamond photomontage framed by hairline chevrons, outlined capitals with an offset black shadow. *(visually checked: AIC)*

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Tarelkin set): 10–12 free-standing pieces in a single row on an empty floor, with uneven gaps: separate at the ends, touching and overlapping in a dense middle cluster; heights vary (stool to cabinet), the tallest pieces near the middle.
- **Colour relations:** one material colour (pale slatted wood) against a dark ground; contrast comes only from value (pale lattice on dark brick).
- **Line and edge:** everything is made of parallel slats; slat and gap of similar width, so every object is half void.
- **The procedure:** design one construction rule (parallel slats, open lattice); build every component from it; place the components separately on an empty field with equal spacing; allow one to be shown in a 'failed' state (collapsed).

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Varvara Stepanova.** *Set a tiny budget first (one unit, one way of building, two inks and the ground) and get every variety from repetition, offset, inversion and use.*
Process:
1. Fix the budget before any layout: one unit cell, at most 3 primitives drawn from a vocabulary of four (circle, square, triangle, bar), two inks and the ground. Nothing later may add to it.
2. Fix one construction rule and build every component from it, for example parallel members with a gap at least as wide as the member, so that every object is about half void. Components then read as one family at any size.
3. Fill by rule, not by composition: repeat the unit with a half-drop offset and alternate the two inks column by column. Inside a patterned area the inks cover about 75% in near-equal shares (≈38 : 36) and the ground shrinks to thin separating lines (≈25%).
4. Outside the pattern, reverse the proportion: components stand as separate pieces in one row on bare ground, heights varied, the tallest near the middle.
5. Differentiate by assignment, not by decoration: each actor, team or series gets a whole kit (an emblem, a stripe and an ink) on the same cut.
6. For a single flat sheet, mass instead of repeating: one block of the ink farthest from the ground at about 55%, the second ink at about 10%, the title filling the top third at full width, type knocked out of the block.
Why it works:
- With the variables fixed in advance, any crop is recognisable (grouping by likeness) and variety can only come from phase and position, which is what lets one rule produce a hundred results. A third ink ends the alternation: two colours are a pair, three are a palette.
- The half-drop breaks rows and columns, so the eye reads diagonals and one continuous texture, not tiles. Thinning the ground reverses figure and ground: the ground becomes the drawn line. Widen it and the units turn into spots on a background.
- When everything is built by one rule, separate pieces read as a set without touching, and the one piece in another state stands out by oddness. Irregularity is legible only against that regularity.
Colour relations: ground plus two inks, no tints. In pattern the two inks are near-equal in area and close in value, differing in temperature, and both darker than the ground. In sheet mode about 55 : 31 : 10 (far ink : ground : second ink). In built mode there is no hue at all, only a light lattice against a dark ground. An ink belongs to an actor or a state and is never the only thing that tells two apart.
Space and line: one unit cell; half-drop; pattern arms steep (60–65°); member equals gap; overlaps keep hard edges; ruler and compass only, with no corner radius short of a full circle; seams and joints stay visible. The share of patterned to bare area is a free parameter (about 30 : 70 is a hint only; it was never verified).
Type requirement: heavy monoline capitals that can be drawn from the same primitives as the unit, fill a third of a sheet at full width and survive being knocked out of a block, with a tabular companion for data. Labels stay horizontal.
Motion and interaction: a mechanism doing a job: hinge, fold, drop; linear or stepped, under 200ms, one pass. A pattern advances one unit per scroll step. Switching context recolours the whole kit in one cut. Hover or focus inverts the unit (ground and ink exchange) and adds a marker bar, so the state is never value alone. One non-control item per view (an empty state, an illustration) may be shown in its other state, collapsed or folded; an action the user needs never misbehaves. Reduced motion: states swap with no travel.
Fails when: one view must separate more than two categories by hue; the content is photographs or rich media (they bring a third and fourth ink); text or controls have to sit on the pattern (equal stripe and gap vibrates, so pattern is decorative and content sits on bare ground); the brief needs one composed hero image.
Signature (do not carry together): a two-dye chevron repeat on unbleached cloth · slatted wooden stage apparatus · boxy striped team sportswear.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Product: an on-call rota for three engineering teams, dark navy, desktop 1440 × 900.
1. *Budget.* One 32px cell holding at most a bar, a circle and a triangle. Two inks, ice blue and amber, on a navy ground (L 14). A build check fails on any third hue; errors are shown by emblem, inversion and a word.
2. *Construction rule.* Everything is made of parallel 4px bars with gaps of 4px or more: shift blocks, button frames and charts are all about half void.
3. *Pattern.* Past weeks are filled with the unit, half-dropped, the inks alternating by day column; inks about 75%, navy reduced to 2px lines. No text or control sits on it; a text summary of the same weeks stands beside it on bare navy.
4. *Bare ground.* The coming week is six free-standing shift modules in one row, 32px apart, height by shift length (the 12-hour shifts mid-row), on bare navy.
5. *Kits.* Team one: triangle, vertical stripe, ice blue. Team two: circle, horizontal stripe, amber. Team three: square, diagonal stripe, both inks. No team is identified by colour alone.
6. *Sheet mode.* The page header is one ice-blue block covering 55% of the header band, the rota's name knocked out of it at full width across the top third, an amber strip of 10%.
- *Interaction.* Hover or focus inverts a module and adds an 8px marker bar on its left edge. Swapping a shift hinges the module on its left edge in four steps over 160ms; with reduced motion it swaps.
- *Empty state.* "No one on call" shows one module drawn collapsed at an angle, with plain text and an ordinary "Assign" button (44px) beside it.
Result: a dark technical scheduler. No chevrons, no cloth, no red. What survived: a budget fixed first, one building rule, variety by offset and alternation, pattern as density against bare ground, and identity as a kit.

## Space & composition
- **The tile is the composition.** Design a unit on a 24×24 or 24×48 px cell and repeat it with half-drop or mirror offsets. A page is an area filled by a rule, not a hero-and-sections scene.
- **Overprinting creates depth, not shadow.** Her textiles stack flat layers so they read in several dimensions with no gradients. Overlap cards with hard edges and a visible z-order, never blur.
- **Discrete apparatuses.** As on the Tarelkin stage, components stand free on an empty floor, some alone and some touching or overlapping in a cluster, and can link to their neighbors. Lay out a dashboard as 4–6 free-standing modules on a bare field, joined by visible hinges (shared edges, connector bars), not a grid of identical cards.
- **Density alternates.** Tight pattern strips run against wide plain bands, about 30% pattern to 70% flat ground (layout rule, unverified). Inside a pattern the ratio flips: the chevron flannel is ≈75% dye and ≈25% ground.
- **Diagonals carry action.** The key path runs on a 30–45° bar across an orthogonal grid.

## Form, line & typography
- The shape vocabulary is circle, square, triangle and the ruled bar. Edges are compass and ruler only: no freehand curves, and no corner radius except a full circle.
- Gaust chaba adds a second register: hand-painted, uneven capitals over printed columns. Use it for one expressive word per screen, as a rough SVG over a low-contrast "newsprint" text layer.
- Fonts (all Google Fonts): **Unbounded** for headings, **Rubik Mono One** for labels and numerals, **IBM Plex Mono** for the newsprint underlayer and data. Set type large, uppercase and tightly tracked.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- Things should move like the Tarelkin furniture, a mechanism doing a job: hinge, fold, drop and spring, with linear or `steps(4)` easing.
- Pattern fields scroll like a conveyor belt, moving exactly one tile width per scroll unit.
- Let some states misbehave on purpose, as stage furniture does in a circus-style farce. A destructive-action button can "fold" flat (`scaleY: 0` from the bottom edge over 120 ms) before it confirms.
- Team logic *(unverified: sportswear not obtained)*: switching user or context recolors the whole uniform (accent token) in one hard cut. Do not crossfade.

## UI translations
1. Build every background from one 24 px SVG tile of no more than 3 primitives, repeated with `background-repeat` and a half-drop offset (confirmed on the chevron flannel). Let the two inks cover about 75% of a pattern band and keep the ground as thin separating lines.
2. Limit each screen to ground `#CDBCA0` plus two inks. Validate in CI that no third hue appears.
3. Overlap adjacent modules by 12 px with hard edges and a 2 px black rule. Express hierarchy through stacking order, not shadows.
4. Run the primary CTA on a single 35° red bar that crosses the grid and bleeds off one edge.
5. Expose construction: show grid lines and connector "seams" as visible hairlines. Treat structure as ornament.
6. Let users dock free-standing modules into larger assemblies by dragging one flush to another.
7. Assign each user, team or data series a full "uniform": an emblem glyph, a stripe and a color. Never tell series apart by color alone. *(Team kits are unverified: the LEF sportswear designs were not obtained.)*
8. Put one hand-painted zaum headline per screen over a 10%-opacity monospaced newsprint underlayer.
9. Animate with `steps()` or linear easing on hinge transforms (`rotate`, `scaleY` from an edge). Keep every duration under 200 ms.
10. Set section labels in uppercase Rubik Mono One, rotated 90° and running up the left edge of each module.
11. Show a hover state by inverting the tile: the ground becomes ink and the ink becomes ground.
12. Alternate dense pattern bands (about 64 px) with wide plain bands (about 160 px) as the vertical rhythm.

## What this artist would reject
- Floral or "lifestyle" decoration (blob art, stock-photo heroes) used to soften a product.
- Borderless cards, frosted glass and drop shadows that hide how things are built.
- Uniform rounded corners and pill shapes that are neither a true circle nor a true square.
- Gradients and big palettes. More inks than the process needs.
- Gold "premium" badges: differentiate by role, not rank.
- One-off bespoke layouts that cannot be repeated as a system.
