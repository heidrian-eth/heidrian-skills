# Lyubov Popova — Constructivism

## Essence
Popova treats a picture as a structure under load. Colored planes overlap, slant and push past the frame, and later thin lines of force cut through them, so the picture reads as energy held in balance. From 1922 she took the same grammar into working objects: a stage set built for actors to use, and cheap two-color prints repeated across cloth by the bale.

## Key works examined
- **Painterly Architectonic, 1917** (MoMA, oil, 80 × 98 cm). Red, black, gray, pink and white planes lie over a pale ground. They avoid right angles and seem to continue past the frame. It teaches layered planes and diagonal tension. *(MoMA 1917 canvas not obtained; checked instead two closely related Painterly Architectonics, 1918, Thyssen-Bornemisza inv. 714 and 716, museum scans.)* Seen: the planes fill 100% of the canvas with no ground showing, and every plane is cut by an edge; the main slants run 25–40°. The planes are not flat: each carries a tonal ramp toward one edge (black turning blue, blue turning white, orange turning brick), and inv. 714 has heavy impasto. Inv. 716 uses 7 colours (blue-black 43%, cool grey-white 21%, brick 15%, light blue 7%, green 6.5%, ochre 4%, blue 3.5%); no pink was seen.
- **Spatial-Force Construction, 1921** (MOMus Costakis Collection, oil and wood sand on plywood, 112.5 × 112.3 cm). A near-square board with a gritty surface. Crossed thin lines run over interpenetrating planes. It teaches vectors laid over fields, texture as information, and a square format. *(visually checked: MOMus Costakis scan, collection no. 0011026, via Europeana)* Seen: a square board (1.01:1) whose bare orange plywood is the ground (≈45% of the surface, `#C87C40`/`#BA6738`, wood grain visible). Over it: three red rings and arcs (`#CB413A`, 12%) cut by black bars of 4–5 different widths (`#2D2E2C`, 13%) that cross off centre and run off all four edges; maroon wedges (`#724335`, 14%); and the gritty planes are white, not coloured (`#DAD0BA`, 12%), with dry, feathered edges while the bars and rings stay hard. One small blue-grey plane. So five colours plus the wood, not three.
- **The Magnanimous Cuckold, 1922** (Meyerhold). A free-standing bare-wood frame on an empty stage, with platforms, crossing struts, turning wheels and windmill blades. It teaches that the interface is an apparatus that actors perform by using. *(visually checked: AIC ink study, dated 1920 by the AIC.)* Seen: two stair ramps at ≈43° and ≈52° climb to platforms; nearly every bay of the frame is X-braced (not one brace); three wheels of different sizes overlap the frame, and the inscription assigns colours: largest wheel black, smallest red, middle unpainted wood, front structure black, back structure wood. *Retry pass 2: the construction itself visually checked (State Central Theatre Museum photograph of the 'constructive setting', object 1504527; it appears to show the model).* Seen: two platforms on X-braced frames joined by a long diagonal bridge, a stair at each end (≈40–45°), windmill blades top left, and three wheels: a large black disc carrying white letters (the start of the author's name), a spoked wheel, and a pale wheel behind. Dark-stained and bare pale members alternate, matching the inscription; nearly every bay is X-braced; a curved bench stands free in front.
- **Textile designs for the First State Textile Printing Works (formerly Zindel), 1923–24**. Circles, stripes, grids turned 45°, and stars, mostly in one color plus black and/or the bare white cloth. It teaches permutation of one motif in an endless repeat. *(visually checked: MOMus Costakis scans 0013047, 0013049, 0013054, 0013056, via Europeana)* Seen: three of four designs use one hue plus black on the paper ground (pink `#DC5373` ≈10% + black 28% on 58% paper; blue `#014A87` 28% + black 23%; violet stripes alone). The fourth uses four inks (gold ochre `#997417` 25%, brick `#762D17` 10%, black 12%, on grey paper): striped discs inside concentric gold rings alternating with nested diamonds turned 45°. Motifs: concentric rings, a disc of horizontal or vertical stripes, a split ring, nested squares; stripes are of equal width to their gaps.
- **LEF no. 2 cover textile design, 1924**. Red and black only. The ЛЕФ letters are sliced horizontally in alternating colors, inside a frame of diagonals and red circles. *(Not obtained. The split-letter device itself was seen on the Lef no. 4 cover, AIC, credited to Rodchenko: red over black, split at 51% of the letter height.)*

- **Muzykal'naia nov' (Musical New Land) no. 10 cover, 1924** *(added in the retry pass as the nearest obtainable 1924 graphic work, since the LEF no. 2 design was not found; visually checked: Cooper Hewitt scan, object 68793051)*. Two inks on cream paper: black (24% of the cover) and orange `#D67C40` (20%) on paper `#E9E0C5` (43%). The masthead letters are drawn, condensed and stretched to fill their shapes: МУЗЫКАЛЬНАЯ runs around a heavy black ring and НОВЬ is set in orange letters that swell to fill the circle inside it. A full-width black band below holds "1924 / Nº 10" in black on cream with the subtitle reversed out of an orange panel; orange bars run up both side edges. No diagonals: the tension comes from the circle against the orthogonal frame.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Spatial-Force Construction 1921): square field; no dominant plane. The figure is a dense knot of crossing bars and three rings whose centre sits slightly left of and above the middle; bars run off all four edges, so the knot is anchored by the frame rather than floating. Ground (bare wood) ≈45%, figure ≈55%.
- **Colour relations** (SFC 1921): 5 colours on a coloured ground. The most saturated colour (red) is ≈12% and is carried only as rings and arcs, i.e. as line, not field; black (13%) is the structure; white (12%) and maroon (14%) are the fields. Red touches black at every crossing; white sits next to the warm ground for the strongest value step; warm dominates, with one small cool grey plane as the only counter-note.
- **Colour relations** (textiles 1923–24): one hue + black + ground, with the ground the largest area (≈40–60%), black second (≈20–30%) and the hue ≈10–30%. The hue never touches the ground without black nearby in 3 of 4 designs.
- **Line and edge** (SFC 1921): bars in 4–5 discrete widths (≈0.4–2.5% of the side); bars and rings hard-edged, the white planes dry-brushed and soft. Hard = vector, soft = field.
- **Line and edge** (textiles): stripes equal to their gaps; rings drawn concentric with even spacing; no soft edges.
- **The procedure** (SFC): lay a coloured ground; place 3 overlapping rings off centre; cross them with straight bars at 3–4 angles in several fixed widths, each running off an edge; put soft-edged light fields behind the crossings; give the strongest hue only to the rings.
- **The procedure** (textiles): choose one motif (ring, striped disc, nested square); permute it once (rotate 45°, split, invert stripe direction); repeat on a lattice; one hue + black + ground.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Lyubov Popova.** *Lay two systems over each other, soft fields beneath and hard vectors above, and tie the vectors to the frame so that the surface reads as a structure holding a load.*
Process:
1. Choose the ground as a working colour and decide how much of it shows: none (fields fill the frame), about half (≈45%), or most (40–60%, the repeat mode). It is never neutral leftover.
2. Lay 3–5 overlapping fields slanted 25–40° off the frame, answered by one opposing slant at a different angle, never a mirror. Fields take the soft treatment: each lightens toward one edge only, or ends in a dry, broken edge.
3. Cross the fields with vectors that follow a different geometry: straight bars in 4–5 fixed widths (0.4–2.5% of the short side) at 3–4 angles, plus 2–3 arcs or rings, all meeting in a knot displaced a little from the centre. Vectors are always hard-edged.
4. Anchor to the frame: every vector (or, with no ground showing, every field) runs off an edge, and the bars leave by all four sides. Round elements overlap the structure instead of sitting inside one of its bays.
5. Assign colour by job. The darkest is structure (13–43%). The strongest hue is carried as line, not as field (about 12%), and touches the structure colour at every crossing. The lightest fields lie against the ground for the largest value step. One small note of the opposite temperature (≤5%) is the only relief.
6. For anything repeated, take one motif and change it once per instance (turn it 45°, split it, reverse its stripe direction), and hold the budget to one hue, the structure colour and the ground, in that rising order of area.
Why it works:
- Edge quality sorts depth. A hard edge reads as a thing in front and a soft edge as a surface behind, so two layers separate with no shadow. Make both hard and they fuse into one mosaic; make both soft and nothing bears a load.
- A line that reaches the frame reads as fixed there and under tension, like a strut, and as continuing beyond. The same knot floating inside a margin is a motif on a background. Displacing the knot makes the spans unequal, which is what reads as force.
- Fixed widths let lines be grouped by likeness and ranked; continuously varying widths are noise. Keeping the strongest hue thin makes it read as energy, not as mass, so it can cross everything without becoming the ground.
Colour relations: one temperature dominates with a single small opposite note. Vector mode: ground ≈45%, structure ≈13%, strongest hue ≈12% as line, light fields ≈12%, dark fields ≈14%. Field mode: no ground, 6–7 colours, the darkest up to 43%, every field ramped toward light on one edge. Repeat mode: ground 40–60 : structure 20–30 : hue 10–30, the hue rarely touching the ground without the structure colour beside it. Cover mode: two inks and the ground at about 24 : 20 : 43.
Space and line: slants 25–40°; ramps of 40–52° in a scaffold; bars in 4–5 widths; knot off-centre; a near-square format in vector mode; nearly every bay cross-braced where a frame is shown; in repeats the stripe equals the gap; hard edge means vector, soft edge means field.
Type requirement: condensed capitals drawn to stretch in both width and height until they fill a ring, a band or a circle, and sturdy enough to be reversed out of a solid band, with a plain grotesque for working text. Reading text sits horizontally on flat platforms between the vectors.
Motion and interaction: mechanical. Fields slide in along their own slant, back to front, 60ms apart; vectors draw inward from the frame (180–260ms, no overshoot). Hover or focus steps the vector that crosses an item to the next fixed width (line weight, not elevation). A control may show its mechanism (a quarter turn) if position and label change with it. Constant rotation exists only as a named idle class: decorative, ≥30s per turn, still under reduced motion, and progress is carried by filled segments and a number.
Fails when: the content is long text or a table and no flat platform is provided; there are two or fewer elements (no knot, so no load); the screen is small enough that the thinnest bar drops under 1px; an equal stripe-and-gap repeat is placed under text (it vibrates; decorative only).
Signature (do not carry together): slanted overlapping planes in brick and blue-black · bright rings crossed by black bars on bare board · a cross-braced scaffold with wheels.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Product: a project dependency planner in pale mint and ice, light and sparse, desktop 1440 × 900.
1. *Ground.* Ice grey shows over about 45% and means "unscheduled". It is a state, not a background.
2. *Fields.* Four phase planes slanted 28°, answered by one at 37°. Each lightens toward its leading edge over its last 25%. Task lists sit on flat, upright white platforms docked on the planes, text horizontal.
3. *Vectors.* Dependencies are straight bars in four widths, 4, 8, 14 and 22px (0.44–2.4% of 900), by number of tasks blocked, at three angles. Two arcs mark recurring review cycles. They meet in a knot at 44% / 42%: the release.
4. *Anchor.* Every bar runs off an edge, toward work before or after the visible range. The arcs overlap the platforms.
5. *Colour.* Deep petrol, the darkest, is the bars (13%). Violet, the strongest hue, appears only as the two arcs and as the one "Resolve blocker" control (labelled, 44px high). The palest planes lie against the ice ground. One apricot note of 3% marks today.
6. *Repeat.* Task types use one ring motif changed once each: ring, split ring, disc striped across, disc striped down, nested square turned 45°. Violet, petrol and ground only.
- *Interaction.* Hovering a task steps its dependency bar to the next width; focus adds the count as a label.
- *Motion.* Planes slide in along 28°, back to front, 60ms apart; bars draw from the edge inward in 240ms. Nothing rotates at rest.
Result: a cool, airy planning tool. No brick, no board, no scaffold. What survived: soft fields under hard vectors, fixed line widths as rank, an off-centre knot, and every line tied to the frame.

## Space & composition
- **Tilt the dominant axis.** Her planes avoid true horizontals. The main slant sits around 25–40° off orthogonal (Painterly Architectonics 1918), and one opposing slant answers it at a different angle, never a mirror.
- **Stack, don't shadow.** Depth comes from overlap order (3–5 planes), and the front plane gets a light edge.
- **Overflow the frame.** At least one major plane is cropped by the edge on two sides; in the 1918 Painterly Architectonics every plane touches an edge and no empty ground is left.
- **Force lines over fields.** In the 1921 works, thin lines cross the planes, meet off center and run off the edge, following a different geometry from the planes.
- **Scaffold layouts (Cuckold).** An exposed, load-bearing frame with empty stage around it: X-braced bays, stair ramps at 43–52°, and circles (wheels) that overlap the frame instead of sitting inside it.
- **Square modules.** The 112 × 112 cm board and the textile repeats suggest a square base tile, for example 96 px.

## Form, line & typography
Shapes are trapezoids, sheared rectangles, arcs and circles. Lines are taut, with square ends. Type should feel constructed, as on the LEF cover: condensed geometric caps, sliced into two colors (unverified for Popova), set on a slant (seen on her 1922 cover for Bobrov's *Vosstanie mizantropov*, Stedelijk 81249: huge stacked condensed letters, the first in orange and the rest black, one word rotated 90° and one on a ≈45° diagonal, over a halftone photo). Seen on the 1924 Muzykal'naia nov' cover: hand-drawn condensed caps stretched to fill a ring or a circle, and words reversed out of solid bands. Use a plain grotesk for working text.
- **Big Shoulders Display**: condensed, strutted headlines.
- **Oswald**: compact uppercase labels.
- **Rubik** or **Archivo**: body and UI text with slightly squared curves.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- **Mechanical, not organic.** Like the Cuckold wheels: constant `linear` rotation (8–20 s) for ambient elements, and decisive `cubic-bezier(.7,0,.3,1)` moves (180–260 ms) for state changes.
- **Planes slide along their own slant.** On entry, a plane travels along its tilt axis, not straight up. Layers arrive in overlap order, back to front, 60 ms apart.
- **Textile time.** Backgrounds can drift as a slow seamless repeat, like cloth running off a roller.

## UI translations
1. Rotate the hero container's main color plane by −30° (25–40°) and let it bleed off the top and right edges (`overflow: clip` on the parent).
2. Build depth from 3–5 overlapping flat planes with `z-index` order only, with no `box-shadow`. Give the front plane a 3 px `#C8C1AE` edge (the 1918 planes show a light band along one edge).
3. Draw 1–2 px black "force lines" in an absolutely positioned SVG. They cross card boundaries and meet at a node off the grid.
4. Show the scaffold: render the layout grid as visible 1 px struts. Put content on platforms between them, and X-brace most bays (the Cuckold frame is braced almost everywhere), with one heavier black ramp at about 45°.
5. Limit each screen to three colors from the palette. Red marks only the one actionable thing.
6. Split display headlines horizontally: top half black, bottom half red, using `background-clip: text` with a hard `linear-gradient` stop at 50%.
7. Fill empty and loading states with a two-color CSS `background-image` repeat (circles on a 45° grid).
8. Use a square base tile for cards and thumbnails. Break the rhythm once per screen with a tile twice as wide on a slant.
9. Treat controls as visible mechanisms. A toggle is a wheel that turns 90°, and a progress indicator is a constantly spinning segmented disc.
10. Add a faint grain (SVG `feTurbulence`, 4–6% opacity) to the largest plane only, like the wood-sand surface (seen on Spatial-Force Construction: white planes with dry feathered edges, against hard-edged bars and rings) or the impasto of Painterly Architectonic inv. 714 (seen). Optionally ramp one edge of a plane toward white or blue with a hard-start linear gradient over its last 20–30%, as the 1918 planes do.
11. In each section, say a feature list, show one motif in 4–6 permutations (circle, half circle, ring, circle with a bar), not a different icon for each item.

## What this artist would reject
- Soft drop shadows and frosted glass that fake depth.
- Soft, decorative gradients and 12-color "brand palettes" (her own 1918 tonal ramps are structural: one edge of a plane, toward light).
- Perfectly centered, symmetrical heroes with everything inside the safe area.
- Decorative illustration that does no work.
- Bouncy springs. Her motion is mechanical.
- Hiding the layout grid. She left the frame bare.

## Signature prompt seed
Design this screen as a Popova construction: a paper ground (#C8C1AE, the largest area) with at most three flat inks: structure black, one warm hue (brick red or orange) and one gray. Tilt the dominant plane about 30° and let it run off two edges. Stack 3–5 planes for depth without any shadow. Draw thin black force lines that cross card borders and meet off center. Show the grid as X-braced struts. Set headlines in condensed caps drawn to fill their shapes, as on the Muzykal'naia nov' cover (the split black-over-red letters are Rodchenko's Lef no. 4 device; Popova's LEF no. 2 was not obtained). Every element must work like apparatus. Motion is mechanical: constant rotation, crisp linear slides along each plane's own slant, and a two-color geometric repeat in the empty areas.
