# Alexander Rodchenko — Constructivism

## Essence
Rodchenko treated every surface as a device that does a job, and every viewpoint as one he could change. He pushed painting to its end (three flat primaries) and then moved the same geometry into posters, furniture, and the camera. The result is a hard diagonal, a low or high angle, a few stock parts that fold into several uses, and no decoration.

## Key works examined
- **Spatial Construction no. 12, c. 1920**: nested ovals cut from one plywood sheet and rotated into 3D. It hangs with no top, bottom, or base. Lesson: build depth from one flat plane, rotated. *(not obtained: unverified; see the check's missing-works log (not shipped))*
- **Pure Red Color, Pure Yellow Color, Pure Blue Color, 1921**: three monochrome canvases shown at *5x5=25*, which he called the end of painting. Lesson: a full flat field of one color is enough. *(not obtained: unverified; see the check's missing-works log (not shipped))*
- **Lengiz "Books in All Branches of Knowledge" poster, 1924** (text by Mayakovsky): Lilya Brik shouts through a cupped hand, and a megaphone-shaped wedge of type comes out of her mouth. Lesson: the type is the voice, and it projects along a vector. *(visually checked: Pushkin Museum collection image, gr_2213, the 1965 reconstruction by Varvara Rodchenko; small, 430 px)* Seen: landscape sheet (1.41:1). The photo of Brik is upright, cut into a circle that overlaps a blue disc on the left; the wedge is black with a thin cream outline, its axis horizontal (not tilted), opening ≈40° from the mouth and reaching ≈40% of the sheet width; КНИГИ is set in red condensed caps inside it that grow from ≈1/3 to full wedge height toward the open end. The diagonals are in the ground: green trapezoids above and below the wedge and red/white rays fanning to the right. The sheet is framed by ЛЕНГИЗ top and bottom, split black/red per syllable on white/red bars. Six flat colours: red ≈29%, paper ≈24%, black ≈20%, green ≈10%, blue ≈7%, plus the grey photo ≈7%.
- **Workers' Club, Paris 1925**: Ripolin enamel in red, white, and grey, with black only on the chess table. It had fold-out tables, a rotating chessboard, and rotating hexagonal display drums. Lesson: components change state, and color marks the zones. *(visually checked: AIC period photograph, black-and-white, so hues cannot be verified.)* Seen: about half of the back wall is a dark panel (black or a dark colour; the photo cannot tell), so dark is not confined to the chess table; the zone word "ЛЕНИН" runs across light and dark wall at the top, each letter built from a grid of outlined square cells (≈2–3 × 4 cells), not stencilled; chairs have semicircular hoop backs; the long reading table has a tilted display surface with periodicals laid along it.
- **Miasnitskaya Street building series, 1925**: shot straight up and straight down at his own apartment block. Lesson: tilting the viewpoint makes ordinary things look strange. *(not obtained: unverified; see the check's missing-works log (not shipped))*
- **Girl with a Leica, 1932–34**: raking bench shadows cut across the figure and the angle confuses depth. Lesson: overlay a diagonal pattern across content. *(not obtained: unverified; see the check's missing-works log (not shipped))*
- **Lef no. 4 cover, 1924** (added; AIC): orthogonal page, black vertical bar at the left edge, staggered bold text with red keywords, a big red "4" top right and the masthead ЛЕФ split red over black at 51% of the letter height. *(visually checked: AIC)*

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Lengiz 1924): the sheet is divided by a horizontal axis through the mouth; above and below it, the ground is cut into converging trapezoids that all point back to the mouth, so the whole sheet works as one perspective toward a single point at ≈1/3 of the width. The figure (photo circle) sits at the left third; the message (wedge) spans the middle; the small text block sits at the right end of the axis.
- **Colour relations** (Lengiz): six flat colours with no tints; the most saturated (red) is also the largest (≈29%), and black (≈20%) contains it: red letters on black inside the wedge, black letters on white in the masthead. Green and blue (≈17% together) are the cool counter-fields behind the figure and above/below the wedge; red and green meet only at ray edges. The photo is the only grey.
- **Line and edge:** all edges hard; the wedge carries a thin light outline that separates black from the coloured ground.
- **The procedure:** fix one source point (the mouth); draw one wedge from it along the main reading axis and set the key word inside, letters growing toward the open end; cut the remaining ground into fields that converge on the same point; frame top and bottom with the sender's name.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Alexander Rodchenko.** *Fix one source and make the whole surface project from it: the message swells toward the reader, the ground converges back on the source, and the darkest colour holds the loudest one.*
Process:
1. Fix the source: one point at about 1/3 of the width on the main reading axis, or, in the orthogonal mode, one bar on the leading edge (about 5% of the width). Everything after this is placed relative to it.
2. Frame with the sender: the name of whoever is speaking runs at full width along the top and bottom edges, or stands as the largest element at the foot (about 1/3 of the height), split into two colours at a hard line (between syllables, or horizontally at half the letter height).
3. Project the message from the source along the reading axis in one widening channel that opens 30–45° and spans about 40% of the width. Set the key word to fill it, the letters growing from 1/3 to the full channel height toward the open end. Orthogonal mode: the lines step away from the edge bar, each indented differently, with the key words picked out.
4. Cut the rest of the ground into flat fields whose edges all converge on the source. No field is left without a job.
5. Colour only in full flat fields, 3–6 of them. The most saturated may be the largest (11–29%), but wherever it carries the message it is enclosed by the darkest: saturated letters inside a dark channel, and a thin light outline between the channel and the coloured ground. The opposite-temperature fields (≤1/5 together) sit behind the source.
6. Label a zone with one word built from a modular cell grid (about 3 × 4 cells a letter) that crosses the zone boundary unchanged.
Why it works:
- Edges that converge act as pointers: the eye is pulled to the vertex and then released along the widening channel, and letters that grow read as approach and as loudness. With equal letters and a centred source the same word is a caption.
- Containment. When the loudest colour is also the largest field, the message would dissolve into it; enclosing the saturated letters in the darkest value gives them the strongest edge on the sheet and keeps figure and ground apart.
- The sender's name at both edges closes the sheet, so the internal diagonals cannot lead the eye out of it, and it says who is speaking in any crop.
Colour relations: flat, undiluted, no tints or ramps; most saturated 11–29% and allowed to be the largest; darkest 13–20% as container, bar and text; light ground from about 24% (convergent mode) to about 75% (orthogonal mode); opposite-temperature fields about 17% together; the saturated colour meets its opposite only along converging edges; a continuous-tone image is the only neutral (≤7%), cut to a simple closed shape.
Space and line: source at 1/3; channel 30–45° over about 40% of the width; edge bar about 5%; every edge hard; rules are bars; the channel's thin outline is the only fine line; the orthogonal mode has no diagonal at all.
Type requirement: capitals that can be fitted to a container letter by letter (a wide width range, or outlines scaled individually), a squared monoline display face that survives being cut in two colours, and a bold plain face for staggered text. The smallest letter of a growing word stays ≥12px and the word remains real text.
Motion and interaction: mechanical: pivot, slide on a track, unfold on a hinge, 180–260ms, no overshoot. A transition is a single wipe opening from the source along the axis. A panel expands in place on a hinge to 2–3× its size instead of opening an overlay; hover widens the channel by a few degrees and thickens its outline (width, not lift). Reduced motion: states swap without travel. (Derived from the process and from written accounts of the folding furniture, which was not seen.)
Fails when: two or more messages have equal rank (two sources cancel the convergence); the content is long reading or a data table (converging fields leave no rectangle); nobody is speaking (a neutral tool has no sender); the key word is longer than about 8–10 letters or changes length in translation.
Signature (do not carry together): a megaphone wedge of type from a shouting photographed face · a masthead cut red over black · a flat poster of red, black, green and blue rays.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Product: a severe-weather alert screen in muted sage and slate, light mode, phone 390 × 844.
1. *Source.* The issuing station's marker at 33% of the width and 46% of the height.
2. *Sender.* The service's name runs across a 56px bar at the top and at the bottom, split in two tones between its two words (slate on sage, then sage on slate).
3. *Channel.* From the marker toward the right, opening 38°, 156px long (40% of the width). The word GALE fills it, its four letters growing from 28px to 84px. The same alert is repeated as horizontal body text below.
4. *Ground.* Four fields with edges converging on the marker: above the channel, the time window; below it, the affected area; at the open end, a two-line instruction panel; behind the marker, a circular map thumbnail.
5. *Colour.* Five flat fields: sage 28% (the largest), slate 20% (the channel and the instruction panel), off-white 25%, dusty blue 10% and clay 7% behind the marker. The word is a mustard, the most saturated colour present, inside the slate with a 2px off-white outline; contrast on slate above 4.5:1.
6. *Zone word.* COAST, built from 3 × 4 cells, runs across the boundary between the area field and the map.
- *Interaction.* Tapping the channel (hit height 84px) hinges it downward to three times its height in 220ms and shows the details; nothing opens over the page. Focus widens the channel by 4° and doubles its outline.
- *Fallback.* For THUNDERSTORM (12 letters) the screen switches to the orthogonal mode: a 5% slate bar on the left edge, lines stepped away from it, the key word in mustard on a slate strip.
Result: a quiet green-grey alert. No red, no photographed face, no megaphone. What survived: one source, a message that grows out of it, a ground that points back to it, and the loudest colour held inside the darkest.

## Space & composition
- **Diagonals, mostly 30–45°.** He tilted the photo frame, the poster's speech wedge, and the edges of the planes (the photographs were not obtained; in the Lengiz poster the wedge axis itself is horizontal and the diagonals sit in the ground around it). Not every work is diagonal: the Lef no. 4 cover (AIC) is fully orthogonal, with a black vertical bar (≈5% of the width) down the left edge, a staggered text block and huge split letters at the foot.
- **Angles from far above or below.** Layout can do the same: an oversized element seen "from below" (huge, cropped at the top) set against tiny UI "seen from above."
- **Zones by color.** In the club, wall tone plus a large word ("Lenin") marked each zone. The word is built from modular square cells, so it reads as construction, and it crosses the boundary between light and dark wall unchanged. Sections get a solid ground and a capitalized label, with no icons.
- **Dense and useful.** No empty space for luxury's sake. Any empty area is a flat field doing work, as in the triptych.

## Form, line & typography
Hand-drawn, condensed, heavy sans capitals, stacked, slanted, and sized to fill whatever bar or wedge holds them. On the Lef no. 4 cover the masthead letters are squared, monoline block capitals about 1/3 of the cover height, split horizontally red over black at 51% of their height; body lines are set in a bold grotesque, staggered (each line indented differently), with keywords in red. Rules are thick bars, not hairlines. Use circles only as targets or lenses, like the Leica lens or the eye in the Kino-Glaz poster.
Fonts: **Oswald** (condensed headlines), **Russo One** (squared display), **IBM Plex Mono** (labels and data, the "technical drawing" voice).

## Rhythm, motion & time
- Motion is mechanical. Things rotate on a pivot, slide on a track, or unfold along a hinge. They don't float or bounce. Use `cubic-bezier(.7,0,.3,1)` at 180–260 ms with no overshoot.
- Things change state rather than appear. A card opens into a form the way the club table doubles. A display flips through faces like the hexagonal drums (`rotateX` in 120° steps).
- Transitions are diagonal wipes along the composition's axis, like a megaphone blast.

## UI translations
1. **Layout:** rotate the hero container −12° to −18° and let it bleed off two edges. Keep body text horizontal underneath for contrast.
2. **Layout:** crop the main image to an extreme angle seen from below, with the subject cut at the top edge and taking up 70% of the viewport height.
3. **Type:** set the screen's one key word in condensed caps sized to fill a red wedge (`clip-path: polygon(0 40%,100% 0,100% 100%,0 60%)`) coming out of the image toward the CTA.
4. **Type:** stack nav labels vertically, rotated 90°, in a black bar 48px wide on the left.
5. **Color:** give each section a single flat ground (off-white, red, grey). Introduce yellow or blue only as one full-screen state, such as success or empty.
6. **Color:** keep black for one "opponent" element per screen, like the club's chess pieces: the destructive action or the active tab.
7. **Component:** make one base panel and derive the others by rotating it or nesting copies at 0.8× scale, with no new shapes.
8. **Interaction** *(unverified: the club's fold-out and rotating parts are known from texts; the period photo shows no moving state)*: cards expand in place along a hinge (`transform-origin: left`) to 2× or 3× width and reveal form fields. Don't use modals.
9. **Interaction** *(unverified, from texts on the club's rotating drums)*: let stats or features sit on a three-faced prism that turns 120° on click or swipe.
10. **Motion:** do page transitions as a 35° diagonal wipe in red, 220 ms, linear-in / hard-stop.
11. **Labels:** mark zones with large uppercase words ("LIBRARY," "ANNOUNCEMENTS") built from a modular cell grid (each letter 3 × 4 square cells, drawn as outlines) instead of icons; let the word cross section boundaries.
12. **Type:** split the masthead word horizontally at 50%: red top, black bottom, as on the Lef no. 4 cover.

## What this artist would reject
- Ornamental gradients, glassmorphism, and soft drop shadows: they decorate a surface without doing anything.
- Eye-level, centered stock photography: the "navel-height" view he worked against.
- Static furniture-like components with a single use, such as a card that can only link.
- Pastel or tinted brand palettes that dilute the primaries.
- Spring or bounce animations that are playful but not mechanical.
