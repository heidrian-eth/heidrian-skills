# Tadao Ando — Japanese Ma / Critical Regionalism

## Essence
Ando builds a dark, closed box out of one material, then cuts a single exact opening to let light, sky or weather in. You arrive by a turned, slightly inconvenient path, so the light reads as an event rather than a feature. He works by subtraction: one material, one module, one aperture, and a void you have to walk through.

## Key works examined
- **Row House in Sumiyoshi (Azuma House), 1976**: a 57 m² plot split into three equal bays (living, open courtyard, sleeping). You cross the open sky in rain to go to bed. The void sits in the middle of the plan, and the inconvenience is intended. Seen in Ando's 1998 print (AIC 239618, street elevation + courtyard perspective): a symmetric blank façade whose only opening is a centred door, 23% of the façade width and 39% of its height; formwork panels laid landscape (2:1), each with 3 tie holes across × 2 down. The three-bay plan itself was not in the image. *(visually checked: AIC 239618)*
- **Row House in Sumiyoshi: the building** *(visually checked: secondary sources, spoon-tamago.com (photos credited to Hiromitsu Morimoto) and archeyes.com (façade photo))* Seen: the street face is a blank concrete box between two-storey neighbours, its only opening a narrow dark door, with the 1:2 panel joints and tie holes visible as a faint grid. Inside, the middle of the house is an open courtyard the full width of the plot and two storeys high, closed by glazed doors at both ends; a concrete stair climbs one side wall and a solid bridge crosses overhead at the upper level, so the sky above the court is cut by one dark band. Floor slate-grey, walls raw concrete, light falling from above onto the floor. The three bays are confirmed by sequence (room, open court, room), though no measured plan was found.
- **Church of the Light, Ibaraki, 1989**: a 5.9 m cube cut by a wall at 15°, with a floor-to-ceiling cross slot behind the altar. It shows that darkness is the medium and light is the content. Seen: the cross runs edge to edge (full height and full width of the end wall), its slot ~5% of the wall width, the crossbar at ~42% of the wall height; the slot is ~6% of the end-wall area and, from the pews, ~half the frame is near-black. Outside, a freestanding wall passes close in front of the glazing (the 15° angle cannot be measured from the photos; unverified). *(visually checked: church's own site photos and AIC 211979 perspective print)*
- **Chichu Art Museum, Naoshima, 2004**: almost entirely underground. Triangular and square courts are cut into a hill and lit only by daylight, so the works change by hour. It teaches hiding the container and letting the cut-outs be the architecture. Seen: from the air only the triangular and square cuts show in the grass; inside, a dark corridor with one long wooden strip (~2% of the frame) on the floor, a linear skylight, and a triangular slit of light in black. *(visually checked: Benesse Art Site images)*
- **Modern Art Museum of Fort Worth, 2002**: form-tie holes "plainly visible" on vast walls. The grid shows up as surface texture rather than drawn lines. *(visually checked: the museum's own photograph, themodern.org. Here the concrete is mostly hidden: glass pavilions with dense, evenly spaced vertical mullions sit under very thin flat roof slabs that overhang well past the glass, each carried by Y-shaped concrete columns, and a reflecting pool fills the lower ≈50% of the frame, doubling every box. Tie holes are not visible at this scale, so that claim rests on the Church of the Light. Colours in the photo are golden-hour and colour-graded, so no palette change.)*
- **Ando's concrete method**: watertight timber shuttering in tatami-sized 3×6 ft (0.91×1.83 m) panels, so construction becomes a visible module.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Sumiyoshi): the plot is cut crosswise into three bands; the middle band is open to the sky and must be crossed; a single bridge spans it at the upper level, so even the void is divided once.
- **Colour relations** (Sumiyoshi): one material colour (concrete grey) on walls, floor and stair; the only other tones are the dark door/glass and warm interior light seen through it; colour is light, not pigment.
- **Line and edge:** the panel grid and tie holes are the only line work; edges of walls and stair treads are sharp, unrounded.
- **The procedure:** divide the container into three equal bands; empty the middle one and open it to light; make the user cross it; give the street side a blank face with one opening on the axis; let one bridge or slot be the only element that crosses the void.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Tadao Ando.** *Close the container in one surface treatment, empty its middle, and admit the opposite end of the value scale through a single exact cut that the user must cross the emptiness to reach.*
Process:
1. Fix one surface treatment in three values (lit, shaded, deep) and one module of 2:1. Every edge, opening and text block lands on a module line; the module shows only as faint seams and dots, which are texture and carry no information.
2. Close the arrival face: a blank, symmetric plane with one opening on its axis (≈1/4 of the width, ≈2/5 of the height) and nothing else.
3. Cut the container crosswise into three bands of about equal depth. Empty the middle one, light it, and route the path through it. At most one element may span the emptied band.
4. Make one extreme of value the medium: 25–50% of the key view sits at the deep value and the rest at the middle value, about 90% neutral in all.
5. Cut the opposite extreme once: a single aperture at the peak value, running edge to edge across its plane, 4–6% of the plane's width and 5–7% of the view. It marks the one destination. Nothing else, text included, takes the peak value.
6. Admit hue only inside an opening (one hue, seen through a cut), never on a surface, a control or text.
7. Mode for a transparent, repetitive screen: cap it with a lid 1–2% of the height and give the lower ≈50% to a still field that repeats the upper half at lower contrast.
Why it works:
- Luminance contrast after adaptation: in a view that is up to half deep value, a patch of 5–7% at the peak is the strongest signal available, so it needs no hue and no size. Multiply the cuts and each is a window; widen one and it is a lit wall with glare and no event.
- The emptied band is an interval between two states. Crossing it resets the eye, so arrival is felt against the stretch before. Fill the band and the result is three stacked sections.
- One treatment removes hue and texture as variables, so only value (light) and module (measure) can be read. A cut that runs edge to edge reads as a property of the wall and not as an object on it.
Colour relations: neutrals ≈90% in three values of one treatment; deep value 25–50% of key views; peak value 5–7%, in one aperture only; one hue, only inside an opening; one warm natural accent ≤2%. Text takes the end of the scale furthest from its plane, short of the peak (light neutral on the deep value, the deep value on the middle value), always ≥4.5:1; quietness comes from low chroma, small size and scarcity, never from low contrast.
Space and line: 2:1 module; three bands with the middle empty; one axial opening on a blank face; one aperture edge to edge; the only lines are module seams and cut edges, all sharp; at most one oblique element (its angle could not be measured). Control boundaries ≥3:1 or carried by a value step plus position.
Type requirement: small, low-chroma, widely spaced text locked to module lines, in a face that stays open at small sizes on a dark plane (regular weight, not thin, below about 18px); one large thin word allowed in the emptied band; text is never the brightest thing on the screen.
Motion and interaction: structure never moves, light does. Arrival starts at the deep value, holds briefly, then light enters from the aperture's side (0.9–1.4s, once per session, skippable, a cut under reduced motion). Focus is made by dimming the surround while the target keeps ≥3:1 and gains a visible focus mark. The crossing costs one scroll or one step and never hides the destination from keyboard or screen-reader order.
Fails when: the task is repeated or urgent (checkout, tools used many times a day, anything safety-critical), where the threshold is a tax; several actions have equal standing; the content is coloured imagery that must sit on surfaces; long reading text has to live on the deep value; poor screens or bright surroundings collapse three near neutrals into one.
Signature (do not carry together): fair-faced concrete with tie-hole dots · a cruciform slot of daylight · a blank street wall with one door.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Payroll approval tool, desktop 1440 × 900, light mode in warm paper tones with one plum hue. Value is inverted: the medium is the lightest value and the cut is the darkest.
1. *Treatment and module.* One matte paper tone in three values (L 96, 90, 82). Module 120 × 60px; seams at 3% opacity. All tables, fields and text baselines sit on module lines.
2. *Arrival face.* The start view is a blank L 90 plane with one centred opening, 360px wide (25%) × 360px high (40%), showing only the pay period and one line: "214 payslips ready for review".
3. *Three bands.* Review list (top band, 300px), an emptied band (300px, L 96, nothing in it but the running total in one thin 72px word), then the approval band (300px). One element spans the emptied band: a 2px vertical rule that carries the total down to the approval.
4. *Medium.* L 96 covers 46% of the approval view; L 90 and L 82 share the rest. Neutrals 91%.
5. *The cut.* One slot at L 12, the full width of the approval panel and 5% of its height (6% of the view). It is the approve action, 1440 × 45px, labelled in L 96 text (15:1). No other element is darker than L 25; body text is L 25 on L 96 (11:1).
6. *Hue.* Plum appears only inside the arrival opening and inside an "exceptions" window in the review band (a masked panel listing 3 flagged payslips, each with a text reason), never on a button.
7. *Motion.* On first load the page is flat L 96 for 250ms, then the slot's dark value enters from the left edge over 1s. Returning users on the same day skip it.
Result: a pale, dry finance screen with one dark bar and a deliberate pause before approval. No grey concrete, no darkness, no cross. Note the failure line: for a clerk approving 40 runs a day the pause would be removed.

## Space & composition
- **Module first.** Everything is built in a 1:2 panel (910×1820) with evenly spaced tie holes. On the Sumiyoshi façade the panels are laid landscape, each 3 holes across × 2 down, with the door edges on panel lines. Seams and holes form a faint grid that every opening and edge lands on.
- **Thirds with a hollow middle.** Sumiyoshi gives exactly one third of the plan to nothing. Use that as a layout law: a three-band layout whose center band is an empty, lit void the user has to traverse.
- **Orthogonal box + one diagonal intruder.** Plans are rectangular systems cut by a single angled or curved wall. The Church of the Light's 15° wall is the only non-orthogonal line, and it governs the entire entry.
- **Blank street face.** Sumiyoshi shows the street a windowless, symmetric wall with one door on the central axis (23% of the façade width, 39% of its height).
- **Low density, high mass.** Walls 38 cm thick read as weight, and content clusters where the light reaches.
- **Processional, not hub-and-spoke.** You slip between volumes and turn before you arrive.
- **Thin lid, doubled base.** At Fort Worth a slab edge only ≈1–2% of the frame tall caps each glass box, and the water below mirrors it, so a whole screen can be one thin header band over a calm reflective field.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

Color comes from material and light only. The palette is concrete under changing daylight plus one natural intrusion.
- `#898785` concrete in shade (primary surface; Church of the Light walls, 23%)
- `#C2C4CA` concrete in direct light (raised/lit surfaces; slightly cool)
- `#161511` interior darkness (the dominant ground in key moments; Chichu corridor 24%, Church pews view ~40% at `#09090B`)
- `#FDFDFD` the slot of daylight (used only at the aperture, never as a fill; photographs as blown-out white)
- `#867158` wood (Chichu's floor strip, ~2% of the frame: a quiet warm accent)
- `#82ADD3` framed sky (the one hue allowed, seen only between walls or through a cut; Church exterior)

Rule: grey and darkness carry ~90%, light ~5–7% of the frame (measured in the Church of the Light, where from the pews ~half the frame is near-black), and blue appears only *inside* an opening. Hexes are sampled from photographs, so they depend on light and exposure: the daylight slot photographs blown-out white.

## Form, line & typography
Ando's line is the precise cut: an untrimmed edge, a slot, a seam. Type should behave like that: small, restrained grotesk with generous tracking, placed on the module grid. Display type is rare, and when it appears it's thin and large. The angled wall maps to one rotated or obliquely aligned element per screen at most. Fonts: **Zen Kaku Gothic New** (Japanese-rooted, calm), **Inter Tight** (precise UI text), **Shippori Mincho** for a single quiet heading where serif warmth is wanted.

## Rhythm, motion & time
Time here is daylight moving slowly across a fixed wall, so motion should be slow, directional and ambient. Transitions follow a compression-release logic (dim, narrow, then open), like the dark corridor at Chichu ending in a line of light. A reveal holds briefly in shadow before the content brightens. Nothing bounces.

## UI translations
1. **Layout:** Build on a 2:1 module (e.g., 112×56 px cells laid landscape) and draw it faintly as tie-hole dots (3 per cell width, 2 per height, `radial-gradient` at 6% opacity) plus barely visible seams.
2. **Layout:** Split the key screen into three equal bands. Leave the middle band empty except for a lit gradient, and make users scroll or swipe *through* it to reach the second content block.
3. **Layout:** Allow exactly one element per screen to sit at 15° off-axis (a divider, card edge or clip-path). Everything else is orthogonal.
4. **Color:** Default to `#898785`/`#161511` surfaces. Reserve `#FDFDFD` for one aperture: a thin vertical or cruciform slot of light (`clip-path` cut-out over a dark panel) that marks the primary action. Run the slot edge to edge across its panel, ~5% of the panel width, crossbar a little above centre (~42%); light totals ~5–7% of the screen.
5. **Color:** Show the only saturated color (`#82ADD3`) inside a masked window, never on buttons or text.
6. **Type:** Keep UI text at 13–15 px with +0.04em tracking, grey on grey (contrast passing AA but low-chroma). Put one hero word at 64–96 px weight 200 on the void band.
7. **Texture:** Apply a subtle concrete noise (SVG `feTurbulence`, 3–4% opacity) to large fields.
8. **Motion:** Use 900–1400 ms ease-in-out for page reveals: the screen opens dark, then a light gradient sweeps across from one edge, as if sunlight is entering.
9. **Motion:** Tie a very slow background gradient angle to the local time of day (morning light from the left, evening from the right).
10. **Interaction (indirect approach):** The landing screen offers no immediate CTA. The user passes one quiet threshold (scroll past a blank wall, or a turn into a side panel) before the primary content appears.
11. **Interaction:** Hover states darken the surround instead of lightening the target, so the focus is carved out of shadow.

## What this artist would reject
- Equal-weight card grids with drop shadows and 12 px rounded corners.
- A hero that announces everything on arrival, with no threshold.
- Multi-hue palettes and gradient buttons. Color enters only through an opening.
- Decorative illustration in place of material texture.
- Snappy 150 ms springs and confetti.
- Plastered-over structure that hides the grid instead of showing it.
