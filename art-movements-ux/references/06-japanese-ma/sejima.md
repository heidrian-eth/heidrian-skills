# Kazuyo Sejima / SANAA — Japanese contemporary architecture (Ma, lightness)

## Essence
Sejima builds a field, not a hierarchy. Rooms of equal standing float inside one thin, continuous outline. Structure is pared down until it nearly disappears, and glass, white and mirror let the surroundings and the people inside become the content. Nothing is the "front" and nothing is the "hero". Ma here is the gap between equal things, and that gap is used for moving through and seeing across.

## Key works examined
- **21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art, Kanazawa (2004)**: a glass circle 112.5 m across with several entrances. Box-shaped galleries 4–12 m tall are scattered inside, and corridors double as gallery space. Lesson: a container with no front, and content that is placed rather than ranked. Seen: a continuous curved glass wall whose roof edge reads as a single thin bright line (~1% of the image height), a plain white box rising above it, and the glass mirroring trees and sky so the wall nearly disappears; inside, whites carry a cool green-grey cast from the glass. The scattered galleries and corridor-galleries were not visible in the images obtained. *(visually checked: kanazawa21.jp images)*
- **Glass Pavilion, Toledo Museum of Art (2006)**: a field of curved glass rooms inside a double-glazed skin, with an air cavity between. Few corners, no mullions. Lesson: borders shown as layered transparency, not as lines. *(visually checked: secondary source, archdaily.com, interior photos and plan/detail drawings credited to SANAA)* Seen in the plan: about 20 rooms, each a rounded rectangle or near-circle of a different size and corner radius, packed inside one rounded-square envelope; every room is separated from its neighbours by a narrow cavity of nearly constant width, and every wall is drawn at the same line weight, so no room outranks another. The straight sides all run parallel to the envelope. In the photos the curved glass walls show no mullions; borders read as stacked reflections and a thin white roof edge; the floor is pale grey, ceilings white.
- **Serpentine Pavilion, London (2009)**: a 26 mm roof of birch panels clad on both sides in mirrored aluminium, set on 50 mm columns in a random mesh. Lesson: the thinnest possible plane, whose surface is a reflection of its context. Seen: a free-form, lobed canopy outline (not a circle) with a few holes; columns and canopy edge each only ~0.15–0.35% of the frame width, i.e. true hairlines; the mirror reads as dark grey-green (`#5A5A53`) because it reflects lawn and trees, not as bright silver. *(visually checked: serpentinegalleries.org photos)*
- **Rolex Learning Center, EPFL Lausanne (2010)**: one undulating floor of about 20,000 m² with no interior walls, pierced by roughly a dozen patios. Slopes separate uses. Lesson: zoning by topography, not by partitions. Seen: the slab lifts off the ground in long soft curves; a patio is an elliptical cut through the floor, framing the glazed level above. *(visually checked: epfl.ch visitor images)*
- **New Museum, New York (2007)**: offset stacked boxes. Lesson: misalignment as the only emphasis. *(visually checked: secondary source, archdaily.com AD Classics: New Museum)* Seen: six boxes of different heights stacked and shifted sideways against each other by roughly 5–10% of the width, all in the same silver-white expanded-metal skin with no windows on the main faces; light enters only at the offsets (skylight slots where a box is set back). Against the red-brick, window-dense street, the uniform pale stack reads by silhouette alone.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Glass Pavilion plan): a packing of rounded rooms of unequal size inside one envelope; constant gaps between them; no room outranks another. The gaps are the double-glazed walls' air cavities, not circulation.
- **Space** (New Museum): one footprint repeated 6 times in height, each copy displaced a little; the displacement is the only expressive act.
- **Colour relations:** colour comes from outside (trees, sky, street) reflected or seen through; the building's own palette is white, pale grey and metal, all within a few L steps; no accent.
- **Line and edge:** one line weight for every wall (plan); edges in reality are glass reflections or a thin bright roof edge.
- **The procedure:** choose one shape family (rounded rectangles or stacked boxes); vary only size and position, never weight or colour; keep the gaps constant; let the only emphasis come from a shift or from what shows through.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Kazuyo Sejima (SANAA).** *Remove rank: pack unequal rooms into one soft envelope at a single line weight and a constant gap, and let the only emphasis be a small shift of position or what shows through.*
Process:
1. Draw one envelope: a single continuous outline with soft corners and no front. Give it 3–4 entry points on different sides, all of equal standing.
2. List the contents as peers: 6–20 units from one shape family. Let size follow content (areas may differ by 10× or more), but give every unit the same treatment. Circulation spaces are units of the same family, not leftover corridors.
3. Pack the units with one constant gap, their straight sides parallel to the envelope. No unit is centred or isolated as a hero.
4. Fix one boundary weight for every unit and the envelope, as thin as the medium allows (0.15–0.35% of the frame width). Thinness is a matter of width, not of faintness: a boundary that marks a region keeps ≥3:1 against both neighbours.
5. Keep the structure achromatic within a narrow band of value (surfaces about 5 L apart, which groups but does not bound). Let colour enter only from the content or the context seen through or reflected: a low-chroma tint over up to 1/3 of a view.
6. Emphasise by displacement only: shift one unit 5–10% of its width out of line with its neighbours, or open the single gap where two units are offset. Never by size, weight or colour.
7. Mode for one large space: remove the partitions and zone a continuous surface by gradual change (slope, tone), with a few cut-out openings.
Why it works:
- With one treatment there is no pre-attentive rank, so the eye scans instead of being led and the user chooses the route. Add a hero and every other unit becomes secondary.
- A constant gap groups by uniform proximity: the units read as one field and the gap as a continuous figure. Vary the gap and clusters appear, which is hierarchy by another name.
- Against uniformity a small deviation is highly salient, so a 5–10% shift does the work a larger or bolder element would do. With structure thin and colourless, the content and the people in it become the figure.
Colour relations: structure in near-equal neutrals about 5 L apart (≈1.1:1, grouping only); one boundary value at ≥3:1 on those surfaces; no accent of its own; borrowed colour at low chroma over up to 1/3 of a view; a reflective plane reads darker than its surroundings, because it shows the ground. Primary and secondary text both ≥4.5:1.
Space and line: one envelope; 6–20 units; one gap; one boundary weight of 0.15–0.35% of the frame width; soft outer corners, with the units' own corners soft or square but of one family; one displacement of 5–10%; no shadows and no elevation.
Type requirement: one family in one or two neighbouring weights from light to regular; headings ≤1.25× the body; a small label centred in each unit or carried in the gap; hierarchy by position alone. Light weights only at sizes where they hold (regular below about 16px).
Motion and interaction: moving is crossing one field: sections pan, they are not replaced; a change dissolves in 400–700ms (a cut under reduced motion). Hover lets what lies beneath show through and also darkens the unit's boundary; keyboard focus adds a 2px mark at ≥3:1, the one permitted exception to the single weight.
Fails when: there is one action that must be found (checkout, sign-up, an emergency control), since equal rank hides it; importance really is unequal; a narrow screen turns the packing into a list, so envelope and gap vanish; more than about 20 units leave users without bearings; a guided, ordered flow is needed.
Signature (do not carry together): white surfaces and clear curved glass · a circular or lobed envelope with a thin bright edge · a mirrored canopy on needle-thin columns.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Smart-home control panel, wall tablet 1180 × 820, dark mode in deep navy. Dark, dense and operational; nothing shared with white glass pavilions.
1. *Envelope.* One outline with a 36px radius, inset 20px from the screen. Four entry points on its four sides (scenes, energy, people, settings), same size, none marked as primary.
2. *Peers.* 11 units from one family (rectangles with 14px corners): eight rooms, plus hall, stairs and garden as units like the others. Areas from 60 × 90px (store) to 330 × 250px (living room), a ratio of 15:1, all with the same fill and line.
3. *Packing.* One 12px gap everywhere, sides parallel to the envelope. Room names are 12px labels centred in each unit.
4. *Boundary.* One 2px line (0.17% of the width) at L 62 on surfaces of L 12, 4.6:1. Nothing is thicker; there are no shadows.
5. *Colour.* Surfaces L 12, 15 and 18, all neutral navy-grey. The only colour is content seen through a unit: the actual colour and level of each room's lights as a tint at 25% strength, and camera stills at 40% saturation. Every lit room also shows "on · 60%" in text at 7:1, so state is not carried by tint alone.
6. *Emphasis.* The room that needs attention (a door left open) is shifted 20px right, 7% of its width, out of line with its neighbours, and its label reads "Door open". No red, no badge, no enlargement.
7. *Motion.* Opening a room pans and zooms the field toward it over 500ms; the others stay visible at the edges. Touch targets are the whole unit (smallest 60 × 90px).
Result: a dark plan of equal rooms tinted by their own lamps, with one room slightly out of line. No white, no glass, no curve beyond the corners.

## Space & composition
- **Equivalence of spaces.** The Pritzker jury praised a non-hierarchical "equivalence of spaces". Kanazawa's galleries differ in size and light but have equal status. In UI, cards differ in proportion (1:1, 2:3, 3:1), not in visual weight.
- **Round or soft outer boundary, orthogonal inside.** A loose, rounded container (large radius, or an ellipse that is almost a circle) holds crisp rectangles. Use 28–40 px radius on the outer frame and 0–2 px on inner tiles.
- **Scatter with even gaps.** The boxes are not on a strict grid, but the space between them stays roughly even. Use a masonry or offset layout with a fixed gutter (24–32 px).
- **Gaps as circulation.** Kanazawa's corridors are galleries too, so gutters carry captions and metadata.
- **One continuous floor.** Rolex splits programs with slopes. Separate sections with tonal steps and vertical offsets, never dividers.

## Form, line & typography
The line language is very thin and very even: 50 mm columns under a vast roof, and slabs that read as drawings. Use a light grotesk with no display weights. Headings stay barely 1.25× body, in sentence case, and labels get +0.04em tracking.
- **Inter** (300/400) for UI text.
- **Zen Kaku Gothic New** (300) for labels and bilingual text.
- **IBM Plex Mono** (300) for tiny captions, like dimension notes on a plan.
Use 1 px rules only, `#44463F` at 40%, and 1.25 px icon strokes. (Serpentine's columns and canopy edge are ~0.15–0.35% of the frame width: 1–3 px on a 1000 px frame.)

## Rhythm, motion & time
Motion should feel like walking a gentle slope, or light shifting on mirror. Nothing snaps or bounces.
- Transitions run 400–700 ms with `cubic-bezier(0.25, 0.1, 0.25, 1)`: opacity plus at most 12 px of travel.
- Panels arrive translucent (`backdrop-filter: blur(12px)`, 70% white) before they turn opaque.
- A slow specular gradient on selected surfaces follows the pointer, the way the Serpentine canopy takes on the weather.

## UI translations
1. **Layout:** Put the main view in one large rounded container. Inside it, float 6–20 rectangles of different proportions at equal visual weight, with no hero card.
2. **Layout:** Give the screen several equal entry points. Don't use a single primary CTA; offer 3–4 calm, peer-level actions placed around the field.
3. **Layout:** Emphasise only by displacement: shift at most one item 5–10% of its width out of line with its neighbours, as the New Museum boxes shift. Keep all other items aligned.
4. **Color:** Use only value steps of near-white. Separate surfaces with a 3–5% lightness difference plus a 1 px `#CBD3D2` hairline, never with shadows.
5. **Color:** Let any colour on screen come from user content (photos, avatars) shown through translucent panels. Chrome stays colourless.
6. **Type:** Cap the heading scale at 1.25× body in weight 300–400. Mark hierarchy with position and spacing only.
7. **Structure:** Make every structural element as thin as it can be: 1 px borders, 2 px progress bars, 0.5 px separators on retina. Toolbars float with no visible background slab.
8. **Motion:** Animate state changes as opacity and blur dissolves (500 ms) rather than slides or scale pops.
9. **Interaction:** On hover, raise transparency instead of elevation. The hovered card turns 85% translucent and you see faintly through it to the layer below.
10. **Interaction:** Replace tabs with a continuous canvas. Moving between sections is a smooth pan across one field, like walking the Rolex floor, and the zoom level hints at the "slope".
11. **Texture:** Add a reflective treatment to one surface only, the "canopy": it takes on a darkened, blurred copy of the content beneath it (like the Serpentine roof mirroring the lawn), shifting with scroll.

## What this artist would reject
- A hero banner with one huge headline and one big button. That is a front door, and her buildings have none.
- Drop-shadow elevation stacks (Material z-levels), which add hierarchy and mass instead of transparency.
- Heavy dividers, thick nav bars and tab strips that act as walls between programs.
- Saturated brand-colour chrome; colour should be borrowed from context.
- Bold display type at 3–5× body size.
