# Ikko Tanaka — Japanese graphic design (Ma meets modernism)

## Essence
Tanaka took the flat, opaque color fields and stylized natural motifs of Rinpa and Edo woodblock prints and put them on a Bauhaus/Swiss construction grid. The result is images built from the fewest possible geometric planes that still carry a full cultural identity. Simplicity, as Cooper Hewitt puts it, means "maximizing a visual message with minimum components." That is different from stripping things out. The poster is assembled like a tangram, and the empty field is an active plane rather than background.

## Key works examined
- **Nihon Buyo poster (1981)**, screenprint and rotogravure, 75.5 x 49.2 cm, for UCLA's Asian Performing Arts Institute. A geisha made from grid-snapped planes: a pale face block, black hair masses, red eyes and lips, and a pink cheek blush. It teaches that identity survives extreme reduction as long as the *signature* features stay intact. Seen: the sheet is an exact 3-column × 4-row grid of near-square cells (235 × 248 px on the 725 × 1024 scan) filled edge to edge; ~15 primitives; the text sits in the top-left cell. *(visually checked: Cooper Hewitt 18645877 scan and V&A O1161237 photo)*
- **The 8th Sankei Kanze Noh (1961)**, screenprint, 103 x 73 cm. Multicolored kanji on a black ground. Type is the image, and a dark field makes saturated color vibrate. Seen: black covers ~77%; vertical columns of Mincho kanji, each character in one of ~6 muted hues or white, with thin white vertical rules dividing the programme blocks. *(visually checked: Cooper Hewitt 18645853)*
- **MUJI identity (1980 onward)**, with Kazuko Koike and Takashi Sugimoto. Kraft and recycled paper, a maroon four-kanji logo, one illustration plus one witty headline ("low price for a reason"). The brand is defined by what it leaves out. *(one late MUJI poster visually checked: secondary source, peoplesgdarchive.org, 'Muji Advertisement', 2000, credited to Tanaka; the 1980 launch material itself was not found)* Seen: an off-white sheet with the maroon-red four-kanji logo top left as a small solid block, a short paragraph of copy under it, and the headline set as one vertical line of serif kanji down the right edge ('20 years ago 40 products; now over 4000'). The image is made of text: the names of all the products, in tiny maroon type, are massed into the outline of one giant kanji 良 ('good') filling ≈70% of the sheet. One ink besides black: maroon; no illustration, no photo.
- **Seibu target pattern (1975 onward)**, concentric green and blue. One geometric motif carries a whole environment. *(visually checked: secondary source, peoplesgdarchive.org, Seibu department store paper bag and wrapping paper)* Seen: a regular grid of target discs (≈7 across × 5 down on the sheet), each an outer ring plus a central disc about half its diameter, the disc ≈70% of the grid pitch, on white (≈60% of the surface). Only two hues, green (≈18%) and blue (≈18% in two depths): each target pairs one with the other, and the ring/core assignment flips from disc to disc in an irregular order, with light and dark shades of each. Tiny SEIBU wordmarks sit at 45° in some of the gaps. No red.
- **Kōchō typeface**: a Bodoni-inspired Mincho with strong thick-thin contrast, bringing Didone drama to Japanese text. *(not obtained; the same Didone instinct is visible in the Hanae Mori wordmark below)*
- **Kimono, Textilmuseum Max Berk (1991)**: a flat black kimono silhouette (~56% of the sheet) on white, the word "Ki-mo-no" stacked in five flat colours inside it, and the details run as one diagonal white line of text. *(added work, visually checked: Cooper Hewitt 18645865)*
- **Hanae Mori poster**: a symmetric black butterfly of two big triangular wings on white, small coloured rings, bars and triangles as accents, a Didone wordmark between two hairlines that form the collar. *(added work, visually checked: Cooper Hewitt 18645879)*

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Seibu pattern): one motif on a strict lattice, evenly spaced, white gaps ≈ the motif's radius; the brand name is a tiny diagonal note in the gaps, never centred.
- **Colour relations** (Seibu): two hues, each in two depths; every unit contains both hues (ring vs core), and the swap between units is the only variation; ground white ≈60%.
- **The procedure** (Seibu): choose one two-part motif; tile it on a lattice; give its two parts two hues; vary only which hue takes which part (and its depth) from unit to unit; tuck the name small and rotated into the gaps.
- **Space** (MUJI 2000 poster): one huge figure made of small text, a small logo block in one corner, one vertical headline along the opposite edge; ≈30% empty off-white.
- **Colour relations** (MUJI 2000): one brand ink (maroon) for both the logo and the figure, black for the headline; no other colour.
- **The procedure** (MUJI 2000): make the image out of the content itself (a list shaped into a sign); keep the logo small and in a corner; let one line of text explain why.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Ikko Tanaka.** *Assemble the image from a few flat planes snapped to a coarse grid and held by one dark mass, so that every edge is two colours meeting and the eye finishes the figure.*
Process:
1. Lay a coarse grid of near-square cells over the whole frame, 3–4 cells a side, with no margin: the composition runs to every edge.
2. Reduce the subject to the 3–5 features that identify it and build it from 10–18 primitives (rectangle, circle, half-cell triangle), each snapped to a cell or half-cell.
3. Size the primitives unequally: 3–4 large planes carry about 55% of the area and the rest are small.
4. Anchor with the darkest value as the largest connected mass (34–77% of the frame in the measured works), then one flat ground plane (20–30%), then one light plane. The ground is a plane with a shape, not what is left over.
5. Spend hue in small flat pieces: 4–6 hues, none above ≈8% and 8–30% together, pale and saturated ones set directly against the dark mass. The strongest hue marks the most important feature and may fill one whole cell. No outlines: every piece is bounded by a neighbour of a different value.
6. Give the text one cell (a small flush block in a top corner), or make the text the image, each glyph a plane. Never give it a band that competes with the figure.
7. Make the whole nearly symmetric, then break it once: one piece offset by half a cell, or one diagonal line.
Why it works:
- Closure: with only the identifying features kept and locked to a grid, the viewer completes the figure, and the completing makes it memorable. Add detail and it is an illustration; drop an identifying feature and it is a pattern.
- Simultaneous contrast against a dark anchor: small patches beside a large dark mass look brighter and more saturated than they are, so 8% of hue is enough, and the mass holds scattered pieces as one body. Equalise the areas and the result is a quilt with no figure.
- With no outline there is no line weight to read, so every boundary is a decision about two planes, and figure and ground can trade places. Outlines would turn the planes into icons on a background.
Colour relations: dark anchor 34–77% : ground 20–30% : light plane ≈15% : 4–6 hues 8–30% in all, each ≤8%; all fills flat (one soft blush seen once); neighbours that mark a functional boundary differ by ≥3:1 in luminance, and two mid-value hues never touch where the edge has to be read; text sits on the anchor or the ground, not on a hue cell, at ≥4.5:1.
Space and line: grid of 3–4 near-square cells a side; full bleed; 10–18 primitives with 3–4 dominant; hairlines only as structure between columns of text; near-symmetry with one deviation. Modes: one two-part motif tiled on a lattice, with ground ≈60% and two hues that swap parts from unit to unit; or an image massed from the content itself in a single brand colour (one late poster, secondary source).
Type requirement: two voices far apart in scale (about 4–5:1): a display face with strong thick-and-thin contrast whose heavy strokes can act as planes and be recoloured one at a time, and a small plain sans block sized to one cell and set flush.
Motion and interaction: the image assembles: planes land one at a time as hard-edged cuts (60–90ms apart, 180–240ms each, under 1s in all, all at once under reduced motion), with no fade and no blur. A state change swaps a plane's fill and also changes its shape or adds a mark, so state is never carried by colour alone; nothing lifts or scales.
Fails when: the content is photographic or continuous (maps, gradient charts); there is long reading text, which has only one cell to live in; the product needs many semantic status colours, which collide with hues already spent on the figure; the subject has no identifying features (abstract services), which yields generic geometric clip-art.
Signature (do not carry together): a face built from squares and circles · black with pale and vermilion chips · many-coloured brush-serif characters on black.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Warehouse pick-status wall display, 1920 × 1080, steel grey and safety amber, read from 10 metres. Utility, landscape, live data; nothing shared with a theatre poster.
1. *Grid.* 4 × 3 cells of 480 × 360px, no margin, no gutters.
2. *Subject.* The building, reduced to four features: the two aisle blocks, the cold room, the dock and the packing line. 14 primitives in all: rectangles for aisles, one circle for the dock turntable, half-cell triangles for the two ramps.
3. *Unequal parts.* Three large planes (aisle block A, aisle block B, the yard) carry 56% of the area; 11 small pieces share the rest.
4. *Anchor.* The yard and unstaffed zones form one connected dark steel mass (L 14), 44% of the screen. Ground: mid steel (L 46), 26%, shaped as the walkways. Light plane: the packing line at L 90, 14%.
5. *Hue.* Five flat hues, 16% in all: amber for the zone that is behind target, filling one whole cell (8.3%), plus four small zone colours of about 2% each. Every hue touches only the dark mass or the light plane, at ≥3:1. No outlines anywhere.
6. *Text.* All figures live in the top-left cell: shift, picks per hour, orders open, in a plain sans block set flush left, light on dark at 12:1. Zone letters are cut out of their planes as shapes.
7. *Deviation.* The layout mirrors about the centre aisle except the dock, which sits half a cell to the right.
- *State.* A zone that falls behind swaps to amber and its rectangle gains a half-cell triangle notch, so the change reads without colour. Planes update as hard cuts, 80ms apart.
Result: a flat, dark steel plan with a few colour patches and one block of numbers. No face, no black-and-pastel poster, no brush lettering.

## Space & composition
- **Construction grid first, figure second.** In the Nihon Buyo poster the face is not drawn. It is *tiled*: squares, rectangles, circles and half-cell triangles snapped to a coarse grid of 3 columns × 4 rows of near-square cells (sheet ratio 0.71). The cells are big; the detail comes from circles and diagonals inside them.
- **Figure fills the sheet; text takes one cell or becomes the figure.** Nihon Buyo's figure runs edge to edge and the type is a small left-aligned grotesk block filling the top-left cell. In Kanze Noh the type *is* the whole image; in Kimono it runs as one diagonal line across the figure; in Hanae Mori the wordmark sits top centre. No checked poster uses a text band at the foot.
- **Void as a plane.** Ground is one unmodulated color field, never "white space". The edges of the figure are defined by where planes stop (negative space expressing form), with no outlines. Hairlines do appear, but only as structure: column rules in Kanze Noh, the collar in Hanae Mori.
- **Low part-count, few big parts.** Nihon Buyo has ~15 primitives, but 3–4 big black and grey planes carry ~55% of the area; the rest are small accents. Count your components and keep most of them small.
- **Symmetry, then one deviation.** The face is near-frontal and symmetric, and a single offset keeps it alive.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

Opaque, flat, screenprint-pure. One exception was seen: Nihon Buyo's pink cheek is a soft vertical blush (gradient) under the eyes. Otherwise no tints within a shape. Pastels (pink, ivory, cyan) sit directly against black and saturated red, and that contrast does the work. In the four measured posters (Nihon Buyo, Kanze Noh, Kimono, Hanae Mori) black is the anchor, not an accent; this does not hold everywhere: the Seibu pattern and the MUJI 2000 poster have no dark anchor (white or off-white ground ≈60–70%). MUJI is the same system turned down to near-zero: an off-white sheet plus one maroon.

## Form, line & typography
Form is made of geometric primitives: square, circle, half-circle, quarter-circle, bar. Line exists only as the edge between two fields. Kanji strokes are treated as rectangles and dots that can be recolored one stroke at a time. For type, pair a high-contrast serif Mincho (the Kōchō instinct) with a quiet, rational sans set small.
- **Shippori Mincho** or **Zen Old Mincho** (Google Fonts) for display, giving Kōchō-like thick-thin contrast
- **Noto Sans JP** for tiny, grid-locked captions and metadata
- **Jost** as a Latin geometric companion with Bauhaus genes

## Rhythm, motion & time
Motion should feel like a screen-printing pass: each color plane lands separately, as a hard-edged cut with no blur. Stagger the arrival of planes by ~60–90 ms with a short `steps()` or crisp `cubic-bezier(.2,0,0,1)` over 180–240 ms, so the figure *assembles* rather than fades in. Interaction recolors a plane rather than moving it: stillness with one decisive event.

## UI translations
1. Build hero illustrations and empty states from 10–18 CSS primitives (`border-radius: 50%`, half-cell triangles via `clip-path`, rectangles) snapped to a coarse 3 × 4 grid of near-square cells, with 3–4 big planes carrying most of the area. No SVG detail, no outlines.
2. Give the screen one flat `background` field per zone. Separate zones by a hard color change with no border, shadow or divider.
3. Let the emblem fill the whole hero edge to edge and give the title, meta and CTA exactly one grid cell (top-left, left-aligned, 12–16px grotesk). Or make the type itself the image.
4. Use vermilion `#C1531E` for the "lips" of the layout (the primary action or live status dot) and at most one full cell: ~8% of the area in total.
5. Set display headings in a high-contrast Mincho at 48–72px against a 12px sans caption. The 5:1 scale jump stands in for decoration.
6. Recolor individual strokes or letters of a short CJK or Latin wordmark in separate flat colors on a black field for splash and section headers (the Kanze Noh move).
7. On load, animate planes in one at a time with hard edges (`clip-path: inset()` reveals, 70 ms stagger). Never use opacity fades or blur.
8. Make hover and selection swap a plane's fill (ivory to pink, ground to blue). Don't scale, lift or shadow anything.
9. For brand or commerce screens, use the MUJI register (one 2000 poster seen in a secondary source: maroon logo block in a corner, maroon as the only colour, the image made of text): warm-grey ground `#CFCCB9`, one maroon mark, one plain line illustration, one sentence of copy that explains *why*.
10. Make the layout near-symmetric, then offset exactly one element by half a grid unit.

## What this artist would reject
- Gradients, glassmorphism, and soft drop shadows that blur plane edges
- Outlined icons and 1px divider lines. Edges should come from color meeting color.
- Big headlines fighting a big hero image, or stock photos standing in for a constructed emblem
- Accent color sprinkled on every button, tag and link
- Busy "brand patterns" that don't come from a single geometric idea

## Signature prompt seed
Design the screen as a screenprinted poster: a coarse 3 × 4 grid of near-square cells filled edge to edge with flat warm-grey, black and ivory planes, plus circles and half-cell triangles in a few saturated hues (violet, cyan, green, blue), with black as the anchor (~a third of the area). Edges come only from colors meeting, with no outlines or shadows. Vermilion marks the primary action and may fill one cell. Put the title in one small left-aligned grotesk block in the top-left cell, with a high-contrast Mincho or Didone for display. Planes snap in one by one with hard clip reveals, and hover recolors instead of moving.
