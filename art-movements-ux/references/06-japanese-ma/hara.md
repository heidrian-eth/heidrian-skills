# Kenya Hara — Japanese Ma / contemporary design

## Essence
Hara designs empty containers. Emptiness (*kū*) is not minimalism but a vessel awaiting the viewer's meaning: one question, many answers. White is a way of perceiving, not a color, and the senses (touch above all) complete the work.

## Key works examined
- **MUJI "Horizon," 2003.** Bolivia's Salar de Uyuni (photo: Tamotsu Fujii) and Mongolia. Earth, sky, a tiny figure, a small logo, and no product or slogan. Teaches that presence can replace message. Seen (Uyuni poster as reproduced in *Designing Design*, pp. 228–229): a full-bleed 3.1:1 photograph, horizon at 50% of the height, one walking figure ~5% of the height at ~82% across, and the white bold MUJI wordmark standing *on* the horizon just right of the figure (cap height ~7% of the image height). Hara's own text on the facing pages is headed "Placing the Logo on the Horizon". *(visually checked: Lars Müller Publishers page images)* *(Retry pass 2, another version visually checked: secondary source, peoplesgdarchive.org, 'MUJI Advertisement: Horizon')* Seen: the same 2.8:1 Uyuni photograph with the horizon at exactly 50% of the height and the walking figure at 79% across, ≈6% of the height, standing below the line on the salt; here the logo is the vertical Japanese 無印良品 in small white characters set in the sky above the figure (≈64% across, ≈27–42% down), not the Latin wordmark on the line. So the logo position varies by version, but it always stays small, white and in the right third near the figure.
- **MUJI "Tea House," 2005.** Two sepia photographs (Togudo's Dojinsai study and the Ginsadan sand garden at Jisho-ji), ~75% of the area dark, with the white MUJI wordmark low on the right. *(added work, visually checked: *Designing Design* pp. 272–273, Lars Müller page image)*
- **RE-DESIGN, 2000.** Shigeru Ban's square toilet roll (it resists free spinning, so it saves paper) and Kaoru Mende's twig matches. Teaches you to redesign the familiar instead of inventing. Seen: a band of five white-on-white object photographs in warm whites (`#F3F1ED` 48%, `#E8E2DC` 29%), the title in wide-tracked serif caps beside a hairline vertical rule and a two-line caption. *(visually checked: *Designing Design* pp. 22–23)*
- **HAPTIC, Takeo Paper Show 2004.** 22 creators designed for touch with no sketching first: logos in animal hair and cultured mold, Fukasawa's fruit-skin juice packs. Teaches that texture carries information. Seen: the hair "H" photographed with a very shallow depth of field on neutral grey-white (`#E7E7E9`); the mould logo in faint ochres. *(visually checked: AGI portfolio image and *Designing Design* pp. 68–69)*
- **SENSEWARE / Tokyo Fiber (Holon 2010).** New synthetic fibers given to architects, fashion designers and an automaker. Teaches that the material is the interface. *(not obtained; unverified)*
- ***Designing Design*, 2007.** Exformation (making the known unknown), MUJI as "nothing, yet everything." Seen: justified serif body in two columns of ~74 characters, chapter titles in wide-tracked serif caps about 3× the body size, folios bottom-left, a letterspaced running head centred at the foot, and a burgundy red-circle = bowl diagram for "emptiness" (`#970A2C`). *(visually checked: 19 Lars Müller page images)*
- ***White*, 2009/2010 (Lars Müller).** 白 is a radical inside the character for emptiness, and the red circle holds many meanings because it holds nothing. Seen (Japanese edition cover, *Shiro*): a large Mincho 白 sliced by the right trim edge, about 3–4× the size of the small vertical author name, and italic lowercase "shiro / hara kenya" set small; ~90% of the cover is blank. *(visually checked: AGI portfolio image)*
- ***Ex-formation* (Lars Müller)**: one light serif title in wide tracking, ~4× the body size, under a single line drawing; all text flush left on one margin. *(added work, visually checked: AGI portfolio image)*
- **Nagano 1998 Olympic ceremony programmes.** *(visually checked: secondary source, Opening Ceremony programme cover, peoplesgdarchive.org, image credited to ndc.co.jp)* Seen: an upright cover (≈0.82:1) of white textured paper. The only ink is one red flame (≈11% of the cover) standing on the vertical centre axis in the upper half (top at 3%, base at 57%, centroid 37% down). Everything else is blind-debossed tone on tone in a pale warm grey (≈3%): the Olympic rings directly under the flame, the Nagano emblem below them, and two small blocks of wide-tracked serif caps (French, English, Japanese) set flush left in the upper-left and lower-left corners. So the hierarchy is ink versus relief: one saturated figure, and everything informational pressed into the paper rather than printed. NDC's text says the paper was developed to evoke snow and ice.
- **MUJI naming.** An "oak table," not a "coffee table". The function is left open.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Nagano opening programme): one figure on the central axis, upper half; the information is pushed to two small corner blocks on the left edge, top and bottom, leaving ≈85% of the surface bare paper. The figure and the debossed emblems form one vertical column; the text blocks sit outside it.
- **Colour relations** (Nagano): one saturated hue only (red, ≈11%), a white ground, and a second 'colour' that is not ink but relief (debossed paper, ≈3%), barely darker than the ground. The red never touches the text or the relief elements; there is a clear gap between flame and rings.
- **Space** (Horizon, both versions): horizon at 50%; one figure ≈6% high at ≈80% across; the logo small and white near the figure, either on the line (Latin) or in the sky (vertical Japanese).
- **Colour relations** (Horizon 2003): the field is the colour (saturated cyan sky ≈38%) and the figure and logo are tiny, so the most saturated area is the largest here, the reverse of Nagano.
- **Line and edge:** no rules; the debossed elements have soft shadow edges from the relief, the flame a hard edge.
- **The procedure:** choose one figure and give it the only saturated ink, centred in the upper half at ≈10% of the area; render all secondary information as near-invisible tone-on-tone relief; push the text to the corners in small tracked caps; leave the rest as material.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Kenya Hara.** *Leave the surface nearly bare, spend its only saturation on one figure, and press every piece of information back to the edges, so the viewer supplies the meaning.*
Process:
1. Write the surface's single proposition and choose one figure to hold it. A second proposition needs a second surface.
2. Choose the ground and leave 60–90% of it bare (≈60% where pictures appear, 85–90% on covers and text pages). Build it from 2–3 values of one temperature a few percent apart, or from one continuous field divided once at 50% of its height.
3. Place the figure alone. On a bare ground: on the vertical axis, in the upper half (centre of mass ≈37% down), ≈10% of the area. On a divided field: small (5–6% of the height), at ≈80% across, beside the dividing line. Keep a clear gap between it and everything else.
4. Give the figure the only saturated colour on the surface. If the field itself carries the colour, reverse it: the figure is then the only dark, hard-edged point. Either way the difference in kind is used once.
5. Press the information back into a second channel with no chroma and no weight: one weight throughout, small, widely spaced, flush on one margin, in blocks at the corners. The identity mark stays small, near the figure or at the periphery, never in a centred bar. The colour of the figure never touches the text.
6. Allow one display element per surface at 3–5× the body size, in the same weight; everything else stays ≤1.5×. Reading text runs in a measure of 65–75 characters.
Why it works:
- Salience by oddness, used once: the single saturated thing in an achromatic field is found at once at 10% of the area. A second coloured thing makes a palette, and the viewer starts comparing instead of looking.
- Completion by the viewer: where little is specified, the viewer projects, and a bare even field acts as a scale reference that makes a small figure read as a presence in a large space. Fill the field and the figure is one item in a layout.
- Two channels for two acts: the figure is seen by chroma, the information is found by position. They do not compete, and the text is there when wanted. Ink the text in the figure's colour and there are two figures.
Colour relations: bare ground 60–90% in 2–3 near values (≈1.1:1 apart, decoration only); one saturated hue on the figure, ≈10% of a bare ground, or a field that is itself one hue graded from deep at the far edge to pale at the dividing line (saturated share ≈40%) with a figure under 1%; information achromatic at ≥4.5:1 on its ground; only emblems that repeat what the text already says may sit tone on tone.
Space and line: one figure; bare share 60–90%; information in corner blocks on one margin; top margin ≥1/5 of the height; no rules except one hairline beside the display element; a divided field splits at 50%.
Type requirement: one regular weight for everything; hierarchy from isolation (a clear space at least 3 lines deep around a heading), position and the single display element; small sizes with wide spacing and generous leading, so the face needs open counters and even colour at 13–15px; labels name what a thing is, not what to do with it.
Motion and interaction: a beat of empty surface (150–250ms) and then a slow arrival (600–1000ms), cut under reduced motion; a control answers like material giving way (a 1px press or a tone step) and never grows or lifts; focus still gets a visible mark at ≥3:1.
Fails when: the content is comparative or dense (tables, dashboards, search results), because hierarchy by isolation needs room; several actions have equal weight; a small screen with 60%+ bare pushes the only action below the fold; the figure is weak or generic, so the surface reads as an unfinished or broken empty state.
Signature (do not carry together): off-white paper with one red figure · a horizon at mid-height with a tiny walker · blind-embossed emblems and wide-spaced serif capitals beside a hairline.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Two-factor authenticator, phone 390 × 844, near-black with one electric violet. Dark, utilitarian and numeric; nothing shared with white paper or a salt flat.
1. *Proposition.* "This is the code for this account, now." One code per screen; other accounts are other screens, reached by swiping.
2. *Ground.* 84% bare, in two near-blacks (L 8 above, L 10 below a soft step at 62% of the height). No cards and no dividers.
3. *Figure.* The six digits, set as one block 300 × 110px = 10% of the area, on the vertical axis with its centre 37% down. At least 96px of bare ground on every side.
4. *Only saturation.* The digits are the one violet on the screen, 7.2:1 on L 8. Nothing else has chroma: no brand colour in the header, no coloured account icons.
5. *Information.* Account name and issuer in the top-left corner, expiry ("18 s left") and "copy" in the bottom-left, all L 72 grey (9:1), 13px, regular weight, 0.08em spacing, flush on a 24px margin. Time left is text plus a 2px bar that shortens, so it is not carried by colour. The app's mark is a 20px glyph, bottom-right. "Copy" has a 48px hit area.
6. *Display.* The digits are the one display element at 4× the body size, in the same regular weight; everything else is 13px.
7. *Motion.* On a new code: 200ms of bare ground, then the digits arrive over 700ms. Pressing "copy" sinks the label by 1px and changes it to "copied".
Result: a black screen with one violet number in its upper half and four small grey lines at the left corners. No white, no red, no horizon.

## Space & composition
- **The horizon split.** The Uyuni Horizon poster is one full-bleed photograph split by the horizon at 50% of its height: sky graded from deep cyan at the top to pale at the line, salt-flat ground pale blue with the sky reflected in it. The human figure is ~5% of the frame height (not 1–2%), placed at ~82% across, and the wordmark stands on the line beside it. Scale contrast between a vast field and a tiny figure does the emotional work.
- **One question per surface.** Each composition has a single focal proposition. Everything else is field.
- **Peripheral anchoring.** The logo sits off-centre in the right third, never centred: on the horizon line in Horizon, low right in Tea House. It is a white bold wordmark ~6% of the image width, small against the field but not a whisper. Metadata (folios) goes bottom-left in the books. It behaves like a signature, not a headline.
- **Density through absence.** In the *Designing Design* spreads ~60% of the pixels are unprinted paper on picture spreads and ~88% on text spreads; covers (*Shiro*, *Ex-formation*) are ~90% blank. Body text runs in justified columns of ~74 characters, not narrow ones. The MUJI campaign posters are the opposite: full-bleed photographs where the emptiness is the subject (a horizon, a bare room), not white paper.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

White is a family of whites distinguished by paper and light: warm in RE-DESIGN, neutral grey-white in HAPTIC. The MUJI campaigns are not white at all: Horizon is saturated cyan sky over pale-blue ground, Tea House is dark sepia. Red appears only as the burgundy circle (*hinomaru* logic: one red dot holds infinite meaning). Colours below were sampled from the publisher's page images of the book reproductions, not from original prints.

| Role | Hex | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Field / paper | `#F3F1ED` | Warm unbleached white, never pure `#FFF` (RE-DESIGN photos, 48%) |
| Secondary field | `#E8E2DC` | Warm second white, for the "ground" band (RE-DESIGN, 29%) |
| Sky / cool white | `#C3D8F0` | The Horizon sky at the line; the salt-flat ground below reads `#A6BBD9` |
| Ink | `#282318` | Warm sepia-black, softer than black (Tea House, 41%) |
| Quiet text | `#665E52` | Captions, metadata: 5.7:1 on `#F3F1ED`, 5.0:1 on `#E8E2DC`. The sampled Tea House mid-tone `#867D6F` is only 3.6:1 on the paper, so use it for non-text marks only. Never put quiet text on the pale-blue sky or ground fields |
| Seal accent | `#970A2C` | Burgundy of the "emptiness" circle diagram, max one instance per screen |
| Horizon sky (top) | `#0282C4` | Saturated cyan at the top of the Horizon image; saturated blues cover ~38% of it |

The logic is near-monochrome fields (warm white, or one blue, or one sepia), shifts of value instead of hue within a piece, and one red point as punctuation.

## Form, line & typography
Hara's type is small, light and straight. Serif (and Mincho) appear in one regular weight, titles in wide-tracked capitals, and a hairline vertical rule separates a title from its small caption (RE-DESIGN, HAPTIC openers). Hierarchy comes from position, space and one big display word, never weight. Forms are rectangles and the circle (vessel, seal).
- **Fonts:** *Noto Serif JP* (Mincho flavor for display), *Zen Kaku Gothic New* or *Noto Sans JP* for UI text, and optionally *Shippori Mincho* for editorial headings.
- Set body at 13–15px with 0.04–0.08em tracking and generous line-height (1.8–2.0). Most type stays within 1.5× body, but each surface may carry ONE display element at 3–5× (chapter titles ~3×, *Ex-formation* title ~4×, the 白 on *Shiro* cut by the edge).

## Rhythm, motion & time
Motion should feel like a breath or a slow change of light. Use long fades (600–1200ms, ease-out), with no bounce. Following Haptic, responses should read as texture: a 1px pressure inset, or a soft shadow that appears only on contact. Treat waiting time as *ma*. Leave a beat of empty screen before content arrives, rather than showing a spinner.

## UI translations
1. **Horizon layout:** split the hero at 50/50 into a sky field graded from `#0282C4` at the top to `#C3D8F0` at the line, over a pale ground (`#A6BBD9`), or the warm-white pair `#F3F1ED`/`#E8E2DC` for a quiet version. Place one small element (~5% of the hero height) on the line at ~80% across, and nothing else above the fold.
2. **Logo on the horizon:** put the brand mark on the horizon line beside that element, right third, cap height ~6–7% of the hero; folios and metadata go bottom-left. Never a centred header bar.
3. **Name nouns, not purposes:** label containers by what they are ("Notes," "Folder," "Board"), not prescribed uses ("Meeting Notes Template"). Let users assign the meaning.
4. **Empty states as invitations:** a blank canvas with one hairline and a single quiet prompt. No illustrations, no mascots, no onboarding checklist.
5. **One red point:** allow exactly one `#970A2C` element per screen (an active state, a save dot). If a second appears, demote one.
6. **Type hierarchy by placement:** use one weight (400) throughout. Differentiate headings through isolation (48px+ space around them) and position, not boldness. Allow one display word per screen at 3–5× body in wide-tracked caps, next to a hairline vertical rule and a two-line caption.
7. **Warm whites only:** ban `#FFFFFF`. Layer 2–3 whites a few percent apart to separate regions instead of borders and cards.
8. **Haptic surfaces:** add a 2–3% opacity paper-grain noise texture to fields. On press, inset by 1px with an inner shadow, so the click reads as touch.
9. **Slow reveals:** content fades in over 800ms after a 200ms empty pause. Hover states shift tone only, never scale.
10. **Exformation pass:** redesign one everyday control (a checkbox, a pager) the way Ban redesigned the toilet roll, slightly unfamiliar so users notice it.
11. **Margins larger than content:** give the page top margin ≥ 20vh and keep 60–90% of a text surface unprinted. Long reading text can run in justified columns of 65–75ch, as in *Designing Design*.

## What this artist would reject
- Hero sections that shout a value proposition with a CTA button. Hara sells presence, not persuasion.
- Over-prescriptive UI: wizards, tooltips on everything, labels that dictate use.
- Pure white backgrounds and pure black text, plus gradient blobs and saturated brand color everywhere.
- Decorative illustration in empty states that fills the void instead of offering it.
- Bold, heavy type stacks and hierarchy by size inflation.
- Springy, attention-seeking motion and confetti.

## Signature prompt seed
Design this screen as an empty vessel in the manner of Kenya Hara. Use one horizon at 50%: a sky field graded from deep cyan to pale at the line over a pale reflective ground (or two warm whites for a quiet screen). Place one small figure on the line in the right third and set the brand mark on the horizon beside it. Set all type in a single regular weight (Zen Kaku Gothic New, Noto Serif JP for one wide-tracked display word at 3–5× body), with hierarchy made by space. Use one burgundy point per screen, a faint paper grain, and fades that take a breath. Name things by what they are, not what to do. Leave the meaning for the user to bring.
