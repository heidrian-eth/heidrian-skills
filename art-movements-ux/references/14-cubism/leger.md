# Fernand Léger — Cubism

## Essence
Léger builds a picture out of oppositions placed edge to edge: flat against modelled, straight against curved, saturated against neutral, big plain plane against tiny repeated detail. Each side is kept whole, so the meeting is the event. The observable engine is strict: saturated colours almost never touch one another (0–5% of strong boundaries in every canvas measured; up to 10% if muted colours count). Black, white and grey do most of the bordering, and, from about 1920, modelling is mostly confined to a separate, nearly colourless class of thing. Late on he splits drawing and colour into two independent layers; in film he does the same to time.

## Key works examined
*Léger died in August 1955. Wikimedia Commons marks the images used here as public domain. Where a file is a low-resolution or book scan, colour is noted as approximate.*

- **The Smokers (Les Fumeurs), 1911–12 (oil on canvas, 129.2 × 96.5 cm, Guggenheim).** The starting point: white smoke puffs as hard volumes wedged between faceted roofs and fields. It teaches round masses against straight-edged planes before colour does any work. *(visually checked: Commons/Guggenheim file, 1280 px view; palette k8, edges, boundary values)*
- **Contrast of Forms, 1913 (oil on canvas, 100 × 81 cm, Tel Aviv Museum of Art; not the MoMA picture of the same title).** One stack of seven straight-sided blocks, colour and white alternating, held between two curved-barrel columns on bare burlap. It teaches contrast with no subject at all. *(visually checked: Google Arts & Culture file on Commons; palette k8, block heights, edges, boundaries)*
- **The Staircase (L'Escalier), 1913 (oil on canvas, Kunsthaus Zürich).** Two figures made of cones: one set all red and white, one set all blue and white, yellow only on the steps. It teaches one hue per body and a bare white strip as the only modelling. *(visually checked: Kunsthaus file on Commons; palette k7, stripe shares, boundaries)*
- **Discs (Les Disques), 1918 (oil on canvas, size not verified; Musée d'Art Moderne de Paris).** Cut rings and discs against girders and slats. *(visually checked: MAM file on Commons, 500 px, colour strong and probably over-saturated; palette k8, boundaries)*
- **The City (La Ville), 1919 (oil on canvas, 231.1 × 298.4 cm, Philadelphia Museum of Art).** The central work: billboard letters, girders, railing, one modelled column, one grey figure and one grey bust on a stair. It teaches simultaneous fragments with no shared space. *(visually checked: PMA file on Commons; palette k9, hue budget, edge orientation, horizontal scans, region samples)*
- **The Typographer (final state), 1919 (oil on canvas, Philadelphia Museum of Art).** Lettering as a shape among shapes: "RO" in fragments, plus dot and bar. *(visually checked: visitor photo on Commons, framed, cropped before measuring; some glare; palette k9, boundaries)*
- **The Mechanic (Le Mécanicien), 1920 (oil on canvas; National Gallery of Canada by recollection, not verified).** One modelled figure in front of a flat rectilinear panel. *(visually checked: WikiArt scan on Commons, contrast boosted; palette k8, region samples, boundaries)*
- **Three Women (Le Grand Déjeuner), 1921–22 (oil on canvas, 183.5 × 251.5 cm, MoMA).** Three near-identical bodies, two grey and one warm, inside a dense patterned room. It teaches repetition of a type and difference by value family. *(visually checked: 4996 px photo on Commons; palette k9, counts, boundaries)*
- **Ballet mécanique, 1924 (35 mm film, black and white; Léger and Dudley Murphy, with Man Ray's camera input).** The same contrasts in time. *(visually checked: archive.org transfer, 970.6 s at 16.17 fps, 15,691 frames; contact sheets, cut detection, loop period, shot lengths and shape-area curves. At least 12 versions of the film exist, so timings describe this print.)*
- **The Siphon, 1924 (oil on canvas, Buffalo AKG Art Museum).** A close-up of one gesture. *(visually checked: 500 px file on Commons; palette k7)*
- **The Baluster (Le Balustre), 1925 (oil on canvas, MoMA).** One enlarged object upright on a black slab. It teaches isolation and enlargement. *(visually checked: MoMA file on Commons; palette k8, proportions, edges, region samples)*
- **Woman Holding a Vase (definitive state), 1927 (oil on canvas, Guggenheim 58.1508).** Body and object interchanged: the vase cuts the torso in half on one vertical. *(visually checked: Guggenheim site image; palette k7, proportions)*
- **Composition with Three Keys, 1930 (oil on canvas, sold Sotheby's 2023).** Unrelated objects floating on grey. *(visually checked: Commons/Sotheby's file; palette k7, boundaries)*
- **Composition with Two Parrots, 1935–39 (oil on canvas; Centre Pompidou by recollection, not verified).** Figure heap against free blue cloud and black-white posts. *(visually checked: WikiArt scan, contrast boosted; palette k8, boundaries)*
- **Adieu New York, 1946 (oil on canvas, 130 × 162 cm, Centre Pompidou).** Colour bands laid independently of black outlines. *(visually checked: WikiArt scan; palette k8, hue budget)*
- **The Constructors, 1950 (oil on canvas; collection not verified).** Figures in a lattice of beams. *(visually checked: WikiArt scan, dark; palette k8, hue budget)*
- **The Great Parade (definitive state), 1954 (oil on canvas, 299.1 × 400.1 cm, Guggenheim).** The colour layer and the drawing layer finally run as two pictures in one. *(visually checked: 3000 px image, arthistoryproject.com; palette k8, hue budget, boundaries)*

## Detailed observations

**The Smokers, 1911–12.** Upright, 0.74 : 1. A ladder of white, swollen smoke forms climbs the right-hand two-thirds in an S: bottom-centre, up the right edge, out at the top. Between them sit angular patches of roof and field, about 1/10 the area of the puffs. Saturated colour is only 5% and sits in small roof wedges (pure red) and one large dark red curtain at the left edge (`#5F313B`, L 27, C 22, 7%). Values run L 15 to 80; half the surface is mid-grey. Edges are mixed: one side of a plane is ruled and sharp, the other dissolves into its neighbour. Median lightness step at edges is only 28 L, about half of what he uses eight years later. 79% of edge energy is neither vertical nor horizontal. The faces (two eyes, a nose ridge) are small and lower right, found last.

**Contrast of Forms, 1913 (Tel Aviv).** A column of quadrilateral blocks runs the full height of the middle third: red, white, blue, white, red, white, blue. Seven of them; heights, read by eye in a 1601 px image, ≈ 250 / 130 / 280 / 155 / 155 / 220 / 210 px (1.0 / 0.52 / 1.12 / 0.62 / 0.62 / 0.88 / 0.84 of the first). White always separates the two hues, and each block is sheared the opposite way from the one above. Flanking it left and right are barrel and ellipse shapes, each barred in colour–white–colour. Contour is a black, charcoal-like stroke of constant width (≈8–12 px of 1280, 0.6–0.9%) that overshoots corners and leaves gaps. Paint stops short of the line, leaving a halo of burlap. Bare or thinly scrubbed ground is the largest class (about half the surface: `#847763` L 51, `#A2977E` L 64, `#5D5448` L 36). Saturated area at C>22 is only 9%: the pigments look strong because they sit on raw grey-brown. Dark red `#5A2426`, blue `#27344C`, maroon, two touches of yellow, one green lens. 75% of edges are oblique or curved. No object, no horizon, no light source: the white strip on each form is a highlight but points in no common direction.

**The Staircase, 1913.** Two figures descend, each a pile of truncated cones. The left body is red + white only, the centre body blue + white only; the steps between carry the yellow (2.7%). No cone carries two hues. Each cone has three vertical stripes: colour, unpainted/white, colour. In the big centre cone the strip is ≈ 90 of 310 px (29%). Contours are black strokes drawn over scrubbed light grey (`#8B8F88`, L 59, 32%). Red `#A53D24` C 51 (7.6%) and blue `#304284` C 38 (12.9%) have nearly equal lightness (41 and 30). Each head is a half-black, half-white lens, the most extreme contrast in the canvas, so the eye lands there. Colour meets neutral at 50% of boundaries and a different colour at 0%.

**Discs, 1918.** Four large discs, each made of concentric bands that are cut along a chord and change colour on either side (red/white, black/yellow, purple/white). They sit on a lattice of vertical bars. Roughly one-third of the surface (35%) has C>22, about a quarter is black (24%) and a quarter white (31%). No band meets a second saturated band without a black or white ring between: colour-to-different-colour boundaries 2%.

**The City, 1919.** Ratio 1.296 (close to 4 : 3). Black 27%, white 27%, grey 18%, chroma 29% at C>22. Saturated area at C>28 is 21%: red 10.4%, yellow 6.7%, green 2%, the rest under 1% each. Blue is present (`#255473`, 7.3%) but low in chroma (21). Ink-like colours in order: red `#B03C38` (L 43, C 53) and `#8D3234` (L 35, C 41), yellow `#DED086` (L 81, C 41), green `#337249` (L 43, C 34), violet `#8F6883`. Off-white `#D6CFBC` (L 83, C 10, warm, aged paper-like), black `#0C0E0B` (L 7).

Structure: a vertical violet column (x ≈ 0.66–0.80 of the width, full height) is the one large modelled solid; mean lightness falls in one direction, from L 56–59 at the left to L 19 at the right edge. It sits right of centre and is crossed by a blue band, a red band and a red collar that change angle as they pass. Everything else is flat and cut. A horizontal scan crosses 16–26 light/dark runs; run widths are 0.5% to 25% of the width, median 1.8–5.4%. Within one scan the widest run is 2 to 14 times the median. 47% of edge energy is neither vertical nor horizontal, 40% vertical, 12% horizontal: the many verticals do not become a grid because diagonals and arcs cut them. 136 colour areas above 0.1% of the canvas, but only 23 above 1%; the largest is 4.4% and the ten largest are 25% together. No plane dominates.

One figure on the stair and one bust below it (bottom centre) are the only modelled human bodies, all grey (C 3–4), small (under about 8% of the surface by eye) and faceless: the bust's head is a plain sphere. About seven white stair treads, at near-equal pitch, separated by dark bars, lead to them, and two tubular handrails run across. The flat silhouettes (violet at upper left, green in the red frame) are people reduced to one cut colour. Other repeats are small and regular: 6 windows down the white tower (6 identical dashes), 4 white windows on the black façade at x ≈ 0.63, 4 black discs stacked. Letters: one pale stencil letter (a "B" or "R" form), ≈15% of the height by eye, cropped by a target disc. A framed figure silhouette on the right red panel. Lattice girder fragments occur in four corners, in four sizes.

How the eye goes: yellow planes (upper centre, lower right and left) and black voids lead; red frames the right and left. Nothing is in perspective except the stair. What is missing: sky, horizon, cast shadow, one light, any face.

**The Typographer, 1919.** Frame cropped. Black 34%, white 20%, chroma 34%. A wheel-like oval of violet/blue/orange/white wedges, a dark red panel tilted about 5° with white letters (a broken "RO" or "BO", dots and bars), and a split red cone (two lightnesses with a soft gradient across the split) stand on a black ground. Near-black `#171719` (29%), off-white `#EAE4DB` (16%), red `#903530` (13%), orange-red `#D6481F` (6.4%, C 71), blue `#2D3D6A` (6%), amber `#D6B65A` (4.6%). Lettering here is measured like any other shape, white on red with the same edge quality, cropped by the neighbouring oval.

**The Mechanic, 1920.** The figure fills more than half the height and width, cropped at the waist (about half the surface by eye). The body is modelled (`#DECBA6` L 82 to `#706645` L 44, C 12–25); the ground behind is flat black, white, red-orange `#C2511B` (C 67), yellow `#D8B644` (C 60). Strongly saturated colour (C>45) is 7%, all of it flat and in the ground; the flesh never goes above roughly C 35 (a count at C>28 gives 15% because it includes warm flesh). The only modelled things outside the figure are a small boiler pipe and the cigarette smoke (a stack of small spheres repeating the fist and shoulder spheres). Limbs are tubes with a dark edge on both sides and the light in the middle, so they show no single light. Black is 32%, white 27%. Colour-to-different-colour boundaries 0%. Face in strict profile, body frontal, hand turned: three views assembled, each clean.

**Three Women, 1921–22.** Ratio 1.387. Three heads repeat: oval, vertical dark-hair strip on one side, same eyes, no expression. Figures: two grey (C<5, L 30–86) at left, one warm brown (`#BDA289`, L 68, C 19) at right. Black 27%, white 35%. Grey and brown are the modelled class; everything saturated is flat and small: the red table `#B8320F` (2.6%, C 69), yellow cushions `#A78A2D` (3.5%), dark navy, green diamonds. Saturated total C>28 is 13%: red 6.0%, yellow 3.7%, orange 1.8%, green 1.6%. Pattern is everywhere but never twice the same: diamonds, stripes of three thicknesses, dots, lattice, checks, concentric bands; 215 colour areas over 0.1% of the canvas, the largest only 2.2%. The warm figure holds the one white open book and cup, and is the only vertical mass among two leaning ones. Diamonds on the floor sit in ≈ 6 columns on a regular pitch; the rest of the ground is a rectilinear scaffold with circles on it (5+ small circles in different colours).

**Ballet mécanique, 1924.** Measured in one transfer (16.17 fps). Cut detection counts ≈ 880 visual breaks between 1:12 and 16:00 (rough: detection trips on fast motion and misses some loop resets); the 30-second counts run from 3 to 81, so cutting density swings about 15- to 25-fold. Highest block: 4:30–5:00 (81); lowest: 2:00, 3:00, 9:00–10:00 (4–5; the stair loop sits here, so its resets are undercounted) and the close (3).
- *The stair.* A washerwoman climbs the same stair in a shot that loops. Auto-correlation peaks at 34–36 frames (2.1–2.2 s). It repeats ≈ 6 times (≈ 13 s), is interrupted by a smiling mouth (≈ 8 s), and repeats again ≈ 10 times (≈ 21 s), ≈ 16 in all, with the last of the first run fading to white. She never reaches the top.
- *Hat and shoe.* A straw hat and a shoe alternate on black, 55 alternations in ≈ 19 s: mean 5.6 frames (0.34 s), range 3–10. Each object is centred, plain and the same size each time; only the hat's ribbon mark is flipped left or right. Equal lengths would be a metronome; the jitter (3–10 frames) keeps it alive.
- *Circle and triangle.* A white disc grows from ≈ 5–6% to ≈ 60% of the bright frame in 4 s, then a triangle or shrinking disc goes back; a full swell-and-fall is ≈ 7 s (13:17.5 small, 13:21.5 large; 13:31 small, 13:34.5 ≈ 50%, 13:38 small). Pure geometry punctuates the photographic passages.
- *Object close-ups.* Mouth, eye, legs, saucepans, spheres, whisk, machine parts, letters and numerals ("ON A VOLÉ UN COLLIER DE PERLES DE 5 MILLIONS", a giant 0 that multiplies to 000, mirror-reversed text). Each is cropped so tight it loses its use; most are centred on black, with nothing else in frame. A paper Charlot (Chaplin) opens and closes it.
- *Order.* Held shots (machine parts ≈ 10–12 s, eyes opening ≈ 3–4 s) are set against bursts; white-out fades separate blocks. No narrative; a moment of text is used as a graphic.

**The Siphon, 1924.** A single hand, the largest light form, tips a blue-green siphon; a glass and a spoon; a patch of table. The hand enters from the right edge, cut at a white shirt cuff. Low chroma overall (largest saturated-ish is the siphon green `#325043` L 31, C 14), mostly brown, grey, white. Modelled volumes against a flat dark striped panel; the siphon is the largest single volume. One action, one object, no head.

**The Baluster, 1925.** Upright 0.749. One architectural baluster, ≈ 76% of the height (y ≈ 0.14–0.90), stands on a black slab (x ≈ 0.33–0.71, so 0.38 wide) with the axis at ≈ 0.51. It is grey only, modelled, with no colour: stacked drums, four cylinders on a square plinth, a lobed body with 4 lobes. Around it: flat orange `#AA451F` (C 58), yellow `#CDC56A` (C 47), navy `#18212B`, cream, black, white. Red and yellow each occupy ≈ 9–10%; saturated total C>28 20%. Orange rectangle (x 0.12–0.36, y 0.48–0.88) sits on the left, the yellow panel with a navy insert on the right (x 0.70–1.0). A half-moon circle in white and black interrupts the left edge (its straight side is the black slab's edge). On the right a modelled pink-brown half-cylinder is cropped by the frame. 45% of edge energy is vertical, 35% horizontal, 19% other: the calmest canvas measured. 93 areas above 0.1%, ten largest 60%. Median lightness step at edges 63 L. Colour-to-different-colour boundaries 0%.

**Woman Holding a Vase, 1927.** Warm grey-cream ground `#D3C4A6` (32%). Figure and vase meet on one vertical at x ≈ 0.53: left half is a blue torso with a full sphere for a breast, right half is a flat red and white vase whose belly continues the body's curve. The hands pinch both. The face is perfectly symmetrical, pale `#DED9D2`, modelled only by a dark rim on one side. Colours: red `#9F1018` (C 61, 7%), blue `#2F3C61` (13%, C 27), grey skirt in four tubes, yellow bead necklace (<2%). A body made of an object, and an object made of a body.

**Composition with Three Keys, 1930.** Grey ground `#AEADA8` (30%, L 70) with scraped white, yellow and blue patches behind. Objects (bunch of keys, a black-white spearhead, a red zigzag, blue cloud) overlap at different scales with no ground line and no shadow. Red `#C33830` (C 64) 4.7%, yellow `#E1C651` (C 59) 10%. The outline is black, heavy, about 1% of the width, and the colour behind is feathered and slips past it: the first clear sign of drawing and colour separating. 79% of edges are oblique.

**Composition with Two Parrots, 1935–39.** Near-square 1.22. Warm-cream, black and grey ground with cloud-shaped patches; three figures in two families (left grey, right warm brown) overlap in a column; blue cloud with six lobes to the right; two black-white posts, one green. Patches of yellow ground show through the gaps. Colour areas float behind bodies. The only saturated small objects are two parrots in the figures' hands (red/blue/green), both held in the lower half. 59% of boundaries are colour-to-neutral; 1% colour-to-different-colour.

**Adieu New York, 1946.** Black-outlined objects, cage, propeller, rope, plant, gourd, on white; behind and across them, free quadrilaterals of red (19% of the surface), yellow (8%), blue (7%) and green (4%). Bands ignore the outlines completely: they cross a propeller, half a fan, the plant. Lettering on a curling ribbon ("ADIEU NEW YORK") ties the middle to the bottom. Saturated total 38%.

**The Constructors, 1950.** Upright 0.70. Beams, ladders and rails in black, white, grey, red and amber form a lattice over blue sky with grey cloud shapes. Saturated total 23%: blue 11.5%, red 5.2%, yellow 3.5%, orange 3.3%. Six men, all small and grey-brown, modelled; four in a row at lower left carry one beam, three faces looking left or out, one in profile. Beams are flat with a black outline ~0.7% of the width and one interior stripe; they run at about 5–10 slightly different angles. Men are the only soft forms. Ropes of round beads loop through the lattice (two long serpentine lines among straight ones).

**The Great Parade, 1954.** Ratio 1.335. Ground grey `#B9B5B3` (33%), white 11%, black lines. Saturated total C>28 27%: red 7.5%, blue 7.4%, orange 5.8%, yellow 5.2%, green 1.4% (from the same palette by hue: the five are each a band or disc). Colour shapes are a few huge planes laid across everything: an orange rectangle, a blue band, roughly a quarter of the height, sloping across the middle, a red ring on the right, a green disc at lower left. Bodies are outlined in a black line of constant width with grey-white shading and inherit whatever colour the plane behind them has: one arm is orange because it crosses the orange. Messer (1962) described it as two or three paintings executed at once, one realistic, the other abstract, and said the letter "C" between the two halves acts as a hinge and is all that remains of the word "cirque". Red, blue, orange and yellow meet only by crossing (3% of boundaries, all inside the ring and band crossings), so most joints are still held apart by black line or white.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Saturated colours almost never touch each other** (all). Share of all strong boundaries (ΔE>30) where one saturated area meets a different saturated hue: Contrast of Forms 0%, Staircase 0%, Mechanic 0%, Baluster 0%, Keys 0%, Woman with Vase 1%, Parrots 1%, Constructors 1%, Three Women 2%, Discs 2%, Adieu New York 2%, City 3%, Great Parade 3%, Typographer 5%. Colour meets neutral at 25–59% (except the burlap Contrast of Forms, 9%), the rest is neutral to neutral. Method: pixels 6 px apart in a 500 px image, colour = C>25, hue difference >35°. A neutral line thinner than that is skipped, so true contact is rarer still. Counting muted colours too (C>15) raises it to 9–10% in the City and Typographer, 4–5% in most others and 0–1% in Staircase, Mechanic, Baluster and Keys: exceptions are small bars and collars.
- **Black and white are working colours in near-equal amounts** (1913–27 group). Black/white area shares: City 27/27, Mechanic 32/27, Baluster 31/32, Discs 24/31, Vase 24/43, Typographer 34/20, Siphon 23/23. Together they make ≈ half to two-thirds of the canvas. The light–dark step is big: median lightness difference at strong edges is 54–64 L in City, Typographer, Mechanic, Three Women, Discs, Baluster, Vase, Keys and Parrots (lower in Siphon 40, Acrobats in Grey 44, Constructors 37), versus 28 for The Smokers and 41 for The Staircase. He turned contrast up as he went.
- **Contrast in five pairs at once, each pair kept whole** (City, Mechanic, Three Women, Baluster). Flat plane / modelled cylinder; rectilinear / round; saturated / grey; large plain / small patterned; straight ground / curved object. The two sides meet at a hard edge and are not blended.
- **From 1920 modelling is a separate, near-colourless class** (Three Women, Mechanic, Baluster, Constructors; mostly City and Siphon). Volumes are grey or brown, C under about 25; strong colour is flat, with a lightness spread in the flat reds and yellows of only ≈ 6–8 L (Mechanic yellow L 72–79; Baluster orange L 40–46). The modelling rule: light in the middle of the tube, dark on both edges, so no common light source. Exceptions: the Typographer's red and violet cones, the City's violet column (one-sided) and the Vase's blue torso are modelled in colour; in 1913 the cones were colour with a white strip.
- **Saturated budget about 7–38%, divided among three to five hues, none over ~19%** (measured at C>28): Mechanic about 7 without flesh, Three Women 13, Keys 17, Baluster 20, Staircase 21, City 21, Constructors 23, Great Parade 27, Adieu New York 38. Red or yellow leads in most works from 1919 to 1930; blue leads the Staircase and Constructors. The City has one hue at 10% and one at 7%, then nothing above 2%.
- **No plane dominates** (City, Constructors, Three Women): the largest single colour area is 2–4% and 23 or fewer areas exceed 1% of the canvas, while 136–215 exceed 0.1%. Size runs are extreme: the widest City run is up to 14 times the median of its scan. Only the single-object works (Baluster, Vase) break this: there the largest area is 13–19% and the ten largest hold 53–60%.
- **One thing at a time is enlarged and isolated** (Baluster ≈ 76% of height; Siphon; Keys; the film's close-ups). The object loses its use. The film crops an eye, a mouth, a hat and a shoe to fill the frame on black and puts them at the same scale; the subject is the object's shape, its edge and its repetition.
- **Repetition as a count, not a texture** (City windows 6, stair treads ≈ 7, façade windows 4; Contrast of Forms 7 blocks; Three Women 3 heads; film loop ≈ 16, alternation 55). Repeats are near-identical in size and spacing and sit together in short runs. In the film they jitter (3–10 frames).
- **Alternation of separate runs with blank** (Contrast of Forms: colour, white, colour; Staircase: colour, white, colour within each cone; City: black slab / white slab / black; film: hat / shoe, circle / triangle, image / white fade).
- **Cropped fragments, named by their parts** (City letter B, lattice corners; Typographer "RO"; Siphon forearm; Baluster's cropped half-cylinder; film). Letters and numerals are shapes measured like others. Text is cut by a disc, mirrored, or enlarged until illegible.
- **Verticals carry, diagonals and circles destabilise** (edge energy, vertical/horizontal/other): Baluster 45/35/19, Typographer 44/11/45, City 40/12/47, Mechanic 32/25/43, Siphon 39/20/41, Discs 30/10/59, Contrast of Forms 17/7/75, Smokers 12/9/79, Keys 14/8/79. As contrast of line increases, right-angled share falls.
- **Bodies are interchangeable with things** (Mechanic, Vase, Siphon, Three Women): same tube, same shading, same outline; the Vase shares one vertical with the torso; the smoke spheres in the Mechanic repeat the fist.
- **Faces without expression, different views assembled** (Mechanic: profile face, frontal torso, turned hand; Three Women frontal).
- **Drawing and colour end up as two layers** (Keys 1930 → Adieu New York → Great Parade). The line keeps the figure; the colour becomes a few large planes that run across the figure and the ground alike.
- **Slow, planned and varied, not improvised.** Drawings, then gouaches, then the canvas, attacked when "80 percent sure" (Léger, quoted by Messer 1962). The definitive-state label appears on several works.
- **Stated rule.** In his 1914 lecture "Contemporary Achievements in Painting" he set billboards in a landscape beside the contrasts in his paintings (Britannica), and wrote "Contrast = dissonance, and hence a maximum expressive effect" (cited in The Spectator).

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Fernand Léger.** *Sort everything into two classes that never mix, flat things that carry the colour and shaded things that stay near-neutral; set them edge to edge with the darkest, the lightest or a grey between any two hues, so that each meeting is a whole contrast.*
Process:
1. Sort before placing anything. Every element is either FLAT (a plain area with ruled edges, no gradient and no shadow, its value held within 6–8 L; only this class may take strong colour) or SHADED (anything with tonal depth; its chroma stays under about half that of the flat colours). Nothing belongs to both. Then name the oppositions the view will run, at least three of five: flat against shaded, straight against round, strong colour against neutral, large and plain against small and patterned, level ground against curved object. Each side stays whole and they meet at a hard edge.
2. Choose the density. Field: many parts and no hero; the largest single area is 2–4% of the view, about 20 areas pass 1% and well over a hundred pass 0.1%, the widest part in any row is 2–14 times the median, and parts run off all four edges. Or isolation: one thing at 73–76% of the height, upright on a dark slab about 0.38 of the width with its axis within 2% of the centre; the largest area is then 13–19% and the ten largest hold 53–60%.
3. Lay the neutrals first, as areas and not as background: the darkest and the lightest value at 20–35% each, together half to two-thirds of the view, with a middle grey for most of the rest. The median value step across a strong edge is 54–64 L.
4. Budget the colour and keep it apart. Strong colour takes 7–38% of the view in three to five hues, none over 19%; in a field one hue leads at about 10%, a second follows at about 7% and the rest stay under 2% each. Every hue is bordered by a neutral: one hue meets a different hue on at most 5% of the strong edges, and only as a small bar or where two bands cross. In a run, alternate colour, blank, colour. A thing that takes colour takes one hue.
5. Place the shaded class: few, and in a field one large one, off-centre (its middle near 0.73 of the width, about 0.14 wide, full height) with any others under 8% of the view. Shade each about its own axis, light in the middle third and dark at both edges, so that no common lamp is implied. Give each part its clearest single view and join the parts by overlap alone: no shared perspective, no horizon, no cast shadow. A part may be cut by the frame or by a neighbour as long as what remains still names it.
6. Repeat by count. Identical units sit together in runs of 3–7 at an equal pitch; outside the runs the intervals are irregular, up to 50 : 1 across one row. No pattern is used twice.
7. Let uprights carry 30–45% of the edges and cut them with diagonals and arcs (20–47%) before they close into a grid; level edges are the fewest (10–35%). Every round form is cut by a straight edge.
8. Treat display lettering as the flat class: a letter is a shape with the stroke weight of the bars beside it, in neutral ink, and one fragment up to about 15% of the height may be cut by a neighbour. Any word that must be read stays whole.
9. Optional layer mode: draw all content in one neutral line of constant width (0.7–1% of the view's width) with neutral shading, and lay three to five large flat colour planes, one about a quarter of the height, across content and ground alike without following any outline. A thing takes the colour of the plane behind it. Planes meet only by crossing (3% of the edges), and a bare margin of 10–15% stays on each side.
Why it works:
- Simultaneous contrast: two hues that touch shift each other and the edge vibrates. With a neutral between, each hue keeps its identity and gains the full value step, so five colours stay nameable in one view. Let them touch and the palette reads as a blend.
- Cues kept apart: hue names a thing, a value gradient gives it volume. A colour that is also shaded changes value across its own area and cannot be matched to the same colour elsewhere; held within 6–8 L, the same colour is the same meaning wherever it appears.
- Salience by oddness: one shaded solid among flat planes is found first without being large or bright. Shade everything and the view becomes a lit scene with one light and no ranking.
- A contrast needs both terms whole. Grade the edge between them and the meeting disappears; equalise the sizes and large against small is lost to an even grid.
- Counting against fusion: a run of 3–7 is read as a number at a glance. Spread the same unit over the whole view and it fuses into a tone that carries nothing.
- Depth from overlap, stated locally: with no shared perspective and no lamp, each part can be shown in the view that names it fastest and moved without redrawing its neighbours.
Colour relations: darkest and lightest values 20–35% each, near-equal, together half to two-thirds, used as working areas and as the separators; strong colour 7–38% (13–27% in most cases) in three to five hues, the leader about 10% and never over 19%, always flat and always against a neutral; the shaded class is the low-chroma family and carries every gradient; hue-to-hue contact at most 5% of strong edges; median edge step 54–64 L. In layer mode a middle grey ground takes about a third.
Space and line: field (largest area 2–4%, widths 0.5–25% of the view with a median of 2–5%) or isolation (object 73–76% of the height on a slab 0.38 wide); the one large shaded solid off-centre near 0.73; uprights 30–45% of edges, cut by diagonals and arcs; round forms cut by a straight edge; runs of 3–7 at equal pitch; no margin except in layer mode (10–15%); either no line at all, only colour edges, or one line of constant width at 0.7–1% of the view's width.
Type requirement: heavy, upright, even-stroked letters with no stroke contrast, whose stem matches the bars and rules around them; neutral ink only, on a neutral or on one flat colour at the full value step; text sits on the flat class and never over a shaded thing. Display fragments are shapes and may be cut; running text, labels and figures are never cut, mirrored or enlarged past reading, and a cut fragment is hidden from assistive technology or given its full string once.
Motion and interaction: the oppositions run in time as they do in space. Change by cut, and pass through a blank neutral frame between two coloured states; never cross-fade one hue into another. Set holds (3–12s) against short bursts, so that density varies 15–25 times across a sequence and is never averaged. A loop of about 2s repeats 6 times before one unrelated interruption; an alternating pair holds each state 0.2–0.6s with uneven lengths and stays under three changes a second; one form may swell from 5% to 50–60% of the frame over 4s and fall back. Selection swaps an item's class or its neutral (dark for light) in place, with no glow and no shadow. Targets ≥44px. Reduced motion: no loops or alternation, one cut to the final state.
Fails when: colour must grade or touch (heat maps, continuous scales, gradients that carry a brand); full-colour photography is the product, since the shaded class must stay near-neutral; more than five categories need their own hue; the view needs one dominant hero and many parts at once; a quiet, low-contrast register is required or a long reading session (a 54–64 L step at every edge is tiring); a strict table or form whose columns must stay a plain grid; hue alone would carry a state (pair it with a label, a position or a shape).
Signature (do not carry together): grey tubular bodies and machine parts shaded dark on both edges · flat red, yellow and blue patches on black and white · stencil letter fragments and cut discs among girders and stairs.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Public library events page, desktop 1440 × 900, a muted palette of plum, apricot and moss with deep ink and pale stone, bookish and unhurried (the works are loud, primary and mechanical).
1. *Sort.* FLAT: navigation slabs, date blocks, category tags, buttons, every text panel. SHADED: event photographs and the two speaker portraits, all converted to an ink-and-stone duotone with chroma under a third of the plum's. Oppositions run: flat against shaded, strong colour against neutral, large and plain against small and counted.
2. *Density.* Field. No panel exceeds 288 × 180px (4% of the view); 21 areas pass 1%. Widths run from 24px tag gaps to a 360px listing column, five times the 72px median. Panels run off the left, right and bottom edges; there is no page margin and no hero.
3. *Neutrals.* Ink (L 18) as slabs and bars 26%, stone (L 80) 30%, a middle grey 24%. Ink against stone is a 62 L step.
4. *Colour.* 20% in three hues: plum 10% (date blocks), apricot 7% (booking actions), moss 2% (the "free" tag), plus 1% in small bars. Each is flat, with no tint or hover gradient. A date block never abuts a booking button: a 12px ink bar or 16px of stone always lies between. Tags in a row alternate tag, stone gap, tag.
5. *Shaded.* One duotone photograph strip of the featured event, full height, from x 950 to 1152px (centre 0.73, 0.14 wide). The two portraits take 1.5% each. No drop shadow anywhere; listing panels overlap the strip's left edge by 24px, which is the only depth.
6. *Count.* A week strip of 7 identical day cells at a 96px pitch, and 4 identical room markers stacked at 40px. Nothing else repeats at an equal interval.
7. *Direction.* Column edges are upright; two ink bars at 12° and the arc of the "today" disc cut across them. The disc is cut by the week strip's top edge.
8. *Lettering.* A heavy upright sans with a 12px stem, equal to the ink bars. One month initial 135px tall (15% of the height), stone on ink, is cut by the "today" disc and hidden from screen readers; the month is given in full in the heading. Every event title is whole, ink on stone.
9. *Layer mode.* Not used.
- *Motion.* Changing week cuts to a stone frame for 80ms and cuts to the new week; nothing fades between plum and apricot. A selected day swaps stone for ink in place.

Result: a quiet, bookish listing page with no primaries, no machines and no outlines. What survived is two classes that never mix, a neutral between every pair of hues, no area over 4%, one shaded solid off-centre, and repeats that can be counted.

## Space & composition
- No common space: things overlap, but each is drawn on its own plane with its own view. Depth comes from overlap and from the one modelled solid.
- Format runs 0.66–1.39 : 1 (one 1930 mural panel 2.06). The largest wall pictures (City 231 × 298, Great Parade 299 × 400 cm) are read as billboards at about body scale.
- Off-centre vertical mass: the City's violet column sits at x 0.66–0.80; the Baluster's slab at 0.33–0.71; the Mechanic fills the right.
- Scale ratio between the largest and the median run in a scan: 2–14 : 1 (City). Small patterned elements cluster beside big plain ones.
- Object height on the isolated works: Baluster ≈ 76%; vase ≈ 73% of the canvas height (y 0.10–0.83).
- Margins exist only in the Great Parade and Keys (bare grey around the group, ≈ 10–15% each side by eye). Elsewhere planes bleed off all four edges.
- Figures cropped by the frame: Siphon arm, Baluster half-cylinder, City girders.

## Form, line & typography
- *Two families of form:* ruled flat planes (bars, slabs, discs, rings cut on a chord, lattices) and modelled tubes, cones, spheres. No in-between curves and almost no irregular outline before 1930; after that, clouds and lobes.
- *Line:* 1913 lines are charcoal-like, gappy and overshoot; by 1919 almost none, just colour edges; by the 1930s a heavy uniform black contour about 1% of the width by eye that holds the figure apart from the colour. Line is never varied for mood.
- *Modelling:* vertical gradient inside a tube: dark edge, light centre, dark edge (with the strip ≈ 29% in a Staircase cone). It is repeated on limbs, pipes, fingers and columns alike (the City column, lit from one side, is the exception).
- *Letters:* stencil-like, heavy, upright, sans serif, no ligatures, fragments rather than words, with dots and bars beside them; the same weight as the architecture around (City "B", Typographer "RO", Great Parade "C"). In the film, plain bold title-card grotesque, mirrored or enlarged.
- *Circles:* half, ring, disc, stacked; always cut by a straight edge (Baluster half-moon; Discs chord-cuts; City's lower-left target at x ≈ 0.1–0.25).

## Rhythm, motion & time
- Film: cutting density between 3 and 81 breaks per 30 s (one print), so fast and slow are set against each other rather than averaged.
- Loop: a shot of 34–36 frames repeated ≈ 16 times, broken by an unrelated image after ≈ 6; the repetition is the content.
- Alternation: mean 5.6 frames, range 3–10, never uniform.
- Growth: one circle swells from 5% to ~50–60% of the frame in about 4 s and falls back, a ≈ 7 s wave.
- Counterpoint: an identical object returns enlarged, mirrored, multiplied (0 → 000) or negative.
- In paintings, rhythm is equal repeats in one place (6 windows, 7 treads, 3 heads) set against irregular intervals elsewhere; run widths in the City scan differ by ≈ 50 : 1.
- Colour as a layer that moves free of the drawing in the late works. Messer reports Léger tracing this to Broadway's lights in the 1940s, where a man beside him turned from red to green.

## UI translations
*For strict studies and homage. A kit takes "Process (derived)" and its own surface.*

1. **Two classes, declared.** Every component is flat (plain fill, ruled edge, value within 6–8 L) or shaded (tonal depth, low chroma). Strong colour goes on flat components only; images, avatars and 3D are the neutral class.
2. **A neutral between every two hues.** No coloured component abuts another of a different hue; a bar or gap of the darkest, the lightest or a grey always lies between. Audit: hue-to-hue contact on at most 5% of strong edges.
3. **Dark and light as surfaces.** The darkest and the lightest values are panels, 20–35% of the view each, not a background and its text.
4. **Three to five hues, rationed.** Strong colour is 7–38% of the view; in a dense view one hue leads at about 10%, a second at about 7%, the rest under 2% each.
5. **No hero in a dense view.** The largest panel is 2–4% of the view; widths vary so that the widest in a row is 2–14 times the median.
6. **Or one thing alone.** A detail view shows one object at 73–76% of the height on a dark slab about 0.38 of the width, with flat colour panels at the sides.
7. **One shaded solid, off-centre.** In a dense view, one full-height image or volume near 0.73 of the width is the only large thing with tonal depth.
8. **Self-lit volumes.** A shaded component is light along its middle third and dark at both edges; no drop shadow, no global light direction.
9. **Counted runs.** Identical units appear in runs of 3–7 at one pitch (steps, days, seats); nothing else in the view repeats evenly.
10. **Colour, blank, colour.** Striped or segmented components alternate a hue with a neutral, never two hues.
11. **Uprights cut by a diagonal.** Columns dominate; one or two diagonals or arcs cross them, and every disc or ring is cut by a straight edge.
12. **Letters as slabs.** One display fragment up to 15% of the height, in the weight of the bars beside it, may be cropped by a neighbour; all text that must be read stays whole.
13. **Line over colour.** In an illustration or a map, draw content in one constant line (0.7–1% of the width) and lay three to five large colour planes across it; an item takes the colour of the plane behind it.
14. **Cut through a blank.** State changes cut, with a neutral frame between two coloured states; holds are set against short bursts; reduced motion gives one cut to the final state.

## What this artist would reject
- Two saturated colours side by side with nothing between them; blended or graded transitions between hues.
- Highlights, shadows and a single light on coloured shapes; depth by perspective.
- A hero that fills most of the area in a many-part composition; even, similar-sized modules (the same size for everything).
- Weak mid-tone steps between neighbours (the mature canvases sit at 54–64 L).
- Faces or objects that express mood; storytelling, sequence for its own sake.
- Decoration that repeats everywhere; a texture without a count.
- Improvised surfaces; loose or wandering line.

## Signature prompt seed
Before placing anything, sort every element into two classes that never mix: flat areas, which alone may take strong colour, and shaded things, which stay near-neutral and carry every gradient. Make the darkest and the lightest value working surfaces of about a quarter of the view each, and put one of them, or a grey, between any two hues so that colours never touch. Keep strong colour to three to five flat hues, the leader near a tenth of the view. In a dense view let no area pass 4% and vary the sizes widely; otherwise isolate one object at three-quarters of the height on a dark slab. Place one shaded solid off-centre, lit along its own middle. Repeat identical units only in short countable runs. Cut the uprights with a diagonal or an arc. Change state by a cut through a blank frame.
