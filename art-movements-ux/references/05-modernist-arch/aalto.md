# Alvar Aalto — Modernist / humanist architecture

## Essence
Aalto bent the functionalist grid around the body. He designed rooms, lights and chairs from the user's actual posture and line of sight (a patient on their back, a reader at a desk), not from the façade. A strict orthogonal frame lets one free curve run loose, in warm, touchable materials.

## Key works examined
- **Viipuri Library, 1927–35.** Deep cylindrical roof-lights give even, glare-free light. A stepped hall puts supervision at the top. A timber wave ceiling shapes the room's sound. *(visually checked: secondary source, archdaily.com AD Classics: Viipuri Library, partly: exterior photos of the restored building, section drawing)* Seen: two plain white boxes joined at right angles, one long low block with a continuous ribbon of glazing and a tall closed box with a stair climbing its blank side wall. The roof of the reading hall is a field of ≈60 identical round roof-lights in a regular grid (≈9 × 7), each a shallow glazed drum; the section shows them as deep cones through the slab and the hall stepping down in levels. The wave ceiling is inside the lecture hall and was not among the photos, so it stays unverified.
- **Paimio Sanatorium, 1929–33.** Ceilings are darker than walls, chosen for a patient lying down *(from texts; unverified in the photos)*. Lights are placed out of the patient's view. A yellow floor marks the route from the lobby to the top floor. *(visually checked: Paimio Sanatorium official site photos, restored interiors. Lobby: yellow on the stair treads, a floor inlay strip, the skirting at column feet and the wave-fronted reception counter, the one free curve in the room; the yellow is a greenish lemon (`#A6A24F` in a dim photo), not a golden amber. A teal stripe runs along the stair stringer and rail. Patient corridor: one whole wall is saturated teal-blue `#186E91`, opposite a continuous ribbon of windows; the ceiling is light, so "ceiling darker than walls" could not be confirmed in any photo (unverified). Ward: warm white walls `#D8D0C6`, light blue bedspreads `#70A2C2`, mint bedside tables `#A4B9A9`. Dining hall: red-brown chairs `#803F2C`, pink-beige walls `#D7BAAC`.)*
- **Paimio armchair, 1931–32.** A plywood scroll angled to ease breathing: the form itself does the ergonomic work. *(visually checked: V&A O121394. One continuous sheet curls into a scroll at the top and the bottom; three slots at the head; the closed-loop laminated birch frame `#A67654` has generous rounded corners; this example's seat is painted orange `#D37E3F`.)*
- **Stool 60, L-leg patented 1933.** One bent "knee" joint, made with Otto Korhonen, grew into a whole furniture system. *(visually checked: V&A O176205, b/w photo: three L-legs screwed under a round seat, one seat with a black lino top; the knee is a tight bend continuing straight runs.)*
- **Savoy Vase, 1936.** First blown among wooden sticks. Its irregular wavy outline looks different from every side. *(visually checked: V&A O3250: green glass `#4C5E3A`, asymmetric lobes of different depths, straight vertical walls.)*
- **Villa Mairea, 1938–39.** Wavy pool and balconies set against right angles. Every column differs, to avoid "artificial architectural rhythms". Floors move from stone to timber as rooms get more private. *(retry pass, visually checked: Villa Mairea foundation photos (villamairea.fi), two front views, rear court with pool, living room, dining room, terrace.)* Seen: the pool is one free kidney curve (turquoise `#0DA5C0`) set against the long orthogonal white L of the house (render `#F3EEEA`); the curved, dark timber-clad studio volume (`#593830`) and a continuous red-brown timber balcony band (`#67301F`) are the only non-white masses on the façade. Columns do vary: in the living room a black lacquered steel column (`#180B06`) wrapped in a rattan band at hand height (`#59290E`), outside slender white posts, on the terrace a single white post under a curved canopy. Floors: flagstones on the terrace, red-brown quarry tile in the living room (`#652E13`, warm-lit) under a honey pine-batten ceiling (`#9F5C2B`, warm-lit); a timber floor in the private rooms was not seen, so the stone-to-timber sequence is only half confirmed (stone → tile).

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Alvar Aalto.** *Start from the body's real posture, keep a strict plain frame, and break it exactly once with a free curve where people gather; strong colour marks only what hands and feet follow.*
Process:
1. Fix the posture before the layout: where the user's eye rests, how far the hand reaches, at what distance and angle the surface is seen. Put the primary action inside the reach zone and keep the resting line of sight free of the brightest and busiest elements.
2. Build a strict frame of plain rectangular masses joined at right angles (an L wrapping an open middle), with blank surfaces. Show rank by stepping the levels, the overview at the top, not by boxing.
3. Break the frame once: one free curve per view, placed where activity is densest, asymmetric, with lobes of unequal depth and straight sides. Nothing else departs from the right angle, so the curve cannot become a style.
4. Make one joint and reuse it everywhere: long straight runs that end in one tight bend of a single radius. No sharp corner and no second radius.
5. Light from many identical small sources in a regular grid over the main area (dozens, not one), so the surface is evenly lit with no hot spot and no visible lamp in the line of sight fixed in step 1.
6. Put the strongest colour where the body goes: the route and the touched parts (the path underfoot, edges, rails, handles) and the one curve of step 3, as lines and strips. At most one whole plane takes a second strong hue, and it faces an open, light side.
7. Vary the repeat inside the system: supports and intervals differ slightly from one to the next, and surfaces grow warmer and more textured toward the private end.
Why it works:
- Scale reference in use, not in elevation: a layout fitted to the real posture puts effort where the body is weakest. Designed for an upright viewer at a desk, the same layout puts the action out of reach and the glare in the eyes.
- Salience by oddness: one curve in an orthogonal frame is the odd thing, so it marks the place of activity without size or colour. Multiply the curves and they become texture; remove the frame and the curve has nothing to differ from.
- Grouping by likeness along a route: one colour on everything the feet and hands follow fuses separate parts into a single line through the building. Spend that colour on decoration and the route can no longer be told from the room.
Colour relations: light low-chroma field as the main surface; one mid-value warm tone for whatever is held; the strongest hue as lines and strips on the route (a few percent), repeated on the single curve; one second strong hue as rails and at most one whole plane opposite an open side; the darkest value underfoot; small soft tints for low-stakes objects.
Space and line: orthogonal frame; one curve per view; one bend radius; ≈60 identical light sources in a ≈9 × 7 grid (one work); stepped levels; intervals varied by roughly a sixth (not measured); the curve's lobes unequal.
Type requirement: reading size and line spacing set for the real viewing distance and angle found in step 1, larger than a desk default; open letterforms that survive glare and tilt; sentence case; a gentle scale with emphasis by weight, so rank steps like the levels and never shouts.
Motion and interaction: movement settles without overshoot (280–420ms; none under reduced-motion); feedback travels from the touched control along the marked route to what it changed; touched parts answer with a pressed state (inset plus a warmer value), not only a colour change; the light may shift slowly with the time of day.
Fails when: several tasks of equal density compete for the one curve (dashboards, multi-pane editors); the product already needs many category colours, so the route colour cannot be reserved; the route is marked by colour alone (it must also be a continuous line, a position or a label); the curve is applied to small components, or controls in the reach zone fall under 44px.
Signature (do not carry together): bent pale laminated wood · a wavy-outlined vessel or counter · a lemon-yellow floor through white rooms.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Aalto's procedure → a warehouse picking app on a handheld scanner (480 × 800, black high-contrast, industrial), used standing, one gloved hand, at arm's length under sodium lights.
1. *Posture.* The device hangs at hip height and is raised to about 60cm, tilted 30°. The thumb reaches the bottom 40%. The eye rests on the middle third. So: the confirm action lives in the bottom 320px, the middle third holds only the bin code, and the top bar holds nothing bright. Body text 22px, bin code 96px.
2. *Frame.* An L: a 64px status rail across the top and a 72px rail down the left (aisle, level), wrapping the open middle. Three stepped levels, each 8px in and one value step lighter than the last: order, line, item.
3. *One curve.* The confirm zone at the bottom has a single asymmetric scalloped top edge, deeper under the thumb's arc (56px) than on the far side (24px). Every other edge on the screen is straight.
4. *One joint.* Every corner, of every control, rail and field, is a 10px bend; nothing is square and nothing is a pill.
5. *Light.* No glowing hero. The pick list is lit evenly: 12 identical rows, each with the same faint top-edge highlight, and no element brighter than 80% of white in the middle third.
6. *Route colour.* A single saturated cyan-green, the only strong colour, runs as one continuous 6px line: down the left rail to the current aisle, along the current row, into the scalloped edge of the confirm zone. It is also a line, a position and a label ("next"), so it survives colour-blindness and the sodium light. One whole plane, the exception screen for a short pick, is a deep orange facing the empty side of the list.
7. *Varied repeat.* Row heights step 64 / 72 / 64 / 76px by item bulk; the item level carries a faint grain that the order level does not.
- *Motion.* After a scan, a tick travels along the route line from the confirm zone to the next row in 350ms and settles with no bounce. Hit areas are 64px or more.
Result: a black, loud, industrial tool with no wood, no white rooms and no softness. What survived is posture first, a strict L frame, one curve at the point of action, one joint, even light, and colour reserved for the route.

## Space & composition
- **Orthogonal frame, one free curve.** Plans are mostly rectilinear. The curve (the ceiling, the pool, the vase) appears once, where human activity is densest.
- **L-plans.** Wings wrap a courtyard. In UI, a top rail plus a left rail wraps the content, which becomes the courtyard.
- **One repeated light source over a plain box** (Viipuri, seen): a regular grid of identical round roof-lights replaces windows over the main hall; the walls stay blank.
- **Terraces, not cards.** Viipuri's hall drops in levels, with the control desk at the peak. Show hierarchy through changes of plane, not shadowed boxes.
- **Diffuse light from above.** Use many even sources, never one spotlight.
- **Planned irregularity.** Spacing varies by about ±15% inside a consistent system, like Mairea's mix of paired and wrapped posts.

## Form, line & typography
Aalto's line is a long, taut straight run ending in a tight laminated bend: the L-leg knee, the scroll of the Paimio chair. There are no sharp 90° corners and no blobs either. Bend the joints and keep the runs straight. For type, use humanist sans faces with open apertures: **Source Sans 3** (UI and body), **Alegreya Sans** (more handmade), and **Literata** for warm display text. Use sentence-case headings and a gentle type scale.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- Motion should settle like bending plywood: overdamped, no bounce, `cubic-bezier(.3,.7,.2,1)`, 280–420ms.
- Let the overhead gradient shift with local time, cooler in the morning and warmer at dusk, like light through the skylights over a day.
- Repeat with variation, so scrolling never feels like a metronome.
- The wave ceiling sends sound toward listeners. In UI, feedback should travel from the touched control toward the content it changed.

## UI translations
1. **Layout:** Keep a strict 12-column grid and break it with exactly one free-form element per screen: a wavy, SVG-clipped panel edge where the main task lives.
2. **Layout:** Show hierarchy as terraces: indent nested levels 8–12px and shift tone by 2%, with no shadows. Put the overview at the top terrace.
3. **Posture-first:** Design for how the device is actually held. On mobile, put primary actions in the bottom 40% and keep the top bar quiet.
4. **Color:** Make the header a calmer green-grey (`#A4B9A9`) than the light canvas, so the eye settles downward (the darker-ceiling logic is from texts; unverified in the photos).
5. **Color:** Reserve saturated yellow (`#A6A24F` greenish lemon, brighter in real light) for the user's path (progress rail, active step) and for the one curved element of the screen, as at Paimio where the stairs, a floor strip and the wave-fronted counter share it. Use it nowhere else.
6. **Light:** Replace hard white cards with `radial-gradient(at 50% -20%, #fff, #D8D0C6 60%)`: glare-free light from above.
7. **Type:** Set body text in Source Sans 3 at 17px/1.55. Use a 1.2 type scale and build emphasis through weight, not size.
8. **Form:** Give every control the same 6px bent "knee" corner, one joint reused everywhere like the L-leg.
9. **Material:** Make surfaces warmer as users go deeper: flat linen for lists, a 2–3% birch-grain noise in detail views.
10. **Motion:** Use overdamped settling. Let a confirmation ripple outward from the control that was touched.
11. **Interaction:** Give frequently touched controls a tactile press state (1px inset plus a warmer tint), the way Aalto wrapped door handles in leather.
12. **Rhythm:** In grids of more than 6 items, vary gutters by about ±15% or pair items, so the grid reads as a forest, not a parking lot.
13. **Corridor:** For a long list, colour one side as a full teal field (`#186E91`) and keep the other side light and open, like the Paimio corridor with its colour wall facing a continuous window ribbon.

## What this artist would reject
- Glaring pure-white surfaces and spotlight heroes.
- Uniform card grids on a metronomic 24px gap.
- Organic curves everywhere, which makes them meaningless.
- Cold glassmorphism and chrome with no grain or warmth.
- Layouts made for the designer's upright monitor, ignoring the user's real posture.
- Decorative color unrelated to wayfinding or touch.

## Signature prompt seed

Hold a strict 12-column grid, then break it once with a single wave-edged panel where the main task lives. Mark the user's path and the one curved panel with Paimio lemon yellow (#A6A24F).
