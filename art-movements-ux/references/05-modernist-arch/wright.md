# Frank Lloyd Wright — Modernist / Organic architecture

## Essence
Wright choreographs the body through space: you are squeezed through a low, dim, hidden passage and then let out into a long, horizontal, light-filled room that seems to slide out toward the landscape. Everything from floor joints to window leading to chair backs comes from one geometric unit, so the building reads as a single grown organism, not a set of parts.

## Key works examined
- **Darwin D. Martin House "Tree of Life" window, 1904**: a stylized wheat form in clear, green and golden glass, repeated three times in a 105 × 67 cm panel. Nature reduced to straight line, used as a repeating module. *(visually checked: AIC scan 38836. Three trees confirmed. Ornament is packed into the top ≈42% of the glazing and a base band ≈15%; the middle ≈43% is clear glass crossed only by vertical leads. Clear/white glass ≈50–65% of the panel, dark leading ≈25%, coloured glass ≈6%: chartreuse-olive `#A0A40D`/`#717113`, amber `#A47C20` (≈1%), and three cream opalescent squares `#F5EDB8` at the base. Leads ≈0.5% of the panel width; each stem is a triple line. The wheat uses diagonal leads, so the line is straight, not strictly orthogonal. Oak frame `#6F3D0C`.)*
- **Robie House, Chicago, 1908–10**: Roman brick with raked horizontal joints and flush vertical ones, a steel-cantilevered roof, and a hidden entrance into a low hall. Teaches horizontality and the entry sequence. *(visually checked: AIC, Richard Nickel photographs 141987/141988, b/w. Zoomed brick shows continuous bright horizontal joints and nearly invisible vertical joints; long stone copings and deep eaves. Robie art glass uses diagonal leads (chevrons). No colour available: brick hex unverified.)* *Retry pass, colour checked: Frank Lloyd Wright Trust (flwright.org) photos, exterior and three interiors.* The brick is a muted pinkish tan, strongly light-dependent: sunlit faces `#DDB394`, faces at an angle `#7B6252`, shade `#554132`; long pale limestone copings and urns, dark brown soffits `#1F160F`. Inside, the walls are ochre sand-finish plaster (`#9E753F`, ≈25% of the living-room view) framed by dark oak trim `#4C3623`, and the long south wall is a continuous row of art-glass doors whose patterns sit in the upper part of each leaf.
- **Fallingwater, 1935–37**: ochre concrete terraces cantilevered from a central stone chimney over the falls. Mass anchored at one core, then thrown outward into space. *(visually checked: secondary source, archdaily.com AD Classics, photos credited to the Western Pennsylvania Conservancy: two exteriors over the falls, living room, hatch stair, kettle, corner window)* Seen: two or three pale ochre terrace slabs cantilever at different heights and in crossing directions from a vertical, rough stone core; the slabs are long, thin bands (each ≈1/10 of its length in depth) with rounded parapet edges, and the stone core is the only vertical mass. Inside: a low, wide room with continuous ribbon windows on three sides, stone floor waxed to a reflective sheen, and a recessed light-box ceiling grid. Cherokee red is confined to steel parts: window sashes, the hatch-stair rods and the hearth kettle; it never covers a wall or slab. The ochre is a pale, warm sand on the slabs, lighting-dependent.
- **Jacobs House I, 1937**: a 2 × 4 ft grid scored into a red concrete slab, a 13-inch vertical module that sets the shelves, windows and eaves, and ceilings from 7′3″ in the bedrooms to 11′7″. The unit system and height as a hierarchy. *(not visually checked. AIC 144272 is a perspective of the second Jacobs house (1944, curved solar hemicycle), not Jacobs I; seen but not used for the 2 × 4 grid claims.)*
- **Hanna–Honeycomb House, 1937**: a hexagonal unit with 120° angles and no right angles anywhere in the plan. Changing the module changes the whole feel. *(not visually checked.)*
- **Guggenheim Museum, 1943–59**: a quarter-mile continuous ramp around a skylit atrium. Visitors take a lift to the top and walk down, and the bands widen as they rise. A path replaces the set of rooms. *(visually checked: guggenheim.org exterior photo. The upper bands overhang the lower ones (confirmed); each white band `#E8E2E1` is separated by a recessed dark strip ≈20–25% of the band height. The name runs along the lowest band in black geometric capitals tracked very wide, gaps ≈2–3 letter widths.)*
- **Coonley Playhouse triptych window, 1912** *(added, related work; visually checked: AIC scan 105203)*: clear glass ≈60% with circles and squares in primary red `#C31E03` (≈4%), blue `#0542A5` (≈2%) and green, plus a small flag. Shows that Wright's glass is not always earth-toned: here the accents are primaries on clear glass.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Fallingwater): mass = one vertical core + several horizontal slabs; the slabs project beyond each other so each casts the next into shadow; voids (the falls, the glazing) run horizontally between the slabs.
- **Colour relations** (Fallingwater): neutral earth field (stone, ochre slabs, forest) covers almost everything; the one saturated colour (Cherokee red) is on thin linear steel only (sash bars, rods) and one round object (the kettle); red touches stone and glass, never another strong hue.
- **Line and edge:** slab edges rounded and soft; window sashes thin, hard, red.
- **The procedure:** set one dark vertical anchor; throw 2–3 pale horizontal bands out from it at staggered heights and in different directions; run glazing as continuous horizontal bands between them; reserve the single strong colour for thin linear fittings.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Frank Lloyd Wright.** *Grow every part from one unit, pin the whole to one heavy core, and throw long low bands outward past their support, so the eye is led out along the horizon.*
Process:
1. Choose one unit and derive every dimension, joint and line from whole multiples of it. Break it only where people pass. Changing the unit changes the whole character, so it is chosen first.
2. Set the anchor: one tall, narrow mass, off-centre, at the value extreme furthest from the ground and the only rough surface. It is the single vertical; nothing else may be tall.
3. Throw 2–3 bands out from the anchor at staggered heights and in opposite or crossing directions. Each is long and thin (depth 1/6 to 1/10 of its length), each projects beyond the one below and beyond its container so that its end is unsupported. Separate stacked bands by a recessed strip of the anchor's value, 20–25% of the band's height.
4. Stress one direction of joint only: the lines that run with the bands are drawn, the lines across them are suppressed. No dividers cross a band.
5. Concentrate detail where the view does not pass: pack it into the top ≈40% and a base band of ≈15% of any panel and leave the middle ≈45% clear. Build the detail from thin lines on the unit (≈0.5% of the panel width, doubled or tripled for emphasis instead of thickened), with small colour units only at their junctions.
6. Squeeze the approach: the way in is off the main axis, low and at the anchor's value, then the main room releases sideways along the bands (about 0.6× the normal height before, 1.6× after).
7. Mode for a long collection: replace the set of rooms by one continuous descending band with a fixed spine, entered at the summary.
Why it works:
- Grouping by likeness: parts that share one unit read as one grown thing even when their sizes differ widely. Mix units and the parts read as bought separately.
- Visual weight and leverage: a long thin band needs a visible heavy mass to hang from. With the anchor, the unsupported end reads as reach; remove the anchor and the same band reads as a floating strip, centre it and the reach becomes symmetry.
- Direction from joint emphasis: with cross joints suppressed, every surface has a grain, and the eye runs along it past the frame. Detail kept to the top and base frames the clear middle, where the content or the view is.
Colour relations: ≈90% neutral in one temperature family, as two or three values of one hue (lit, turned, shaded) and not as different hues; the anchor at the far value extreme; line ≈25% of a detailed panel, clear field ≥50%, colour ≈6% as small units at line junctions (1–2 hues, the rarest ≈1%); colour is never a plane.
Space and line: one unit; bands 1:6–1:10; upper band overhangs lower; recessed strip 20–25% of band height; one tall mass only; lines thin, straight (diagonals allowed), ≈0.5% of the width; detail 40 / 45 clear / 15.
Type requirement: letters that look built on the unit (even stroke, wide, geometric), with line height a whole multiple of it; labels run along a band in capitals spaced widely enough to read as part of the band (up to 2–3 letter widths at building scale, one work); reading text in columns no wider than about 70 characters, whatever the band's length.
Motion and interaction: everything travels along the band axis (slide 24–40px, 500–800ms, never scaling from the centre; none under reduced-motion). A header starts compressed and gains height and light past a threshold. Selection thickens the chosen band's leading edge and lowers the contrast of the others, plus a visible focus outline.
Fails when: the format is tall and narrow (a phone in portrait leaves no length for a band or a cantilever); the content is column-led and needs cross dividers (comparison tables, calendars by day); there is no single core to anchor to (a feed of equal posts); reading text is forced to the full band width.
Signature (do not carry together): long low brick bands under deep eaves · a plant drawn in leaded glass with small coloured squares · a continuous spiral ramp.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Wright's procedure → a video editor in cold graphite with one cyan (desktop, 1920 × 1080), dark, technical, synthetic.
1. *Unit.* 12 × 24px. Track height 72 (3 units), ruler ticks every 24, line height 24, inspector rows 48, every control at least 48 high. The only break is the 60px scrub lane, where the pointer travels.
2. *Anchor.* The inspector: a column 336px wide (17.5%), full height, set at 22% from the left, the lightest surface on a dark screen (the scheme is inverted) and the only one with a visible grain.
3. *Bands.* Three, thrown from the anchor: the viewer strip to the right at the top (1140 × 150 visible as a band of thumbnails, 1:7.6); the timeline to the right, lower, 1560 × 216 (1:7.2), running 96px past the window edge so its end is cut off unsupported; the bin to the left, between them in height, 420 × 60 (1:7). Stacked bands are split by a 48px recessed strip in the inspector's value (22% of the timeline's height).
4. *One joint direction.* Track separators are drawn at 2px. Clip boundaries, which cross the tracks, are a value step only, with no line.
5. *Detail at top and base.* In the timeline panel the ruler, markers and waveform peaks occupy the top 40%; the transport sits in a base band of 15%; the middle 45% is clear track. Marker lines are 1px, tripled for chapter marks. Cyan appears only as 6px squares where a marker meets a track, 5% of the panel, and each marker also carries a letter.
6. *Squeeze.* The project opens from a 44px-high dark strip docked bottom-left (project name, last save), then the workspace releases to the right: the timeline band grows from 72 to 216px.
7. *Mode.* The render queue is one descending band with a fixed spine, summary first.
- *Motion.* Panels slide 36px along x in 600ms; nothing scales.
Result: a cold, dark, synthetic tool with no earth tones, no brick and no ornament. What survived is one unit, a single vertical anchor, staggered overhanging bands with a grain, detail at the top and base, and a squeezed entry.

## Space & composition
- **Compression → release.** The entrance is never on the main axis. At Robie it sits at the end of a long side walk and opens into a low hall with a stair turning up. Treat height as something you spend: about 0.6× at thresholds, about 1.6× in the gathering room.
- **Horizontal dominance.** Bands run at roughly 1:6 to 1:10. Horizontal joints are emphasized and vertical ones suppressed, with deep overhangs.
- **Anchor + cantilever.** One heavy off-center core with floating planes reaching out from it in opposite directions.
- **Sliding volumes.** Robie is two offset rectangles, not a centered symmetry.
- **Anchor, then cantilever** (Fallingwater, seen): one vertical rough core; 2–3 horizontal pale bands thrown out from it at different heights and in crossing directions; the bands are thin and long, the core is the only tall mass.
- **One unit governs everything.** Gutters, cards, icons and line height all come from one module. Break it only for circulation, as when Jacobs widens halls to 3 ft.
- **Path as structure.** At the Guggenheim, scroll is a ramp, not a stack of pages.

## Form, line & typography
Line is thin, straight and leaded: orthogonal strokes plus diagonals (Martin wheat, Robie chevrons), or 120° in hexagonal work, with small squares of color set at the intersections. In the Martin window the leads are ≈0.5% of the panel width and stems are drawn as triple lines; Coonley adds full circles. Wright signed his drawings with a red square. Type should feel drawn on a grid: geometric, wide and slightly archaic, with tracked caps for labels. Fonts: **Josefin Sans** (headings and labels), **Alegreya** (Arts-and-Crafts warmth for body text), **Poiret One** (thin-line display only).

## Rhythm, motion & time
Motion is horizontal and slow: elements glide along the x-axis like Robie's roof, with long eases (`cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.1,1)`, 500–800 ms). Section headers start compressed (short and dark) and expand in height and light once scrolled past a threshold. Lists unspool as one descending ramp instead of paginating. Rhythm is a repeated module with one variation per band, like the wheat motif repeated three times.

## UI translations
1. **Layout:** Build on a 2:1 unit (16 × 8 px) *(unverified: no visual basis for 2:1 in the works checked)*. Cards, gutters, icons and line-height are multiples of it. Show it as faint hairline joints on the background, like the scored slab.
2. **Compression/release:** Open key flows with a short, dark 56px entry strip, then release into an ochre band ≥60vh. Users pass through the narrow part first.
3. **Off-axis entry:** Put navigation at the left edge or bottom-left, never centered at the top.
4. **Cantilever:** Anchor content to a dark, off-center "hearth" column about 1/5 of the width. Let panels overshoot the container by 10–15% with no visible support and a deep shadow beneath.
5. **Horizontality:** Use 2px oak horizontal rules and no vertical dividers. Band tables by tone.
6. **Color:** Keep 90% of the surface neutral (clear/limestone ground plus earth tones). Use Cherokee red on exactly one element per screen.
7. **Type:** Labels in Josefin Sans caps, `letter-spacing: .18em`, 11–12px; a building-scale name may track much wider (Guggenheim lettering: gaps of 2–3 letter widths). Body in Alegreya, line-height snapped to the unit.
8. **Art-glass ornament:** Dividers and empty states are SVG leaded-glass patterns: thin dark lines (≈0.5% of the panel width, stems as triple lines, diagonals allowed) with chartreuse and amber squares at intersections, repeated in threes. Pack the ornament into the top ≈40% and a thin base band (≈15%) and leave the middle clear, as in the Martin window.
9. **Motion:** Panels slide in 24–40px horizontally. Never scale up from the center, and never bounce.
10. **Ramp navigation:** Galleries and timelines are one descending path with a sticky progress spine. Start at the summary and walk down into the details.
11. **Interaction:** On hover, thicken the card's top border and dim its surroundings, compressing attention onto the chosen item.
12. **Module swap:** For a radical variant, switch to hexagons (120° clip-paths on avatars, chips and tiles) everywhere.

## What this artist would reject
- Centered, symmetric heroes with the CTA dead center.
- Uniform "ceiling height": the same padding and elevation everywhere.
- Cool grays and pure #FFF/#000.
- Decoration unrelated to the grid: stock illustrations, blobs, gradients.
- Grids of identical boxes. He "broke the box."
- Several bright accent colors competing.
