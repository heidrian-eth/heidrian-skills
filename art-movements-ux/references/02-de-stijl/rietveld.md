# Gerrit Rietveld — De Stijl

## Essence
Rietveld was a cabinetmaker who built space out of separate parts that slide past each other and never quite meet. He made rooms that rearrange themselves over the day and chairs whose rails run past every joint. Each part keeps its own colour, and the space is the gap between parts.

## Key works examined
- **Red Blue Chair, 1918 (painted c.1923)**: square beech rails pegged where they cross. The frame is black, the cut ends yellow, the back a red plane and the seat a blue one, tilted against the frame. Lesson: a structure can be legible as layers that never touch at their edges. *(visually checked: V&A O142927 photo, AIC 72246 photo (made 1920/21, worn), and the unpainted 1918 version V&A O58654 in natural wood with side panels. Each rail runs past the post it crosses by about one rail thickness, and the post rises about one thickness above the top rail. On the V&A chair the red back is 32% of the silhouette, black frame 35%, yellow ends ≈1%; the blue seat is a very dark navy that reads almost as black and shows as <1% from a three-quarter view)*
- **Rietveld Schröder House, Utrecht, 1924**: the upper floor is one open room by day. In the evening, sliding walls split it into three rooms (two children's rooms and a living room). Lesson: the layout is a state, not a fixed fact. *(visually checked: Het Utrechts Archief photographs via Europeana, interior views 11F13F6D…, 4AE7D746…, 997924AD…)* Seen: the upper floor open, with a glazed, framed partition panel standing at the edge of the living area; dark blue-black window frames and a dark floor, grey walls, white ceiling, and the colour carried by furniture and soft parts (red-orange table cover and bench cushion, a blue table top, a red-and-blue chair). The sliding of the walls itself cannot be seen in still photos.
- **Schröder House corner window**: two casements open at right angles and the corner post disappears. Lesson: remove the corner and the edge turns into a threshold. *(Het Utrechts Archief photos show the upper corner only closed: two runs of windows meeting at a slim dark mullion, with red bars on the outside; the post-free open state is not shown, so this stays unverified.)*
- **Schröder House façade**: wall planes and linear bars in white, grey, black and the primaries, sticking out past the volume. Lesson: colour identifies parts; it doesn't set mood. *(visually checked on two models only, not the building: the Centraal Museum model 12589 (museum photo) and a model photographed by John Aldus, V&A O198683. Wall planes are white and four greys, from light blue-grey to a dark grey base slab; the primaries appear only on thin linear members: a yellow pole, red and blue window bars, a red post, a blue bench. Primaries cover ≈0.6% of the façade view; black is window frames and railings)* *Retry pass: also checked Rietveld's own painted elevations of the house (street, rear and side façades, 1924, Stedelijk 83923–83925) and two more models (Stedelijk 16979, 36391); the real building is still not obtained. The elevations confirm the rule: planes are off-white `#EAE8DE` and three greys (`#B2ACA6`, `#94928A`, a blue-grey `#5C6774`), black frames ≈7%, and the primaries sit only on linear members (yellow pole `#A18A27` 1.4%, red `#A32E34` 0.5%, blue `#24428F` 0.4%: ≈2.3% together, more than in the model photos). Bars and slabs overshoot the volume's outline on every façade, and no façade repeats another's arrangement.* *Retry pass 2: the real building visually checked (Het Utrechts Archief colour photographs via Europeana, street view F3F993A6… and 21C537BA…, b/w 096BBC7E…).* Seen: the street front confirms the rule at full scale: white and light-grey planes ≈55% of the house, dark window glass and black frames ≈8%, primaries ≈1% and only on linear members (a pale yellow pole rising past the roof slab, red window bars, one blue lintel bar). Every canopy and balcony slab projects beyond the wall below it, and the slabs are thin (≈1–2% of the façade height) against deep wall planes.
- **Zig-Zag Chair, 1934**: four beech planks with mitred joints, bolts and triangular blocks. No legs, a Z shape about 66 × 78 × 76 cm, and a slot in the back to lift it by. Lesson: one continuous folded path, with its fasteners left on show as the only ornament. *(visually checked: AIC 239015 photo, white-painted example: a row of 8 bolt heads along the seat-to-diagonal joint is the only detail; raw wood shows where paint has chipped at the edges)*

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Schröder House, street front): a box broken into 6–8 planes at different depths; each horizontal slab overshoots the plane below it; the tall vertical yellow pole crosses two floors and the roof line, tying the levels together. No two planes share an edge flush.
- **Colour relations:** planes white/light grey (≈55%), voids dark (≈8%), primaries on lines only (≈1%); the most saturated colours are the smallest and always linear, never a plane. Inside, colour moves to movable things (cushions, table top, chair).
- **Line and edge:** all edges hard; linear members are thin (≈1–2% of the façade height) and always extend past the planes they touch.
- **The procedure:** build from neutral planes at varied depths; let every plane overshoot its neighbour; put the strongest colours only on thin linear elements that run past the joints; keep fixed surfaces neutral and give colour to the parts the user moves.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Gerrit Rietveld.** *Keep every part whole and readable as itself: let parts pass each other at the joints instead of merging, and spend colour only on what ends or moves.*
Process:
1. Name the parts before composing, by kind: planes (carry content), linear members (carry structure), ends and handles (where a part stops or is gripped). Each kind gets one treatment, and a part never changes kind.
2. Set 3–8 neutral planes at different depths and unequal sizes (tracks near 1 : 1.6 : 0.6). No two share a flush edge or a corner: wherever two meet, one passes in front of the other.
3. Overshoot every joint: each member and slab runs past the thing it meets by 1–1.5× its own thickness. Keep members thin (1–2% of the format's height) against deep planes.
4. Rank colour by the inverse of size. At the scale of the whole format the planes stay neutral (light ≈55%, dark voids ≈8%) and saturation goes only on linear members and ends (1–3% in all, the most saturated the smallest). At the scale of one object, one plane may take the strong colour (up to ≈1/3), structure takes the darkest value (≈1/3) and the ends the brightest (≈1%).
5. Fixed is neutral, movable is coloured: surfaces that stay put take the neutrals; the things the user moves, holds or sits content on take the colour.
6. Give the plan at least two named arrangements of the same parts (open and divided). Each part travels on one axis only, and no arrangement repeats another's composition.
Why it works:
- Where one thing runs on past another, the eye reads two objects at two depths; where two things stop flush at a corner, it fuses them into one box. Overshoot gives depth by overlap with no shadow. Trim the overshoot and the layout collapses into bordered containers.
- A thin element can hold full saturation without flooding the view, and bright ends act as counters: the eye ticks off the parts. Put the same colour on the big planes and they merge into one painted volume, a mood and no longer a construction.
- Because every part persists and only translates, the user tracks it from one arrangement to the next (object constancy). Fading or replacing breaks the identity, and the new arrangement must be learned as a new screen.
Colour relations: neutrals in 3–5 values carry the planes; the darkest value is structure and voids; saturated share 1–3% at format scale, each hue one kind of part; an object-scale component may invert to ≈1/3 strong plane : ≈1/3 dark structure : ≈1% bright ends; saturation never tints a fixed surface.
Space and line: 3–8 planes at different depths, unequal; overshoot 1–1.5× thickness at every joint; members 1–2% of the height; no shared edges, no closed boxes; every slab projects beyond the plane beneath it.
Type requirement: type names parts: small, plain, tabular labels placed at the crossings, and one plain upright voice for content on the planes; a face with square terminals that holds at small sizes; a label may run along a member only if it is one or two words and is repeated upright nearby.
Motion and interaction: translation along a track, nothing else. Parts move one after another (280–360ms each, 60ms apart) so the division can be watched; under reduced motion they cut to the end state in the same order. A drawn handle may be a thin notch, but its hit area is ≥44px, and "movable" is shown by the handle's shape as well as by colour.
Fails when: the content is one continuous text (a single part, nothing to articulate); it is many equal items, so every joint's overshoot becomes noise; the width is under about 360px, where depth and overshoot eat the margins (cut to three parts); the two arrangements are really two unrelated screens.
Signature (do not carry together): black rails with yellow cut ends · a red back plane over a blue seat · white and grey slabs with thin primary poles.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Rietveld's procedure → a team scheduling calendar in warm dark browns and ochres, rounded, dark mode (desktop, 1440 × 900).
1. *Parts.* Planes: week grid, people list, day detail, unscheduled tray. Linear members: the time rule, the now-line, the day dividers. Ends and handles: event start caps, resize grips, the tray's pull.
2. *Planes.* Four slabs at four depths, tracks 1 : 1.6 : 0.6 (people 270px, week 720px, detail 430px after overlaps), in browns at L 14, 18, 22 and 26. The week slab passes 14px in front of the people slab; the detail slab passes behind the week slab and 20px above it. No flush edges, 10px radii.
3. *Overshoot.* The time rule is 10px thick (1.1% of the height) and runs 12px past the week slab on both sides. Day dividers are 2px and run 3px past the header band. The tray projects 16px beyond the slab above it.
4. *Colour, format scale.* Planes neutral brown: light share 54%, darkest voids (weekends, past days) 9%. Saturation only on lines and ends: the now-line in ochre (0.4%), event start caps in a brick red (1.1%), resize grips in a pale straw (0.6%). Total 2.1%, straw the smallest and brightest.
5. *Fixed and movable.* The grid, the list and the header stay neutral. Events are the movable things: each carries its start cap and grip; a dragged event takes the brick red across its whole face (object scale: red 30%, dark frame 30%, straw ends 1%).
6. *Two arrangements.* "Open": one week across all people. "Divided": the week slab slides left 300px on its track, then the detail slab slides in, then the tray rises, 320ms each, 60ms apart, giving three working areas. Same four slabs, different composition.
- *Type.* Tabular labels sit where the time rule crosses each day divider ("09 · TUE"); event titles in one plain upright face.
- *Interaction.* Grips are drawn 6 × 28px with a 44 × 44px hit area and a notch shape, so they read as grips in greyscale too.
- *Phone.* Three parts only (day, tray, time rule); overshoot kept at 1× (4px).
Result: a warm, dark, rounded calendar. No white slabs, no primaries, no chair. What survived: parts passing at joints, colour only on lines, ends and movable things, and a plan with two states reached by sliding.

## Space & composition
- **Planes overshoot their joints.** At every crossing, each element runs past the edge of the one it meets by about its own thickness (1–1.5×), like the chair's rails: a 6px rail runs 6–9px past. Containers are overlapping slabs on different z-levels, not boxes with shared borders.
- **Plan as state machine.** Each screen has at least two named layouts, "day" (open) and "night" (divided), built from the same panels. Panels slide on tracks to switch between them, with no navigation to a new page.
- **Asymmetric 3-track grid** in roughly 1 : 1.6 : 0.6 proportions. The narrow track holds controls. Columns are never equal.
- **Dissolved corners** *(unverified, not a rule)*. The corner window is said to open with no post, but the photos show it only closed, so treat an open corner as an optional idea, not a required step.
- **Density:** two registers. In the house, planes are white and greys and primaries are ≈1–3% (0.6% in model photos, 2.3% in Rietveld's side elevation), only on thin bars and small fittings. In the chair, one red plane takes about a third of the object and black about a third. Content sits on planes, never on bare background.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

Colour marks what kind of part something is, and each colour does one job only.
- `#F4F3F2` white plane (main ground; Centraal Museum Schröder model)
- `#D7D9DE` grey plane (recessed or inactive panels, the night state; light blue-grey wall of the Centraal Museum model, which also has a darker base-slab grey `#838792`)
- `#191815` black (structure: rails, rules, dividers, frames; V&A Red Blue Chair)
- `#B70000` red (the backrest: the one surface that holds the user's main thing; V&A chair, a deep pure red; the worn AIC chair is `#864B44`)
- `#1D1E3B` blue (the seat: the surface where you put input; V&A chair, a near-black navy, so pair it with a lighter ground)
- `#DFD457` yellow (only on ends: cut edges, handles, endpoints of the active selection; V&A chair, a lemon yellow, ≈1% of the object)

The rule: black lines carry the structure and coloured planes carry meaning. Yellow appears only where an element ends. In the house the primaries move onto the lines themselves (pole, mullions, post), and the planes stay white and grey.

## Form, line & typography
The form language is square-section rails plus flat rectangles. There are no curves, no gradients, and only 0–2px radii. In type, that means a straight-sided grotesque with square terminals for the body, condensed vertical labels running up the rails, and mono labels at the joints as visible bolts. Fonts: **Archivo** (variable width, good both expanded and condensed), **Space Mono** (labels and bolts), and optionally **Big Shoulders Display** for tall section numerals.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- Motion is **translation along tracks only**. Panels slide in x or y over 280–360ms with `cubic-bezier(.6,0,.2,1)`, like a heavy door on a runner.
- Reconfiguring is shown in steps: one partition moves, then the next, staggered 60ms apart, so the user can see the room being divided.
- Time of day is a real input: "divided" closes partitions for focus, "open" gives overview.

## UI translations
1. **Layout:** Build the main screen from 3–5 absolutely positioned slabs with different `z-index` values. Each overlaps its neighbour by 12px, so no two share an edge.
2. **Layout:** Extend every horizontal rule beyond the container it divides by about its own thickness, both left and right (a 6px rule: `margin-inline: -8px`).
3. **Interaction:** Add a single "partition" toggle that slides 2–3 panels into place over 320ms, turning one open dashboard into three separate workspaces without leaving the page.
4. **Interaction:** Make panels draggable along a fixed track only (one axis each), with snap stops at grid lines.
5. **Color:** Use red for exactly one surface per screen, the primary content plane. Blue goes on input surfaces only, yellow only on ends: the leading cap of a progress bar, a drag handle, the end of a selected range.
6. **Color:** Draw structure as 4–6px black bars, never grey hairlines or shadows.
7. **Type:** Put mono labels like `01 / SEAT` exactly at the points where rails cross, as visible bolts.
8. **Motion:** Animate only `transform: translate`. Drop opacity and scale transitions entirely.
9. **Layout (optional, unverified):** An open corner on the main card is not a required step; the post-free open corner window was never seen in the photos.
10. **Interaction:** Give movable panels a cutout handle (a 6 × 32px notch in the edge) instead of an icon button, like the slot in the Zig-Zag back.
11. **Flow:** Build onboarding as a single Z-path ribbon: right, down, right. Each fold is a step and fasteners (step numbers) show at each fold.

## What this artist would reject
- Rounded cards on soft shadows: elevation faked with blur, not real overlap.
- Modals and route changes for what should be a rearrangement of the same room.
- Decorative colour, gradients, or brand tints used for mood instead of to name a role.
- Hidden structure: borderless layouts where alignment is implied but never drawn.
- Springy, scaling, or fading motion that hides how a part actually moves.

## Signature prompt seed
Design this app screen as a Rietveld interior. Build it from flat slabs and 6px black rails that cross and run about one rail thickness past every joint, so nothing shares an edge or a corner. Use an off-white ground and grey recessed panels. Put red on the single main content plane, blue on input surfaces, and yellow only where elements end. Give the layout two states, open and divided, toggled by panels sliding on one-axis tracks over 320ms with no fades. Use Archivo, with Space Mono labels placed at the joints like visible bolts.
