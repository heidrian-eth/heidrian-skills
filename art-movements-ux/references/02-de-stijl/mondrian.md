# Piet Mondrian — De Stijl

## Essence
Mondrian builds balance from inequality. Black orthogonal bars split an off-white field into cells that are never equal, and a few flat primaries sit at the edges, so a small, heavy color area offsets a large, quiet void. In New York he removed the black and turned the grid into syncopated traffic. The system was about rhythm, not boxes.

## Key works examined
- **Tableau I, 1921**: off-white grounds and small primary blocks, with lines that stop short of the edges. Teaches: a rule can end. *(visually checked: RKD catalogue raisonné image, B128, Kunstmuseum Den Haag)* Seen: near-square (≈0.97:1); off-white/pale grey fields ≈78%; black lines ≈1.5% of the width thick, black total ≈9% including two small solid black blocks in the top-right and bottom-right corners. Colour sits on the edges: a thin red strip along the top edge (≈0.5%), a yellow bar down the lower right edge (≈7%), and the only inset colour, a blue rectangle left of centre (≈6%). The two largest fields are white and sit at the centre. Some lines end on another line rather than at the frame (the left vertical starts at mid-height; the bottom horizontal stops at the black corner block). *(Earlier substitute: the same-year **Lozenge Composition with Yellow, Black, Blue, Red, and Gray, 1921**, AIC, was checked instead: visually checked, AIC scan)*
- **Tableau No. IV; Lozenge Composition, c. 1924/25** (142.8 × 142.3 cm, NGA): an upright grid in a canvas turned 45°. The edge cuts the colored cells into triangles, and the lines were thickened in a repaint. Teaches: let the container crop the system. *(NGA image not obtainable; the AIC 1921 lozenge shows the same move: yellow and blue cells cut into triangles by the edge, two bars floating free of every other line, and a solid black plane, 14% of the surface with the bars)*
- **Composition C (No. III) with Red, Yellow and Blue, 1935** (Tate): the mature grid, with doubled lines and color at the margins. Teaches: put weight at the edges and keep the center empty. *(visually checked: RKD catalogue raisonné image B261, 350 px)* Seen: colour at the margins confirmed: a big red block (≈17.5%, about 45% × 42% of the canvas) fills the top-left corner, a blue block (≈8%) stands on the bottom edge right of centre, a narrow yellow strip (≈3%) on the left edge at the bottom; white ≈66% and the centre cells are empty. No doubled line is visible at this size: the bars are single, ≈1.2–1.8% of the width (black ≈6%). Colour together ≈28%, well above the 13% of the other works checked. *(Earlier, only a 125 px Tate thumbnail could be obtained; checked instead: **Composition with Red, Yellow and Blue, 1927**, CMA scan, and **Composition (No. 1) Gray-Red, 1935**, AIC scan)*
- **Composition with Yellow, Blue and Red, 1937–42** (Tate): a dense mesh of bars, with thin ones bunched close together. Teaches: the gaps between lines carry rhythm. *(visually checked: Tate 600 px image; 8 verticals and 6 horizontals, black 25% of the surface, and colour also appears as short bar segments: a blue bar and a red stub replace parts of black lines)*
- **Broadway Boogie Woogie, 1942–43** (127 × 127 cm, MoMA): no black. Yellow lanes are broken up by red, blue and gray blocks. Teaches: turn borders into signals. *(visually checked: Commons 1280 px reproduction of the MoMA painting)*
- **Victory Boogie Woogie, 1942–44** (Kunstmuseum Den Haag): his last work, an unfinished lozenge still carrying taped paper strips. Teaches: a composition can stay open to change. *(visually checked: Commons 1280 px reproduction; taped paper patches visible at the left)*

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Tableau I, 1921): an unequal grid of 3 columns (≈45/37/16% of the width) and 2–3 rows; the largest cells are white and central; the narrowest column and the top band carry the colour. Lines are of one weight (≈1.5% of the width).
- **Colour relations** (Composition C, 1935): one primary large (red ≈17.5%, a corner block touching two edges), the other two small and on the opposite edges (blue bottom, yellow left); the sizes step roughly 6:3:1. Again no two primaries touch.
- **Colour relations** (Tableau I): 3 primaries + black on white; white ≈78%; colour together ≈13%, each hue a different size (yellow 7 > blue 6 > red 0.5%) and a different shape (bar, block, strip). The primaries never touch one another: each is separated by black lines and white cells. Black also appears as two small corner blocks (value accents at the edge).
- **Line and edge:** hard everywhere; line ends are part of the composition (a line may stop on another line instead of at the frame).
- **The procedure:** divide the field unequally into a few large cells; keep the largest cells empty and central; push each colour into a different edge cell, each at a different size and shape, so no two touch; anchor one or two corners with small dark blocks; let one or two lines stop short.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Piet Mondrian.** *Strike an equilibrium between unequal parts: one large quiet mass held in place by small intense counterweights at the frame.*
Process:
1. Fix the frame and ONE separator weight `t` (1.2–2% of the short side; a line, a gap or a value step). It never varies, so from here on only interval can vary.
2. Place the dominant: the emptiest, quietest cell, 22–53% of the area, off-centre. A large one (≈50%) sits 5–10% of the format from the middle; a small one (≈22%) sits up to a quarter of the format away. It takes the main content and no accent.
3. Subdivide only the leftover strips, never the dominant. Neighbour pairs differ by 1:1.6 to 1:4; a strip may be as thin as 2–4% of the width. Optionally add one interval of about 1.5`t` (a doubled separator) so sizes run from tiny to huge with nothing "almost equal".
4. Load intensity last, as counterweight, in one of two ranges. Chips: the strongest signal fills a cell of 1–8%, the others ≤7% each, total ≤15%. Block: the strongest fills one corner cell touching two edges, up to ≈18%, the others step down about 6:3:1 on the opposite edges, total ≤28%, quiet ground still ≥65%. In both: every signal has a different size and shape, sits in an edge cell or one cell in from it, never in the dominant, and no two signals touch. The larger the signal, the farther out it goes.
5. Open the system: let one or two separators stop at another separator or short of the frame, and step the quiet cells 1–3 L apart, so they read as planes and not as boxes on a background.
6. Test by covering: hide any signal cell. If the layout does not tip, the cell was decoration; remove it or move it outward.
7. Mode for dense live content: move the signals out of the cells and into the separators (separator ≈2.3% wide, carrying separator-wide units; signal share may rise to 35–40%).
Why it works:
- Visual weight is roughly intensity × area × distance from the centre. A small saturated cell far out balances a large pale one near the centre, like a small mass on a long lever. Centred, it becomes a target and the rest a frame; a large one that is not pinned into a corner becomes the ground and the tension is gone.
- With one separator weight, thickness cannot signal rank or depth, so the eye reads the only variable left: spacing. Hierarchy becomes cell size and rhythm becomes interval.
- A signal cut by the frame reads as part of a field that continues beyond it, not as an object on a background. The surface stays flat and the empty cell reads as a thing, not as what was left over.
Colour relations: quiet ground 65–90% in 2–3 near-equal values of one temperature; structure 6–9% at maximum value contrast to the ground; 1–3 signals at full saturation, ranked unequally in area (the largest 2–6× the next), each a flat field separated from the others by quiet cells; a neutral cell (mid-value, or the structure's own value in a corner) may stand in for a signal.
Space and line: one separator weight (max/min ≤1.3–1.5); dominant 22–53%, off-centre; neighbour ratios 1:1.6–1:4; thinnest strip 2–4%; an optional doubled separator with a 1.5`t` gap; one or two separators that stop; right angles only inside the frame.
Type requirement: the same inequality in type: one heavy element and everything else light (two weights, far apart), seated flush against a separator in a cell corner and never centred; a family with a wide weight range and widths that can be fitted to a cell. Text on a signal cell must still reach 4.5:1, or the cell stays empty.
Motion and interaction: a state change is a new equilibrium. Selecting moves the separators so the chosen cell takes area and its neighbours give it (250–300ms, linear or stepped; an instant cut under reduced motion); signals travel only along separators, in discrete steps and never blinking; nothing fades in, floats or overshoots. A signal cell that is a control also carries a label or a mark, never colour alone.
Fails when: the content is n equal items or a single column with no periphery; a signal sits inside the dominant, or the total passes ≈15% as chips or ≈28% with a corner block; separators take several weights; the dominant fills with saturated imagery that outshouts the counterweights.
Signature (do not carry together): black orthogonal bars · red, yellow and blue primaries · white ground.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Mondrian's procedure → a calm note-taking app in muted blues (desktop, 1440 × 900). Pilot sketch C1, re-run against the revised steps 2–4.
1. *Separator.* One weight: a 10px channel (1.1% of the short side) showing a blue-grey ground about 6 L darker than the panels. A gap, not a drawn line.
2. *Dominant.* The editor: 68% of the width × 74% of the height = 50% of the area, centred at 60% / 57% (a large dominant, so displaced only 7–10%). Empty except for the note.
3. *Strips.* Top strip 18% high (breadcrumb, search). Left strip 20% wide, split 1:2.6 into pinned and all notes. Right strip 4% wide (outline minimap). Bottom strip 8% high, split 1:1.7. One doubled channel (10px, 15px gap, 10px) between left strip and editor. No two neighbours within 1.6× of each other.
4. *Counterweights, chip range.* The deepest, most saturated blue fills the top-left cell, touching two edges, 5%: quick capture, labelled, text at 7:1. A warm sand cell on the bottom edge, 2%: unsynced items. A pale teal segment on the right edge, 1.5%: position in the outline. Total 8.5%, three sizes, three shapes, none touching.
5. *Opening.* The channel between pinned and all notes stops 32px short of the left edge. Panels sit at three blue-whites 1, 2 and 3 L apart.

7. *Block range (focus view).* The editor shrinks to 24% and moves to the top right, a quarter of the format from the middle; the deep blue grows to a 17% corner block (today's agenda) touching the top and left edges; sand 8% on the bottom edge, teal 3% on the right; quiet ground 66%. The bigger signal went farther into the corner, not toward the centre.
- *Interaction.* Opening backlinks moves the bottom channel up (strip 8% → 30%) over 280ms; the editor gives the area and the top strip drops to 10% so the dominant stays above 40%.

Result: a pale blue asymmetric workspace with soft channels (corners may be rounded; the procedure is indifferent). No black, no primaries, no hard grid. What survived: the off-centre empty dominant, the single separator weight, intensity small and at the frame.

## Space & composition
- **Unequal cells.** No two neighboring cells are the same size. Neighbor ratios run from about 1:1.2 to 1:4 (1:1.6 is not a hard minimum: Tableau I has neighbours at ≈1:1.2). The largest cell takes 22–53% of the area and is pushed toward a corner, not left of center: in the 1927 CMA work the white near-square (72 × 73% of the sides, 53% of the area) is centred at 59% / 55%, lower right, with all three colours in strips along the top, left, right and bottom edges; in Gray-Red 1935 the biggest cell is top-right (22%).
- **Edge-loaded color.** Primaries sit in cells that touch the frame, often cut off by it. The biggest cell stays white.
- **Lines of one weight.** Within a painting the bars are nearly the same width: 1.6–2.1% of the canvas width in 1927, 1.2–1.9% in 1935, 2.0–3.0% in 1937–42 (max/min ≤ 1.3–1.5). Hierarchy comes from length and spacing, not weight. A doubled line is two equal bars separated by about 1.5× the bar width (Gray-Red 1935: 22 px bars, 33 px gap), and it reads as one "chord." Edges are hand-painted and slightly soft, not ruled.
- **Lines that stop.** Some rules end short of the edge or float free of every other line (two such bars in the 1921 lozenge), so the grid never reads as a spreadsheet. In 1927 and 1935 most bars run edge to edge and the open ends are bars that stop at another bar (the short left-column bar in 1927, the lower-right vertical in 1935).
- **Lozenge.** Rotate the frame, not the grid. The 45° crop activates the edges and suggests space beyond the canvas.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

He used primaries sparingly against what he called non-colors. Measured on the checked works, red, yellow and blue together cover 1.2% (Gray-Red 1935: red only), 6.6% (1937–42), 12.7% (1927: red 7.6, blue 2.6, yellow 2.5) and 14.7% (1921 lozenge: yellow 9.6, blue 5.1, red a chip of about 0.2%). Retry pass 2: Tableau I 1921 ≈13% (yellow 7, blue 6, red 0.5); Composition C 1935 ≈28% (red 17.5, blue 8, yellow 3), so one large red corner block is also within his range. One primary is large and the others are chips; a black plane (1921) or grey planes (26% of Gray-Red) can do a colour's job. The whites are near-neutral to cool, not cream (C* 2–3), and differ by 1–3 L* from cell to cell. In the Boogie Woogies colour takes over: Broadway is 60% white, 24% yellow, 8% red, 6% blue; Victory is 42% white, 13% grey, 16% yellow, 13% red, 10% blue. Broadway's yellow lanes are a constant 2.2–2.5% of the width, and the chips in them are squares the lane's width; larger blocks nest a square of another colour (red with grey, blue with yellow).

## Form, line & typography
No letterforms, but the line language maps directly: right angles only, with weight contrast doing all the expressive work. Use one geometric grotesk at two very different weights, 900 for a single corner-flush headline and 400 for everything else. Seat text against a rule the way color sits against a bar, and never center it. Fonts: **Archivo** (variable width, good for fitting text to a cell), **Space Grotesk**, and **Inter Tight** for dense data.

## Rhythm, motion & time
The Boogie Woogies pulse: MoMA describes blinking blocks moving from intersection to intersection like street traffic. Motion should run along the lines and never float. Show loading as chips moving down a yellow rule in an uneven 3-against-2 beat. In transitions, the bars slide and cells resize. Use `steps(6)` or `cubic-bezier(.7,0,.3,1)`, and never bounce. On hover, fill the cell with its primary in 120 ms, with no shadow and no scale change.

## UI translations
1. **Layout:** Use irregular grid tracks, such as `grid-template-columns: 3.2fr 1fr .45fr 1.6fr`. Never use `repeat(n, 1fr)`.
2. **Layout:** Make the main content a white near-square of 22–53% of the viewport, pushed toward one corner. Put navigation and metadata in thin strips along the opposite edges; a strip can be as narrow as 2–4% of the width (the 1927 yellow strip is 2.3%).
3. **Line:** Draw the rules with `gap` over a black background, not borders. Use one rule weight of about 1.5% of the panel width (e.g. 14 px on 960 px), varying by no more than ±30%. Add one doubled pair: two equal rules with a gap of 1.5× the rule width.
4. **Color:** Put the brand primary in one edge-touching cell only, at 1–8% of the area. Add chips of the other primaries, each under 3%, on opposite sides, and keep all colour together at 13% or less. A grey or black filled cell can replace a colour.
5. **Color:** Tint each white panel 1–3 L* differently, cool-neutral rather than cream, so the field reads as painted, not `#FFF`.
6. **Type:** Set one 900-weight headline flush in the top-left corner of its cell, with line-height .95, sized so the cell edge clips it slightly.
7. **Type:** Align body text to cell edges with padding of 0.75× the adjacent gap.
8. **Motion:** Animate state changes by moving the `grid-template-columns` tracks over 300 ms. The selected panel grows and its neighbors yield.
9. **Interaction:** In a "Broadway mode" for live dashboards, drop the black rules and use yellow lanes (about 2.3% of the width) as dividers. Red, blue and light-grey square chips the lane's width tick inside them as real events. Colour may rise to 35–40% of the surface here, and large cells can nest a square of another colour.
10. **Interaction:** Make one feature card a lozenge: rotate the wrapper 45°, counter-rotate the inner grid, and let the edge clip the colored cells into triangles.
11. **Layout:** Stop one internal rule 24–40 px short of the edge so the system stays open.

## What this artist would reject
- Uniform card grids with equal gutters and identical tiles.
- Rounded corners, drop shadows, glassmorphism and gradients, which all bring back illusion.
- Centered, symmetrical hero sections with a single middle CTA.
- Color spread evenly as decoration, like tinted sections or rainbow tags.
- Curves or diagonals inside the grid. Only the lozenge's frame may be diagonal.
- Rule weights mixed for hierarchy (thick for major, thin for minor); all bars in one work are about the same width.
- Springy easing and floating particles.

## Signature prompt seed
Design this app screen as a Neoplastic composition. Use a cool off-white field split by black bars of one width (about 1.5% of the frame, plus one doubled pair) into strictly unequal rectangles, with no two neighbors the same size. Make one large white near-square, pushed toward a corner, hold the main content. Confine red to a single edge-cropped cell under 8% of the area and add tiny chips of blue and yellow on opposite margins. Set one ultra-bold grotesk headline flush in a corner. Use no shadows, curves or rounded corners. On interaction, the bars slide and cells resize. In live states, yellow lanes carry blinking color blocks like traffic.
