# Emil Ruder — Swiss International Typographic Style

## Essence
Ruder treated the unprinted part of the page as the real material. The white inside an "o", the gap between words and the field around a block are all shapes to compose. Drawing on the Tao Te Ching and the Japanese tea aesthetic, he made asymmetry calm rather than aggressive. He worked with one sans family, mostly in black and grey, and got his contrast from rhythm, texture and interval, not decoration.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (TM 1961 No. 10): one text unit repeated in a column with size stepping down and up in waves; the empty top third is the counterweight; the shape of the whole is generated by the size sequence alone.
- **Colour relations:** black on off-white only; grey values come from type size (small lines read lighter).
- **The procedure:** take one phrase in one style; repeat it line by line while stepping a single parameter (size) down and up in a regular wave; let the repetition draw the shape; keep a large empty field above.
- **Space** (Typography of Order figures): a square module repeated (6 frames of 'buch'; a 6 × 6 field of 36 squares) and content assigned to multiples of it; white channels between black masses are treated as shapes in their own right.
- **Colour relations** (Typography of Order): black and white only; value contrast is set by the width of the white intervals.
- **The procedure** (Typography of Order): fix one square module; place a single word or element in a series of identical frames, changing one property per frame (rotation, mirroring, order); or lay content onto multiples of the module so every page differs while the grid stays constant.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Emil Ruder.** *Compose the unprinted area first, as the largest shape on the page; make every dark thing a grey texture of one family; and produce variety by stepping one variable at a time.*
Process:
1. Fix two constants: one square module (the page as a field of about 6 × 6) and one type family with several widths, weights and a slant. Nothing else may enter.
2. Budget the empty area before any content: 50–90% of the surface, gathered into one or two large open fields (the top third, or the middle half between a head block and a foot block), not spread as even padding. Inverse mode: the dark takes 55% and the light is packed into it.
3. Set the content as compact blocks on narrow measures, anchored to the head or the foot. Parallel content runs in columns of equal width and honest, unequal length under a header that repeats the same split. Each block occupies a different multiple of the module.
4. Vary ONE property per step: within a series, or down a stack of lines, change only size, or only weight, or only width, or only orientation, in regular steps, and let the sequence draw the overall shape.
5. Tune by grey value: squint, and treat each block as a tone. Small, light, tight setting is a lighter grey; a narrow light channel inside a dense dark mass is the brightest thing on the page. Adjust intervals (tracking, leading, gaps) until there are three or four distinct tones and one of them is full dark.
6. For density, repeat instead of adding: set one phrase several times, offset or overlaid until it knots into a texture band, then once clean beside it. Never fill the open field of step 2 to do this.
7. Hue, if any: one small mass (≤8% of the area) against the dark, or the single ink that carries all the type. Never beside a second hue.
Why it works:
- Figure and ground are reversible: an empty field with a deliberate shape and edge reads as a thing, so the dark blocks need not fill the page to hold it. Spread the same emptiness evenly and it becomes margin, and the blocks float.
- Fine detail fuses into tone: a block of small text is a grey, so hierarchy can be built from grey values inside one family. A second family adds a difference of kind, and the tonal scale is no longer readable.
- A sequence with a single changing variable is read as a rule and as rhythm; the eye compares neighbours and sees motion. Change two variables at once and each step looks like a new, unrelated object.
Colour relations: ground 50–90% (or 38% in the inverse mode); full dark ≤25% as the densest blocks; two intermediate tones made by setting, not by tint; optional hue as step 7; a tonal mode with two inks close in value and no extreme also occurs.
Space and line: near-square proportions; module 1/6 of the side; open field ≥1/3 of the height in one piece; equal columns of unequal length; hairline rules span the full width or a column, never a box; display lines may stagger left and right line by line.
Type requirement: one family with a real matrix: at least three widths, four weights and an oblique, even in colour at small sizes, with lowercase that forms clean word shapes; ragged, flush left.
Motion and interaction: motion changes intervals and axes, never position: a row opens its leading or tracking, or moves one step in width or weight (≤300ms, then still). Selection is never value alone: the selected row steps to full dark AND gains one weight step AND a hairline rule at its head (or an indent of one module); the unselected state still meets 4.5:1.
Fails when: the content must fill the surface (data grids, maps, feeds) so no open field of 1/3 survives; items need icons or colour coding to be told apart; text is too short to form a grey (single-word labels throughout); the available family has one width and two weights.
Signature (do not carry together): one sans family in lowercase · a vermilion square or circle on black and white · a near-square page.
Provenance: no museum scan was obtained. Checked from publishers' reproductions (photographs of book pages and spreads) and two secondary web reproductions; posters were small in those images, so shares are approximate and fine type detail was not judged.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Service status page in a dark theme (desktop, 1440 × 900; navy ground, pale blue-grey text, a monospaced variable family because the content is hostnames and figures).
1. *Constants.* Module 150px (a 6-row field on the 900px side; 9.6 across). One family: three widths, weights 300–700, an oblique. No icons, no second face.
2. *Open field.* 64% of the screen is unlit navy, in one piece: the whole area from 150px to 600px high across the right four modules, plus the top band. It has a straight left edge at 450px.
3. *Blocks.* Three regions (EU, US, APAC) as three equal columns 2 modules wide, anchored to the foot, 11, 7 and 15 lines long, not equalised. The header row repeats the three-way split. The overall state sits at the head, one line.
4. *One variable.* Down each column the service names keep one weight and width; only size steps, 22, 19, 16, 14, 12px, by distance from the edge of the network, so each column tapers.
5. *Grey values.* Four tones: the state line at full light; service names at weight 400; latency figures at weight 300, 12px, tight, reading as a mid grey; timestamps lighter still but held at 4.5:1 on the navy.
6. *Repeat, then resolve.* An incident sets the failing hostname nine times, overlaid at 2px offsets into a dense band 1 module high, then once clean below with the cause.
7. *Hue.* One warm mass, 5% of the screen: the incident band's ground. Nothing else is coloured, including "healthy".
- *Selection.* A selected service goes to full light, weight 400 → 600, and gains a 1px rule above it; its row is 44px high.
- *Motion.* On hover a row's tracking opens by 0.02em and its width axis moves one step over 200ms.
Result: a dark monospaced operations page. No paper, no lowercase grotesque, no red square. What survived is the shaped empty field, honest columns, one stepped variable and hierarchy by grey value.

## Space & composition
- **Counterform first.** Enclosed white (counters, tight gutters) reads muted, while open white reads bright. Compose with both temperatures.
- **Grid as a source of variety.** Use one square-module field (the grid seen in 'The Typography of Order' is 6 × 6; the 3×3 field with 24 combinations is *unverified*, never found) and choose among its module combinations, so no two sections share a footprint. The grid gives formal unity while every page differs.
- **Calm asymmetry.** Like the stones at Ryoanji, place 3–5 masses off-centre at unequal intervals. Never mirror.
- **Density versus void.** Set tight, dark blocks on narrow measures and float them in fields that are 50–90% empty (posters measured: Kalligraphie 49%, knüpfteppiche 77%, basler bauten 90% unprinted). On a Typographie page the image sits at the top, the text block at the foot, and the middle half of the page stays empty. Covers can invert this: black ground 56%, white letters packed into it.
- **Parallel columns.** Typographie runs three languages side by side (visually checked): three equal columns, unequal length, under a three-part running head that repeats the column split, so the grid shows even on a picture page.
- **Repeat, then resolve.** Set the same words several times, overprinted, mirrored or offset until they knot into a texture band, then once clean (knüpfteppiche 1964, INSIDON). Density comes from repetition of one word, not from more content.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

Black is the primary colour, grey values do most of the work, and red is the accent. Seen in the works, red has two modes: a single small mass against black (the Kalligraphie square 6%, the Kunsthaus circle 8%), or the only ink on the sheet, carrying all the type (knüpfteppiche: red ≈23% including tints from overprinting). Never red plus a second hue. Tonal two-ink prints also occur (beckmann: khaki type on brown-grey, no black or white). Ruder warned against pushing any contrast to the extreme.

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|
| Paper (warm uncoated) | `#F1F1EA` (sampled: knüpfteppiche `#F0F0E9`, Kalligraphie `#F3F4EE`; aged copies to `#EFEFDF`) |
| Ink (body, display) | `#0E0E0D` (Kalligraphie stroke `#0C0E0D`) |
| Grey value 1 (secondary text, rules) | `#6E6E6A` |
| Grey value 2 (tints, disabled, texture fields) | `#BDBBB4` |
| Signal red (one mass per view, or the only ink) | `#D62813` (knüpfteppiche; Kunsthaus circle `#D54117`, Kalligraphie square `#DF521E`: an orange-leaning vermilion, not a cool red) |
| Deep grey field (inverted panels) | `#3C3A37` (beckmann field, sampled through a book photo) |

The greys are warm steps of the ink, and light, small, tight type *becomes* a grey. The two grey rows remain text-derived: the reproductions were too small to sample real tints apart from anti-aliasing.

## Form, line & typography
- **One family, many voices.** Univers (Frutiger, 1957) gave Ruder a numbered matrix of weights and widths plus a steep oblique. Get hierarchy from width, weight, slant and size within one family, never from a second typeface.
- **Typographic texture.** Judge each text block by its grey value, and compose the page as a pattern of greys.
- **Optical over geometric.** A true circle looks too wide, so trust the eye. Kern by counter area, not sidebearings.
- **Lowercase, flush-left, ragged-right.** A run of words reads as a line and a paragraph as a plane. Confirmed on the posters (all-lowercase title and data blocks); display lines may instead stagger left and right line by line under full-width hairlines (basler bauten), and the light condensed oblique is a real display voice, not just an emphasis.
- **Free fonts:** **Archivo** (variable width 62–125 and weight 100–900, the closest Univers-style matrix), **Instrument Sans** (has a width axis), **Hanken Grotesk** (neutral grotesque text).

## Rhythm, motion & time
Ruder's rhythm is interval-based, like music. Repeat one element at sizes that grow and shrink (the 1961 covers), or change the gaps between equal elements.
- Animate **intervals** (gap, leading, tracking), not position. Rows breathe from 1.1 to 1.5 line-height over 400ms.
- Shift hierarchy by interpolating `font-variation-settings` (`wdth` 75→100, `wght` 300→600).
- Stagger entrances in irregular, fixed steps (0, 60, 150, 270ms). Never bounce: ease out for about 300ms, then stillness.

## UI translations
1. **Layout:** Build on a 6-column grid. Each section takes a different module footprint (4/2, 2/4, 3/3 offset by one), and none repeats on a screen.
2. **Void:** Make the hero 60% empty paper, with the headline block pinned to the lower-left third and the body text 1.5 columns to its right, top-aligned to the headline's x-height.
3. **Counter-aware spacing:** Space display text optically (`font-kerning: normal` plus manual tracking of -1 to -2% on bold widths). Check that the gaps between letters match the area of the letters' counters.
4. **Type:** Use one variable family everywhere, varying only axes and size: H1 at `wdth 75, wght 700, 64px`, labels at `wdth 112, wght 400, 11px`.
5. **Texture:** Set metadata at 11px weight 300 with tight leading, so it reads as a grey plane balancing one black headline.
6. **Color:** Allow one red per view, for the most important state (unread, live, error), never for decoration.
7. **Parallel columns:** Show comparisons as equal-width columns of honest, unequal length. Don't equalise card heights.
8. **Rules:** Separate with 1px Grey 1 rules that end at column edges. No boxes or shadows.
9. **Motion:** On hover, a row's `wdth` goes from 85 to 100 and its gap grows by 4px. Nothing translates.
10. **Interaction:** Show selection as a change of grey value (Grey 1 → Ink, weight 400 → 500), not a filled pill.
11. **Scale jump:** Pair one numeral 8–12× body size with tiny text in the same family. No imagery.

## What this artist would reject
- Centred hero stacks, which put symmetry where tension should be.
- Pairing two or three typefaces for "personality" when one family's widths and weights can do it.
- Equal-height card grids with uniform padding: a monotone rhythm and dead counterforms.
- Colour as decoration: gradients, brand-tinted backgrounds, multicoloured icons.
- Letterspaced uppercase labels everywhere, and geometric spacing that ignores optics.
- Bouncy, scale-pop motion that moves objects instead of adjusting intervals.

## Signature prompt seed
Design the screen as ink on warm paper. Use one variable grotesque and build all hierarchy from width, weight, slant and size. Treat white as material: compact, tight-leaded blocks float in fields that are 60% empty, set asymmetrically on a 6-column grid where no section repeats a module footprint. Compose text blocks as grey values, so a light, dense caption balances one black, condensed headline. Use only black and tonal greys plus a single red accent for the one critical state. Separate with thin rules, not boxes. Motion only adjusts intervals and axes, then stops.
