# Josef Müller-Brockmann — Swiss International Typographic Style

## Essence
Müller-Brockmann treated a page as a measured field. Every element snaps to a modular grid, so meaning comes from position, interval and scale, not from decoration. Inside that rigid system he allowed one large, almost musical gesture: arcs, rotated squares or giant overlapping letters that move against fixed, flush-left Akzidenz-Grotesk type. The structure is strict and the main move is free.

## Key works examined
- **Beethoven, Tonhalle Zürich (1955)**: 127.6 × 91 cm lithograph. Black arc segments whose spacing follows a doubling rule turn a mathematical progression into a visual crescendo. Lesson: derive spacing from a ratio, not by eye. *(visually checked: Stedelijk scan)* Measured along one radius, the **band thicknesses double exactly: 9, 19, 38, 76, ~150 px** (708 px wide scan) with constant thin gaps; dark covers 53% of the sheet. The ground is a **light warm grey**, not white. The small text block uses a **two-sided axis at 27% of the width**: labels ("tonhalle", "leitung", "solist") set flush-right against it, values flush-left from it; the title "beethoven" is only ≈2.4× the body size and hangs from a second axis at 6%.
- **musica viva series (1957–1971)**: Constructivist bars and squares stand in for harmonic structure without imitating musical notation. One poster uses a grid 4.5 fields wide by 4 deep, and its small program text aligns to the letters of the headline. *(visually checked: four musica viva / Tonhalle posters 1956–58, Stedelijk)* Each takes its own colour pair: overprinted blue/green squares rotated 45° with the type rotated with them (Végh-Quartett); black circles on light grey (1958); pink and green halves cut by horizontal bars of unequal width (Strawinsky 1956); black vertical bars below a three-column text block on cream (Rosbaud/Messiaen 1957).
- **der Film, Kunstgewerbemuseum Zürich (1959–60)**: 128 × 90.6 cm offset print. A grey "der" sits behind a white "Film" at the **same size**, the white F overprinting the grey "er", which turns cinematic montage into type layers that read as earlier and later in time. *(visually checked: Cooper Hewitt and Stedelijk scans; the earlier text had the layers reversed)* The headline is 20% of the sheet height and sits in the lower third; the grey word bleeds off the left edge, and the white word starts on the same axis (29% of the width) as the small **red-orange** information text, which is ≈1/13 of the headline height. The ground is a dark warm grey (85% of the area).
- **Schützt das Kind! (1953)**: photography by Ernst Heiniger, 127.5 × 90.5 cm. A car too big for the frame charges at a small fleeing child. Lesson: let the cropping carry the threat. *(visually checked: Cooper Hewitt scan)* Black-and-white photo (black ≈59% of the area), a red top line, and a flat **yellow strip** (≈5%) as the ground the child runs on.
- **Grid Systems in Graphic Design (1981)**: a manual for grids of 8 to 32 fields, including three-dimensional grids. It argues that working to a grid means submitting to universal laws. *(retry pass, visually checked: Niggli cover and four spreads)* The spreads show 8-field grids (2 columns × 4 rows) drawn with different margin proportions, pictogram and alphabet grids, and the *musica viva* poster beside its grid. The cover is its own demonstration: a 4-column × 8-row grid (32 fields) drawn as thin light lines on orange `#EC6224`, gutters ≈1/8 of a column, rows ≈2/3 of a column width, with the two headlines sitting on row lines and the small text hanging from the starts of columns 1 and 3.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Josef Müller-Brockmann.** *Measure the field from the text line, hang everything from one off-centre axis, and let a single figure generated by a ratio carry all the scale while the words stay small and exact.*
Process:
1. Derive the field from the reading line: the gutter is one line of body leading, the format divides into 8–32 fields (2×4 up to 4×8; gutter ≈1/8 of a column), and every edge of every element lies on a field line.
2. Set ONE vertical axis at 27–29% of the width. All text hangs from it and nothing is centred; in label and value pairs the labels end at the axis and the values start from it.
3. Choose one carrier of scale, a word or a figure, never both. If the word carries it, the small text is 1/8–1/13 of its height. If the figure carries it, the title stays at ≤2.5× the body and the figure takes half the format or more. No middle sizes.
4. Generate the figure from one rule: one shape, one parameter stepped by a fixed ratio (each step 2× the last, 4–5 steps) or one fixed transform (a 45° turn, one overlap). It bleeds off two edges, covers 45–55% of the area, and the text of step 2 aligns to its geometry.
5. Colour last: one ground and one or two inks for the whole piece; in a series each item takes its own pair while steps 1–4 stay fixed. A third colour exists only where two inks overlap. The most intense hue is spent on the smallest text, never on a field.
6. Test by ratio: measure any two neighbouring intervals. Each is equal, a whole number of fields apart, or one step of the rule in step 4. An interval that is none of these was placed by eye; snap it.
Why it works:
- Grouping by common edge: text on one axis reads as one object however far apart the blocks are, so the remaining 70% of the width can stay empty or hold the figure. Centre the axis and the page becomes symmetric, and the figure reads as decoration around it.
- A progression at a constant ratio is read as a rule, not as a set of shapes: the eye predicts the next step, which gives direction and crescendo without depiction. Equalise the steps and it becomes a pattern; choose them by eye and it becomes an illustration.
- Scale contrast with the middle removed gives two distances of reading (across the room, in the hand) and no ambiguity about rank. A mid-size third level makes the big thing merely "large".
Colour relations: ground 45–85% in one flat value; figure or second ground 15–53% at the opposite end of the value scale or as a second hue of equal weight; overlap tone where two inks cross; signal ≤5% as small text or one thin strip; a recessive mid-value copy of an element may sit behind it as an earlier layer.
Space and line: fields from the leading; one axis at 27–29%; half the format or more empty or given to the figure; no drawn rules needed (edges are alignments); hint from one work: one block may sit half a field off, never two.
Type requirement: one family, at most two weights, lowercase and flush from the axis, ragged; a face that holds at both ends of a 1:13 jump (tight at display size, open at 12–14px); figures that align in columns.
Motion and interaction: movement runs along field lines only. Entrances use ONE fixed stagger of 80ms (inside the 70–120ms calibration range), at most four items, each move ≤320ms, no overshoot; the ratio of step 4 belongs to space (thickness, interval), not to delay. Mode from one work: on a view change the outgoing headline stays 300ms as the recessive layer behind the new one, then clears. With reduced motion, cut.
Fails when: the content has no small-text tier (a single call to action) or no room for a half-format figure (dense tables, small widgets); the figure is drawn instead of generated; text needs three or more ranks of size.
Signature (do not carry together): concentric arc bands · lowercase grotesque flush left · grey, black and one small red line of text.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Sleep summary in a soft dusk palette (phone, 390 × 844; plum ground, peach and lilac inks, rounded system sans).
1. *Field.* Body leading 22px, so gutter 22px; 2 columns × 6 rows = 12 fields; every card edge on a field line.
2. *Axis.* At 108px (28%). Stage labels ("deep", "light", "awake") end at 100px; durations start at 116px. Nothing centred, including the date.
3. *Carrier.* The figure carries the scale, so the title "last night" is 30px against 14px body (2.1×) and there is no third size.
4. *Figure.* Vertical peach bands for the last 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 nights: widths 9, 18, 36, 72, 144px with 6px gaps, heights set by mean hours slept. It starts at the axis, bleeds off the right and top edges and covers 48% of the screen. The stage rows align to the bands' left edges.
5. *Colour.* Plum ground 52%, peach bands 44%, lilac only where tonight's band overlaps the 16-night band. The one hot coral is a single 13px line: "42 min short of your target".
6. *Ratio test.* The "tips" card sat 30px below the list; snapped to one field (22 + row height).
- *Motion.* The five bands rise in order, 80ms apart, 240ms each. Selecting a night keeps the old title 300ms in lilac behind the new one.
- *Tap targets.* Each band is ≥44px tall in its hit area even where it is 9px wide (padding to 44px, shared with its gap).
Result: a warm, rounded bedtime screen. No arcs, no grey and black, no poster. What survived is the leading-derived field, the 28% two-sided axis and one doubling figure that carries the scale.

## Space & composition
- **The field is the unit, not the pixel.** Divide the format into n×m fields, with the gutter equal to one line of body leading, so text lines and module edges share a single vertical rhythm.
- **Asymmetry anchored to one strong axis.** Text hangs from a single vertical line at roughly 27–29% of the width (Beethoven, der Film), and nothing is centred. In information blocks the axis is two-sided: labels flush-right to it, values flush-left from it. Half or more of the format is often empty or holds only the one geometric gesture.
- **Half-field offsets.** The 4.5-field-wide musica viva grid shows he used fractional modules to create tension. Shift one block by exactly half a field and leave everything else locked.
- **Scale jumps of 1:8 or more.** In der Film the information text is ≈1/13 of the headline height. In Beethoven the type stays small (title ≈2.4× body) and the jump is carried by the graphic. Pair one huge element, type or figure, with tiny, precise detail and drop the middle sizes.
- **Bleed as meaning.** In Schützt das Kind the car runs off the frame because its scale is the message.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

Color is functional and flat: one ground, one or two signals, no gradients. Each poster takes one ground and one or two inks; the concert series changes the pair per concert (blue/green, pink/green, grey/black). Red appears as small information text (der Film, Schützt das Kind), never as a field.

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|
| Ground (print paper; musica viva 1957 cream) | `#EDE5D4` |
| Ink / primary text (Beethoven dark) | `#393A3E` |
| Signal red (der Film info text; orange-leaning) | `#D05239` |

Use at most three colors per screen. The grey is for depth, as a layer behind, never as a disabled state.

## Form, line & typography
His typeface of choice was Akzidenz-Grotesk: lowercase where possible, flush-left, ragged-right, never justified, and never more than two weights. His geometry is pure circles, arcs, bars and squares, built mechanically and shown without illustration. Size and position create hierarchy, not color or ornament.

Free fonts: **Inter** (closest neutral grotesk, use the tight display optical size), **Work Sans**, or **Archivo** for condensed headline weights.

## Rhythm, motion & time
The arcs in the Beethoven poster are a crescendo frozen on paper. In der Film the grey layer is "before" and the white layer is "now". So motion should **reveal the system rather than decorate it**. Elements enter along grid lines on a geometric timing series (80, 160, 320 ms, doubling each step), the way the arcs double. Use linear or strong ease-out curves, no bounce, no springs. A view change works like a montage cut: the old headline drops to 40% grey and stays visible behind the new white one for a beat before it clears.

## UI translations
1. **Layout:** Build a 6-column by 8-row CSS grid in which `row-gap` equals the body `line-height` (e.g. 24px), and size every card in whole fields.
2. **Layout:** Hang all text from one vertical axis at column 2. Columns 1 and 5–6 stay empty except for one geometric figure.
3. **Layout:** Offset exactly one block by half a field (`translateX(calc(var(--field)/2))`) to break the symmetry on purpose. Never offset two.
4. **Type:** Use two sizes per screen at a ratio of at least 1:6 (e.g. 14px and 96px), lowercase headlines, `letter-spacing:-0.03em` on display text, and `text-align:left` everywhere except key/value labels, which set flush-right against the shared axis (Beethoven).
5. **Type:** Set the hero as two words at the same large size (≈20vh): the first in ghost grey `#B6B6A8` bleeding off the left edge, the second in white overlapping its tail and starting on the text axis, as in der Film.
6. **Color:** Use one flat ground, black ink and a single red signal reserved for the primary action and the current state. No tints, shadows or gradients.
7. **Graphic:** Generate one decorative SVG from a rule (concentric arc bands whose thickness doubles, 9/19/38/76/150, as in Beethoven, or squares rotated 45°), place it bleeding off the top-right edge, and let it behave as part of the grid.
8. **Motion:** Stagger entrances along columns at 80/160/320 ms with `cubic-bezier(.2,0,0,1)` and translate only along grid axes.
9. **Interaction:** On navigation, keep the previous screen's headline as a grey layer behind the incoming one for 300 ms (a montage cut), then fade it out.
10. **Imagery:** Crop photos so the subject overflows the frame (`object-fit:cover` with an off-centre `object-position`), with scale doing the talking.

## What this artist would reject
- Centred hero blocks with a centred CTA and a symmetric three-up feature row.
- Rounded "friendly" cards, soft drop shadows and glassmorphism, which simulate material where his work only measures.
- Spacing tokens chosen arbitrarily (13px here, 18px there) and not derived from the leading.
- Justified text, title case and more than two weights in a view.
- Gradient buttons and decorative color with no structural meaning.

## Signature prompt seed
Design this screen in the manner of Josef Müller-Brockmann. Lay a 4×8 modular grid (32 fields, as on the Grid Systems cover; 6×8 on wide screens) whose gutters equal the body leading, and snap everything to it. Hang all lowercase Inter text flush-left from one axis in column two, using only two sizes at least 1:6 apart. Use an off-white ground, black ink and one signal red for the single primary action. Fill the empty half with one rule-generated figure, arc bands doubling in thickness, bleeding off the edge. Set the first word of the title in grey and the second in white at the same size, overlapping like a montage. Animate on a doubling timing series along grid lines, with no shadows, rounding or ornament.
