# Umberto Boccioni — Futurism

*How figures were obtained. Line angles: structure tensor on the greyscale image, weighted by edge strength and coherence; 0° = horizontal, 90° = vertical, positive = rising to the right. R = concentration of direction (1 = all lines parallel, 0 = no preferred direction). Colour: `measure_palette.py`, k = 7 unless noted. Positions are fractions of width and height from the top left. "By eye" is said where it applies. Web reproductions vary a lot in exposure: for Those Who Stay (second version) one file gives L 11–43 and another L 27–74, so relations are more reliable than hexes.*

## Essence
Boccioni lets the direction of the marks carry the meaning: one field, one governing angle per state (tangled for parting, oblique for leaving, vertical for staying), with figures and numbers caught inside it. Objects do not stop at their outline. They continue as force-lines into the space around them, and the space cuts back into them, so the subject is a joint of body and surroundings and not a thing on a ground. Motion is given as one continuous synthesized shape, not as a strip of repeated positions.

## Detailed observations

**The triptych, line direction across the three.** This is the clearest evidence in the whole body of work, because subject, size and date are held constant and only the direction changes.

| Panel | First version (Milan oil) | Charcoal (MoMA) | Second version (MoMA oil) |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Farewells | mean 44°, **R 0.35** | mean 86°, **R 0.20** | mean 34°, **R 0.17** |
| Those Who Go | mean **16°**, R 0.83 | mean **26°**, R 0.83 | mean **33°**, R 0.58 (lower-left field alone: **35°**, R 0.92) |
| Those Who Stay | mean **82°**, R 0.89 | mean **87°**, R 0.92 | mean **86°**, R 0.73–0.76 |

In The Farewells no direction wins: in the second version no 15° bin holds more than 12% of edge weight and none less than 5% (an even share would be 8%). In Those Who Go 35–44% of the weight sits in a single 15° bin; in the lower-left field it is 71%. In Those Who Stay the two bins around vertical hold 69–84%. The parting is therefore disorder of direction, not a "wavy" direction; leaving and staying are 50–65° apart.

The Sackville text agrees and adds a twist. The preface assigns perpendicular, undulating, worn-out lines to discouragement and *horizontal*, fleeting, jerky lines to those going; the catalogue entry beside it says the departing state is shown by *oblique* lines on the left. The measured panels show both: the small Rome study at about 0° (178°, R 0.89, measured below its sky), the first oil at 16°, the second at 33–35°. The two texts fit the two versions, though that link is my inference. The large study has two systems at once: short strokes at about 17° (R 0.54) and long poles falling the other way.

**The Farewells, first version.** Long S-shaped ribbons, each roughly one brush wide, flow from the lower left to the upper right around dark blue-black whirlpools; embracing couples are only notches in the flow. Neutral greys dominate (`#9B9A91`, `#6C7570`, `#3F4441`: 51% together, C 3–6) with short ribbons of brick (`#553021` 11%) and ochre (`#9D6B41` 5%), so colour is carried by thin lines on grey.

**The Farewells, second version.** Underneath is a large S: one ribbon, red, then yellow, then pale cream, drops from (0.87, 0.18) through (0.55, 0.65) to (0.35, 0.85) and returns along the bottom. It is the only continuous line in the panel; everything else is broken. Over it are laid: the locomotive in its own straight black geometry (a front cone at 0.32–0.43 of the width, the boiler profile and funnel at 0.38–0.55, 0.0–0.4 high, seen from front and side at once), pylons and wires in hairlines, and two olive-green clusters of faceted couples to the left and right, kept to one hue (`#4F4223`, `#715D30`). **The number 6943** is upright and unrotated, in a fat slab-serif stencil face, centred near (0.575, 0.375), spanning about 12% of the width with a cap height about 6% of the panel's height. It is flat orange, with no modelling and no tilt, over a dark field, the only thing in the panel with a fully resolved outline. Red-orange of high saturation covers about 2.5% of the panel and collects at three kinds of place: number, buffer disc, ribbon edge. The steam is the lightest area (about `#AA9159`, in an aged warm reproduction), at the top centre, above everyone. The catalogue calls the number, the profile and the wedge front the features that stay fixed in the memory: they are drawn sharp because they are what is remembered.

**Those Who Go, first version.** Nearly the whole canvas is covered by short tapered strokes at 16°. Two bright yellow hooks, about (0.25, 0.55) and (0.75, 0.55), are the only clean shapes, about 0.5 of the width apart, and lean against the stroke direction. Two reddish posts bend in the same wind. Faces appear at 0.3–0.4 of the height only as hue changes inside the strokes. Palette: near-black to dark green 53% (`#0C1614`, `#222F27`), green-grey 17%, navy 10%, ochre 6%.

**Those Who Go, second version.** Two zones split on a diagonal from bottom centre to mid left. The lower left is pure field: bands of blue, blue-green and black at 35°, with a spacing measured across the strokes of about 1.5% of the panel width (10 lines sampled). The lower right goes almost black (median `#20221B`), with the same strokes thinned out to a few blue slivers. In mean luminance the quadrants go upper left 71, upper right 68, lower left 56, lower right 42 (of 255). A row of five or six grey egg-shaped heads runs along a band at 0.28–0.5 of the height, tipped at varied angles, in grey only (`#64716B`, C 6), each built from two or three overlapping circles and crossed by the strokes so that the line of rain passes *over* the faces. Above them a wedge of yellow houses (0.4–0.75 across, 0.05–0.3 down; about 1.7% of the panel is saturated yellow) tumbles with its windows still square. A **red block** is cut off by the left edge at (0–0.055, 0.31–0.42): about 0.3% of the panel, the only red. Numerals I and III are stencilled black on near-black at (0.12, 0.60) and (0.88, 0.81), upright while all else leans; they are nearly invisible and give the class of the carriage. A purple telegraph pole leans about 60°, the one long line crossing the field. Thin pale wires run flat across the top third.

**Those Who Stay, first version.** One hue family: yellow-green from `#314A28` to `#BAC7A0`, L 27–78, C 21–43, one hue covering everything. Vertical strokes cover the sheet from edge to edge with a median spacing of 2.1% of the width (10th–90th percentile 1.1–4.0%). About ten bowed figures are made of the same strokes, only darker and slanting 20–30° off vertical; pixels of value below 0.32 cover 4.2%. The figures have no outline and no ground line; size (large at lower right, small at the top) is the only depth.

**Those Who Stay, second version.** The same curtain of verticals, finer: median spacing 1.1% of the width (10th–90th 0.6–2.0%; 33 lines sampled in 43% of the width). Blue-green, very low chroma in the single-panel Commons file (L 11–43, C 3–8); brighter in others (L 27–74, C 13–22). At the bottom the verticals flare sideways into a hem, as if weighted. The figures, built of cylinders and cones, are bent forward and sit in front of and behind the curtain. The lower-left pair is lighter than the curtain (mean Y about 80 against 37). Two tiny non-conforming notes: a pale crescent (cream) on the far-right head at (0.885, 0.245) and a speck of purple at (0.295, 0.66). No numerals, no red.

**Charcoal drawings.** Farewells: closed loops and interlaced bands, the paper showing as thin light ribbons *between* the dark ones, so the light lines do as much as the dark. Those Who Go: strokes thick at the root and thinning upward to the right, with a slight arc, not ruled; faces and a window-latch survive as small islands. Those Who Stay: 76 vertical strokes counted along a strip at the top (median interval 1.1% of the width); cowled figures come only from bunching and hooking at the top of some strokes.

**Those Who Go, ink (Met).** Three kinds of mark and nothing else: long tapered wedges rising right, solid black polygons with one ragged, combed edge and one clean edge, and thin ruled construction lines. Black solids turn into rays on their lee side. I and III stand upright in slab serif.

**The City Rises.** A 3 : 2 canvas (1.506). The top fifth is a separate register: scaffolding poles and chimneys, thin and vertical (mean 88° in that crop, R 0.28). Below it the mass leans: mean 154–156° (that is, falling to the right at about 25°), R 0.26, the men all straining backward. The main horse fills from about 0.28 to 0.98 of the width; its red (saturated) covers 14% of the canvas, centred at (0.56, 0.49). The harness collar is one blue-black tongue, a curved blade rising out of the horse from (0.48, 0.5) to a tip at (0.485, 0.02): about 2% of the canvas, opposite in hue, the darkest thing in the upper half (`#221C29` against `#9F3123`), crossing from the mass into the vertical register. In the 2.5× detail the strokes, fine hair-like threads, run *across* the collar (nearly horizontal) while they run *along* the curve of the neck, and both edges dissolve into bristles: no outline anywhere. The rest is light and warm (`#DECCB8` 21%, `#C5A78F` 26%): shadowless dust. Near-black is under 6%. Faces are not individualised. Edges of legs turn to pale smoke at the bottom right, so contact with the ground is missing.

**Study for The City Rises.** A clear diagram: horizon/rampart line at about 0.2, verticals above, two or three groups below each drawn as 6–12 overlapping long arcs leaning back 40–60°. The traction line (the rope) is one continuous curve at (0.52–0.85, 0.25–0.4). The centre is left empty.

**The Street Enters the House.** A square. A woman from behind occupies 0.40–0.78 of the width and 0.45–1.0 of the height: the largest, coolest and lightest solid (blue-white `#9097AC`, skirt `#1A3770`), placed right of the centre axis. Her head at about (0.49, 0.48) is near the centre of the canvas. Houses lean inward from both sides: the right block's front edge runs from about (0.70, 0.04) to (0.90, 0.45), roughly 64° from horizontal, and the windows on the purple mass rotate progressively as they come down the side, so the street bends like a bowl. Best fit for a centre that the edges radiate from is at (0.55, 0.05), high on the central axis (radial score 0.18, weak); all-over R 0.31. The construction site in the middle is the hot core (warm 47%, cool 27%; mean Y 123) and the right edge is purple (warm 15%, cool 55%; mean Y 93). Dozens of near-vertical poles in the core are the only regular repeat. Small things refuse scale: horses about 0.15 of the width run over her hip and along the bottom, workers are 0.05–0.1 tall, a balcony at left tips 20°. Rail and ironwork of her own balcony (the dark bar and spiral at lower left) are in the same paint as the street, so the boundary between room and street is missing.

**Simultaneous Visions.** Also a square. Two copies of one face, orange, at (0.31, 0.55) and (0.53, 0.55), turned different ways and joined by one purple-black mass of hat and hair that spans about 0.55 of the width. Above, façades are seen tipped back; at lower left the same street is seen from above with figures as tiny slivers; at bottom right are a large blue jug and bowl in smooth shells at close-up scale. Five or six pale **pointed light-wedges** stab from one zone into another (for example from the crossing at upper right down to (0.67, 0.62), and from the middle to (0.77, 0.75)): each has a wide base outside and a tip inside the neighbouring zone. The scale jump between jug and street figures is about 8 : 1 (by eye). Hue by zone: core 48% warm, lower left 78% cool, lower right 100% cool. R 0.19.

**Elasticity.** Square, no preferred direction (R 0.05). Horse and rider fill about 0.1–1.0 across. Each limb is a shell: a convex segment with one crisp edge and a gradient going to the other, overlapped like scales, one shell repeating across a joint three to five times. The boot is the single dark, near-neutral form, about (0.36–0.6, 0.37–0.66): the still pivot of the picture. Straight lines belong to the landscape only (pylons, chimneys, roof-edges), and they are tilted in as far as the lower left corner, where a black lattice post crosses the horse's path. Core 82% warm against corners 48–60% cool at the top. Grass and road show through gaps between shells: the ground enters the body.

**Materia.** Upright 0.66. Seated frontal figure, hands clasped at (0.50, 0.48), near the centre of the panel, enlarged to about a third larger than the head (by eye) and red. Pale light-wedges fall from the houses at the top, fan out from above the head and pass in front of the face and shoulders, narrow at the bottom: at least seven, spaced 0.06–0.12 of the width. Dark mass at the bottom (55% of area is L 18–32), all light in the upper third, so the background comes forward at the top and the figure recedes at the bottom.

**Dynamism of a Soccer Player.** Near-square. A cluster of curved, shaded facets spans about 88% of the width and 82% of the height, rotating around roughly (0.45, 0.45); edges are most tangential about that point (score −0.26; only the Cyclist's −0.29 is stronger among the single-subject oils). R 0.02: no direction. Straight light-rays come only from the outside: from the upper left corner falling at about 34°, from the upper right at about 65°, and a yellow beam from the right edge. Warm core (62% warm, 20% cool) against cool corners (70–95% cool in three of four, warm 0–2%), and the corners are lighter (mean Y 166–184 at the top against 124 in the core). In the 2× detail every plane has one hard edge and dissolves at the other: across a central lobe the luminance climbs 55 → 224 over about 7% of the width, then falls to 40 in about 8%. Planes are transparent: a blue plane passes over red flesh and turns violet. A calf at (0.45–0.55, 0.5–0.8) is the most legible fragment; the head is a dot; there is no ball outline and no ground line.

**Dynamism of a Cyclist.** Landscape 1.38, small (70 × 95 cm). A dark blue-ochre wedge points left and down; the cyclist's hump (blue disc, centre about (0.70, 0.27)) is at the upper right, the prow at about (0.2, 0.4), an axis about 15–20° off horizontal. Edges' mean 27°, R 0.16, with the most weight between 15° and 60°. The edges are strongly tangential about (0.55, 0.80) (−0.29), that is, arcs swing over a point below centre, like the wheel. In front, a crisp pink-red arc and a long violet blade sweep back from (0.2, 0.07) to (0.01, 0.78): the air in front is drawn, firm, as shapes. Behind, facets open out to the lower right. Black ruled lines (about ten; frame and handlebar) are the only straight dark marks and stop short, unjoined. Strokes inside each facet are parallel dashes set across its length. Field: cream `#B9AE97` 22%, yellow `#BF9E4B` 18%, against blue `#395283` 12%, near-black 15%. No wheel is drawn as a circle.

**Development of a Bottle in Space.** The base is 60 cm long and the whole is 39 cm tall (1.53 : 1); in view 1 the bottle body is about 0.22 of the base's length. The axis stays upright and off-centre. Around it the bottle is peeled: one side keeps the round convex wall, the other is cut away to a concave inside, with the wall running down as a spiral ramp to the plate. In the opposite view the bottle is cut open in section from shoulder to foot, a dark hollow with a full bottle-profile inside it. The dish is half circle, half cut straight, hung out over the base. The table is not a neutral plinth: it rises in three stepped, wedge-tilted slabs with chamfered corners, whose edges run into the glass and bottle walls. Curves belong to the objects, straight lines to the ground, and the two meet along shared edges. One material, two states: brushed light convex, dark concave.

**Unique Forms of Continuity in Space.** 121 cm high, 89 cm long, 40 cm deep (length : height 0.73). The torso leans only about 6° forward of vertical (hip to head, both views), while the trailing leg is about 40° from the horizontal: the speed is read from the legs and the wake, not from a falling body. The two feet stand on separate blocks; block : gap : block is about 1 : 0.94 : 0.86 in view 1, so the void under the stride is as big as each support. Each limb has flame-shaped ridges pulled *backward* (calf, thigh, heel), with a blunt convex leading side and a sharp trailing lip: the wake is the same bronze as the leg. No arms; the face is a cross-shaped blade; the skull is open like a shell at the back. The rectangular blocks and the stepped plate are the only straight edges. Light breaks on ridges, so contour is read on the inside of the form as well as at its edge.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **One state, one governing direction** (both triptych versions, charcoals). Leaving 16–35°, staying 82–87°, R 0.56–0.92; parting has no mean direction worth naming (R 0.17–0.35). The concentration of the marks is as expressive as the angle.
- **Same set-up, different field** (triptych). Three panels of equal size and one story; direction and hue are the only things that must change. Stay: one green or blue-green. Go: blue-black with yellow. Farewells: olive and black with red-orange.
- **The field covers the figure, not the reverse** (Those Who Go, both; Those Who Stay, both; ink drawing). Strokes cross over faces and bodies at the same angle and spacing as in empty areas. In Those Who Stay 1 the figure is only the field made darker and tilted 20–30°.
- **Even density from edge to edge** (Stay 1.1–2.1% of width per line; Go about 1.5%). Line spacing is about 1–2% of the width, varying about 3× around the median; never ruled, never equal. Lines bleed off all sides.
- **Sharp fragments where memory is sharp** (Farewells 6943; Those Who Go I, III, red block, yellow houses; Stay's cream crescent). At most three or four resolved items, each 0.3–3% of the area. Text is upright, flat, stencilled and unmodelled while everything else leans and blends.
- **Accent small and against the edge or inside the dark** (red block 0.3% cut by the left edge; saturated orange-red 2.5% in Farewells; cream crescent far below 0.1%). In the calmest panel the accent is almost nothing.
- **Interpenetration by wedges** (Simultaneous Visions, Materia, Street, Soccer Player). Pale pointed planes have their base in one zone and their tip in another. Five to seven per canvas; they are the lightest values and they cross outlines without stopping.
- **The surroundings lean toward the participant** (Street, Simultaneous Visions, Elasticity). The viewer's stand-in is near the centre and is the calmest, largest, lightest form; façades rotate inward, by more as they approach the edges. The preface wants the spectator "placed in the centre of the picture" and made to take part.
- **Curves for the body, straight for the world; they share an edge** (Elasticity pylons, Soccer rays, Cyclist black rods, Bottle slabs, Unique Forms blocks). In each work straight lines belong to the environment or support, curves to the moving thing. Where they touch, one edge serves both.
- **One hard edge, one lost edge on every plane** (Soccer Player detail, Elasticity, Cyclist; ink drawing in black only; Unique Forms' blunt front and sharp trailing lip). Luminance steps at one side and fades over 7–8% of the width on the other. The crisp side faces the motion or the light, and the lost side joins the next plane.
- **Warm core, cool and lighter rim** (Soccer 62% warm against 0–2% in three corners; Elasticity 82% against 23–27% at the top; Street 47% against 15% at the right edge). Simultaneous Visions agrees at the bottom only; Cyclist reverses it (cool-dark wedge in a warm field), so the rule is temperature contrast between mover and medium, not "warm in the middle".
- **Motion as one synthesized form** (Unique Forms, Bottle, Soccer, Elasticity). No numbered positions. Joint shells overlap 3–5 times inside one limb; the wake is solid and attached. (His own contrast was synthetic continuity against the analytical discontinuity of others.)
- **A still pivot inside the swirl** (boot in Elasticity; clasped hands in Materia; upright axis in Bottle; head and torso only 6° off vertical in Unique Forms; number in Farewells). The most mobile works keep one upright, near-neutral or fully resolved element.
- **Scale by importance, not distance** (Street: horses 0.15 against woman 0.38 of the width; Simultaneous Visions jug to figures about 8 : 1; Materia hands larger than head; Farewells number). The near and far, small and large, lie on one plane.
- **Colour hatched, rarely filled** (all oils; the Farewells numerals are the flat exception). Parallel threads or dashes of two or three hues per area. The direction of the threads is a second drawing: along the form (horse's neck) or across it (collar), and it changes at the boundary of things where no contour is drawn.
- **Evolution between versions** (triptych). Version 1: fluid, two-value, figures nearly lost, high unity (R 0.83, 0.89). Version 2: the same field plus Cubist solids and legible data, so unity drops (R 0.58, 0.73–0.76) while recognition rises. The field came first and the facts were inserted.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

*Context, stated and not carried: Futurism glorified war, held women in contempt and tied itself to Fascism. None of that enters the process, and no propaganda or war text is used as content.*

**Umberto Boccioni.** *Give each state of a view one governing direction, carried by a quiet all-over field that runs through the subject as well; draw the route as the view's only continuous line; then stand a few facts upright, flat and fully sharp inside it.*
Process:
1. List the states the view can be in (three at most) and fix one frame for all of them: same size, same landmarks. Between states only two things may change, the direction of the field and its hue family. Give each settled state one direction, taken from the layout's own axes first: with the upright for what stays (82–87°), along the reading axis or up to 35° off it for what is under way. Any two states sit 50° or more apart. An unresolved state gets no direction at all.
2. Lay the field before any content: the view's own fine marks (rules, row lines, ticks) cover it from edge to edge and run off it, at an interval of 1–2% of the width that varies up to 3× around its median and is never equal. In a settled state at least a third of the marks fall within one 15° band, and 70% or more where nothing else is going on; in the unresolved state no band holds more than about 12%. The field stays in one hue family and one narrow value band, and with its quiet tones keeps 85% or more of the view.
3. Reserve one strip of about a fifth of the height along an edge whose marks take the other axis. Fixed things (navigation, the horizon of the view) go there, and the governing direction gets something to push against.
4. Put the participant, the user's own item, near the centre as the largest, lightest and calmest surface, upright, about 0.4 of the width. Arrange the surroundings on both sides at about 0.15 of the width each and face them inward. Size by importance, not by nesting or distance: up to 8 : 1 between a detail that matters and a crowd that does not.
5. Run the field through the subjects. A subject is the field in a second state (one value step away, its marks 20–30° off the governing direction) or a quiet neutral that the marks pass at unchanged interval; no outline and no ground of its own. Use two line families by role, one for what moves or changes and one for the fixed structure, and where they meet let a single edge serve both.
6. Give every plane one hard edge and one lost edge. The hard side faces the direction of travel or the participant; the lost side fades over 7–8% of the width into its neighbour. Where a route turns, overlap 3–5 segments of one shape instead of spacing out copies.
7. Draw the route as the only continuous line in the view; every other line is broken. It enters at an edge, stops at each fact and leaves the way the field runs. The mover on it stays upright (within about 6°): a blunt crisp side ahead, its trace attached behind as part of the same shape, the next stretch drawn ahead as one firm arc.
8. Insert the facts last: three or four fully resolved items, each 0.3–3% of the area, upright, flat-filled, untilted, on the most solid plane of the thing they identify. One is set at the highest contrast in the view; the others sit near the ground, there for whoever looks.
9. Spend the strongest chroma on 0.3–2.5% of the view, at no more than three kinds of place (a fact, a terminal, the edge of the route), or as one block cut by the edge where the eye should enter. The calmest state gets almost none. In the busiest state keep one near-neutral, upright element near the centre that no state changes.
Why it works:
- Grouping by texture: the direction of many small marks is read at once, across the whole view and in the periphery, before any single item. So the state is known without a badge. Equalise the direction across states and the state falls back onto a label; mix directions inside a settled state and it reads as the unresolved one.
- Salience by oddness: in a field where everything shares a direction and a soft edge, an upright, flat, sharp item is the odd thing and needs no extra size. Tilt it with the field and it joins the texture; raise the count past four and the facts become a second texture.
- Figure and ground: a subject built from the field's own marks cannot be lifted off it, so it reads as being in the condition. Close it with an outline on a ground of its own and the field turns into wallpaper behind a card.
- Good continuation: one unbroken line among broken ones is followed from end to end. Add a second and the eye must choose; break the first and the route becomes a set of places.
- Depth and heading from unequal edges: a plane that is crisp on one side and fades on the other reads as passing in front toward its crisp side. Two hard edges make it a standing object; two lost ones make it fog.
Colour relations: one hue family of low chroma fills the field in each state, and the family is what names the state; the medium is the largest share, either the darkest value (40–55%) or a light low-chroma tone (20–30%); subjects inside the field are near-neutral (about 8%); a second hue as two or three clean shapes set against the field's direction (2–4%); the strongest chroma 0.3–2.5%; mover and medium opposite in temperature, the rim of the view lighter than its core; where two planes cross, a third tone shows; no cast shadow, dark is the medium or the hollow side of a form.
Space and line: interval 1–2% of the width, spread 3×; states 50–65° apart, the oblique one no more than 35° off the reading axis; second register 0.2 of the height; participant about 0.4 of the width against 0.15 for surroundings; lost edge 7–8% of the width; 3–5 overlaps at a turn; 5–7 elements at most straddle a zone boundary, base in one zone and end in the next; free marks taper and arc, ruled lines belong to the fixed structure only.
Type requirement: facts in one heavy weight with sturdy, closed forms that survive low contrast, flat fill, cap height about 6% of the view's height and a block about 12% of its width; always upright and on a solid plane, never along the field, never graded. Loudness is set by contrast and not by size: the same face and size serve the fact that must be remembered (highest contrast) and the fact that is only on record (lowest contrast the screen allows, 4.5 : 1). Reading text sits on the participant's calm surface. Facts state identifiers, classes and quantities, never slogans.
Motion and interaction: static: facts, participant, pivot, route line, second register. May move: the field's marks drift one way along the governing direction and off the frame, slowly, and the mover advances along the route with its trace attached. A change of state re-aims the marks and cross-fades the hue family over 400–600ms while every fact stays where it is. Focus or hover resolves an item fully (both edges hard, flat fill) with a visible focus ring; targets ≥44px. Reduced motion: no drift and no travel; states switch by cross-fade, and nothing is lost because direction, edges and route already say it in the still frame. The state is also written in words.
Fails when: the view is a dense table or long reading text (marks under text cost legibility, and there is no participant); more than four facts must be equally loud (dashboards); more than three states are needed (directions under 50° apart are not told apart); nothing moves and nothing changes state, so the field is decoration; the interval falls below about 4px on a small screen and shimmers; the state is given by direction or hue alone with no words.
Signature (do not carry together): threads of divided colour raining at an oblique over half-dissolved figures · pale pointed wedges driven from one zone into the next · a striding or galloping body as overlapping shells with its wake cast solid.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Library hold-status page, desktop 1280 × 800, pale paper ground with one deep green, bookish and slow (the works are dark, crowded and urgent).
1. *States and frame.* Three states on one frame with the same landmarks: on the shelf (marks at 90°), in transit between branches (marks at 0°, along the reading axis), requested with no copy assigned yet (no direction). The two settled states are 90° apart.
2. *Field.* The page's own hairlines, 4 L from the paper, cover it edge to edge at a median interval of 19px (1.5% of the width), varying between 8 and 38px. On the shelf they are column rules; in transit they are row lines. Field and quiet tones keep about 90% of the page.
3. *Second register.* The top 160px (0.2 of the height) holds the branch navigation, with its rules in the other axis from the field below.
4. *Participant.* The reader's own book is a panel 490px wide (0.38) at the centre, the lightest surface on the page, upright. Other holds stand to its left and right at 190px each (0.15), aligned toward it.
5. *Field through the subject.* The panel has no border. The hairlines run through it at the same interval and it is only 6 L lighter than the page. The route is the one curved thing; the frame is straight; where the route touches the panel, the panel's edge is the route.
6. *Edges.* Each side panel is crisp on the side facing the centre and fades over 90px (7%) outward.
7. *Route.* One unbroken 2px line enters at the left edge (the lending branch), stops at the three facts and leaves at the right (the pickup desk). Every other line on the page is a broken hairline. The book's marker is upright, crisp on its leading side, with a 90px trace attached behind; the next leg is one firm arc.
8. *Facts.* Three: the pickup-by date, the hold number and the shelf code, in one heavy weight, cap height 48px (6% of the height), each block about 150px wide (12%) and about 1% of the page. The date is at the highest contrast; the other two are at 4.5 : 1.
9. *Accent.* The deep green covers about 1.5%: the date, the pickup terminal and the leading edge of the route. On the shelf it shrinks to the date alone. The page title is the element no state changes.
- *Motion.* In transit the row lines drift toward the pickup side and off the frame while the facts stand still. A change of state re-aims the hairlines over 500ms. Reduced motion: no drift, a cross-fade between states. Each state is also named in words beside the date.

Result: a pale, quiet page for readers, with no obliques, no fragments and no rush. What survived is one direction per state in an all-over field, the subject made of that field, one continuous route, and three upright facts.

## Space & composition
- **Formats:** square or near-square for being in the middle of things (Street 1.01, Simultaneous Visions 1.01, Elasticity 1.01, Soccer 1.04); 4 : 3 landscape for states and passage (triptych 1.33–1.36, Cyclist 1.38, City 1.51); tall for a seated mass (Materia 0.66).
- **All-over, no margin.** Every oil runs to all four edges and crops objects (red block, houses, wheels). Empty ground is at most a pale corner crossed by a ray.
- **Two registers of angle.** City: vertical strip (about 0–0.2 of the height) over a 25° diagonal mass. Go 2: flat wires at the top, 35° field below. The second direction gives the first something to push against.
- **Participant in the centre, place folded round it.** Street: figure from behind, head about (0.49, 0.48); radiating edges best fit (0.55, 0.05).
- **Value diagonal.** Go 2 quadrants fall 71, 68, 56, 42; Materia reverses it, dark at the bottom.
- **Depth by overlap and transparency,** never by a horizon (present only as a shifted strip at the top of City, Elasticity, Street). No ground line under the moving bodies.
- **Sculpture:** axis off-centre, base 1.5 × as long as tall (Bottle); length : height 0.73, void of the stride equal to each block (Unique Forms). Base and object are one profile.

## Form, line & typography
- **Line is tapered and arced, never ruled,** in charcoal and oil (thick root, thin tip, slight curve). Ruled lines are reserved for machinery and architecture.
- **Two families:** curves (S-ribbons, shells, spirals) and straight (wedges, poles, rays, wires), with a shared border. The sculpture manifesto makes the straight line the chief means, severe and still alive.
- **Shells:** convex, one crisp rim, gradient to nothing, 3–5 overlaps per joint (Elasticity, Unique Forms).
- **Numerals and letters** (Farewells 6943, Go I and III; the Estorick drawing after The Farewells keeps the numerals): bold slab-serif, Clarendon- or stencil-like, flat colour, upright, about 6% of the panel's height, placed on a dark plane of the machine; low contrast in Go (black on near-black), high contrast in Farewells (orange on black). They state a fact about the scene (an engine's number, a class of carriage). No other words appear in the works checked.
- **Opened form:** the manifesto's call to "open up the figure like a window" is the Bottle's section and the skull of Unique Forms: one wall taken away, the inside finished as a second surface.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- **Frequency:** about one line per 1–2% of the width, spread about 3×, read as rain or a curtain. Direction is the beat; spacing varies.
- **Field flow is one-way and leaves the frame;** accents are still. In Go, strokes rise to the right at 33–35° over still grey heads and a still numeral.
- **Staying = falling:** verticals gather to a hem that flares at the bottom (Stay 2), bowed figures at 20–30° from vertical.
- **Motion by continuity:** no duplicated outline sequence in any work checked; the trace attaches to the body as a ridge (Unique Forms) or as a drawn arc in front and an opening behind (Cyclist).
- **The eye:** Farewells runs the red–yellow S down from the upper right, stops at 6943 and the disc, drops along the ribbon; Go enters at the red block, slides up the houses and is carried off to the right dark; City follows the blue blade up and out of the mass; Street goes in from the sides to the woman and up past her to the sky.
- **Time is stacked, not sequenced:** Simultaneous Visions shows one face twice; Farewells shows an engine from front and side; the text says the picture is the sum of what one remembers and sees.
- **Deliberately absent:** individual portraits (except by fragments), ground lines, horizon depth, outlines in paint, cast shadow, sequential frames, empty margin.

## UI translations
1. **State as direction.** Give each state of a strict study one stroke direction for the whole view: 82–87° for holding, 16–35° for passage, and no mean direction for the unresolved one. Change nothing else but the hue family between states.
2. **All-over field.** Cover the view edge to edge with tapered, slightly arced strokes at one per 1–2% of the width, spread about 3×, bleeding off all sides. Never rule them and never space them equally.
3. **Field over subject.** Let the strokes cross the subject at the same angle and interval as the empty areas; build a subject as the field made darker and turned 20–30°.
4. **Upright facts.** Set three or four identifiers upright in a heavy flat face, cap height about 6% of the view, on the darkest solid plane, while everything else leans and blends. One at high contrast, the rest near the ground (and still at 4.5 : 1 on a screen).
5. **Accent at the edge.** Keep the saturated accent to 0.3–2.5% of the view; cut one block of it with the entry edge, or put it at a fact, a terminal and the route's edge.
6. **One continuous line.** Draw the route as a single ribbon that changes hue along its length; every other line in the view is broken.
7. **One hard edge, one lost edge.** Each panel is crisp on the side facing the motion and fades over 7–8% of the width on the other; overlap 3–5 at a joint.
8. **Curves for the mover, straight for the world.** Live or moving elements take convex shells; rails, frames and supports take straight lines, and the two share an edge.
9. **Mover against medium.** Set the moving element opposite in temperature to its surroundings, with the rim of the view lighter than its core. No cast shadows.
10. **Participant in the centre.** Put the user's own item near the centre as the largest, lightest, calmest form (about 0.38 of the width) and turn the surrounding blocks inward, more toward the edges.
11. **Second register.** Keep a strip of about 0.2 of the height with marks in the other axis.
12. **Still pivot.** In the busiest view keep one near-neutral, upright, fully resolved element.
13. **Motion.** Only the field drifts, one way and off the frame; facts never move. Under reduced motion the field is still and states cross-fade.

## What this artist would reject
- A complete closed figure set against an empty or neutral ground, or an object on a plinth that does not belong to it.
- A numbered strip of repeated positions as the picture of movement.
- The same direction, spacing and hue for different emotional states.
- A symmetrical, balanced, static equilibrium, and a frame read as a window that limits what is shown (the catalogue says he goes beyond what a photographer sees in the window frame).
- Decorative tilt: letters or facts leaning with the crowd; a fact in his pictures stands upright.
- Outlines that stop one thing from entering another; a full rendering of both sides when one side suffices (the preface says drawing the left shoulder after the right is useless).
- Costly single materials for prestige and traditional "sublime" subjects (sculpture manifesto, points 2 and 4).

## Signature prompt seed
Give this view one governing direction per state and carry it in a quiet, all-over field of fine marks at one per 1–2% of the width, never equal, running off every edge and straight through the subject. Keep the user's own item near the centre as the largest, lightest, calmest surface. Draw the route as the only continuous line. Give every plane one hard edge toward the motion and one lost edge. Last, stand three or four facts upright, flat and sharp, one at full contrast, with the strongest colour on under 3% of the view. Only the field may drift; with reduced motion nothing moves.
