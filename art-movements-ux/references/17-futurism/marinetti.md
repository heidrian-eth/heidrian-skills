# Filippo Tommaso Marinetti — Futurism

## Essence
Marinetti made the page a score. The size, weight, spacing, angle and path of a word say how loud, heavy, long, fast and in what direction it is, before anyone reads it. Several strands run on one sheet at once, the dominant one set largest. The disorder is bounded: a quiet, level, mostly empty page surrounds one violent passage, and the scale jump between the two (up to about 35 : 1) does most of the work.

War is the stated occasion of most of these pieces (Adrianople 1912, the Marne, the Isonzo). It is context only. What is described below is weight, size steps, angle, path and white space.

## Detailed observations

**Zang Tumb Tuuum, cover.** Portrait, about 1 : 1.41. Black on one saturated yellow-orange paper stock (`#C18123`, L 59, C 59; the BNCF copy is an orange-red stock). Ink covers 9.2%. Nine lines of type at eight or more different angles, and only one of them, the dateline at mid-height, is level. From the top: author line rising 7°; ZANG rising about 23° in a thin-and-thick display face; TUMB TUMB rising 11° in a solid block grotesque, its cap height ≈12% of the cover height and its width ≈ 80% of the cover; the level dateline at about 3% cap height; then TUUUMB falling about 33°, and three smaller copies falling along the same line at ≈37°. Each small copy shrinks inside itself, from a cap T of ≈70 px to a final m of ≈20 px (3.5 : 1), and they repeat at equal intervals (≈117 px across, ≈90 px down). "Parole in libertà" is bent into a rising arc that passes through the falling line, crossing it once at the bottom of the B. The publisher's block falls 30° in the left corner. The title therefore has a rise, a hit, a level line and a decay, in the order they are read. The right-centre, between dateline and echo, is left bare. No rule, frame, ornament, picture or second ink.

**Balloon page (p. 120).** A circle of 19 capitals (PALLONE 7, FRENATO 7, TURCO 5) with three word gaps, diameter ≈0.46 of the page width, each letter standing on the circle's radius. Inside it only two small level lines, the altitude. Below hangs a vertical string of letters, each turned 90°, from the ring's lowest O to the last text line: the tether is a sentence. Four masts, "TSF" stacked over a vertical line, stand at x ≈ 0.18, 0.26, 0.36 and 0.78 of the page width, with different lengths and different feet, so they read as depth. From each comes one slanting word whose r's are multiplied, at 42° down, 37° up, 43° down and 43° up. One signal starts outside the ring and ends inside it; two run off the axis. Four centred lines of ordinary text above and one below close the picture like a frame. Cap height of the ring is ≈2× the body capitals; nothing else is enlarged. Ink 4.1%. No drawn contour: the circle exists only as letter positions.

**Synoptic table (p. 150).** A box of firm rules with columns 1 : 0.9 : 2.5 (thing in bold condensed, weight in bold condensed, association in ordinary roman). Rows are as tall as their content (190, 335 and more than 230 px). The table runs off the bottom of the page with no closing rule and goes on for five pages. The left two columns are mostly white; the right one is full. A cold commercial form is kept exact and one column is let loose inside it.

**Speeds (p. 44).** Three rows, 80, 95 and 100 km/h, each a label joined by a brace to a paragraph. The engine word is set three times with cap heights of 27, 44 and 67 px (1 : 1.63 : 2.5) while its width stays almost constant (141, 161, 155 px), so the same block gets taller and tighter. The r's are many and small at 80, fewer and huge at 100. The faces go from light lower-case to condensed black capitals.

**Growing word (p. 35).** NASCERE steps 25, 31, 38, 46, 44, 59, 82 px from a common baseline: about ×1.2 per letter, 3.3 : 1 end to end, and the last E is ≈5.5× the body capitals. It opens upward only.

**Scatter, strands and mixed faces (pp. 105, 43, 140, 112, 122).** On p. 105 nine capitals (M U T O H D R A L) wander down an otherwise empty page in two weights and two sizes (54–58 and 87–90 px, ratio ≈1.55), a few tilted 5–10°: ink 1.8%, read as an uneven walk. On p. 140 seven strands are turned 90° and run side by side, each in its own face (bold italic capitals, fat grotesque, light roman, wide italic), so they can be followed in parallel. On p. 43 the same rotation is braced into four stages. On p. 112 four or five display faces share a centred stack of four lines over three thin words set as upright columns of smoke: ink 3.5%. On p. 122 three dates are braced against one triple word at 3× size.

**Text texture throughout.** Body pages keep one measure and a single roman. Long spaces inside the line (1–8 em) replace punctuation; bold lower-case marks sound words; bold italic capitals in parentheses mark manner. Display words enter at 2–5× the body. Half-title pages hold one short condensed heading in the upper middle and nothing else. Display pages are a minority (by eye on the contact sheets, roughly one in six or seven); most are steady text.

**Après la Marne.** Near-square sheet, cream (`#D1C2B2`, L 79), one black. Ink 12.9%; by thirds the densest cell is the middle one (21%), the bottom right is the emptiest (3.5%). 805 separate pieces: the median is 12 px, the 95th percentile 47 px, the largest 482 px. Six pieces (woodcut letters M, M, S, S, the giant V-H-U string) hold 39% of the ink. The letters are long and few-stroked, so they behave as road and mountains: three chained S curves go from upper left down to lower centre, a chain of zigzag M's crosses the sheet from upper left to lower right. Small texts follow the sides of the large strokes at 41–45° ("victoire", "vive la France"). One phrase falls from upper centre to lower right, first 29° and then 42°, shrinking from ≈100 px capitals to ≈25 px letters. Rows of + − × = make ladders along the same slope. Two columns of pasted numbers sit at the top-left and bottom-centre corners, steeply tilted (by eye ≈75° and ≈55°). In the bottom right a level, justified block of invented words with a black brace and a bold condensed label occupies ≈7.6% of the sheet. It is the only passage with a right angle in it. The outer 8–15% of the sheet is empty on all sides.

**Le soir, couchée dans son lit.** Portrait 1.5 : 1. Ink 25%, in a few shapes: the top-left black swell alone is 9% of the sheet (35% of the ink), the six largest shapes 67–70%. Ink by thirds runs heavy at top-left and top-centre (47–50% and 39%), empty at top-right (5%), then spills diagonally to the right-centre (32%) and bottom right (31%), where a small black silhouette of the reader lies, about 12% of the sheet's height, head propped, facing out. Everything else is thrown towards her. The largest word strokes decrease from top to bottom along arcs: one word bends over the whole upper half with capitals getting smaller toward the right and then breaks into black shards. Handwriting, printed roman, stamped lower-case, hatching and dotted rows share the sheet at different angles with no common baseline. Clear islands stay around the small readable phrases (upper right, left-centre, lower left). The quiet words (cool, soft, peaceful) are among the smallest on the sheet.

**Une assemblée tumultueuse.** Landscape, cream, one black, ink 21–24%. Two populations: pasted number tables with strokes of a few px, tilted at many angles and cut with ragged edges, and about twenty woodblock signs cut as solid silhouettes (discs with clock hands, slab capitals, a key-like bar). One diagonal orders the crowd: the two thick bars, "del 100 per 100", "50 mila", "50.000" and the rising number trail all run at 33–35°. The long bar from the centre to the right runs near-level, ≈5–8° down. The right edge holds a column of discs and capitals; the left half is grey with numerals. White is left in the centre right and the upper middle, so the silhouettes stay legible.

**Bataille à 9 étages.** The page is a section through a valley: two straight lines lean in from 9.5° on the left and 13.6° on the right. Nine altitude labels (bold extended, underlined) hang inside the left line, so their left edges step right by 60, 53, 22, 36, 10, 24, 23 and 11 px. The vertical positions are not to scale: 3000 to 2500 m takes 65 px and 2500 to 2070 m takes 304 px, 1200 to 1190 m takes 57 px. Spacing follows the amount of text. Three narrow strips, bold condensed between dotted lines, fall at 43–49° from one level to another. Small zigzag peaks stand outside, left and right. Ink 2.8%. The title is the largest type and is level.

**Décagone.** A decagon ≈240 px across, centred (0.46, 0.54), ten spokes from its corners. The measured directions are 29–41° apart where an even division would give 36°, because the free ends are trimmed to a rectangle (x 0.10–0.81, y 0.16–0.92 of the page). Captions sit beside the ends at three different tilts, about a third of them level. The centre holds the heading, all capitals, four lines. Ink 2.5%. The whole page is thirteen strokes of rule, ten captions and a title.

**Lettre d'une jolie femme.** The page is set as a landscape line and turned 90°. One word, CHAIR, is broken into woodtype at 370 and 376 px, then 223, 126, 70 and 55 px (steps ×0.59, ×0.57, ×0.56, ×0.79), all on one baseline; the thin sentence of ≈18 px runs in the gaps between its letters, so the word frames the phrase. A line of one vowel repeated about fifty times, first capitals and then lower-case, is a decay. Ink 9.8%, all in the outer half of the page; the heading and the inner half are quiet.

**The tin book (1932).** Fifteen metal leaves with rolled edges turning on a tube: the hinge and the sheet are visible objects. Every poem has a leaf of small type, black on bare grey tin, whose facing side carries a plate that enlarges one fragment of it. "A sinistra / rose … A destra / viole" is a small two-column detail in the text and becomes a plate of two fields, orange (`#C86031`, 47% of the plate) and ochre (`#9E6E45`, 46%), with the two words repeated five times on the inner and outer edges; the enlargement is about 4–6× (by eye). Plates use two or three inks plus the tin. "SI no SI no SÌ" goes diagonally down a split orange and green field, with letter heights of 105, 160, 341, 337 and 860 px (the final word 54% of the plate height): the red yes grows 8 : 1, and the red darkens where it overlaps green, because the ink is transparent. "Freddo / Caldo" alternates nine equal rows (five navy, four red) on one dark green, with a border of shivering syllables on three edges; the hot rows are empty to their right. "Libertà – correre" is a yellow field (88%) with letters of one line centred at 50% and a border of f's that gets sparser from the corners. "Pausa" is three even bands of navy, slate and green-grey with one dot and one small word in the lowest. "1 + 1 = 2" at the top and "1 + 1 = 0" at the bottom are black numerals 19% of the plate height; between them a huge yellow ghost word on a tan ground (ΔL ≈ 5) sits under black and orange lower-case. "Volontà" repeats its word twice in orange on bare tin with two black syllables printed over it. The text leaves keep a wide left gutter (≈15%) and run flush to the right edge. Colour is never a background accident; each plate's ground is the subject's temperature, smell or pause.

**Vive la France.** Nearly square, light paper (`#E7E0CE`, 69%, L 89), brown-black ink ≈4–7%, a few grey pasted strips, one blue crayon number. Letters are drawn in a single continuous gesture, thickening only on some strokes; a thin compass arc and curling hairlines link them. About nine strips of figures are pasted beside and inside the large letters, from near-upright columns to ones leaning ≈35° (by eye). One horizontal line (a long row of shrinking u's) and one slanting line of the same words cross near the centre. About a third of the sheet is empty. The printed Marne sheet keeps the giant letters and number columns and adds weight.

**Comparisons.** Carrà packs the surface to the edges with no white ground (ink-dark cluster 16%, ochre papers 30–40%, six hue-value groups within ~15%) and gives it a centre, spokes and rings, text bent round discs, so all sides are active. Soffici keeps the ordinary prose page and interrupts it with found advertisements, bent arcs of type that shrink along their length and one figure built of letters. His 1919 cover uses two inks, blue (14%) and red (13%), on bare cream (60%).

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Size steps are ratios, never decoration** (pp. 35, 44, Lettre, SI/NO). Measured ladders: 25–82 px (3.3 : 1, ×1.2 a step), 27–44–67 (1 : 1.63 : 2.5), 376–223–126–70–55 (≈×0.57), 105–160–341–337–860 (SI/NO alternating; the three yeses alone go ×3.2, ×2.5). Steps of 1.2–1.6 read as growth; steps of 2 and more read as a hit.
- **Extreme span between the biggest and the rest** (Marne ≈35 : 1 largest stroke to text, p. 120 only 2 : 1, cover ≈4.3 : 1 between title and author line). One or six shapes hold most of the ink: 39% (Marne), 45–49% (assemblée), 67–70% (Le soir), 81% (p. 105).
- **Angle gives direction, level gives rest** (cover, Marne, Altissimo, balloon). Rising lines 7–23°, falling lines 29–43°, signals 37–43°; the level line is used once and belongs to the plain fact or the key (cover dateline, the brace block on the Marne, the labels of Altissimo).
- **One diagonal per sheet, shared by several elements** (assemblée 33–35°, Marne ≈42°, cover echo 33–37°). Many angles appear, but the main ones agree.
- **Shrinking along a path shows distance or decay** (cover echo 3.5 : 1 inside a word, Marne phrase 4 : 1, Lettre, Le soir's first arc, SI/NO reversed for growth).
- **Letter count and width trade with size** (p. 44: width held within 141–161 px while height goes 27 to 67; NASCERE).
- **Parallel strands in different faces, the dominant set largest** (pp. 140, 43, and the 1913 manifesto). Face says which strand, size says which leads.
- **A cold form is left intact and filled with something loose** (p. 150 table, p. 187 and 77 text lines, Altissimo's level labels, the Marne key block, the tin book's freddo/caldo rows).
- **Paper does the work of punctuation** (pp. 44, 77, 105; Décagone 2.5% ink; Altissimo 2.8%; balloon 4.1%). Coverage goes 2–4% in the diagrams, 4–7% in typographic pages, 9–13% on cover and Marne, 21–25% on the two dense fold-outs.
- **Figures are made of letters or rules, not drawn objects** (balloon ring, masts, zigzag M's, decagon, valley). Pictures appear as flat silhouettes only (Le soir's reader, assemblée's keys and discs).
- **Clear margin around loud passages** (Marne 8–15% empty edge, cover's bare right-centre, Le soir's top-right third 5% ink).
- **Text and its enlargement stand back to back** (tin book): the plate takes one fragment and enlarges it about 4–6×, with a ground colour of its own.
- **The generative rule seen in the works:** take one ordinary page of one face; decide how loud, fast, distant and directed each word is; set size, weight and angle from that, with at most one or two things far larger than the rest; put one set of elements on one shared diagonal; leave one line level; pay for it with white.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

*Context: Futurism, which Marinetti founded and led, glorified war, declared contempt for women and tied itself to Fascism. None of that is carried here, and no manifesto, war or propaganda text is used as content.*

**Filippo Tommaso Marinetti.** *Hold the page steady in one level face, then score it: bind weight, size, width and position each to one measured property of the content, on stated ratio ladders; give one bounded passage most of the weight along one shared direction, and pay for it with empty field.*
Process:
1. Set the steady state before any emphasis: one text face, one size, one measure, every line level. It fills at least five views in every six or seven and the larger part of any view. Marks cover 2–7% of an ordinary view. Inside a line, separate units with a gap of 1–8 em, not with a rule or a mark; between sections, leave a nearly empty view holding one short heading.
2. Write the score. List the ways the items really differ (how forceful, how much, which stream, how far along) and assign each to exactly one variable, in this order of use: weight for force, width for stream or compression, size for amount, position for place in a sequence. A variable with nothing assigned stays constant across the view. No variable is moved for looks.
3. Take every size from one stated ladder per view. A step of ×1.2–1.6 reads as continuous growth (seven sizes at ×1.2 span about 3.3 : 1); a step of ×2 or more, or ×0.57 going down, reads as a hit or a drop. As a block grows taller, hold its width within about 15%: fewer, narrower, heavier characters in the same box.
4. Allow one loud passage per view, and only in about one view in six or seven. Inside it one to six shapes carry 40–70% of the view's marks, the largest 4–35 times the body size. Total marks stay at 9–13%, with 25% as the ceiling. Keep an empty margin of 8–15% of the short side around the passage and leave one whole region of the view (a third or a ninth) under 5% marked.
5. Give the passage one direction. The members of its sequence step along one straight line of offsets, each displaced from the last by the same vector, every word still level: a shallow line (under 25° from level) for a build, a steep one (30–45°) for a fall or a fading. Size falls 3.5–4 : 1 along the line for distance or decay and rises for approach; equal intervals, about three, make a beat. The plain fact (date, unit, key) stays off the line: level, justified, at body size, about 8% of the view, in the emptiest corner, tied to its label by one bracket.
6. Run concurrent streams side by side as parallel columns, each in its own cut of the family (weight, width or slant), two or three as a rule and five at most. The cut says which stream; size says which leads, and only the leader is enlarged.
7. Where the content is tabular, keep the form strict and loosen exactly one part: ruled columns near 1 : 0.9 : 2.5, rows as tall as their content, the narrow columns in the heavy narrow cut and almost empty, the wide column in the text face and full. Where a relation needs a figure, build it from the labels' own positions and at most a dozen rules, so that where a label sits is the quantity; such a view carries 2–4% marks. If spacing follows the amount of text and not the scale, say so on the figure.
8. For detail, take one fragment of the steady text and show it beside its source 4–6 times larger, alone, on a ground whose colour states the fragment's property: one field (70–90% of the panel) or two near-equal fields for a two-sided fact, with two or three inks at most.
Why it works:
- Salience by oddness, after adaptation: an enlargement registers against the steady text the eye has settled into. Equalise the views (something loud on each) and loudness becomes texture; remove the steady state and nothing has anything to be larger than.
- Weight and size are seen before a word is read. When each is tied to one property, the reader learns the code within a view and reads amount and force from the texture; when one variable also moves for decoration, the code is broken and the reader stops trusting any of it.
- Scale reference: a ratio is judged against the neighbour. Steps of 1.2–1.6 fuse into one growing thing; a step of 2 breaks the series, so it marks an event. Mixed ladders in one view remove the reference.
- Grouping by common direction: items displaced by one vector fuse into a single path that reads in order with nothing moving. With several unrelated directions there is no path, and with everything on the line there is no level fact to measure the fall from.
- Visual weight: when a few shapes hold most of the marks, the eye has an order of entry. Spread the same marks evenly and the view has to be searched.
Colour relations: one ground at 55–80% of the view and one near-neutral darkest ink at 9–25%; variety comes from weight, never from a second hue. In the coloured mode the ground carries the meaning as whole fields (one at 70–90%, or two at about 47% each), the meaning-bearing word is the highest-chroma and smallest colour (2–16%), a repeated or background word may sit within 5 L of its ground, and overlapping inks darken. Emphasis is never by colour alone.
Space and line: marks 2–4% in figure views, 4–7% in text views, 9–13% in a loud passage, 25% ceiling; 40–70% of them in up to six shapes; margin 8–15% of the short side; one direction per view; key block about 8%; table columns 1 : 0.9 : 2.5; lines are few and structural (brackets, table rules, the dozen strokes of a figure).
Type requirement: one family with a wide range of weight and width (light to black, condensed to extended) beside one plain text face, or a variable face with both axes; equal-width numerals; emphasis by weight first, width second, size third; tight default spacing, spread tracking only where it states a stretch; every word upright and level, and the smallest piece never below body size.
Motion and interaction: everything above is static, and the path, the ladder and the streams read complete in one still frame. What may move: the members of the passage may arrive once, in reading order, along the shared direction (80–120ms apart, no overshoot, no loop); a live value may change size only by whole ladder steps; the fragment of step 8 may grow from its place in the source and return to it. Selecting a stream gives it the leader's size. Under reduced motion all states are simply present. Nothing shakes, rotates or repeats. Reading order for assistive technology follows the leading stream, then the others, then the key, and each coded property is also given in words.
Fails when: every item has the same rank, so there is nothing to score; forms and long reading, where each field or paragraph needs equal weight; localised or user-entered text whose length breaks the held width; more than five concurrent streams; exact comparison of amounts (text size is judged far less precisely than a bar on an axis); alert-heavy views where many things claim the one loud passage; small viewports where the span from body to largest cannot reach 4 : 1.
Signature (do not carry together): words set at many angles on one sheet · one letter multiplied to spell a noise · giant wood letters used as roads and mountains.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Household energy summary, phone 390 × 844, a pale sage ground with deep green ink and a rounded humanist variable sans; domestic, soft and slow (the works are black, hard and loud).
1. *Steady state.* One face at 16/24px on one 326px measure with 32px side margins. Rows are parted by 24px of ground, with no rules; label and value are parted by a 3 em gap, with no colon. An ordinary view carries about 5% marks.
2. *Score.* Four real differences, four variables: over or under the plan's allowance → weight (400 or 800); electricity or gas → cut (upright or italic); kWh → size; month → position. Width is kept for step 3. Colour and case are assigned nothing and stay constant.
3. *Ladder.* ×1.2: 16, 19, 23, 28, 33, 40, 48px. The seven days of the week take a size by usage band, each in a 40px column; the tallest figures use the condensed end of the width axis so the column still holds.
4. *Loud passage.* Only the monthly overview has one: the month's total at 112px (7 : 1 over the body), heavy when over the allowance. Its three numerals are about half of the view's marks; the view totals about 10%. A 48px empty margin (0.12 of the width) surrounds the passage, and the upper right third holds nothing.
5. *Direction and key.* The three earlier months step away from the total by the same offset, 72px right and 40px down (29°, a fading), at 48, 33 and 23px, every figure level, each with its month beneath at body size. The tariff and billing date stay off the line: a level justified block 326 × 80px (7.9% of the view) at the foot, bracketed to the label "your plan".
6. *Streams.* Electricity and gas run as two columns. Electricity leads at 23px upright; gas follows at 16px italic.
7. *Strict form.* The appliance table is ruled at 74 : 67 : 185px (1 : 0.9 : 2.5), rows as tall as their notes; appliance and kWh in the heavy condensed cut, the note in the text face. No figure is needed, so none is built.
8. *Fragment.* Touching "standby 38 kWh" in the table opens a panel beside it with that phrase alone at 80px (5×), on one field at 85% of the panel whose tint is the warmer of two when the value is over the allowance. The words "over allowance" are also written.
- *Motion.* On first view the three earlier months arrive in order, 100ms apart, and stop. Reduced motion shows them in place. A screen reader hears total, earlier months, plan, then the table.

Result: a pale, soft, level bill with nothing tilted, no repeated letters and no black. What survived is the steady text, one variable per property, one ladder, one heavy passage paid for with empty ground, one shared direction and one level key.

## Space & composition
- **Two densities.** Steady pages and one or two eruptions, never an even scatter (by eye, about one display page in six or seven).
- **Ink budgets.** Diagram pages 2–4%, typographic pages 4–7%, cover 9%, Marne 13%, Le soir and assemblée 21–25%.
- **Concentration.** 40–70% of the ink in six shapes; the remaining pieces have a median of 10–16 px on ≈1500 px sheets.
- **Diagonal flow.** Mass enters top left or centre left and exits bottom right (Le soir, Marne, SI/NO), or falls left to right in the lower half (cover).
- **Anchors.** One level block or line and one plain heading, usually at top or lower right.
- **Edges.** Books keep a wide inner margin and the page's measure; loud plates on tin go to the edge on all four sides.
- **Nested shapes.** Text inside the mouth of a letter (Lettre, CHAIR), inside a bar (assemblée), inside a ring (balloon).

## Form, line & typography
- **Faces seen:** roman text; fat grotesque; condensed grotesque; extended bold; bold italic; light condensed; slab and wood display; high-contrast fancy (ZANG, CHAIR); hand lettering; handwriting (Le soir); typewriter-like figures. Four or five display faces on the most mixed page seen (p. 112), usually two or three.
- **The 1913 text (reprinted in the BNCF copy) prescribes** italics for rapid sensations, bold for violent sounds, and up to 20 faces and 3–4 inks on a page: "tre o quattro colori diversi d'inchiostro" (Italian text). *The pages measured stay well under the maximum: black only, about five faces at most.*
- **Widths and weights, not decorative forms.** Emphasis is by weight first (block grotesque against roman), width second, size third.
- **Line is type.** Rules are few: braces, table rules, masts, valley sides, hairlines in the drawing. Everything else is made of letter strokes, including + − × = used as ties.
- **Case.** Capitals for hits and names; long runs of a single lower-case letter for duration (r, e, u, o); mixed-case rises inside one word (POESIA, NASCERE).
- **Spacing.** Letters stay tight and upright inside the body; spread tracking appears only where it means stretch (the tin book's LIBERTÀ, MARINETTI, pausa).
- **Type to read beside these forms:** a sturdy condensed grotesque, a fat grotesque and one plain roman (for study, such as Archivo Narrow, Archivo Black and Old Standard TT).

## Rhythm, motion & time
- **Time runs along the path.** Rise, hit, level, echo (cover); walk (p. 105); drive and turning (Marne's S curves).
- **Duration is letters.** Repeated r, e, u; a word of about fifty e's is a long note. Acceleration is the number of small letters falling while the size rises (p. 44).
- **Simultaneity.** Parallel strands, crossing arcs and several baselines, so more than one thing is under way; the eye is expected to jump, not to follow a single line. The dominant strand still goes first.
- **Equal repeats as beat.** Three echo words at equal intervals on the cover; nine equal rows of freddo/caldo; five repeats each of rose and viole.
- **Pause is a page.** Blank half-titles and the tin book's three-band plate; long spaces inside lines.
- **Turning.** The tin leaves rotate on the tube; the fold-outs open to a size beyond the book's measure (Marne opens to ≈1.8 × the page width).
- **Reading as performance.** The frontispiece portrait plate (tin book) shows Marinetti declaiming; the pages are cues for voice.

## UI translations
1. **Steady first.** Most screens are one text face on one measure with 2–7% marks; a loud screen is about one in six or seven, never two in a row.
2. **One variable, one meaning.** Declare in the kit what weight, width, size and position each stand for. Weight states force or urgency, size states amount, cut states stream, position states order. Nothing changes weight or size for decoration.
3. **Sizes on a stated ladder.** ×1.2–1.6 a step for a value that grows (25 → 82 over seven sizes in the source), ×2 or more for an event. One ladder per screen.
4. **Taller, not wider.** When a figure is enlarged, hold its box width within 15% and move along the width axis instead (27 → 67 high in 141–161 wide in the source), so layout does not jump when a value changes.
5. **Few shapes hold the weight.** In a hero passage, one to six glyph shapes carry 40–70% of the marks; the rest stays at body size. Total marks 9–13%, 25% at most.
6. **Empty margin as the price.** Keep 8–15% of the short side clear around the loud passage and one region of the screen nearly bare.
7. **One shared direction.** A history, an echo or a countdown steps along one line of equal offsets, shrinking 3.5–4 : 1 toward the oldest, every word level. A strict study may tilt a single display word, rising 7–23° or falling 29–43°; body text, controls and labels never tilt.
8. **The level key.** The plain fact (date, unit, legend) is one level, justified block at body size, about 8% of the screen, bracketed to a heavy label, in the emptiest corner.
9. **Parallel streams.** Concurrent threads (channels, speakers, sensors) run in side-by-side columns, each in its own cut; the leading one is larger. Two or three as a rule.
10. **Strict table, one free column.** Rule the table firmly at about 1 : 0.9 : 2.5; keep the two narrow columns heavy, condensed and sparse, and let the wide column run as ordinary prose. Rows grow with content and the table may continue across views with no closing rule.
11. **Gaps for punctuation.** Replace separators, bullets and dividers inside a row with gaps of 1–8 em.
12. **Figures out of labels.** Build a small diagram from the labels' positions plus a few rules (a ring of labels, stepped levels, ten spokes), with 2–4% marks and no drawn illustration. State when spacing is not to scale.
13. **Fragment and enlargement.** A detail view shows one phrase from the list 4–6× larger on its own ground, facing the text it came from. In a strict study the ground may be a full saturated field (orange `#C86031` beside ochre `#9E6E45`, or yellow `#C29410` at 88%) with black type.
14. **Black on one stock.** In a strict study use one ink (`#282624`) on one ground (`#D1C2B2`, or `#C18123` for a cover) and get all variety from weight.
15. **Still and accessible.** Every state reads complete without animation. An entrance runs once in reading order; reduced motion shows the final state. Each coded property is also written in words, and the smallest type is the body size.

## What this artist would reject
- A page where every block has one size, weight and distance from the next.
- Symmetrical, centred, classical title pages with ornament and one decorated initial.
- Emphasis by colour alone while the type stays flat.
- Tilting everything equally, so there is no level line to fall from.
- A crowded surface with no white (Carrà's answer, not his; his densest sheet is 25% ink).
- Enlargements that do not follow a ladder, or effects that repeat on every page.
- Illustration laid beside text as a separate picture, where letters could carry it.

## Signature prompt seed
Set the product in one plain text face on one measure and keep most screens that quiet, with marks on a twentieth of the surface. Then score it: weight states force, size states amount on one stated ratio ladder, cut states which stream, position states order, and nothing varies for looks. On about one screen in six, let one to six shapes carry half the marks, up to thirty-five times the body size, inside a wide empty margin. Step that passage's sequence along one shared line of equal offsets, shrinking toward the oldest, every word level. Keep the plain fact as one level justified block in the emptiest corner. Run concurrent streams as parallel columns in different cuts. Keep tables strict with one free column. Enlarge one fragment beside its source on a ground that states its property.
