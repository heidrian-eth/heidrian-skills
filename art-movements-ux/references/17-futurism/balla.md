# Giacomo Balla — Futurism

## Essence
Balla keeps the viewer still and lets the subject pass, then paints every position at once on one fixed surface: a limb becomes a fan, a run becomes a row, a flight becomes a drawn path with the birds repeated along it. Between 1912 and 1914 the body drops out and only the law of the movement stays (an interval, a path line, a wedge, a translated arc). In the light studies the same repeat carries colour instead of a body, and in the speed panels the forms run past the edge onto the frame.

## Detailed observations

**Dynamism of a Dog on a Leash.** Seen from above, close, at dog height. The owner is cut at the hem by the top edge; only skirt and shoes remain. Two dark masses: the skirt and shoes fill the upper right (x 0.31–0.95 of the width, y 0–0.45, about 19% of the area under L-value 45 of 255), the dog the lower left (x 0.06–0.70, y 0.47–0.81, about 8%). Everything else, roughly 65%, is pale ground. The two masses sit on a diagonal and are joined only by the leash, four chains of white double-dots that bulge as arcs and cross in the middle; the space they sweep is filled with rows of fine dashes, so the sweep reads as a transparent net. Where the net passes over the dark skirt it turns white, over the pale ground it turns grey-violet: the line always takes the opposite value. The bodies are single, opaque and unrepeated. Only the ends multiply: the tail shows about 8 crescents in two bundles (up and down), the front and rear feet open into two fans of about 10–12 each (by eye), the ears into 3–4, the shoes into a scalloped row of about 8–9 along the hem. Opacity falls outward: each fan goes from near black at the joint to a brown-violet veil dotted with tan dashes at the rim. A blue halo of about 1% of the width runs along the back of the dog. The ground is streaked with parallel lilac, cream and grey-green lines that are nearly level at the top (2.6° at the top left) and steepen to 25.8° at the bottom left, but stay at 7–11° along the right edge: a slow fan that opens toward the lower left. The dog faces left, against a ground that slopes down to the right. Absent: horizon, sky, owner's face, cast shadow. The frame (original, beige) carries one line of painted brown dashes along all four sides, the same dash that dots the bodies, so the mark of movement continues onto the moulding.

**Girl Running on a Balcony.** Square. Built only from separate, roughly square dabs of about 20–28 px on 1402 (1.4–2% of the width, roughly 2 cm), laid with a pale ground showing between them. The figure is repeated across the whole width: feet and boots make a row of about 8 positions at centres 160, 322, 484, 642, 817, about 1000, 1174, 1302 px, so a nearly constant step of about 160 px (0.115 W; individual gaps 128–186). Heads run along the top as five to six red-orange clusters, wider and looser spaced (gaps 120–250 px) than the feet. The railing is a second, independent beat: uprights about every 135 px (0.096 W, read from the blurred image), drawn through the body rather than behind it, so step : bar is about 6 : 5 (five steps span six bars) and the two rows drift in and out of phase. Horizontal bands: heads 0–0.16 of the height, dress and railing 0.16–0.60, legs 0.60–0.80, boots 0.80–0.93, floor below. Contrast rises downward: SD of L is 19 in the head band, 21 in the railing, 24 in the legs, 30 in the boots. Chroma falls (36 to 29) as contrast rises. Blue is 34% of the railing band and 5% of the head band. Colour also drifts along the run: warm pixels in the boot band fall from 26% at the left to 8–12% at the right, and the dark dabs go from slightly yellow (b* +7) to blue (b* -7), while the dark share rises (36% to about 50–69%), so the figure gathers weight toward the direction of travel. Dark dabs are often an orange or red core inside a blue ring. No contour lines anywhere; nothing is the "real" girl. Painted narrow strips at left and right act as window jambs, the only still parts.

**The Hand of the Violinist.** The picture is an upside-down trapezoid: full width at the top (1707 px), about 605 px at the bottom, a ratio of 2.8 : 1. The sides lean 24.8° (left) and 23.7° (right) from vertical, so the whole surface is one wedge opening upward, the shape of a fan. The neck of the violin runs as a pale strip at about 46° from upper left to lower right and is repeated four or five times with the fingers stacked above it. Nothing has a contour: every form is hatching of short parallel strokes that turn direction with the form, on the hand across it, on the sleeve along it, in the background nearly vertical (about 70–75°), with small crescents on the yellow part. The hand's four to five positions are told by changes in hatch density and value, not by outlines. The background keeps two clean horizontal bands at the top, which hold still. The range is warm and narrow: yellow #EFBE49 (14.7%), ochres, browns, near black #353333 (20.8%); a thin cool green-blue appears only in the sleeve at lower right. Absent: face, body, room, bow drawn as an object.

**Street Light.** An upright picture 1 : 1.54. The lamp sits at about (0.49, 0.34). Around it thousands of V strokes point back at the source; they are white, pink and yellow near it, then yellow with green, then orange-red and violet, then green-black. Measured in rings of 100 px (0.08 W) the mean L falls steadily: 90, 87, 74, 67, 60, 51, 47, 34, 31, 32, 29, 26, 17. Chroma peaks one step outside the core (C 14 in the core, 29, then 36 at ring 3) and then falls to 14 at the edge, so the brightest place is not the most coloured one. Red strokes are rare near the lamp (2–4% of pixels) and common at 0.4–0.5 W away (10–13%). V size grows with distance: short and crowded at the lamp, long and spaced at the rim. The halo is a closed keyhole shape (wide circle above, narrower below) with a hard blue outline; the corners outside are dark green swirls, about 10–15% of the surface. The moon is in the upper right, a crescent about 0.19 W wide, made of the same marks but overwhelmed and outside the hottest rings. The pole is a thin diagonal-striped line, slightly off the axis. Absent: street, ground, people, cast shadows.

**Swifts: Paths of Movement + Dynamic Sequences.** Horizontal. Two systems on one surface. The first is six to seven pale granular lines about 8–10 px thick (0.6% W) that cross the whole width, swing as long waves and never touch the frame at the same height. Crest to trough is 55–140 px (5–12% of H) and half a wave is 280–610 px (20–43% W). At x = 600 the lines are stacked at gaps of about 160, 185, 190, 120 and 140 px, so they run roughly parallel and 0.11–0.17 H apart. The crests of neighbouring lines fall at similar places (a crest near x = 555–600 in three of them), so the group breathes together but does not repeat. The second system is the bird, a black body with two curved wings, repeated along paths that the lines cross as a separate layer; the dense lower rows overlap to the point that the gaps between wings make a second set of small pale lens shapes. Behind both, the building (gutter, blinds, slanted roof boards, a green post) is static, straight and narrow, with verticals leaning a few degrees. Contrast goes by place: the upper left is broad, dim and architectural, the lower right is dense and black-and-white. Colour is nearly all brown to black (#26211F 24%, #493A35 22%) with ochre and a little blue-white. No outline around any single bird is the "main" one.

**Lines of Movement and Dynamic Succession.** The same in five strokes: four long white lines on brown ground, a row of birds with bodies at about x = 660, 835, 990, 1130 (intervals 175, 155, 140 px: the pitch shortens along the flight by about 11% a step), plus wings as paired crescents above and below each body. The white lines are the lightest and most even marks in the sheet, lighter than any bird (L 84 against 14).

**Iridescent Interpenetration No. 7.** A square field of three rows, 432, 438 and 440 px high (thirds within 2%), crossed by two ragged pale seams. Each row is made of slim upright lozenges and triangles that share apexes at the seams: an apex in one row lands in the middle of a base in the next. Apexes on the upper seam sit at 131, 268, 403, 539, 676, 817, 959, 1118, 1283 px: pitches 137, 135, 136, 137, 141, 142, 159, 165. So the module is constant for the first two thirds and then widens by about 20% on the light side. A lozenge is about 136 px wide and 870 px tall over two rows, 1 : 6.4. Each lozenge is a nested set: a core stripe, two flanks, and the outer triangles; the blue core widens from 41 px at the left to about 72 px at the light side while the flanks thin into outlines. Colour runs left to right as one ramp: mean L by column 20, 22, 27, 36, 49, 69, 83, 89, 91, 90 while mean C goes 21, 22, 23, 31, 33, 24, 17, 12, 9, 8, peaking just before the step in light. The steepest change (about 20 L per column) is between 0.45 and 0.60 of the width. At the far right the pattern is 90 L and 8 C: still there, hardly visible. The dark neighbours are deep red, deep blue and grey-green; the light ones pink, pale blue, yellow-green, warm white (hue order kept, only value and chroma move). A flat warm grey frame with bowed edges follows the same triangle logic. Signature "Futurballa" in yellow at lower right.

**Iridescent Interpenetration No. 4.** Two hues plus cream: blue #679EB1 (24.5%), yellows from lime to ochre (together about 40%), cream #DAD398 (20%). 15 apexes of the same colour sit at a pitch of 77.9 px with SD 2.3 px (3%) across the full width. Rows at y = 0, 137, 250, 421, 568, 687, 850: heights 137, 113, 171, 147, 119, 163, no two equal, with a strong seam at 421, near the middle. Full triangles are about 39 px wide and 171 tall (1 : 4.4). Apexes flip direction at each seam and every second row offsets, so figure and ground trade places and no row has a background.

**Iridescent Interpenetration No. 13.** A hexagon lattice on near black (#34393B, 43%): tall hexagons (47 × 130 px) in columns every 88 px (0.145 W), linked by diagonal ribbons and small squares. Hue goes by horizontal band (yellow, green, blue, red from top to bottom), value by column inside each band (the hexagons alternate light and dark within the same hue). The lattice is the same throughout; only the colour is the event. Squared pencil construction lines remain visible.

**Study for Iridescent Interpenetration.** A quarter fan from a lower right corner: about 13 red spikes at the top with the paper left white between them, ring by ring from outside to inside red, violet, green, yellow, each ring of spikes offset by half a step against the next. White paper is about 60% of the sheet. Spikes are drawn with a hair gap between neighbours, so the white reads as light coming through.

**Speeding Automobile (MoMA).** Read as wedges and slivers converging on the axle at lower left; black 34%, cream 18%, brown 17%, lime green 10% as the single lively hue. Verticals (posts, window bars) stay thin and upright through the diagonals. A bundle of red, white and blue curls upper left of centre is the only dense decorative area.

**Velocity of an Automobile.** Monochrome (no colour above C 5). A row of eight wheel hubs at x = 165, 325, 463, 633, 825, 1025, 1262, 1535 px on 1676: intervals 160, 138, 170, 192, 200, 237, 273, so after the second hub the pitch grows by about 10–20% each step, and the white hub opening grows from about 22 to 55 px. The car therefore opens up toward the right as one perspective-free growth series. Under it three rows of open spirals drift out of it, each looser than the row above. Over it, straight black bars make an M: from a peak at (565, 0) two legs run down at about 35° (left) and 48° (right), and from (1120, 30) another at about 45°: the force lines are straight, wide and few (4–6), the wheels are curved and many. A radial burst of about 20 lines at (0.22, 0.25) gives the light.

**The Speed of an Automobile.** On brown board, six thick vertical black bars at 125–163, 277–307, 420, 580–641, 785–820 (broken) and 981–1000 px: gaps of roughly 130–190 px, deliberately uneven, the width of each bar between 20 and 60 px. Yellow (#DDAD4A, 13%) is the only light and only on one side of the arcs. Semicircles roll along the bottom third; arcs rise above. Dark is 25% of the surface, mid browns 45%.

**Abstract Speed (Philadelphia).** Ten or so white dashes at 25–31° from horizontal, parallel, rough-ended, over dark wheel spirals at pitch 235, 270, 235 px. Pale blue crescents (headlights) sit every 330, 315, 255 px, shortening. Two directions only: the long horizontals of the body and the oblique dashes.

**Abstract Speed + Noise (Philadelphia).** A pencil rectangle (910 × 705 px on 1200 × 855) marks the picture; the brush passes it on every side, up to about 115 px right and 70 px left. Three blue arcs, the same curve shifted by about 280 px (0.31 of the pencil rectangle) each time, sweep from upper left to lower right. A green strip at mid height is the land. The orange marks are small zigzag crosses in about a dozen clusters, hatched, placed off the arcs. White strokes are streaks between. The sheet is cream and largely empty (more than half bare paper).

**Abstract Speed + Sound.** Frame included, 2322 px wide. The frame takes 3.4% of the width each side, 4.7% at the top and 5.7% at the bottom, about 17% of the whole area, and repeats the picture's bands and arcs: blue and white diagonals step across it at the positions they have in the panel, green and red blocks sit on the right rail. But it is quieter: rails L 67–80 and C 4–20 against a panel mean L 52, C 25. Inside: one horizontal dark green strip across the middle (land), red-orange masses along the bottom and at the right (red about 16% of the panel), blue-grey sky above. Long thin arcs cross the sky and go through the land; a pink zigzag runs as a stair of five or six right angles from the left; about 14 small crosses in teal and lime (by eye) sit along the pink stair and the sky, the sound marks, with soft shadow on one side only. A white inverted shape in the middle is the void where the car would be. No car is drawn.

**Abstract Speed — The Car Has Passed.** Same land (green 19%), but the crosses, red, zigzag and white car-shape are all gone. What remains: three blue arcs, one curve translated by about 525–565 px (0.35 W, by eye) each time, thinner and sharper on the right edge and fading to the left; a pink line dropping at 26° to a node at (0.64, 0.41), then continuing at 15° to the right edge; a second pink line at the lower left; one white road shape. The gradient in each arc goes hard edge to soft edge, always on the same side. Only blue (about 52% in three values), green, cream-white, pink. Edges fade where the car went.

**Mercury Passing Before the Sun.** The orange disc of the sun (radius 0.16 W) sits at (0.58, 0.25) inside a black ring of 0.22 W, with the arcs widening outward as a telescope's nested tubes. At (0.31, 0.11) a white star-burst breaks in as the same sun seen by the naked eye. A grey-green triangle, apex at (0.55, 0.14), legs spreading to the bottom edge (0.26 and 0.83 W), overlays everything in half-transparency: the shape of the viewing cone. Blue and orange are the two masses (blue #586EB2 about 15%, orange-browns #B45B31 and #E29A34 about 25%), black 17%, white only 6% and only for the sunburst. Surfaces are scratched: short dry-brush strokes run with the direction of each plane. Planes pass over each other with visible transparency; no object is complete.

**The Worker's Day, 1904.** A frame painted like bricks divides the field into three: a landscape day panel (upper left, 0.44 W × 0.33 H), a second day panel below it (0.44 W × 0.45 H) and one tall night panel (0.51 W, full height). The light changes by panel (blue-grey day, warm pale midday, dark with lamplight), the building stays the same.

**Poster and numerals.** In the Bragaglia poster the wordmark BALLA is about 0.95 of the width and its letters are the widest of the sheet: tall bows, thin hairlines, thick stems. ## Process and relations (observed)
- **One body, many ends** (Dog on a Leash). The mass stays single and opaque; only what moves fast multiplies: about 8 tail crescents, 10–12 positions per foot fan, 3–4 ears, 8–9 shoes. The repeat count rises with the speed of the part, and value falls from near black (L 8) at the joint to a dotted veil at the rim.
- **A constant step across the width, against a second beat** (Girl Running, Swifts study). Foot positions every about 0.115 W (gaps 128–186 px, mean about 163), crossed by a railing at about 0.096 W: a ratio of 6 to 5, so five steps span six bars. In the swifts study the pitch drops about 11% per step (175, 155, 140).
- **Pitch that grows along the travel** (Velocity of an Automobile; No. 7 at its light end). Wheel hubs 160, 138, 170, 192, 200, 237, 273 px; hub opening 22 to 55 px. In No. 7 the pitch is constant (135–142) and then 159–165, +20%. In Abstract Speed (Philadelphia) the headlights go the other way (330, 315, 255).
- **The path is drawn separately from the positions** (Swifts, study). In the study the line is the lightest mark and the birds the darkest (L 84 against 14). It is thin (0.6% W in the oil), continuous, and passes over the rows as its own layer, never as an outline of a bird. Lines lie 0.11–0.17 H apart, roughly parallel; crest to trough 5–12% of H.
- **One curve, translated, never redrawn** (Abstract Speed + Noise; The Car Has Passed). Three arcs of the same shape at equal shifts: 0.31 and 0.31 of the pencil rectangle; about 0.35 W in the Tate work. Each arc has one hard edge and one fading edge on the same side, so all three point the same way.
- **The edge of the surface is a point on the way** (Dog frame dashes; Abstract Speed + Sound and Noise; Violinist). Paint passes the pencil rectangle on all sides; the painted frame holds 17% of the area and carries on the panel's bands about 15–28 L lighter and at lower chroma (C 4–11 on three rails, 20 on the right rail, against 25). In the violinist the surface itself is the wedge of the gesture.
- **Chroma does not peak at the light** (Street Light, No. 7). In Street Light C rises from 14 in the core to 36 at the third ring (0.16–0.24 W) and falls to 14 at the rim while L falls from 90 to 17. In No. 7 C rises from 21 to 33 just before the light step (L 49) and drops to 8 at L 90.
- **A pattern with no ground** (No. 4, No. 7). Triangles share apexes at seams, the next row offsets by half; heights differ between rows (113 to 171 in No. 4; equal thirds in No. 7) while the horizontal pitch is kept within 3%. Same colour count, different shape of row.
- **Hue by band, value by column** (No. 13; the ramp in No. 7). The lattice is untouched; the colour order is the event.
- **The mark tells the speed, not the outline** (Violinist, Street Light, Dog). Hatch of parallel strokes turned with the form; V marks that point back at the source and grow away from it; dashes on bodies and frame. No outline is closed around a moving thing.
- **Weight gathers toward the direction of travel** (Girl, Swifts). Dark share in the boot band rises from 36% to about 50–69% toward the right and warm pixels fall from 26% to about 10%; in Swifts the dense rows are lower right.
- **Something stays still** (all). Railing and jambs in Girl, the building lines in Swifts, the two horizontal bands behind the violinist, the land in Speed + Sound, the panels in The Worker's Day. The repeat is legible against a few straight, upright, unrepeated parts.
- **Subject subtracted step by step** (Speeding Automobile, then Speed + Sound, then The Car Has Passed). Car visible; car replaced by a white void; crosses, zigzag and red removed. Red falls from about 16% of the panel to none.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

Context: Futurism, which Balla joined in 1910, glorified war, scorned women in its founding manifesto and later allied itself with Fascism; none of that is carried here, and no manifesto, war or propaganda text is used as content.

**Giacomo Balla.** *Hold the frame and a few upright references still and show a change as all its positions at once: one whole body, its changed part repeated at a ruled interval and fading with age, the path drawn as its own line, and the surround continuing the content at a lower volume.*
Process:
1. Fix what stays still before anything is repeated: the viewpoint, the frame and 2–5 straight, upright, unrepeated references (rules, bands, a scale). Keep 55–65% of the view as calm, low-chroma ground. Every later step is counted against these.
2. Split the subject into what holds and what changes. The body (the thing itself, its name, its present value) stays single, opaque and sharp and takes the widest value step in the view, on about a fifth to a quarter of the area. Only the part that changed repeats, and its count follows its rate: 3–4 positions for a slow change, 8–12 for a fast one.
3. Lay the positions along one axis so the whole interval is visible at once, about 8 across the width (a step near 0.115 of it). Space them by one of three rules and never at random: constant (gaps within 3–15%) for a steady rate, growing 10–20% a step for a rising one, shrinking about 11% a step for a falling one. The gap is the rate.
4. Age every position: full strength at the newest, stepping down to a thin dotted trace at the oldest. Give each one hard edge and one fading edge, the same way round on all of them, so the row has a direction, and let weight gather at the leading end (dark share rising from about a third to a half or more).
5. Draw the path separately from the positions: one thin continuous line (about 0.6% of the width) on the opposite side of the ground in value from the positions, laid over them as its own layer and never closed round one. Where it crosses a mass it flips value. Several paths run roughly parallel, 0.11–0.17 of the height apart.
6. Give the fixed scale of step 1 a pitch that differs from the step of step 3: for a constant series about 5/6 of it, so five steps span six divisions and the two realign every fifth step. Draw the scale through the positions, not behind them.
7. Where a field carries an intensity and not a moving thing, hold one lattice (pitch within 3%) and make the colour order the only event: hue by band, value by column, the steepest value change inside about 15% of the width. Put the highest chroma one step outside the lightest zone, at middle value; at the lightest end chroma falls to a quarter to two fifths of its peak.
8. Let the surround take part: frame, margin or chrome (up to about 17% of the area) continues the content's bands at the positions they have inside, 15–28 L nearer the ground value and mostly at under half the chroma. The edge cuts the series on its way and never stops it short.
9. Show what comes after by subtraction: a later state is the same view with the same still parts, minus the event's marks and its hottest colour (from about 16% of the view to none). The path and the trace remain, the states sit side by side in order, and the order tells the time.
Why it works:
- Grouping by likeness: identical shapes at a ruled interval are read as one thing at several moments, and the gap is read as its rate. At random gaps they become several things; as a single position there is no before or after; smeared whole, there is nothing to count.
- Scale reference: a repeat is only countable against something that does not repeat. Remove the few fixed uprights and the row turns into texture; give them the same pitch as the step and the two lock into one static pattern.
- Figure and ground: a single sharp body keeps identity while its fading end gives direction and age by value order. Repeat the body too and no position is the present one.
- A path that is its own mark separates where from when. Used as an outline it closes each position into an object and the series falls apart into a list.
- Simultaneous contrast: the lightest zone reads as light because the strongest colour lies beside it, not in it. Fill the light with colour and it becomes a coloured patch of middle value.
Colour relations: calm ground on 55–65%, the lowest chroma; the body the darkest value, about a fifth of the view; positions between body and ground in value, stepping toward the ground with age; the path line under 1%, at the value extreme opposite the positions; at most one lively hue, on about a tenth of the view; the event's hot colour up to about 16% while the event lasts and none after; chroma highest at middle value beside the lightest zone; the surround 15–28 L nearer the ground than the content it continues.
Space and line: 2–5 fixed upright references; about 8 positions across the width, step near 0.115 of it; gaps constant within 3–15%, or +10–20% or about −11% a step; scale pitch about 5/6 of the step; path line about 0.6% of the width, paths 0.11–0.17 of the height apart; one hard and one fading edge per repeat, always the same way round; no closed outline round anything that changes; the surround up to 17% of the area.
Type requirement: text belongs to the body and the still parts: upright, single, never repeated, ghosted, slanted or set along the path. One family in three sizes far apart, loudness by width and size and not by added faces (the one name of the view may span up to 0.95 of the width; observed in a single poster). Numerals of constant stroke and equal width, so a row of values under the positions keeps the interval readable; labels sit on the fixed references.
Motion and interaction: static by design: the still parts, the series, the path and the ramp show the change completely with nothing moving. Only the body may move, once per real change, along the drawn path (200–300ms, no overshoot, no loop), leaving its old place behind as the newest trace while the others step down one level; nothing streaks, pulses or drifts for effect. Reduced motion: swap the state with no travel. The series is one stepped control (arrow keys or drag, ≥44px), each position announcing its value and age; age is never told by opacity alone (order, a label and a table alternative carry it), and any trace that carries information keeps 3:1 against the ground.
Fails when: nothing changes, or the items have no order (the repeats become decoration); more than about 12 positions, or several series crossing, so the traces fuse into a pattern; running text or a table has to sit under the trace; exact values must be read off the positions (use a chart with an axis); the view is so full that less than half can stay calm.
Signature (do not carry together): a limb or wheel fanned into many overlapping positions · slanted dashes, wedges and arcs running off onto a painted frame · a divided spectrum of small dabs or thin triangles on cream.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Savings-goal screen in a retail bank's app, phone 390 × 844, warm white with the bank's deep green, serif headings, quiet and slow (the works are about speed; this is about patience). Goal 6,000; seven monthly deposits so far, each about 15% larger than the last.
1. *Still parts.* The view does not pan. Three fixed things: a baseline rule, a goal rule at the right end, and a scale of upright ticks. Warm white ground (L 95) on about 64% of the screen.
2. *Body and repeats.* The balance is the body: the figure "3,345" in 56px and one solid marker (L 12), single and sharp. Only the marker's place repeats: the six earlier month-end positions.
3. *Interval.* All seven positions lie on one horizontal strip 358px wide (1px ≈ 16.8 of the currency), at 18, 39, 62, 90, 121, 158 and 200px. The gaps are 18, 21, 24, 27, 32, 36 and 42px, growing about 15% a step, so "saving faster" is visible without a chart or a sentence.
4. *Age.* The six earlier markers step from L 20 (newest) to L 50, and the oldest is a dotted outline. Each has a hard right edge and an 8px fade to the left. A single month initial sits under each, and the same data is a three-column table below.
5. *Path.* The strip is a mid-value band (L 62, 72px tall). One 2px line (0.6% of 358) at L 94 runs along it from zero through every marker and on to the goal rule; past the end of the strip, over the pale ground beside the goal label, it flips to L 20.
6. *Second beat.* Ticks every 500 (29.8px), twelve of them, drawn through the markers. No gap equals the tick pitch, so markers and ticks never lock.
7. *Ramp.* Not used: the view has no intensity field.
8. *Surround.* The strip runs to both screen edges and is cut by them. The tab bar repeats the tick pitch as hairlines at L 84 (22 L nearer the ground) with under half the strip's chroma.
9. *After.* When the goal is reached the same view stays: markers, the one lively hue and the button are removed; strip, scale, path line and "Reached in 9 months" remain. Finished goals stack below as such strips, oldest last.
- *Lively hue.* One, on the "Add money" button (358 × 52, about 6% of the screen) and nowhere else.
- *Motion.* On a new deposit the solid marker travels once along the line to its new place in 240ms and stops; its old place stays as the newest trace. Reduced motion: the new state is swapped in with no travel.
- *Type.* One serif family in three sizes (56, 17, 12px), all upright, tabular numerals, labels on the rules.

Result: a pale, level, patient banking screen with no slanted line, no wedge and no machine in it. What survived is the fixed scale, one sharp body, a ruled and ageing row of earlier positions whose gaps state the rate, the path as its own line, a surround that continues the strip, and the finished state as the same view with the event taken out.

## Space & composition
- The viewer is fixed and the subject crosses or leaves; most works are read left to right or along a diagonal. The dog faces left, the ground lines slope 2.6° to 25.8° so the ground fans open at the lower left.
- Two masses, one diagonal (Dog): upper right 19%, lower left 8%, about two thirds pale ground.
- Rows stacked as bands (Girl): heads 0.16 H, dress/railing 0.44, legs 0.20, boots 0.13. The contrast step is at the boots (SD of L 19, 21, 24, 30).
- Radial, off-centre (Street Light): lamp at (0.49, 0.34), closed halo, corners dark.
- Regular triangle grids have no focus: the ramp, not the composition, says where to look (No. 4, No. 7).
- The frame is included in the composition: 17% of the area in Speed + Sound; dashes in Dog; shaped in No. 7 and the violinist.
- Void is the subject in the last speed panel; the white car-shape in Speed + Sound holds the centre at about 0.15 of the panel (by eye).

## Form, line & typography
- **Vocabulary:** the dash, the square dab, the V, the hatch stroke, the wedge, the arc, the spiral, the zigzag cross, the lozenge. Each picture uses two or three of them, not all.
- **Curve for passing, straight for force, right angle for noise** (Velocity of an Automobile, Speed + Sound): many wheels are curves, few force lines are straight at 35–48°, sound is right-angled crosses.
- **One hard edge, one soft edge** per arc (The Car Has Passed).
- **Line is light as often as dark** (leash net, path lines, Philadelphia dashes), and the value inverts where it crosses a mass (leash).
- **Lettering (Bragaglia poster, 1918).** Display letters with thick stems and thin hairlines, very wide (BALLA about 0.95 of the width), all caps, one family in three sizes, drawn to the same thick-thin as the picture panel. Numerals in Numbers in Love: constant-stroke solids with parallel-projection sides. Signatures are often worked as a motif: "Futurballa" and the angular "BALLA" in the corner, sometimes slanted with the composition (Swifts, Mercury). Lettering is a minor part of Balla's work: the observation covers two pieces only.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- **Time is laid left to right or along a path;** the whole interval is visible at once (Girl, Swifts, Velocity).
- **Three kinds of interval:** constant (No. 4, 3% variation; Girl within about 15%), growing (Velocity hubs, +10–20% a step; No. 7, +20% at the light end), shrinking (Swifts study, about -11% a step; headlights in Philadelphia).
- **Beats in near ratio** (6 : 5 feet to bars): five steps span six bars, so they realign about every fifth step.
- **Time as subtraction** (Speed + Sound, then The Car Has Passed): first the event, then its trace; the order of the panels, not any movement in them, tells the time.
- **Time as states** (The Worker's Day, 1904): three light states of one view, divided by a frame.
- **Fading tells age:** the rim of a fan is thinner, more dotted and lighter than its root (Dog); each arc has one hard and one fading edge, always the same way round (The Car Has Passed; which side leads is not stated).

## UI translations
1. **All positions in one still view.** Show a change as its earlier positions beside the present one, on one axis, readable with nothing animating.
2. **One body, many ends.** The item, its name and its present value stay single, opaque and sharp; only the part that changed repeats, 3–4 times for a slow change and 8–12 for a fast one.
3. **The gap is the rate.** Space the repeats by a rule: constant within 3–15% for steady, growing 10–20% a step for rising, shrinking about 11% a step for falling. Never space them at random or by what fits.
4. **Fade by age, one hard edge.** The newest repeat is full strength and the oldest a thin dotted trace; each has one hard and one fading edge, the same way round on all. Pair the fade with order and a label, never opacity alone.
5. **Path as a separate line.** Draw the route as one thin continuous line (about 0.6% of the width) at the value extreme opposite the positions, over them, never as their outline; it flips value where it crosses a mass.
6. **A fixed scale at another pitch.** Cross the row with 2–5 upright rules or a tick scale at about 5/6 of the step, drawn through the positions, so they can be counted and realign every fifth step.
7. **Two thirds calm.** Keep 55–65% of the view as low-chroma ground and the body on about a fifth; the repeats and the path live in what is left.
8. **Chroma beside the light.** In a heat or intensity field, hold one lattice (pitch within 3%), order hue by band and value by column, and put the strongest colour one step outside the lightest cell, at middle value.
9. **The surround takes part.** Header, margins or frame (up to about 17% of the area) continue the content's bands at the same positions, 15–28 L nearer the ground and at under half the chroma; the edge cuts the series and does not stop it.
10. **After is the same view, less the event.** A completed or past state keeps every still part and removes the event's marks and its hot colour; show the states side by side in order.
11. **Text stays still.** Type is upright and single, in one family and three sizes far apart, with equal-width numerals; no word is repeated, ghosted, slanted or set on the path.
12. **One move per change.** Only the body moves, once, along the drawn line (200–300ms, no overshoot, no loop), and leaves its old place as the newest trace. Reduced motion swaps the state.
13. **The series is one control.** Step through positions with arrow keys or drag (target ≥44px); each announces its value and age, and a table alternative holds the same data.

## What this artist would reject
Inferred from what the examined works never do, not from his statements; the 1910 Technical Manifesto he signed says "a running horse has not four legs, but twenty".
- One frozen position standing for a movement, with nothing before or after it.
- A blur that smears the whole object: the body stays sharp and single, only the moving end repeats.
- A picture that stops at its frame: the edge is where the movement passes, never a wall.
- Outlines closed around a moving thing; the path and the positions are drawn as separate marks.
- Identical repeats at random spacing: the interval is either kept or changed by a rule.
- Filling the light with white: the most coloured zone lies one step outside the brightest.
- A background that is simply empty or simply scenery: a few upright straight things stay fixed so the repeat can be counted.

## Signature prompt seed
Keep the frame and a few upright rules still and leave about two thirds of the view calm. Show the change as all its positions at once: the item single and sharp, its changed part repeated along one axis at a ruled interval (constant, growing or shrinking, never random), fading from full strength to a dotted trace. Draw the path as its own thin line at the opposite value. Cross the row with a fixed scale at another pitch. Let the surround continue the content's bands more quietly. Text stays upright and single; only the item moves, once.
