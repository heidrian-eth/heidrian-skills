# Antonio Sant'Elia — Futurism

## Essence
Sant'Elia never drew a street with buildings beside it; he drew one machine in which the street, the lift, the bridge and the house are the same section. Circulation is pulled out of the mass and stood in front of it as a separate thin, dark lattice; the mass itself is left as blank, leaning, ornament-free planes. Stillness is made to look like movement by a few oblique families (about 11°, about 23°, about 35° off vertical), by lines that run past their corners, and by a horizon placed so that everything climbs away from it.

## Detailed observations

**La Città Nuova. Particolare.** Nearly square ruled frame (about 1780 × 1815 px inside a triple hand-ruled border). The building group occupies the left two thirds (0.00 to 0.66 of frame width); the right third is bare paper above two leaning pylons, and a sampled rectangle there has 0% ink. The highest point, four lattice masts, stands at 0.50 across and 0.03 down, each mast about 20 px lower than the one to its left, so the crown itself descends. Every tower has the same anatomy: a blank, jointed shaft, then a dark lattice and glass head, then masts or chimneys. The lift tower is always detached: it stands in front of the facade and is tied back by arched catwalks at each storey (about ten on the second tower: the top three run as open dark platforms, the rest as paired pale arches). The gap between the lift and the facade is visible as a stack of slivers. The right towers hide a setback: the dark jagged strip at x 1055 to 1120 px moves 65 px over 480 px (8° from vertical). The detached pylon leans 12° on one side and is vertical on the other, so it reads as half a wedge. Foreground platforms are battered far more, 35° to 41° from vertical, with a 22° spur between. Horizontal lines break into levels: from the bottom I count sunken track bed, roadway with parallel track lines, open trussed footbridge (9° to the horizontal), vehicle bays in the base, a terrace with the only planting, a vaulted underpass, glazed truss galleries, and an upper enclosed truss link: at least six heights of circulation in the lower 35% of the sheet. Ink coverage is 39% in the base and middle and zero in the sky. The horizon (where the curved joints on the round towers flatten) is around 0.72 to 0.75 of the frame height: the viewer stands roughly at terrace level, looks up at the towers and down into the cuttings at once. The lettering sits at the very bottom left: narrow, squared, broken capitals inside quotes followed by a cursive "Particolare"; the title is under 1.5% of the area. Human presence is limited to specks: a hatched cluster at the bottom edge and marks on the stair.

**Stazione (inked).** A strict mirror plan: the four lift towers sit at 532, 320, 322 and 530 px from an axis at 0.49 of the width (spacing ratio 1 : 1.66), so the symmetry error is under 1%. One vanishing point, computed from the two V lines and checked on the street edges, lands at 0.49 across and at the top edge of the sheet (y about 0). So this is a high bird's-eye, unlike the other sheets. All lateral edges belong to a fan: V lines 40° from vertical, outer streets 24° to 26°, ramp edge 9°, axis 0°. Seven bright sloped track bands alternate with six darker recessed ones, end at a full-width canopy with rounded ends, and re-emerge as five rounded noses. Between each pair of lift towers a ladder of about 19 identical arched catwalks fills the gap. Tower heads step back twice with a wire-like crown of four antennae each. The bottom third carries the empty plane: 14% ink against 37% in the middle band and 19% at the top; light grids of parallel hairlines describe ground by lanes alone. Small two-car trains (four on the ramp, three on the ground tracks) are the only moving objects, each a short dark dash on a hairline. The OpenEdition chapter says the sheet shows no vehicles and an incoming plane; I see the cars and could not see a plane on the inked sheet (a small cross-shaped mark sits in the apex triangle of the pencil one). Distant blocks are shaded grey and unfenestrated so that detail drops off with distance.

**Stazione (pencil).** The same sheet before inking shows the method: full-width horizontals ruled lightly through everything, a big triangle from the apex, grid continuing to the paper edge. The inked sheet keeps the overrunning ends of those guides.

**Casa a gradinata (watercolour).** Frame 1 : 1.49. Three quantities of area: orange-brown mass about 33%, blue-grey openings and steel about 31%, cream sky and paper about 36% (62% of the top half is cream). Roles are strict: solid wall is orange, anything open, glazed or moving is blue-grey. Two blank orange slabs stand like bookends; between them a gridded blue-grey curtain of about 16 rows and 7 columns, and on the right flank a serrated blue-grey profile of about 15 ledges receding 12° from vertical on average, not straight: about 5° near the foot and about 15° near the top, so the lean grows with height. The tallest slab is 5° off vertical on its left edge. The lower fifth is a curving road with five short black dashes (vehicles read as marks, aligned with the road at about 20° to the horizontal) and a blue truss footbridge at the same 20°. The road deck line at 0.78 of the height is nearly flat and is the horizon; everything above climbs, the base curves away below. Building edges carry a warm orange halo drawn just outside the black contour on the sky side, and thin vertical lines project 40 to 120 px above the roofs.

**House with external lifts (pen).** The same view as the watercolour without colour. Line density replaces the blue: closely hatched or cross-hatched strips stand for openings, and the walls stay empty. Lines past the corner are visible everywhere.

**Via secondaria.** The left half is a full perspective with hatching; the right half is bare. Ink coverage is 44% left of the lift tower and 6% right of it, so the right half is a section diagram of the same street. The tower (0.16 of the width) is exactly on the midline, rises through eight levels, and each level throws one catwalk left (in depth) and one right (as three flat lines). On the right the facade is eight ledges, seven measured intervals: rises of 138, 132, 117, 111, 132, 127, 136 px (mean 128), recesses of 26, 27, 25, 22, 26, 26, 26 px, rise to recess 5 to 1, hence an 11° line. The catwalks shorten by that same 25 px per level as they go up the tower, so the gap stays in step with the setback. The left lines converge at 0.50 across and 0.57 down (computed): the eye is just below the sixth catwalk of eight, inside the traffic stack; the left-hand parapet nearest that height is the only one drawn nearly flat. A tiled arched glass roof of the lowest walk fills the foreground wedge. The frame lines of the paper are ruled, and construction verticals extend above the tower head and below its base.

**Parabolic-gallery stepped house.** On a dark patch of tracing paper the elevation is drawn in outline and one orange hatch fills the solid. Nine visible steps of 45 to 60 px height (mean 54) and 7 to 17 px recess (mean 11, again 11° from vertical). The right edge is a parabolic curve opening to the street, with a tower mast above it carrying a lantern head. Long guide lines run out to the right across empty sheet at shallow falls, farther than the building. Ink (pencil) is heavy only at the ledges.

**La centrale elettrica.** Portrait, 0.67. Sky teal in the upper 40% with drips left visible; blue-grey hatched backdrop of retaining walls; building in yellow ochre and red-brown; the lower strip bare. Lean families: the four ribs of the forebay stand about 17° to 21° from vertical and sit at a constant pitch of about 113 px (7.5% of width); the penstock slab on the right leans 22° to 28°; power cables on the left run 51° from vertical; the flat roof slopes about 10° from horizontal. Three chimneys stand at a pitch of about 115 px, equal to the rib pitch, and step down 170 and 150 px. The ground is at 0.93 of the sheet: the lowest viewpoint of the set. Cables and arcs of light cross the sky as bare pencil lines that end outside the frame. The title "LA CENTRALE ELETTRICA" is lettered in square capitals, and the date uses numerals stacked around a vertical bar.

**Studio per centrale elettrica.** A chute of seven dark pipes and two stair edges drops from upper right to lower left at 23° to 24° from vertical on both edges (parallel, not converging), over 38% of the sheet height. A flat datum line at 0.74 of the height stops the diagonal dead; below it arched openings and leaning piers at about 7°. Orange sky (L 59, C 60), dark green hills (L 15 to 27) and cream machine: three values, three roles.

**Studio per edificio.** All line and wash. The ground line is at 0.75 of the frame; below it there is one flat orange field with no drawing except verticals that overshoot it by 30 to 120 px (up to 5.5% of the sheet). Three round-fronted shafts step up to the right and the tallest is cut off by the top edge. One long roof edge falls 28° from vertical and a second, inner one 27°; the left wing falls 57° from vertical, matching the roof ramps at 59°. Median ink stroke is about 4 px in 1920 px (0.2% of width); the heads of the shafts are solid vertical hatching. Orange covers about 49% of the image by k-means (two clusters), cream sky about a third. Line and wash do not agree: the wash runs past the contour.

**Monumental Building.** The mass is paper tone (bare, 53%). Blue strokes mark only lantern heads, window strips and a canal; an orange band (about 8%) sits behind the building between the horizon and 0.43 of the sheet height, inside a hand-drawn border. A path rises at 27° from horizontal to the ground. Side buttress 10° from vertical, roof slats 16°, ridge falling 40°.

**Torre faro.** Two bodies joined at the base and at the top with a slot of dark blue-green between; buttress 17° on one side and 20° on the other; pink hatched sky, gold ground, one small figure in the foreground (about 7% of the sheet height) with a cast shadow. It is the only Città-Nuova-period sheet I saw with a legible person; the Monza drawing has a crowd.

**Viaduct.** No verticals, no datum: the whole structure is tilted, four stepped pipes run up from lower left into an arch. Red is the only hue, 13% of the area, seen in the sky, inside the arch and in two slots, so it appears only through openings. Everything else is black pencil on cream.

**Dinamismi.** Contour only: two to five vertical lines, one leaning edge, one blind head. The head is the only hatched area, and the body is unbroken. In one tower a single orange triangle marks the leaning side. The ledger line at the bottom overshoots both ends. The Lombardy catalogue calls the series abstractions without a precise purpose, outlines only, and says the name came from Escodamè in 1930, not from the artist.

**Detail study, Library of Congress.** A front section: a trussed lift shaft, three curved brackets, catwalks and a U-shaped bridge in heavy black; walls are pencil only. Ink is 20% of the sheet, and stroke runs go from 2 px to about 15 px (90th percentile) with blots to 93 px: a range of roughly 1 to 7. Red is used for one lift cabin (about 0.5%).

**Manifesto page 1.** A title in condensed heavy sans capitals fills the full column width (1015 of 1160 px) and is followed by a double rule; "Manifesto" is centred in a large Bodoni-type serif with a short rule; text is one justified block of about 40 lines over about 73% of the sheet height, with only one bold inline phrase. The ink is 1.7% dark; the paper carries the rest. It is an ordinary printed page: typographic fireworks belong to Marinetti, not to this leaflet.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Circulation is pulled out of the mass and stood in front of it** (Particolare, casamento, via secondaria, stazione, LoC study). Lift tower is a separate element, linked by one bridge per level: about ten on the Particolare, eight on via secondaria, about 19 between the station towers. Both the tower and each bridge are rendered darker than the wall.
- **Solid is blank, opening is dark or lattice** (all). Wall planes carry only hairline joints; hatching is spent on heads, shafts, bridges and glass. In the watercolour the same rule is colour: orange is mass (33%), blue-grey is opening and steel (31%).
- **Setback at one angle, about 11°.** Measured: gradinata flank 12° on average (5° to 15°), via secondaria 11°, parabolic house 11°, Particolare hidden flank 8°, iron-and-glass pylon 10° and 11°. The rule in plan is a rise of 5 units for 1 unit of recess, repeated 8 to about 15 times; on via secondaria the rise varies from -13% to +8% of its mean and the recess from -13% to +6%, so it is regular but hand-set.
- **A small set of oblique families.** Battered faces and setbacks 8° to 12° from vertical; buttress ribs and penstocks 17° to 28°; power-plant and stair families 32° to 41°; roof and road lines 50° to 70°. Within a family the lines are parallel (both chute edges at 23° to 24°; both rib and penstock pairs in the centrale), so each family reads as one direction.
- **Asymmetric batter.** One side leans while the other stays vertical: Particolare pylon 12° against 0°, studio per edificio long roof edge 28° against vertical shafts. Torre faro is the exception: buttressed on both sides (17° and 20°).
- **Stacked levels of movement.** At least six heights in the lower 35% of the Particolare; three street levels named in two titles; runway over rail over road in the station. Each level is told by a single thin element (truss bridge, hairline rails, parallel lines) and not by a platform of its own colour.
- **Horizon inside the traffic.** Computed or read: 0.57 (via secondaria), about 0.72 to 0.75 (Particolare), 0.78 (gradinata), 0.75 (edificio), 0.93 (centrale). The viewer sits at a circulation level; only the station has its vanishing point at the top edge (bird's-eye). The low-viewpoint idea holds for the power plants and buildings, not for the station. The OpenEdition chapter agrees: it notes a low view for the power plants and a bird's-eye for the station.
- **Line overruns its corner** (all ink sheets, strongest in studio per edificio: 30 to 120 px past the ground, up to 5.5% of the sheet). Guides kept after inking: pencil triangle on the station, ruled lines on via secondaria.
- **Ink where the machine is, paper where it is not.** Station 37% in the middle band against 14% at the bottom; Particolare 0% in the sky; via secondaria 44% left against 6% right. The empty half is a design element, not a leftover.
- **One half rendered, one half diagram** (via secondaria; echoed in Particolare's bare right third). The same object is shown as surface and as section in one picture.
- **Repeated heads, descending crown.** Identical shaft, dark lattice head, masts. Crowns step: four masts (Particolare), three chimneys dropping 170 and 150 px (centrale), shafts rising to the cropped right one (edificio).
- **Equal pitch across unlike things.** Centrale: ribs at about 113 px, chimneys at about 115 px. Station: lift towers at 320 and 532 px on both sides of the axis, ratio 1 : 1.66.
- **Line weights in three grades.** Hairline for guides, joints, lanes; a medium contour; a dense black hatch for moving or lit parts. Measured stroke ratio from 2 to 15 px in the LoC study and 4 px median to 45 px in the 99th percentile in edificio.
- **Colour only in a few places and flat.** Viaduct red 13% and only through openings; Monumental Building blue about 4% on lantern heads and strips, orange about 8% in one sky band; parabolic house one orange hatch on the solid; LoC study one red cabin.
- **Early ornament is dropped by 1913.** Monza (1911–12) is dense with surface pattern, tiered ornamental towers and cloud scrolls (seen at 600 × 400 px only); the 1913 dinamismi are contour only. The manifesto says "the decorative must be abolished" and that "oblique and elliptic lines are dynamic".

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

*Context: Futurism glorified war, declared contempt for women and tied itself to Fascism; none of that is carried here, and no propaganda or war text is used as content.*

**Antonio Sant'Elia.** *Pull the route out of the content and stand it in front as one thin dark spine, bridged once to every level; step the blank levels back from a straight edge at one fixed ratio, and spend line and tone only on what moves or opens.*
Process:
1. Draw the section before any surface: list every level the user can stand on (3 to about 15 in one view; the stacks run 8 to about 19) and every way of moving between them. Each later step is cut from this list.
2. Fix one flat datum: a single full-width horizontal at 0.57–0.78 of the view's height is the level the user is on. It is the only unbroken flat line; what lies ahead stacks above it and takes the larger share, what is done continues below it, compressed. One overview mode is allowed for the whole system: datum at the top edge, strict mirror symmetry (error under 1%), all levels seen from above.
3. Take the route out of the masses: one spine about 0.16 of the width, standing in front of the content with a visible gap on each side, tied to every level by exactly one connector. Spine and connectors are the densest things in the view.
4. Step the levels from the datum at one ratio: each sets back from the one before by a recess of 1/7 to 1/5 of its rise (about 5 to 1), 8 to 15 times, within ±13% by hand. Only the edge facing the spine steps; the opposite edge stays straight, and every connector changes length by the same recess. The implied lean is built from upright steps: no edge and no text is rotated.
5. Make the surroundings obey the subject: every secondary series takes its pitch or its direction from the primary one (within about 2%), and a view holds at most 3–4 implied directions, each parallel within itself and each given to one kind of thing.
6. Blank the masses: a content mass carries hairlines only. Dense tone goes to the spine, the connectors, the head of each stack and anything that opens or moves. Three line grades (guide, contour, dense), spanning about 1 to 7 in width. The busiest band is 37–44% marked; a third or more of the field stays at 0–6%.
7. Render one side and diagram the other: on one side of the spine the levels appear in full; on the other the same levels are a bare section, a few flat lines each at the pitch of step 4, at about 1/7 of the density.
8. Show transit as a short dense dash on a hairline lane, aligned with the lane; several at once, none larger than the rest. A route that continues beyond the view runs off the frame; it stops inside the frame only where the path really ends.
Why it works:
- Figure and ground: a route at the same value as the content, buried in it, has to be searched for. Detached, darker and separated by a gap it is its own figure, and one connector per level lets the levels be counted at a glance. Hide it in the mass and the count is gone; give some levels two connectors and the count is wrong.
- A lean is only read against something straight. One stepping edge beside one upright edge gives the stack a direction; step both edges and it becomes a symmetric pile that reads as a monument and stands still. A constant rise to recess fuses the steps into one line by continuity; unequal steps read as noise, equal ones with no recess as a plain list.
- Salience by density: where the masses are blank, the few dense marks are the figure, so the eye lands on what moves. Spread the detail evenly and nothing leads; fill the calm third and the dense band loses its scale.
- One flat line among stepped ones is the odd thing, so it says "you are here" with no marker. Centre it and the view splits in two equal halves with no ahead and no behind; remove it and the stack floats.
Colour relations: two roles, about 20 L apart and opposite in temperature: what stands and what opens or moves. In the full mode the standing role, the moving role and the open ground each take about a third (33 / 31 / 36), and the ground is lightest. In the sparse mode the whole view is ground plus one dark, and one hue enters flat at 0.5–13%, only on a moving part or seen through an opening. The ground is a mid-to-light value with some chroma, not the extreme of the range. No gradient inside a role.
Space and line: datum at 0.57–0.78; spine about 0.16 of the width with a gap each side; one connector per level; rise to recess 5 : 1 (7 : 1 to 4 : 1), 8–15 repeats; one edge stepped, one straight; 3–4 implied directions at most; three line grades across 1 : 7; contour about 0.2% of the width; busiest band 37–44% marked, a third or more at 0–6%; the mass off the axis except in the overview mode.
Type requirement: upright, narrow letterforms that stay legible at label size, in three weights matched to the three line grades; always horizontal, never set along a lean. Labels sit small at the foot of what they name (a view title under 2% of the area, at the lower left). Loudness comes from measure and position, not from many sizes: at most one line per view runs the full measure (about 7/8 of the column), followed by a rule; running text is one plain block with one bold phrase.
Motion and interaction: static: the datum, the spine, the connectors, the steps and every label. May move: the dash along its lane, and the stack passing through the fixed datum when the user changes level (one rise per move, 200–300ms, no overshoot). Nothing drifts, loops or moves without a cause in the data. Focus order follows the spine, level by level; each connector is a target of 44px or more and names its level in text. Reduced motion: the dash sits at its position, the level change is an instant swap, and the new level is announced in words.
Fails when: the content has no levels and no route (one flat list, a single form, a long text, which he set as an ordinary page); more than about 15 levels must show at once on a phone; the view is narrower than spine, gap, stepped mass and diagram need (under about 360px, drop step 7 first); peers of equal rank have no order; many category colours are needed, so the two roles cannot hold; dense content must sit on the masses.
Signature (do not carry together): a leaning, stepped tower block with a detached lift shaft bridged at every floor · ruled ink lines that overshoot their corners on browned paper with a flat orange wash · dark lattice heads crowned with masts under an empty sky.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Purchase-request approvals, desktop web app at 1280 × 800, in a pale pastel theme with a rounded brand sans, friendly and soft (the works are monochrome, hard-edged and monumental).
1. *Section.* Nine stages from draft to paid; one forward route, one "sent back" route. Seven stages fit a view.
2. *Datum.* One full-width line at y = 496 (0.62). The user's stage (budget check) sits on it. Four coming stages stack above it at a 104px rise; two finished ones lie below at 56px.
3. *Spine.* A 200px column (0.16 of the width) at x 640–840 with a 6px route line down its middle and a 24px gap to the stage panels. Seven connectors, one per stage.
4. *Step.* Stage panels lie left of the spine. Their left edges share the page margin at x = 64; the edge facing the spine steps back 20px per stage going up (104 : 20, about 5 : 1), so the connectors measure 24, 44, 64, 84 and 104px. Nothing is rotated.
5. *Shared pitch.* The rows of the diagram side and the date ticks on the spine use the same 104px pitch as the stages. One implied direction only, read against the straight left margin.
6. *Density.* Panels hold text and 1px dividers. The dense grade (7px) is kept for the route line, the connectors and a status strip at the head of each panel; contours are 3px. The band between y = 180 and 496 is about 40% marked.
7. *Diagram side.* The right third (x 864–1280) repeats the seven stages as three hairlines and one label each, about 5% marked.
8. *Transit.* Requests waiting between two stages are 20 × 6px dashes on that connector, each with a count in text ("3 waiting"). The route line runs off the top edge, because two more stages lie beyond the view, and ends in a cap at draft.
- *Colour.* Standing panels are a pale warm tint; the route, connectors and status strips are a cool tone 20 L darker; the pale ground keeps about 40%. One accent, 0.5% of the view, marks the one request waiting for this user, with the word "yours".
- *Type.* The brand sans in three weights, horizontal throughout; stage names small at the lower left of each panel; the view title is the only full-measure line.
- *Motion.* On approval the stack moves down one rise (104px) through the fixed datum in 240ms and the dash travels its connector. Reduced motion swaps the state at once and announces "Moved to legal review".

Result: a soft, rounded, pastel approval ladder with no slanted edge, no ink and no tower. What survived is the route as its own dark spine, one connector per level, one stepping edge against one straight one, blank masses, and a flat line for the present level.

## Space & composition
- **Portrait, with the mass off the axis and the sky empty.** Mass at the left two thirds and the crown at 0.5 across (Particolare), or bookend slabs with the hollow between (gradinata). The station is the one symmetric sheet.
- **Horizon at a traffic level, 0.57 to 0.78 of the height**, with the ground continuing below as cuttings, ramps and lanes that deepen the space downward.
- **Depth by layered horizontals.** Footbridge, deck, underpass, terrace, gallery: each crosses in front of another and none is continuous across the sheet.
- **Cropping.** The last shaft (edificio) and the cables (centrale) leave the frame, and the road runs off the bottom-left corner (gradinata).
- **Frame.** A hand-drawn double or triple border encloses the Particolare, edificio, centrale and Monumental Building; in edificio the tallest shaft breaks through it.
- **Absence.** People are near absent, there is no tree except a few blobs on the terrace, no sign, no street furniture, no interior plan, no shadow on the ground in the inked sheets.

## Form, line & typography
- Forms: cylinders with rounded fronts, wedge pylons, stepped profiles, arches as bridges, lattice shafts; no cornice, capital or moulding on the 1913–14 sheets I saw.
- Line: ruled in the presentation sheets (Particolare, station, via secondaria) and freehand in the studies. Corners cross and continue. Three weights (hairline, contour, dense hatch). Hatching follows the direction of the plane.
- Lettering: titles in narrow squared capitals with odd gaps and a following cursive (Particolare); a date built around a vertical stroke (centrale, Monumental Building). Lettering sits low and small, below 2% of the sheet. The leaflet uses a full-width condensed grotesque headline, double rule and a centred modern serif, then plain justified text.
- The manifesto's argument on line: oblique and elliptical lines carry more emotive force than verticals and horizontals; the drawings do use both, with verticals as the fixed reference against which the obliques lean.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- **Counted repeats with a drift.** Eight near-equal steps (via secondaria), about 15 (gradinata), about 19 bridges (station), four ribs at a constant pitch (centrale).
- **Directions as speed.** Road dashes at 20° to the horizontal, chutes at 23° from vertical, cables at 51° from vertical, light arcs in the sky.
- **Levels as simultaneity.** Several movements happen in the same frame (trains on the ramp, bridges, lift shafts, vehicles on the deck, aeroplanes per the station's title) and none has priority.
- **Time of making is left in.** Overrun guides, uninked pencil, drips, inscribed dates and places.
- **Time of use.** The manifesto's closing claim is that each generation should build its own city; the drawings show no weather, season or age. A vehicle or a person is at most a short dark mark.

## UI translations
1. **Detached route rail.** Draw navigation between levels as its own dark rail about 0.16 of the width, standing clear of the content by a visible gap, with one bridge per level; never hide it inside a card.
2. **Five to one.** Step stacked panels back by 1/5 of their height per level on the side that faces the rail, and keep the far side on one straight margin. Build the lean from upright steps; rotate nothing.
3. **Flat line for "here".** Put the current level on the only full-width horizontal, at 0.57–0.78 of the viewport height, with coming levels above and finished ones compressed below.
4. **Blank walls, dark openings.** Panels carry hairline joints only; hatch or dark tone goes to openings, controls, the rail and its bridges. Aim for 37–44% marked in the busiest band.
5. **Keep a third empty.** One third of the view stays at 0–6% marked (in the Particolare the right third is bare paper); do not fill it with secondary cards.
6. **Rendered half, section half.** Beside the full view, repeat the same levels as a bare section of flat lines at the same pitch, as on Via secondaria (44% against 6% marked).
7. **Three line grades.** Hairline for guides and lanes, a medium contour, a dense grade about seven times the hairline for moving or active parts.
8. **Dash on a lane.** Show an item in transit as a short dark dash on a hairline, aligned with it; give its count or state in text beside it.
9. **Two-role colour.** For a homage palette, wall `#965E3E` against opening and steel `#3C3B3F` on paper `#E7DCC0`, about a third each, about 20 L between wall and opening; or one flat hue at 0.5–13% only on a moving part or seen through an opening.
10. **Shared pitch.** Give unlike series (rows, ticks, rail stops) one interval, as the ribs and chimneys of La centrale elettrica share about 113–115 px.
11. **Crop what continues.** A route that goes on beyond the view runs off the frame; a route that ends is capped inside it.
12. **Low, small titles.** Titles sit at the lower left under 2% of the area; at most one headline runs the full measure with a rule under it, and body text is one plain block, as on the leaflet's first page.
13. **Motion.** Only the dash and the level change move; the rest is still. Under reduced motion, state changes swap at once and are announced in text.

## What this artist would reject
- Ornament added after the structure, or a facade that hides how people get up and down.
- A plain rectangular symmetric mass with horizontal cornice lines and no lean.
- Stairs hidden inside as the way of moving; a lift concealed in a core.
- The single ground plane, with every movement on one level.
- Evenly distributed detail; a drawing that ends neatly at its outlines.
- Permanence, weight and expensive traditional material as a value.

## Signature prompt seed
List the levels and the ways between them first. Stand the route in front of the content as one thin dark spine with a gap on each side and one connector per level. Put the current level on a single flat full-width line about two thirds down. Step the other levels back by a fifth of their height on the side facing the spine and keep the far edge straight; rotate nothing. Leave the masses blank and spend dense tone only on the spine, the connectors and what moves or opens. Repeat the levels as a bare section on the other side, keep a third of the view nearly empty, and show anything in transit as a short dash on a hairline. Only the dash and the level change may move.
