# Fortunato Depero — Futurism

## Essence
Depero treats a letter, a word or a paragraph as a solid building part: it has faces and weight, it can be stacked, tilted, hollowed out, lived in or given legs. He packs such parts edge to edge into one plain compact outline (a square, a disc, a wedge, a giant initial) and then turns the parts inside it, so the page stays a rigid block while the reading direction keeps changing. Colour is a small set of flat, hard-edged areas; where form needs shade or a colour must change, the change happens on a cut line (a face edge, a diagonal, a row of teeth), and seldom by blending.

## Detailed observations

**Depero Futurista: the object.** Two large hexagonal nuts stand proud on the left margin of the cover, set on a strip marked off by a crease about 0.13 of the cover width; a small vertical line between them credits the binding. All four edges of the block are open; remove the bolts and every leaf is loose. The photographed openings show that most of the left side is blank verso: the content falls almost wholly on one right-hand leaf at a time, like a card in a tray. Across 123 right-hand pages the median ink (including photographs) covers 17% of the visible page, the middle half 8–32%, the fullest 67%. Paper changes are sudden: about 18 of 123 right-hand pages (15%) are on coloured or grey stock. Measured paper: cream `#EEE7D4` (L 92, C 10) as the norm; orange `#FA9E11` (L 73, C 79); red `#D9574E` (L 54, C 60); sky blue `#7CA3C2` (L 65, C 21); violet-grey `#A799B1` (L 65, C 15); pale green `#ADCCB5` (L 79, C 17); straw `#E2D4AF` (L 85, C 20); grey-olive `#7C7C6D` (L 52, C 8). Ink on all of them stays black. The violet sheets act as section doors: a two-word title in heavy condensed lower case ("manifesti murali", "plastica in moto", "onomalingua") and nothing else. Glassine sheets lie over portraits and colour plates, so the next image is seen as a ghost (L 86, no contrast) before it is seen. A second ink (orange-red) appears on roughly 17 of 123 pages (14%), usually as small vertical mottos or one date, seldom as a full page.

**Title page ("DEPERO / FUTURISTA / 1913–1927").** The type forms an exact square: x 126–830, y 126–831, so 704 × 705 px on a leaf about 830 px high, with about 0.13–0.15 of the page height left clear around it. DEPERO is the lintel: cap height 277 px = 0.39 of the square (0.33 of the page), six letters of nearly equal width (112, 116, 112, 102, 121, 112 px) set 6–11 px apart, ink filling 71% of the word's box. The two dates are the feet, 123 px high, so 1 : 2.25 to the lintel, flush with the square's left and right corners. Between lintel and feet a V: FUTURISTA falls at −49° and DINAMO climbs at about +41°, perpendicular to each other (90° within a degree), with thin light lines ("Edizione della", "Milano", "Parigi") running parallel to DINAMO as hairline echoes. No frame, no rule. The white wedges left over are part of the drawing. Ink on the leaf 26%.

**"DINAMO" page (two inks).** A left column of seven stacked lines, each in a different width of type but forced to the same measure (about 200 of 460 units wide), then a Z-shaped bracket of rules. To the right an eight-armed star: a black DINAMO block at the hub and orange words in ruled lanes at 0°, 45° and 90°. Only the hub is black; everything else is orange, so a single colour change marks the centre. Bounding box 723 × 714, ratio 1.01.

**W fan (plate 08).** A four-line square slab on top, hung between two vertical bars, then nine word-lanes fanned over 180°, 22.5° apart, each lane a pair of rules holding one word that reads outward or inward; eight Ws sit in the gaps, so spoke and gap alternate. Inner radius about 130 px, outer about 330, ratio 0.39. The centre is an empty half-disc with one W. The lane rules are all the same weight and length; type width adapts to lane length (condensed for long words, extended or padded with a rule for short ones).

**"Architettura tipografica" page (plate 49).** Two layers. Below, orange solid capitals (stacked slab T shapes, an A, an inverted A, an arrow) cover 27% of the leaf; `#EF7C42`, L 64, C 65. Above, black text (about 11% of the leaf) runs straight across them without any knock-out, in four blocks: one horizontal block with a 385 px measure, a vertical title on the left edge, a block rotated 90° at lower right, and a short foot block. Whole forme 732 × 718 (1.02). The rotated block begins where the A and arrow stop, so its edge is drawn by the letters beneath. Reading requires one quarter-turn of the book. The text states his programme: the style of a pavilion should come from the lines, colours and construction of the things shown in it, and that the pavilion's letters are gigantic, interpenetrated, packed and superposed; inside, shelves are words hollowed out of the walls and each letter is a show-case, stools are Es and Rs, and the building is white, grey and blue.

**Padiglione del Libro (plate 50).** Photograph on black, 56% of the visible leaf; only 31% of the photo is lit. A low plinth runs the full width (582 px of a 765 px photo). Central block about 350 px wide and 368 high with a single cubic R on top, whose counter is a real opening. Two round-topped Bs seen from the side make the bulge at left: the letter has been extruded through the wall and reads from one direction only. LIBRO and TREVES are piled vertically as corner piers; the door jambs are BESTETTI and TUMMINELLI cut in dark relief. Free-standing at left and right are two towers of stacked letters (TREVES 260 px high, 0.71 of the main block; width 68, so about 1 : 3.8), each letter one storey with no gap, joined by full contact. All surfaces are bare plaster, one value; all legibility comes from shadow on depth. Asymmetry: one R on the axis, towers of unequal shape.

**Giant-letter text (plates 21, 33, 34, 39, 46).** In the Marinetti gatefold three unfolding panels spell DEPERO in six areas, where body text fills the letter (about 40 lines per letter, ragged only at the counters) and the final O is built of horizontal hairlines; a black headline runs along the top of all panels on one line. In plate 46 the text fills a capital A whose crossbar is two bold lines and whose counter is simply left out; side words run vertically as pillars, so the block becomes 730 × 720 (1.01). In plate 33 a disc is divided into quarters, each quarter's text turned (upright, 90°, 180°, 90°), with a black arrow fixed outside the disc pointing at the start. Plate 35 rotates the entire page by 90° and cuts a path of 2 px rules through it as steps.

**Tilted notices (plate 111).** Six small text blocks and five heavy rules; three rules are tilted by 20.3°, 21.0° and 21.6°, and one stands at 69.4°, perpendicular to them; the remaining two are upright and level. So there are exactly two axes systems, 0/90 and 21/111, and no third. The blocks butt against the rule like flags on a pole. Forme 716 × 706 (1.01), ink 5%.

**Cover wedge (plate 03).** A three-sided arrow from upper left to right, split into two faces, blue `#6282B7` (L 54, C 31, 34%) and orange `#FD8705` (L 68, C 82, 17%), on cream. DEPERO climbs over it as steps, the line from D to O rising about 38–39° by eye, each letter upright but shifted up and growing thinner; where the letter lies on blue it turns orange and where it lies on paper it turns blue, so one shape changes colour at every crossing. On the sold cover the same plate is black and pinkish white on sage card.

**Campari, 1931.** Seven capitals and the date 1931 stand face to face on a tilted plane with all edges running to a single point at (0.02, 0.98), the lower left corner. The fan spans from about 71° to about 14° above horizontal, 57° in all. Four tones do it: face `#CAD4DD` (L 84) with a slight gradient, side `#7F95A4` (L 60), deep side `#436070` (L 38), black `#050908` (L 5), on cream card `#E2D6BA` (L 85). Cream (two clusters) is 36% of the sheet; black 21%; face tone 15%; the two sides 28%. Word faces carry the light and stay readable; the streaks behind are longer than the word by about three to one. MILANO (top left) and "creazione DEPERO" (lower right) are flat black captions in empty corners, about one tenth of the size of the word.

**Palestra tipografica, 1931.** CORDIAL is held aloft on poles by the five letters A M P A R, each with a circle head, cone hat and two-line legs; the C is the rear of a wagon and the I its front, so the word CAMPARI is completed across two lines and three scales. Pyramids of lower-case m form the load. Wheels are concentric rings. Black is 29% of the sheet. An arrow introduces the caption.

**Black and white Campari in the bolted book (plate 106) and Bitter Cordial, 1928.** Each ad is a compact block, letter on top, figure, letter below, the word width equal to the block width. Inside the block black is 43% in both ads measured (27% of the whole leaf); Bitter Cordial 39%, Con un occhio 35%, Palestra 29%. Rain is three concentric targets; a shadow ends in a comb of long teeth, not a fade; stools and glasses are faceted with 2–3 px white edges where black face meets black face. In Bitter Cordial the figure is symmetrical, checkered, and black flips to white at each crossing of belly, knee and arm. The A of CAMPARI points down past the baseline with a small triangle; crossbars turn into wedges; the double T of BITTER leans so the word ends as one block.

**Bitter Campari poster, 1928.** Black 61%, orange-red `#E5593B` (14%), straw orange `#F6B868` (11%), white `#EBE2DC` (5%), blue `#4B84BD` (4%); the scene covers 27% of the inner sheet and the wordmark only 6%. The drinker is made from boxes: each limb has one straw face and one red face that meet at a ruled edge; the turn from light to dark at cheek, thigh and table rim is a row of 5–9 sawtooth points, one colour biting into the other. The head is a rhombus with one ring eye and a notch for a mouth. White shadows are cast to left and right onto black, so shadow is the lightest area. The product (siphon and glass) is drawn from the figure's own colours. Wordmark below, in capitals as wide as 0.64 of the sheet, with the M and P sharing strokes. A red line frames the sheet, about 2.4% of the width, with orange corners.

**Squisito al selz, 1926.** A bar built of tilted planes: green 23%, orange 17%, navy 16%, purple-brown 13%, red-brown 11%, ochre 9%; six hues in comparable shares. Tables are thick discs seen from above, bodies faceted. The word BITTER CAMPARI is painted on a green plane slanted like the other planes, so the sign is furniture. Each solid has two or three flat steps.

**Campari pavilion, 1933.** A cube in two-point perspective: CAMPARI as capitals with deep side walls stands on the cornice as a wall; "1933" is the porch beam; CORDIAL runs down a pier; the horizontal strips at the right corner are ledges, 6 of them. Cream ground 34%, black 24%, two greys 27%, deep slate 10%. No picture, no logo: the letters are the only ornament.

**Secolo XX, 1929.** Diagonals cut a grey field (34%) from yellow (28%) with black (20%) and orange (10%). The title SECOLO changes colour where the line passes through it, orange on yellow above and black on grey below, in the same letter. XX is an orange solid, then the same XX again behind it as a black flat shadow about twice as large. The small lists are 10 lines at one size, flush left in a corner; the bottom title sits on the yellow wedge.

**Vogue, 1930, and Vanity Fair, 1930–31.** VOGUE is stacked one letter above another as a pier inside a dark trapezium, from V (about 1.0 unit wide) to E (about 2.3 units), each with an extrusion to the upper left in two tones; colours are lilac 41%, yellow 26%, maroon 16%, brown-orange 13%. The July 1930 face is a set of five or six cut shapes (red, maroon, grey-green, pale) on near-black 43%; the hat brim and collar rings are concentric ellipses. The magazine name is a flag on the horn. March 1931 has two similar figures in red, blue, yellow, green on cream 38%, no shading at all; the figure and dog are one symmetrical group of nested arcs and rectangles.

**Skyscrapers and Tunnels, 1930.** Black 34–43% closes both upper corners (100% black in the top left corner). A straight blue bar at 0.68–0.72 of the height divides a city above from tunnels below. Below, two thick rings (each about 0.40 of the width) are almost touching on the axis, with pipes at both edges as matching brackets. Above, four facades lean toward a point above the centre: left red one leans right, the orange one leans left by about 26° from vertical, the red and yellow ones lean right and left (about 35° for the yellow edge), so the skyline is a fan of unequal blocks. Three discs of concentric rings with ticks in bundles of 3–4 spin behind them, plus two small ones lower down; windows repeat in a 4-column grid with unequal rows. Stairs, rails and an arrow in the left tunnel lead the eye in. Exception to the flat rule: cylinders and towers are shaded by short stepped brushstrokes. No figure, no car.

**Cloth: Ballerino di gomma cushion, 1920 / Diavolo, 1920 / Mucca in montagna.** The cushion: red ground `#A41819` (L 35, C 66) with one ochre figure covering 22% of the plate, bounded by x 0.11–0.87, y 0.17–1.0, whose body is three or four ellipses, the limbs long tapers with sharp ends; light hits are pieces of yellow and cream cut to a curve, with a few teeth along one edge. Diavolo puts the same silhouette in black and grey on a diamond quartered in red, yellow, purple, wine. The tapestry has a four-part border, each border a single motif repeated about 8–10 times with alternating colours, separated by narrow grey strips and small corner squares; inside, trees are black or blue combs of 4–8 teeth, and yellow sky is bitten off by green hills. The 1919 oil (Little Rubber Devils) is a stage box of flat-walled steps and stairs in green, yellow, blue and grey with one large red and black figure in front; rows of 4 identical figures shrink in perspective; limbs are the same tapers but softly modelled in smooth gradients, so the cloth pieces drop what the paint still blends (oil plate, frame included: grey-beige 33%, greys 22%, olive 15%, red 11%, ochre 8%).

**Balli plastici, 1918.** Two marionettes on a half-beige, half-blue ground split down the middle by a straight line, the title changing green to ochre as it crosses the line. Bodies are truncated cones, cylinders and discs; the joint is a disc. Beige 38%, blue 20%, green 13%, red 12%, ochre 9%, navy 9%. Red and ochre alternate at each joint. A harlequin lattice fills the gap between the legs. Big letters below and above act as the floor and the ceiling.

**Campari Soda bottle, 1932 (modern example photographed).** A truncated cone with no shoulder and no neck. Ratio on this photo (tilted view): cap 400 px wide, base 560 px, height 1580 px, so about 1 : 1.4 from cap to base and about 1 : 2.8 base to height (approximate). Lettering is in relief on the glass in four curved bands; no paper label; the red liquid (`#B01010` class) is the only colour. *(letter style of the modern edition, not measured as 1932.)*

## Process and relations (observed)
- **One compact outline, usually a square** (title page, DINAMO, A page, Architettura tipografica, "Ha meccanizzato", "Pubblicità"; the disc page is a circle with an arrow outside it). Ten of nineteen formes measured are 0.95–1.02 wide to high; 04, 07, 31, 34, 39, 46, 49, 100, 111 are 0.97–1.02. On an oblong leaf (32 × 24.2, 1.32) the square is held clear by about 0.13–0.15 of the page height per side. What is inside may turn, but the outer edges stay straight and full.
- **Parts are cut to one measure by width, not by size** (DINAMO column, W-fan slab, "DEPERO / NOVANTA" orange page). Several lines in different weights and widths, condensed for long words and extended for short, all forced to the same left and right edge, so the stack reads as courses of brick.
- **Heavy head, small feet: 2 : 1** (title page 2.25; W fan slab vs lanes; pavilion block 1 : 0.71 towers). One dominant element takes 0.33–0.39 of the height and the next is half or less.
- **Word used as a wall, pier, lintel, stool** (Monza pavilion, Campari pavilion 1933, Vogue, Palestra). A letter is a load-bearing unit with no gap to its neighbour, and it is read from one side only. Counters become openings. The same word in relief works with no colour change.
- **One angle pair, perpendicular, per page** (title −49°/+41°; tilted notices 21°/111° against 0°/90°; wedge and stepped word about 38°; Secolo cut). A page has a level system and at most one turned system. Where a turned line meets the level system it does so at a rule or at the edge of the square.
- **Text fills a silhouette that must be recognised at a distance** (D, E, P, E, R, O; A; disc; arrow point). The outline is made only by line lengths; the outside is empty page; the counter is empty page. Emphasis inside is a bold line or wider face at a structural place (the crossbar, the centre cross, the equator).
- **Turn the page to read** (Architettura tipografica, racconto grafico, disc quarters, sideways photographs). Text runs at 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°; one reader action per section. An arrow marks where to begin (plates 33, 34, 100, Palestra, tunnel painting).
- **Counterchange at a cut** (cover, Secolo, Balli plastici title, Bitter Cordial, Se la pioggia). A shape that crosses a boundary swaps colour exactly at the line; the same form therefore stays continuous in two conditions.
- **Shade as facets and teeth** (Bitter Campari, black and white ads, cushions, tapestry trees). Light and dark meet at a straight edge, or, where the surface curves, in 5–9 pointed teeth. Smooth tone exists (Skyscrapers' cylinders, 1931 letter faces) but only in painted work, not in print.
- **Extrude to a direction, then keep the face flat** (Campari 1931, Campari pavilion, Secolo XX, Vogue). Three values: face lightest, side mid, underside darkest, in a ladder of about 20–25 L (84 / 60 / 38 / 5 in Campari 1931). The face is the only readable part; the body trails to one vanishing point.
- **Few colours, flat, in large areas** (Bitter 61% black + 4; Campari 1931 one hue + cream; Secolo 4; bolted book black + one ink). One field dominates (34–70%), two or three hard colours take 10–25% each. The multi-hue exceptions are Squisito al selz and Vido (roughly 6 hues).
- **Same figure language across objects** (marionettes 1918, devils and dancer 1919–20, drinker 1928, Vanity Fair 1930, devils in cloth). Cone, cylinder, disc, ellipse, box; eye as ring; joints as discs; limbs as tapers. The product is put in the figure's hand and made of the same colours.
- **Repetition is a grid of unlike units** (6 ads per page, 9 cushions per page, five DEPERO rows with alternating colour, borders of 8–10 repeats). Plate 78 sets DEPERO five times as a 5 × 6 grid filling the page to the edge, each letter's colour chosen so colours run diagonally; neighbours differ in slope by a few degrees.
- **A change of paper is a change of scene** (violet doors, orange publisher's page, glassine over portraits). One sheet of colour per section, the same black ink.
- **Connection is visible and reversible** (bolts, joint rules). The thing that holds the parts together is the largest, shiniest, unpainted thing, and it is never hidden.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*
*Context: Futurism glorified war, held women in contempt and tied itself to Fascism. None of that is carried here, and no propaganda or war text is used as content.*

**Fortunato Depero.** *Pack one idea into one plain compact block on a quiet ground, fit every line to the same measure by width, let the names of the parts be the structure, rank loudness in steps of two from one head, and swap an element's colour exactly where it crosses a boundary.*
Process:
1. One idea per view, in one compact block with a plain outline: near-square where the format allows (0.95–1.05 wide to high), all four outer edges straight and full. Hold it clear of the frame by 0.13–0.15 of the view's short side. Marks cover 5–20% of a reading view (25–45% inside the block); the rest is one undivided ground, collected around the block and never spread between its parts.
2. Inside the block the parts touch. No gutter: a boundary is a constant-weight rule (the heavy one about 1% of the block's height) or a change of value. Force every line of a stack to the same left and right edge by changing letter width (narrow for long lines, wide or rule-padded for short ones), not size, in stacks of up to about seven lines.
3. Rank loudness in steps of two. One head takes 0.33–0.39 of the block's height and sits at the top or at the hub; the next level is half of it or less (1 : 2.25); credits, place and signature are about a tenth of the head and stand in the corners left over. The whole range reaches about 11 : 1. Size states rank only, because step 2 has already absorbed line length.
4. Make the names the structure. The head spans the block's full width as its top course, group names run as its sides or its foot, flush to the corners, and content sits in the openings they leave. No frame, border or picture does a job a name can do, and each name must still read in one colour by a value step alone.
5. Keep one level system for everything that is read as a sentence. A view may hold one second direction, at right angles within itself, that joins the level system only at a rule or at the block's edge; never a third. On a screen the second direction carries rules and one short label at most.
6. Show the way through. Fix one marker outside the block that points at where reading starts, and ask one action of the reader per section. A new section changes the whole ground and keeps the ink: about one view in seven stands on a different ground, each ground keeping the ink at 4.5 : 1 or better, and the view that opens a section carries a two-word title and nothing else.
7. Spend colour as flat areas with hard edges: one ink on text views, a second colour on at most one view in seven and then on small marks or on the hub alone, so a single change of colour marks the centre. Whatever crosses a boundary between two grounds swaps colour exactly on the line and keeps its outline.
8. Show depth as steps: three flat values 20–25 L apart in one family, meeting at straight edges, the extreme value on the one face that is read. No blur and no blend.
Why it works:
- Lines forced to one measure share two edges, so the stack groups into one object with an outline of its own, and the outline can stay plain while the inside varies. Fit by size instead and the long line shrinks: loudness then reports word length, not rank. Leave the lines ragged and the block's edge becomes noise.
- Empty area collected in one place is ground; the same area spread as equal gutters makes every part a separate figure and no whole. A block with marks on a fifth of the view or less is found at once because nothing else has an edge.
- A size step of two or more reads as rank where a smaller step reads as inconsistency, and a contour that continues across a boundary is seen as one shape, so a colour swap on the line reports which region each half lies in without an outline, at full contrast on both sides. Without the swap the shape sinks into one of the two grounds.
- The eye adapts to the standing ground, so a changed ground registers as a new place before a word is read, and the unchanged ink ties the places together. Change both and the sections share nothing.
Colour relations: ground one quiet field, 80% or more of a reading view; ink one extreme value, the same on every ground; second colour the highest in chroma, on at most one view in seven, as small marks or the hub only, or as one flat layer under level text (27% in the one case measured); section grounds any hue from mid to light value provided the ink holds its contrast; in a display view one field takes 34–70% and two or three flat colours 10–25% each, none tinted toward the ground; depth is a three-value ladder with steps of 20–25 L.
Space and line: one block, near 1 : 1, margins 0.13–0.15 of the short side; full contact inside; rules of constant weight, 0.25–1.2% of the block's height, as rails, lanes and dividers; one level system and at most one perpendicular pair besides; head 0.33–0.39 of the block, next level half or less, smallest a tenth; repeated things as equal slots with unlike content, two colours alternating along the row.
Type requirement: one family with a real range of widths and a heavy weight in the narrow cut, so a line meets the measure without a change of size; display lines in capitals or short-extender lower case, set close enough for courses to touch; a light wide cut for the smallest level. Width fitting is for display stacks only: sentences keep their natural width, a level baseline and normal line spacing, and reflow when the user enlarges text.
Motion and interaction: static are the block, the measure, the ladder, the grounds and every sentence; direction is stated by position, as a row of equal slots in which one property steps the same way each time. What may move: the boundary of step 7 (a fill line travelling under a name, the colour swap following it), a section change as a cut of the ground in one action, the next section shown beforehand as a faint decorative ghost that carries no information, and parts re-posing about fixed joints. The control that holds or reorders the parts stays visible on its own strip (about 0.13 of the width). Reduced motion: every change is a cut to the end state, and nothing is lost because position already states it.
Fails when: the content is long continuous reading (touching courses and fitted widths tire); the screen is narrow enough that the margins and the near-square leave too short a measure; labels change length too much between languages for one width range; a view must hold several ideas of equal weight (dashboards, multi-pane tools); a section ground cannot keep the ink's contrast.
Signature (do not carry together): letters drawn as extruded blocks, piers and furniture · text poured into a giant initial or turned to be read sideways · box-built puppet figures shaded in sawtooth teeth.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Savings-goal view for a credit union, tablet landscape 1180 × 820, pale sage ground with deep green ink, a soft humanist family, calm and domestic (the works are loud, black and commercial).

## Space & composition
- One right-hand leaf per idea; the left is blank. Empty area is the page around a dense block, not between loose parts: ink 5–20% on type pages, 25–45% within an ad block.
- Forme margins about 0.13–0.15 of the page height (smaller on display pages, where rows bleed off).
- Dominant element at top or at the hub; small signatures and place names in the corners left over.
- Scenes are centred but not symmetrical: one tilted product, one leaning body, on a flat ground; the figure spans 0.82 of the poster width and 0.46 of its height.
- City scenes: straight divider at 0.68–0.72, rings below, leaning blocks above; black closes the corners so the bright elements float.
- Absent: gutters between words, soft cards, texture inside an area, landscape horizon lines, ordinary borders round text.

## Form, line & typography
- Letterforms: heavy condensed grotesque capitals for titles; extended light capitals for credits; a few lines of bold italic; lower case for section doors and for the whole introduction of the 1931 Campari booklet (March catalogue notes it has no capitals). Custom lettering has wedge crossbars, joined strokes, dropped triangles at A vertices, letters of unequal height inside a word. Widths are stretched to fit a measure.
- Ratio of sizes on one page reaches about 11 : 1 (277 px to roughly 25 px, title page).
- Lines: constant-weight rules (2–10 px on an 830 px page, the heavy ones about 1% of page height) used as rails, lanes, steps and flag-poles; no hairline shading; curves are circles or ellipse arcs.
- Solids: box, cone, cylinder, disc, ring; edges crisp, outlines rare (white separators of about 2–3 px in black art).
- Reading directions: level, vertical stacked upright letters (A-page pillars, tower letters), vertical rotated, 180°, diagonal, radial. Word spacing is tight; leading is close enough that lines nearly touch in display stacks.
- Marginal signature: small, outside the block, on a different angle.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- Motion is stated by position, not blur: a stepped word climbing, a fan from a corner, a wheel with tick bundles, arrows, leaning blocks, legs split wide.
- Rhythm comes from repeated equal slots with changing content (letter slots, nine spokes at 22.5°, border units 8–10 per side) and from alternation (two colours, black/white) along the row.
- Time passes by handling: turning the book, lifting glassine, taking the bolts off, walking round the pavilion whose words read from different corners. In the 1918 theatre the puppets' disc joints make the same body re-pose.
- Simultaneity: the caption, sign, product and figure hold the same scale of weight and can be seen together; there is no order by distance except extrusion.

## UI translations
*For strict studies and homage. For design, use "Process (derived)".*
1. **Layout:** Put each screen's content in one square block held clear by 13–15% of the viewport height, on cream (`#EEE7D4`); leave the rest of the screen empty.
2. **Layout:** Remove gutters inside the block. Cells touch, divided by black rules 2–10px thick or by a flat colour change.
3. **Type:** Set the screen title as heavy condensed capitals across the block's full width at 33–39% of its height, with the next level at half that or less and credits at a tenth.
4. **Type:** Fit stacked display lines to one measure with a width axis (`font-stretch`), condensed for long lines and extended for short, like courses of brick.
5. **Type as structure:** Let navigation labels form the block's left pier as stacked capitals and the section name its foot; draw no frame.
6. **Color:** Black ink on cream, with one orange-red (`#EF7C42`) on at most one screen in seven, as a small motto, a date or the hub of a diagram.
7. **Color:** For a display screen use black at about 60% with two or three flat high-chroma areas at 10–25% each.
8. **Counterchange:** Where a title crosses a colour boundary, flip its colour on the line (`mix-blend-mode: difference` or two clipped copies).
9. **Depth:** Give a raised element three flat faces 20–25 L apart (face lightest) with hard edges; no `box-shadow` blur, no gradient.
10. **Sections:** Open each section with a full-screen coloured ground (violet-grey `#A799B1`, orange `#FA9E11`, sky blue `#7CA3C2`) carrying a two-word lower-case title in the same black.
11. **Angles:** Allow one tilted system per screen, perpendicular within itself (about 21° and 111°), for rules and short notices only; body text stays level.
12. **Start marker:** Fix one black arrow outside the block pointing at where reading begins.
13. **Repetition:** Lay repeated items in equal slots filling the block to its edge, alternating two colours so the colours run diagonally.
14. **Motion:** Cut between section grounds; show the next section as a pale ghost before it opens; under reduced motion show end states only.

## What this artist would reject
- Elements that float without touching: cards separated by equal gutters.
- Soft shadow, blur or gradient used to describe a solid in print.
- A headline and a picture as separate things, one above the other, when the word can carry the building.
- Several tilt angles on the same surface with no shared axis.
- Text columns whose outline means nothing; ornament brought from another style (his complaint about pavilions in Greek, Roman or baroque dress).
- Hidden fasteners, and a page that can only be held one way.

## Signature prompt seed
Build each screen as one compact, near-square block held clear in an empty ground, with marks on a fifth of the screen or less. Pack the parts edge to edge, divided by constant-weight rules, and fit every display line to the same measure by letter width, not size. Give one head a third of the block's height, the next level half of that, and the smallest a tenth. Let the labels form the block's edges, with no frame. Keep sentences level. Flip an element's colour exactly where it crosses a boundary, show depth as three flat value steps, and change the whole ground, never the ink, for a new section.
