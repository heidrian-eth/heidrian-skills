# Wassily Kandinsky — Bauhaus

## Essence
Kandinsky treated the picture plane as a field of forces in which every point, line and colour has a temperature, a direction and a sound. At the Bauhaus he made this a teachable grammar: point, line, plane; triangle-yellow, square-red, circle-blue. Composition becomes orchestration: tuned dissonances held around one dominant circle.

## Key works examined
- **Composition VII, 1913** (Tretyakov): pre-Bauhaus maximal density, still readable because one centre-left vortex organises it. *(visually checked: secondary source, thehistoryofart.org, 400 px only)* Seen: the vortex is real: a dark blue-black oval knot with concentric strokes at ≈30–45% across and mid-height, with lines and colour patches swirling round it; a second, smaller dark cluster sits at the right edge at mid-height. Pale low-chroma ground is only ≈10% of the surface and dark ≈16%; the rest is saturated, overlapping colour patches. So this is the inverse of the Bauhaus works checked (ground 65–75%): an all-over field held together by one dark centre of gravity.
- **Composition VIII, 1923** (Guggenheim): his first methodical colour–form painting, made at Weimar. A large black circle sits upper-left as an anchor, with sharp lines and triangles scattered across a cool, light ground. *(visually checked: Guggenheim image. The black disc has a violet core and a soft red-pink halo; it is 20% of the width (25% with halo), centred 12% from the left and 20% from the top. The ground is cream at the top fading to cool blue-grey at the bottom. 40+ straight lines in two weights)*
- **Bauhaus questionnaire, 1923**: students matched three shapes to three primaries; a design system can start as a mapping table.
- **Yellow-Red-Blue, 1925** (Pompidou): warm angular yellow left, cool round blue right; one screen, two opposed temperatures. *(visually checked: Centre Pompidou image. The yellow is an upright rectangle, not a triangle; a thick black serpentine line and a black diagonal bar cross the right half; violet clouds vignette the edges)*
- **Several Circles, 1926** (Guggenheim, 140.7 × 140.3 cm): about 30 overlapping translucent circles on a near-black square. It teaches layered transparency and hierarchy carried by size alone. *(visually checked: Guggenheim press image. The dominant disc is a black core inside a blue ring, 46% of the width, centred 42% from the left and 36% from the top, with a feathered white corona; the ground is a mottled charcoal, not black)*
- **Point and Line to Plane, 1926** (Bauhaus Book 9): the "Basic Plane" (BP), whose edges and orientations carry weight and temperature. It shows that a layout canvas is never neutral.
- **Concerning the Spiritual in Art, 1911**: colour as instrument (yellow = trumpet, light blue = flute, dark blue = cello, white = pause). It supplies a ready vocabulary for sound and motion design.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Composition VII, 1913): an all-over field with no empty ground; one dark knot centre-left acts as the centre of gravity, a second smaller dark mass answers it at the right edge.
- **Colour relations** (Composition VII): saturated hues everywhere, in overlapping patches; value, not hue, creates the hierarchy (the darkest marks gather at the vortex); pale areas (≈10%) act as breathing gaps, not ground.
- **The procedure** (Composition VII): fill the field densely with overlapping coloured patches and lines; concentrate the darkest values in one off-centre knot so everything reads as orbiting it; answer it with one smaller dark mass at the far edge.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Wassily Kandinsky.** *Set opposed forces against each other on a charged, mostly open plane: one heavy pole that holds the field, lighter counter-forces that pull away from it, and every role distinct in several properties at once.*
Process:
1. Tone the whole plane first as an uneven ground: graded from one side to the other by 5–10 L or a shift of temperature, never flat. In the open mode it keeps 65–75% of the area.
2. Place the heavy pole: the darkest mass, round, 20–46% of the width, its centre 12–42% across and 20–36% down, so it sits toward a corner. Give it the largest soft edge on the surface.
3. Answer it on the far side with a counter-mass 1/3–1/2 its size that opposes it in at least two properties (lighter, warmer, more angular, harder). The surface now has two poles and a direction between them.
4. Scatter satellites at 1/3 to 1/12 of the pole in clusters, almost all hard-edged, a few echoing the soft edge at small size. Keep density uneven and leave one quadrant nearly silent (under 5% covered).
5. Bundle the contrasts: every role differs from every other in two or more properties together (shape, temperature, value, edge). The most advancing bundle (warm, light, acute) is the smallest, ≤5% of the area.
6. Where two forms overlap, give the overlap its own third tone, lying between the two and never darker than both.
7. Optional: add lines in two weights about 1 : 5 apart (≈0.2% and ≈1% of the width), oblique, starting in a cluster and ending in open ground, never closing a box.
Why it works:
- Visual weight: a dark, round, soft-edged mass off-centre acts as a centre of gravity, and everything else reads as held by it or escaping from it. Centred, it becomes a target; with no answer on the far side, the plane tips and reads as one object on a background.
- Redundant coding: when shape, temperature and value change together, a role is recognised from any one of them, and the opposition reads as a force, not a colour choice. Change one property alone and the roles blur into decoration.
- A soft edge reads as atmosphere or emitted light and detaches a form from the plane. Kept rare, it marks the pole; given to everything, the surface turns to fog and nothing is near or far.
Colour relations: uneven low-chroma ground 65–75%; the pole holds the darkest value; warm, light, high-chroma notes are the smallest (2–5%); the largest coloured mass is cool; overlaps are a third tone; flat uniform ground and pure paper white are excluded.
Space and line: pole 20–46% of the width, off-centre toward a corner; counter-mass 1/3–1/2 of it; satellites 1/3–1/12; clusters beside open fields, one silent quadrant; two line weights 1 : 5, oblique, open-ended; lines are optional.
Type requirement: two voices that oppose in at least two properties (round and even against sharp and narrow; heavy against light); one word or figure may act as the pole; text sits only on quiet ground, never over clusters, overlaps or the graded soft edge.
Motion and interaction: interaction moves an element along the bundle, never in size or height. An action steps lighter, warmer and harder-edged, fast (120–160ms); focus steps darker and cooler and takes the soft edge as its ring, slowly (400–600ms); committed states change one property and stop dead. Leave a rest of ≥200ms after a burst. Interactive satellites keep a 44px hit area whatever their drawn size.
Fails when: the content is uniform rows, a form or long reading (no poles to set); the ground falls under about half without switching to the dense mode below; satellites are decoration behind text; every element is interactive, so small scattered targets multiply.
Signature (do not carry together): a black-cored, haloed disc at upper left · yellow triangle, red square, blue circle · crossing black diagonals on cream.
Mode (dense): pale ground about 10%; saturated patches cover the rest; the darkest values gather in one off-centre knot (30–45% across) with a smaller dark mass at the far edge. One work only.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Product: personal-finance overview, desktop 1440 × 900, monochrome grey-green, numeric and sober. The source is polychrome, lyrical and non-figurative.
1. *Ground.* Pale grey-green graded from L 93 at top left to L 86 at bottom right, with a slight coolness toward the bottom. 70% of the screen stays open.
2. *Pole.* Net balance: a round-cornered dark panel (L 22), 400px wide (28%), centred at 24% across and 30% down, with a 40px feathered edge two steps lighter than itself. It is the only large soft edge.
3. *Counter-mass.* Bills due this week, lower right at 78% / 70%: 170px wide (0.42 of the pole), lightest surface (L 97), square corners, hard edge. Opposite in value, corner and edge.
4. *Satellites.* Category figures as hard-edged tiles 34–130px wide in two clusters, one beside the pole and one above the counter-mass. The bottom-left quadrant carries only a date.
5. *Bundles.* Three roles. Hold (balance, savings): dark, rounded, soft. Commit (scheduled payments): mid value, square, hard. Act (transfer, pay): the lightest and warmest tone on the screen, a sharp-cornered wedge-ended button, 2% of the area. Each differs from the others in three properties.
6. *Overlap.* Budget and spending bars overlap; the shared length is a third green between the two, so overspend is visible as the part outside the overlap.
7. *Lines.* A 2px rule and a 10px bar run obliquely from the pole's cluster toward the open quadrant as a cash-flow trend and stop in empty ground.
- *Interaction.* Pressing "transfer" steps it one value lighter and warmer in 140ms with its corners unchanged; nothing grows. Keyboard focus puts a soft ring on the focused tile over 450ms and removes it from the pole's siblings. Tiles under 44px keep a 44px hit area.
Result: a calm grey-green sheet with one dark anchor and one pale answer. No primaries, no circles, no black diagonals; a viewer would not name the source. The two-pole structure and the bundled roles survived.

## Space & composition
- **The canvas has a weather.** In *Point and Line to Plane*, horizontal reads as "cold rest" and vertical as "warm rest". Top is light and loose, bottom heavy; left is "airier", right compressed. A light element floats top-left and feels trapped bottom-right, so place content by urgency.
- **One dominant circle, many satellites.** Composition VIII and Several Circles both hang everything off a single large disc, offset from centre toward the upper left (Composition VIII: 20% of the width; Several Circles: 46%). In both the disc has a black core inside a coloured ring and carries the largest soft, feathered halo. It is not the only haloed form: in Composition VIII two smaller discs also have soft halos (the yellow disc lower left in a blue-violet halo, the blue disc lower centre in a yellow halo), while most other satellites are hard-edged. Smaller elements orbit it at 1/3 to 1/12 its diameter.
- **Diagonals carry the drama.** The structure is a lattice of crossing diagonals, not a column grid. Lines start at a point and end in empty space. Composition VIII has 40+ lines in two weights: hairlines ≈0.2% of the width and heavy bars ≈1%. Several Circles has no lines at all, so lines are optional, not required.
- **Density is uneven, never uniform.** (Composition VII 1913 is the maximal exception: pale ground ≈10%, organised by one dark vortex centre-left.) Clusters sit beside open fields. Measured ground share is 65–75% (Composition VIII: cream plus grey ≈76%; Several Circles: charcoal ≈70%). The ground is a mottled, tinted field, cream, warm grey or charcoal, never white.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

Temperature plus movement. Yellow pushes outward (eccentric, sharp, acute angles); blue pulls inward (concentric, calm, the circle); red is the steady, earthbound square. White is a silence before sound, black a final silence. He called gray soundless, yet the measured grounds are low-chroma greys: Composition VIII's ground is 39% warm grey `#D6D1C7` plus 11% cool grey `#A5A8AB`, and Several Circles is 70% neutral charcoal (chroma ≈0). Avoid flat, uniform grey; a mottled warm or charcoal grey as ground is faithful.

| Role | Hex | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Ground (light mode) | `#E9E0C4` | warm parchment, not white (Composition VIII, top) |
| Ground (dark mode) | `#2E2E2F` | Several Circles charcoal, lighter than near-black |
| Primary action / alert | `#F3BD0D` | trumpet yellow, triangle (Yellow-Red-Blue) |
| Structure / commit | `#BA1B1E` | earth red, square (Yellow-Red-Blue) |
| Focus / calm / selected | `#193370` | cello blue, circle (Composition VIII) |
| Accent line | `#0C0B0D` | black linework and disc cores |
| Secondary harmony | `#75569A` | violet, bassoon, rare (Composition VIII) |
| Ground fade (light mode) | `#A5A8AB` | added: cool blue-grey the light ground fades into toward the bottom (Composition VIII, ≈11%) |

Keep yellow rare: measured 2.1% (Composition VIII) and 5.3% plus 7.7% ochre (Yellow-Red-Blue). Blue takes the largest coloured mass in Yellow-Red-Blue (9.7%) and Several Circles (blue ring 5.5%); in Composition VIII the red-pink halo is larger (4.2%). Several Circles' colours are muted in the museum image (its yellows sample `#AE9E4A`).

## Form, line & typography
A line is the track of a moving point: straight lines are tense, curves lyrical, zigzags high-pitched. In type, a geometric sans with a true-circle "o" is the circle; a sharp display face is the triangle. Dividers are hairlines (0.75–1px) that stop short and never close a box. Pair them with a few heavy bars (≈1% of the width), as in Composition VIII.

Fonts: **Jost** (Futura-like Bauhaus geometric, for body and UI), **Syne** (angular, eccentric display for headlines), **Space Mono** (for "point"-like numerals and data labels).

## Rhythm, motion & time
Treat motion as instrumentation:
- **Yellow/triangle elements** move fast and outward: 120–160ms, `cubic-bezier(.2,.9,.3,1.4)` with a slight overshoot, scaling up.
- **Blue/circle elements** move slowly and inward: 400–600ms, ease-in-out, scaling down and settling toward the centre.
- **Red/square elements** don't bounce. They translate on a single axis and stop dead.

Pace interactions with rests: a white "pause" of 200ms or more after each burst. Stagger lists in a musical meter (3+2 beats), not an even 50ms cascade.

## UI translations
1. Build a shape–colour token table: `--alert: triangle/yellow`, `--commit: square/red`, `--focus: circle/blue`. Never mix pairs.
2. Anchor each screen with one large circle (avatar, progress ring, hero disc) at 20–46% of viewport width, centred in the upper-left third. Give it a dark core, a coloured ring and a soft halo (`box-shadow: 0 0 0 12px` blurred, the largest soft edge on screen; at most one or two small satellites may carry a smaller halo, as in Composition VIII). Arrange mostly hard-edged satellites around it.
3. Run one key divider or progress path as a 1px diagonal (8–20°) across the layout, not horizontal.
4. Set light-mode grounds to `#E9E0C4` (fading toward `#A5A8AB` at the bottom) and dark-mode grounds to `#2E2E2F`, with a faint mottled texture. Forbid pure `#FFFFFF` and flat, uniform greys.
5. Make overlapping cards translucent (normal blend at ≈50–60% opacity, not multiply) so each overlap becomes a third tone, as in Several Circles, where a pink circle over the black disc reads as a lighter mauve.
6. Put calm, reading content top-left and urgent, compressed content (CTAs, warnings) bottom-right, where the plane is heaviest.
7. Animate primary CTAs with a fast eccentric scale-out; animate selection and focus with a slow concentric scale-in ring.
8. Pair Syne headlines with Jost body. Set one headline word at 3–4× body size and rotate it −6°.
9. Map sound to colour: confirmation is a low soft "cello" tone, an alert is a short bright "trumpet" ping, a disabled tap is silent.
10. Leave at least one quadrant nearly empty except for a single 8–12px dot. That is the "point", silence with speech. Keep 65–75% of the screen as open ground.
11. On hover, let lines extend from points (draw with `stroke-dashoffset`) toward the cursor, so the user's movement turns points into lines.

## What this artist would reject
- Neutral gray UI chrome. Gray is "soundless immobility".
- Centred, symmetrical hero sections with a uniform 12-column rhythm.
- Colour used only as brand decoration, with no meaning attached to form.
- Rounded rectangles for everything. Shape should signal a role (circle, square or triangle), not be a default radius.
- Even, metronomic stagger animations and identical easing on every element.
- Pure white backgrounds treated as "nothing". White is an active pause.

## Signature prompt seed
Design this screen as a Kandinsky Bauhaus composition. Start from a mottled warm parchment (or charcoal) basic plane, then hang everything off one dominant dark-cored disc with a blue ring and a soft halo, offset to the upper-left. Map role to shape and colour strictly: yellow triangles for alerts, red squares for commitments, blue circles for focus. Cross the layout with one or two hairline diagonals that start at dots and end in empty space. Leave one quadrant silent. Overlaps are translucent third tones, not multiplied. Typography is Jost with angular Syne accents. Motion is orchestral: yellow snaps outward fast, blue settles inward slowly, and rests sit between phrases.
