# Paul Klee — Bauhaus

## Essence
Klee treated a picture as a small machine for growing form: a dot moves and becomes a line, the line closes into a plane, planes are tiled into a grid of slightly-off squares, and colour is stepped across that grid like notes on a stave. Everything rests on a strict, teachable system, yet the result is small, wry and handmade, with a pictographic wit (arrows, suns, stick birds) laid over it.

## Key works examined
- **Pedagogical Sketchbook (Bauhausbuch 2), 1925.** Distilled from about 3,900 pages of lecture notes, it runs point, line, plane, balance, gravity, then colour and motion. It teaches growth as a sequence of operations.
- **Twittering Machine, 1922 (MoMA).** Oil transfer, watercolour and ink, 64.1 × 48.3 cm. Wire-thin birds on a sine-wave branch ending in a crank, over a blue-to-pink wash: hairline over gradient. *(visually checked: secondary source, paulklee.net)* Seen: the wash is not a top-to-bottom ramp but a centred field: cool blue in the middle, rose and violet bleeding in at the top, the right side and along the bottom, inside a thin grey-blue border. The drawing is one even hairline (≈0.3% of the width) with blotted oil-transfer smudges; the four birds stand on one sagging wire across the middle band (≈35–60% down), the crank handle breaks out to the right, and a thin pink table plane sits at the bottom. The figures fill only ≈1/3 of the sheet; the rest is wash.
- **Static-Dynamic Gradation, 1923 (Met).** Oil and gouache, 43.5 × 29.2 cm. Rectangles step from dark edges to a light core: gradation as structure. *(visually checked: secondary source, arthistoryproject.com)* Seen: ≈8 columns × 11 rows of hand-painted, slightly irregular tiles (each ≈12% of the width), not quite aligned row to row. Lightness and chroma both climb ring by ring toward a vertical core: measured from the outer band in, mean luminance 59 → 78 → 94 → 101 and chroma 24 → 55 → 75 → 82. The outer ring (≈44% of the area) is dark browns, greys and near-black; the next ring warm oranges and olives; the core two columns hold the only pure accents: two white tiles (≈1.5% together), one green, two blues, bright reds. No line drawing at all.
- **Ancient Sound, Abstract on Black, 1925.** *(not visually checked)* Oil on cardboard, about 15 in. square. Muted squares on black, bright cells pulsing: the magic square as colour score.
- **Castle and Sun, 1928.** Stacked rectangles, triangles and one red disc: modules read as a city. *(visually checked: secondary source, thehistoryofart.org)* Seen: a deep maroon ground (≈46% of the surface) runs through as the colour of many cells and the open sky; on it, cells in 6–7 horizontal bands of roughly equal height, divided by thin light hairlines. Cells get smaller and denser toward the bottom (wide blocks and tall towers above, small squares and rows of triangles as roofs below). The disc is not a bright red but a rust-orange only a little lighter than the ground (≈16% of the width, ≈2.4% of the area), set upper centre-right in the open sky. The brightest notes (vermilion ≈13%, yellow ≈4%, a few cobalt and turquoise squares) are scattered as single cells, never grouped.
- **Ad Parnassum, 1932 (Kunstmuseum Bern).** Oil, 100 × 126 cm. Muted squares, then stamped small white and tinted squares, then stamped lines tracing a pyramid and orange sun: polyphony as layers at different scales. *(visually checked: secondary source, paulklee.net; earlier Mosaic-Like and Sunset were used as same-period substitutes)* Seen: the three strata are confirmed: soft colour blocks of varying size (roughly 1/8–1/12 of the width) as the bass; over them an all-over lattice of small stamped light dashes in rows (pitch ≈1% of the width, finer than the 1/60 stated below); and on top a few thin dark lines: the pyramid's two sides meeting near the top edge, a round-arched doorway and a wedge at the bottom, plus one bright yellow beam crossing the middle. The orange sun (≈9% of the width across, ≈0.8% of the area) sits upper right, outside the pyramid. Warm oranges dominate inside the pyramid, cool blue-greys outside it.)*
- **Sunset, 1930 (AIC)** *(added; visually checked)*: warm terracotta ground; translucent tinted planes; dot lattices in blue and red that fill and define shapes; clean thin contour lines in red and blue over them; one red disc (0.7% of the surface, ≈8% of the width) with a tiny down-arrow beneath it, and an eye-sun drawn in hairline at top left.
- **Mosaic-Like, 1932 (AIC)** *(added; visually checked)*: meandering bands, each filled with a stippled dot texture, separated by dark warm-brown channels; blue the only cool note.
- **Tightrope Walker, 1923 (CMA)** *(added; visually checked)*: colour lithograph; pink field darkening toward top and bottom edges, a pale cross of two white bands, and a grainy, broken transfer line; the balance model named below. Mounted with a ruled line and a handwritten date/number beneath.
- **Architecture, 1921 (AIC)** *(added; visually checked)*: oil-transfer drawing over watercolour; dark brown edges vignette a light centre; one tiny red flag is the only saturated note.
- **Carnival in the Snow, 1923 (CMA)** *(added; visually checked)*: watercolour of pale figures on a mottled violet-grey field with many small red, blue and black accents: the exception to the one-accent rule.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Static-Dynamic Gradation): one tile size over the whole field; hierarchy is concentric, a tall core two columns wide. Nothing is cropped or empty.
- **Space** (Ad Parnassum): one large triangle whose apex nearly touches the top edge, filling ≈60% of the field; one small disc outside it, upper right; one horizontal beam at mid-height. Three scales stacked (blocks, dashes, lines) rather than placed side by side.
- **Colour relations** (Ad Parnassum): warm inside the figure, cool outside it; the single most saturated element (orange disc) is the smallest (<1%) and stands in the cool zone.
- **Space** (Twittering Machine): figures occupy only the middle third; the rest is atmospheric wash, centred cool, warm at the margins.
- **The procedure** (Ad Parnassum): lay a coarse patchwork of blocks; stamp a fine regular lattice of light marks over everything; draw a few thin lines to define one big shape; add one small saturated disc in the opposite temperature zone.
- **Space** (Castle and Sun): horizontal bands of equal height, subdivided more finely toward the bottom; the top band left mostly open as sky with one disc in it.
- **Colour relations** (Castle and Sun): one dark warm ground (≈46%) shared by sky and many cells; bright hues each confined to single, non-adjacent cells; the disc is close in value to the ground, so it reads by shape and texture, not contrast.
- **The procedure** (Castle and Sun): pick a dark ground; cut the field into equal bands; subdivide each band more finely the lower it is; give a few scattered cells a pure hue and leave the rest in the ground colour; add one disc near the ground's value in the open top band.
- **Colour relations** (Static-Dynamic Gradation): colours step by rings; the most saturated and lightest cells are the smallest group and sit in the centre; the outer ring is the largest and dullest (≈44%). Complements are placed side by side only in the core (blue beside orange, green beside red); the edge tiles are close neighbours in hue and value.
- **Line and edge:** no lines; tile edges are soft, hand-brushed, separated by thin darker seams.
- **The procedure:** tile the field in one module; assign each tile a ring by its distance from a central axis; raise lightness and chroma one step per ring inward; place the few pure or white tiles only in the core, next to their complements.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Paul Klee.** *Grow the surface in strata from one small module: tile the whole field, step the tone tile by tile toward a core, then let a fine lattice, a single thin line and one odd note travel over it.*
Process:
1. Choose one module, 1/8–1/12 of the width, and tile the entire field with it. Nothing is left empty and nothing is cropped; tiles may be slightly unequal, but no tile is promoted by size.
2. Choose a core two modules wide (an axis or a zone) and give every tile a ring number by its distance from the core.
3. Step tone by ring, 3–4 steps: value and chroma rise together toward the core. The outermost ring is the largest (40–45% of the area) and the dullest, and its tiles are near neighbours in hue and value.
4. Reserve the extremes for the smallest group: the lightest tiles (≤2% of the area) and the pure fills, each a single tile, no two alike adjacent, set in the core beside their opposites. Or replace them with one round note of 1–2.5% of the area placed in the open zone of the opposite temperature.
5. Lay a second stratum over everything at about 1/10 of the module: an even lattice of small marks (pitch ≈1% of the width, mark ≈40% of the pitch). Its density, not an outline, fills and defines areas.
6. Draw last, with one line weight (≈0.3% of the width): a few lines that outline one large figure (up to about 60% of the field) across the tiles without displacing any. A line may end in a pointer.
Why it works:
- Grouping by gradation: with size held constant, the eye reads the tone steps as a slow approach to the core, so hierarchy needs no large element. Make the tiles unequal in importance by size and the field breaks into cards.
- Scale separation: strata about ten times apart in grain do not compete; the coarse one is read as colour, the fine one as texture, the line as a figure. At similar scales they merge into noise.
- Salience by scarcity: purity and extreme value confined to under 2% make those cells ring against a dull majority. Spread them and the field flattens to confetti.
Colour relations: outer ring dull and dark, 40–45%; each ring inward lighter and more chromatic; pure fills as single non-adjacent tiles; opposites touch only in the core; the one odd note stands in the zone of opposite temperature; the ground tone may double as the fill of many tiles.
Space and line: one module over the whole field; hierarchy concentric, not by size; three grains (module, 1/10 module, hairline); one line weight; an outer margin of 4–8% of the short side with a hairline and small caption beneath, so the whole reads as an object of hand size.
Type requirement: letters that sit in a module like a tile (even widths, tabular figures), stroke close to the drawn line's weight, modest sizes with rank carried by ring tone; text only on tiles that give ≥4.5:1, with no lattice under reading text.
Motion and interaction: things appear in the order they were made: tiles step tone ring by ring (30–40ms a step), the lattice follows, and the line draws itself last (900–1400ms; instantly under reduced motion). Selecting a tile makes it the core: the rings re-step around it by value and chroma, and nothing moves or grows. Pointers carry a text label.
Fails when: the content is one hero image or video (the tiling competes with it); long reading text; fewer than about a dozen units (no rings can form); several saturated alarms must show at once, which destroys the scarcity the core depends on.
Signature (do not carry together): hand-wobbled colour squares · a small red sun with an arrow · wiry pictograms with a handwritten caption.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Product: team availability calendar, desktop 1440 × 900, dark cool slate, operational. The source is warm, small, handmade and playful.
1. *Module.* The day cell: 7 columns, each about 1/9 of the width with margins, 5 rows, 35 tiles covering the whole work area. No day is larger than another.
2. *Core.* The current week's Wednesday and Thursday columns across today's row; every day gets a ring number 0–3 by its distance in days.
3. *Steps.* Ring 3 (15 tiles, 43%): slate L 16, chroma 2. Ring 2: L 21, chroma 5. Ring 1: L 27, chroma 8. Ring 0: L 34, chroma 11. Past and far-future days are the dull majority.
4. *Extremes.* Today is the single lightest tile (L 82, 1.4% of the area). Two "all hands" days are pure-fill single tiles, never adjacent. The release deadline is one round warm marker, 1% of the area, placed in ring 3 where everything is cool and dull.
5. *Lattice.* Booked hours as a tick lattice at 14px pitch, tick 6px, across every tile; dense ticks read as busy days with no outline or number. Day numerals sit in a lattice-free corner at L 90.
6. *Line.* The critical path is one 2px line running tile to tile from today to the deadline, drawn over the cells and ending in a pointer labelled "release".
- *Type.* A tabular grotesque, 13px, one weight; rank comes from the ring, not from size.
- *Interaction.* Selecting another day makes it the core: rings re-step in 4 × 35ms, then the path redraws in 1.1s. With reduced motion both swap at once.
- *Check.* Enlarge today to a 2 × 2 tile: the grid reads as a card on a background, so it stays one module.
Result: a dark slate month whose tone rises toward now, with one warm dot far out. No painted squares, no sun, no pictograms.

## Space & composition
- **The grid wobbles** *(not verified: no magic-square painting obtained)*. Klee's "magic squares" are a near-regular grid of roughly 8–12 columns. The cells vary by ±10–20% in width and none are perfectly square. Tracks should look hand-ruled, not snapped to a framework.
- **Structure first, then figure.** The ground is a full tiling, with no empty margin. The figure (a pyramid, a sun, an arrow) is drawn *over* the tiling as a hairline, so it never knocks cells out.
- **Polyphonic layering.** Ad Parnassum uses three strata. Large blocks, about 1/8 of the width, make the bass. Small stamped dashes, about 1/100 of the width (seen, retry pass 2; earlier given as 1/60), are the melody. Linework is the voice.
- **Framed small.** Painted borders or mount margins (about 4–8% of the short side) make works read as cards, not full-bleed spreads. On the prints and watercolours a hairline rule runs under the image with the date and title handwritten small beneath it (Tightrope Walker, Carnival in the Snow).
- **Vignette.** Confirmed in Architecture and Tightrope Walker: the field darkens toward the edges and opens to a light core.
- **Balance by counterweight, not symmetry.** The tightrope walker and the black arrow of gravity are the model. One heavy accent (a red disc, a single arrow) balances a whole field.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

The logic is gradation plus one outlier. Hues step through 5–9 near-neighbour values across the grid, from dark at the edges to light at the core, or warm to cool. Then a single odd note breaks the series, odd by shape, temperature or saturation rather than always the brightest: in Sunset the red disc is 0.7% of the surface, in Architecture the red flag is smaller still, and in Castle and Sun the disc is a dull rust only a little lighter than the ground, reading by shape (Carnival in the Snow is the exception, with many accents). The checked grounds are warm: terracotta and sand (Sunset, Architecture) rather than violet-black; the darkest real tone is the warm brown-black of Mosaic-Like's channels. Colour is also motion: Klee pictured it travelling between pure black and pure white.

## Form, line & typography
Klee's line is a single unbroken stroke that wanders. It loops, zigzags and ends in arrows or tiny pictograms. In the painted contours (Sunset) it is a clean, constant thin line; in the oil-transfer drawings (Tightrope Walker, Architecture) it is grainy and slightly broken, with blots and smudges, so a little texture on the stroke is faithful. Type should feel drawn with the same pen: slightly naive, geometric, not engineered. Use **Josefin Sans** for headings (its 1920s geometric vintage fits), **Gaegu** or **Patrick Hand** sparingly for annotations, and **Karla** for body text. He painted letters as image elements, so a display character can sit in a grid cell like a tile.

## Rhythm, motion & time
His motion models are the pendulum, the spinning top, darting aquarium fish and the line "on a walk."
- **Lines draw on.** Animate SVG paths with `stroke-dashoffset` at a slow, slightly irregular pace (900–1400 ms, `cubic-bezier(.3,.1,.3,1)`), as if being drawn.
- **Grid cells step tone in sequence**, staggered 30–40 ms in reading order like an arpeggio, never all at once.
- **Pendulum ease.** Settle interactive feedback with one damped overshoot, not a spring wobble.
- **Counterpoint.** Dots drift slowly while blocks stay still.

## UI translations
1. Build the page background as a CSS grid of 8–12 tinted tiles whose `grid-template-columns` use unequal `fr` values (`1.1fr .9fr 1fr 1.2fr…`), not a flat colour.
2. Draw the primary navigation path (onboarding progress, a breadcrumb, a step flow) as one continuous 1.5px SVG line that "walks" between steps and ends in an arrowhead.
3. Tint cards from a 5-step `oklch` ramp. The card nearest the user's focus is lightest and the rest darken toward the edges.
4. Allow exactly one odd note per screen, set apart by shape and temperature and not necessarily the most saturated (e.g. `#D02911`, or a dull rust disc near the ground's value as in Castle and Sun; ideally a circle ≈8% of the width and ≤1% of the area, with a small arrow under it as in Sunset). Everything else stays in the muted gradient.
5. Use arrows as semantic glyphs. A heavy black downward arrow marks destructive or "sink" actions. A thin feathered arrow marks forward or creative actions.
6. Layer polyphonically. Put a staggered dot lattice (`radial-gradient`, pitch ≈1% of the panel width, dot ≈40% of the pitch, as measured in Sunset) over the tile layer, then hairline icons on top, with deliberately different spacing on each layer. Dots may fill a shape outlined by a hairline instead of covering everything.
7. Frame key panels with a 6% inset painted border (`outline` plus a contrasting `padding` band) so they read as small framed works, with a hairline rule and a small handwritten caption beneath.
8. Set headings in Josefin Sans at modest sizes (no larger than 2.5rem) and put hand-drawn annotations in the margins.
9. On hover, let a cell step one tone lighter and grow a pictogram (sun, eye, bird) in a hairline stroke.
10. Animate loaders as a dot that becomes a line that closes into a square. Point, line, plane in about 1.2s.

## What this artist would reject
- Perfectly uniform 12-column grids with identical card sizes.
- Flat single-hue surfaces with no tonal steps.
- Multiple competing accent colours and a gradient on every button.
- Heavy, filled, rounded-blob icon sets (he drew with a thin, inquisitive line).
- Bouncy spring physics and decorative motion with no growth logic.

## Signature prompt seed
Design this app screen as a Klee "magic square." Lay a hand-ruled grid of 8–12 unequal tiles stepping in muted ochre-to-twilight-blue gradation across the whole background, darker at the edges and lighter toward the content. Over it, run independent layers: a fine stamped dot texture, then hairline monoline icons and one continuous wandering line that links the steps of the task and ends in an arrow. Allow a single red-orange sun as the primary action. Use modest geometric type in a framed, intimate card. Paths draw themselves on, and tiles brighten in staggered arpeggios.
