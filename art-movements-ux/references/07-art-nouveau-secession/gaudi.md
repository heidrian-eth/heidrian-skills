# Antoni Gaudí — Catalan Modernisme / Art Nouveau

## Essence
Gaudí didn't draw his forms first. He let forces find them. He is said to have hung weighted strings and turned the result upside down, so every curve is the shape a load wants to take *(the hanging model was not obtained; unverified here)*. Over that honest structure he laid a skin of broken, glittering colour that shifts with the sun. Structure is ornament, nature is the grid, and there are almost no straight lines.

## Key works examined
- **Colònia Güell hanging model (c.1898–1908)**: a 1:10 model of strings and weights, with cloth cutouts to show the inverted building. It teaches you to derive form from constraints rather than impose it. *(not obtained; unverified)*
- **Park Güell (1900–1914)**: 86 Neo-Doric columns that collect rainwater, inclined viaduct columns, and the modular trencadís serpentine bench (1909–14, with Jujol). Infrastructure doubles as delight. *(visually checked: parkguell.barcelona photos of the bench, the hypostyle room and the lower viaduct. On the bench, white/cream broken tile is the ground (~37–40% of a close view), holding framed inset panels of reused patterned tile in cobalt, green, ochre and red. Shards vary about tenfold in size, the grout is thin and pale, and a continuous rounded glazed rim (ochre, olive, green) caps every edge. The hypostyle's sandstone columns wear a white tile collar on the lower ~17% and carry one radiating sun medallion overhead. The viaduct's rubble columns lean visibly.)*
- **Casa Batlló facade (1904–06)**: bone-like columns, cast-iron mask balconies, a wavy ceramic-and-glass skin and a scaled roof whose colours shift end to end. A single surface can tell a story. *(visually checked: casabatllo.es photos. The facade is pale lime plaster (`#C6C5BB`) speckled with a sparse confetti of glass discs and shards (blue ~9%, ochre ~2%), not a dense trencadís. Behind the skin is a strict grid: three window columns, each floor carrying identical cream mask balconies and sage shutters. The roof scales shade from blue-grey (`#3C5C71`) to a mauve ridge (`#604A4E`) under green ball caps. The bay-window mullions are tapered, bone-like and thickest at the joints.)*
- **Sagrada Família nave columns (designed 1910s–20s)**: star-section shafts that double-twist into circles and branch like trees. A strict generative rule yields organic results. *(visually checked: sagradafamilia.org official photo gallery, images 1744193 and 1744199)* Seen: pale grey stone shafts rise unbroken for roughly half the nave height to an ellipsoidal knot, then split into 2–4 thinner, inclined branches that fan out under the vault; the inner columns near the crossing are darker (reddish-grey porphyry) and thicker, so material and girth step by position. The vault is white, studded with star-shaped openings in regular rows. The point-count geometry itself (12/10/8/6 points) cannot be counted from these photos.
- **Sagrada Família stained glass (with Joan Vila-Grau)**: cool blues on the east (Nativity), warm reds and oranges on the west (Passion). Colour is assigned by orientation and time of day. *(visually checked: sagradafamilia.org official photo gallery, images 1744145, 1744184, 1744190, 1744214, 1744229 and 1744232)* Seen: the gallery's own caption confirms the Nativity windows are bluer; the opposite side reads amber and orange (1744184). Within one wall the hue does not stay constant: neighbouring bays step yellow → green → turquoise → blue (1744229), each bay having one dominant hue built from many small panes. Colour lives mainly in the aisle windows; the high vault and its openings stay white or clear. The coloured light lands on the pale columns, floor and benches as large soft-edged fields, so the stone itself becomes the coloured surface.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Sagrada Família nave): a field of identical vertical shafts at a regular interval; each splits at about mid-height into 2–4 inclined branches, so the lower half is orderly columns and the upper half a dense canopy. Mass is heavy and dark only at the centre (thicker porphyry columns), lighter and thinner outward.
- **Colour relations** (glass): many small saturated panes per window, but each bay is read as one dominant hue; adjacent bays differ by one step around the wheel (yellow → green → turquoise → blue), so the wall is a slow hue gradient made of discrete units. The neutral stone (≈ most of the view) is the carrier, and the colour reaches it as projected light: soft-edged fields on hard-edged architecture.
- **Line and edge:** structure edges hard and pale; light edges soft. Saturation sits low (aisle windows), the high vault stays white.
- **The procedure:** build a neutral, repetitive structure; give each unit along one axis a dominant hue that steps one notch from its neighbour, ordered by orientation (cool at the start, warm at the end); let the colour spill onto the neutral surfaces as soft fields; keep the top of the hierarchy white.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Antoni Gaudí.** *Let position decide everything: a strict repeated structure whose members change girth where they carry more, coloured one step at a time along its length, with the colour broken into fragments and allowed to fall on the neutral parts.*
Process:
1. Lay the regular structure first: identical units at one interval along one axis (and identical rows across it). Every later irregularity sits on this grid and never moves it.
2. Size each member by what it carries: thickest and darkest where load gathers (the centre, the joints), thinner outward; let a member run unbroken for about half its length, then split into 2–4 thinner inclined members, so one end is orderly and the other dense.
3. Keep the structure neutral (lowest chroma, one value band) and hard-edged, at least half of any view.
4. Give colour by position, not by kind: each unit takes one dominant hue; neighbouring units differ by one step round the wheel; the two ends of the run have opposite temperatures.
5. Stratify by height: the strongest colour sits in the lowest zone, at hand and eye level; the top level stays plain and carries no colour.
6. Build each unit's hue from many small pieces of unequal size (about 10:1), and let it leave its unit: a soft-edged tint on the neutral structure beside it.
7. Where a surface is coloured directly, set contrasting patches inside a neutral fragment ground (≈40%) and cap every edge with one continuous smooth border. Sparse mode: scattered fragments ≤10–12% on a neutral ground.
Why it works:
- A strict grid underneath is the constant against which the broken surface is read as variation. Remove the grid and the same surface is noise; remove the variation and it is a spreadsheet.
- One hue per unit groups the fragments inside it; one step per neighbour makes the run a readable gradient, so colour tells you where you are. Random hues give a patchwork, one hue gives no location.
- Small unequal pieces fuse into one tone at a distance and become detail up close; the continuous border restores a clean contour so a broken surface still reads as one object. Girth that follows load makes mass itself the hierarchy.
Colour relations: neutral carrier ≥50%; one dominant hue per unit in 3–4 near values; neighbour step one notch, ends opposed in temperature; saturation highest low down, none at the top; colour on the carrier only as soft tint; inside a coloured surface, pale ground ≈40% and contrasting patches framed within it.
Space and line: one interval; member girth steps by position (centre to edge about 1.5–2:1, a visual estimate); split at mid-length into 2–4; structure edges hard, tint edges soft; members thicken at joints and may lean with what they carry; fragment sizes ≈10:1.
Type requirement: one family with a wide weight range, so weight can step with level as girth does (heaviest at the trunk heading, lighter at each split); soft-shouldered forms; text sits on the neutral structure or on a single flat tone at reading contrast, never across a fragmented surface.
Motion and interaction: animate the light, not the layout. Moving along the axis shifts the tint one step per unit (≈300ms); arriving items settle once with damping and no overshoot; no glinting or flashing. Position and state are always also given by label or order, since the hue ramp alone is not readable to everyone.
Fails when: the units have no natural order (the ramp invents one); hue must also mean status, which collides with hue as position; fewer than four units (no ramp to read); small screens where fragments fall below legible size; long reading text, which needs the flat carrier and leaves the method nothing to do.
Signature (do not carry together): broken-tile mosaic with a glazed rim · leaning, bone-like catenary forms · a rainbow of stained glass.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Gaudí's procedure → a shift dispatcher's week planner in cool greys (desktop, 1440 × 900, text-dense).
1. *Structure.* Seven day columns of 180px at one interval, twelve hour rows of 56px. Nothing leaves the grid.
2. *Members.* Column rules are 1px for the outer days and 3px, two steps darker, for today and its neighbours, where the shifts pile up. Each day runs as one lane until the midday handover (row 6), then splits into 2–4 crew lanes.
3. *Carrier.* Pale grey (L 95) with hard 1px edges fills about 65% of the view.
4. *Hue by position.* Each day has one tint, stepping 12° per day from slate blue (Monday, 250°) to plum (Sunday, 322°): a narrow arc, cool end to warm end.
5. *Strata.* The top summary strip and day headers stay white and uncoloured; the strongest chroma is in the lower two thirds.
6. *Fragments and spill.* A shift is a stack of job chips 8–80px high in 3–4 near values of the day's tint. The day's tint washes 40px into the neutral gutter at 6% opacity with a soft edge.
7. *Border.* Each stack is capped by one continuous 6px rounded border in the day's darkest value; about 40% of the stack is pale ground between chips; an overdue job is a contrasting patch framed inside it, marked with a glyph as well.
- *Type.* One variable sans: 650 for day headers, 500 for lanes, 400 for chips; every day is named in text.
- *Motion.* Paging a week shifts the ramp one column at a time over 300ms; dropped chips settle once, no overshoot.
Result: a grey-violet scheduling tool on a hard grid. No tile, no curves, no rainbow. What survived is the strict structure, girth by load, one hue per unit stepping with position, and colour as fragments with a clean cap.

## Space & composition
- **Form follows the load path.** Gaudí's columns lean because the thrust leans. Let alignment follow a curve set by the user's reading path, not the 12-column grid.
- **Rule-generated hierarchy** *(unverified: from texts; the point counts cannot be read from the photos, which show only that girth and material step by position)*. At the Sagrada Família, a column's height in metres is twice its star points, and its base height in decimetres equals them: 12 points gives 24 m in porphyry, 6 points gives 12 m in sandstone. One integer drives size, material and position.
- **Progressive subdivision** *(unverified, from texts)*. Each section of a column halves in height and rotation while its points double, so the star resolves into a circle.
- **Forest, not hall.** Columns branch into vaults that filter light like a canopy. Gaudí's emptiness is dappled, never blank.
- **Organic skin, strict grid.** Casa Batlló's street face is three identical window columns, with the same mask balcony repeated on every floor. The waves and colour live in the skin, not in the plan.
- **The edge is the room.** The serpentine bench is both the guardrail and the place to sit. Its concave bays make small social pockets along a long edge. Treat page edges as habitable, wavy zones, not hard frames.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

Colour runs on direction and height. Cool belongs to dawn and beginnings, warm to dusk and endings. The most saturated glass sits low, at reading height. Higher up it bleaches to clear textured glass that splits the light, the reverse of Gothic practice. Gaudí wanted the sun to do the colouring ("sunshine is a great painter"). Trencadís adds a second rule, which the Park Güell bench shows differently from the old text. It is not a field of related hues: a white or cream broken-tile ground holds framed panels of contrasting patterned tile, and a rounded glazed rim caps every edge. Casa Batlló uses a sparse confetti of coloured discs on pale plaster. The Sagrada Família glass rules above are now seen in the Basilica's own photos (retry pass 2): blue on the Nativity side, amber on the other, a step-by-step hue ramp across neighbouring bays, and a white vault above. ## Form, line & typography
Line is catenary and parabolic: curves under tension, never compass circles or arbitrary béziers. Corners are tangent parabolic arcs, like the rounded points of the column stars. Gaudí's own lettering is hand-cut into tile and ironwork, fused with the material. Type should be soft-shouldered but structured:
- **Fraunces** (Google Fonts) with SOFT at 100 and WONK on, for display. Its bulbous terminals echo carved stone.
- **Young Serif** for warm headings, if Fraunces is too playful.
- **Commissioner** (variable flare) for body text, quiet under heavy ornament.

## Rhythm, motion & time
Motion behaves like gravity. Elements hang from anchors and settle like a chain, with a damped spring and no overshoot. Animate the light rather than the layout: hues drift cool at morning and warm at evening, and glossy fragments glint under the pointer or when the device tilts. Repetition works like the bench modules, with the same module in different colours and no identical neighbours.

## UI translations
1. **Hang the layout.** Draw section dividers as flipped catenaries (`y = a·cosh(x/a)`) in SVG, not generic waves. (The hanging-model origin is unverified; the catenary is a prescription.)
2. **Drive components from one integer.** A `--points: 6|8|10|12` token sets height, radius, material colour and font size, like the column specifications (the star-point arithmetic is unverified).
3. **Nest by halving.** Each level down halves the padding, doubles the grid divisions and increases the corner radius until the deepest chips are pills, star to circle.
4. **Assign colour by orientation.** Start panels and morning states use `#1B2B63`; end panels and evening states use `#83382E`, keyed to the local clock. Between them, step the hue one notch per unit (blue → turquoise → green → yellow → amber), as the Sagrada bays do (seen in the Basilica's photos).
5. **Saturate low, bleach high.** Put the strongest colour in the bottom third of the screen (tab bar, CTAs). Let the top fade to `#EFEFEF` with a faint spectral fringe. (Seen in the Basilica's photos: saturated aisle windows, white vault.)
6. **Trencadís surfaces.** For hero areas, use a Voronoi mosaic with a white/cream shard ground (~40%) and a few framed inset patches of contrasting patterned shards (cobalt, green, ochre, red). Vary shard size about tenfold, use 1–2px pale grey grout `#AAA7A1`, and cap the container with a rounded glazed rim. Generate it once per user, so no two people see the same pattern. For a quieter surface, scatter sparse discs (≤10% coverage) on `#C6C5BB` plaster, as at Batlló.
7. **Habitable edges.** Give long lists a serpentine edge, concave bays every 4–6 items that hold contextual actions, like the seats along the bench.
8. **Skeletal frames.** Give media containers bone-like mullions: tapered supports thicker at the joints, not uniform 1px borders.
9. **Gravity motion.** Use `spring(stiffness 120, damping 18)` for drop-ins. Dismissed sheets fall along a catenary arc instead of a straight line.
10. **Infrastructure as ornament.** Make functional affordances (scroll progress, loading, dividers) the decorative elements, like the columns that carry rainwater.
11. **Light that moves.** Let the hero gradient's hue angle drift 1° per minute of local time, and add a pointer-driven specular highlight on glossy tiles.

## What this artist would reject
- Rectangular cards on a rigid 12-column grid with the same radius everywhere.
- Flat single-hue fills that don't respond to light.
- Decoration pasted on top of structure, like a hero illustration with no function.
- Linear or `ease-in-out` motion with no weight.
- Dead 1px borders and identical, repeated list items.

## Signature prompt seed
Design this screen as if its forces shaped it. Section dividers are inverted catenary arches, and containers have tapered, bone-like frames. Clad the hero in white broken-tile trencadís with inset panels of cobalt, green, ochre and red shards, edged by a rounded glazed rim, over a strictly repeated grid. Put saturated colour low near the thumb and let the top bleach to prismatic white. Theme cool blue at morning and red-orange at evening. Lists run along a serpentine edge with bays for actions. Motion settles under gravity, like a hanging chain. No straight lines, no flat fills, no ornament that doesn't do structural work.
