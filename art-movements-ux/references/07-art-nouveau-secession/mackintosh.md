# Charles Rennie Mackintosh — Glasgow Style / Art Nouveau

## Essence
Mackintosh draws strict, tall frames and lets one small organic thing live inside each: a stencilled rose, a swallow cut through a headrail, a square of pink glass. Every room has one register, either dark oak focused on a single point or white enamel with tiny jewel-coloured insets. Furniture does the job of walls.

## Key works examined
- **Argyle Street Tea Rooms high-back chair, 1897.** Tapering uprights, square at the foot and round at the top, meet in an oversized oval headrail with a swallow cut through it. A crowd of these chairs screens a table and makes a room inside the room. *(visually checked: V&A O11281. The chair is 1:2.3 overall (width to height), with the back rising to ~2.6× the seat height. Two flat back slats run between the tapering posts, the oval headrail carries a crescent/bird-wing cut-out, and the oak is stained dark (`#524237`, deep shadow `#211916`). Also checked: AIC 156437, the 1897 Argyle armchair, with the same crescent cut-outs in its sides.)*
- **Glasgow School of Art, 1897–99 / 1907–09.** A grid of huge north studio windows on a plain field, with a few sculptural moments set against it. *(visually checked: secondary source, Bluffton University course pages (photos and the GSA archive elevation to Renfrew Street))* Seen: the north front is a long, flat ashlar wall in which eight very large studio windows, each a grid of small square panes, sit four on each side of a narrow central entrance bay; the windows fill roughly half of the façade. The centre is deliberately asymmetric (off-axis door, stair, small bay and turret) against the regular window runs. The sculptural moments are small and linear: wrought-iron brackets in front of the windows ending in pierced square-and-ball finials, and curved railings. The west façade (1907–09) is a sheer stone cliff with three very tall, narrow oriel windows.
- **GSA Library, 1907–09.** *(not obtained; unverified)* A dense timber cage with small balusters enamelled in bright colours. Colour appears only in small notches of the structure.
- **Willow Tea Rooms, Salon de Luxe, 1903.** Light, with silver, grey and pink, and a mirrored frieze. Eight silver chairs in purple velvet enclose two tables. *(visually checked: V&A O321656, a Salon de Luxe chair photographed in storage, so lighting-dependent. The frame is painted silver (`#CFCFCE`), and purple velvet (`#472B4E`) covers the whole tall back and seat, ~35% of the chair's area; the back is ~2× the seat height. Also checked: V&A O62507, the Willow fireplace, a black frame around a white tile field with a violet-blue tile surround (`#575199`, ~8% of the view) and five ovals cut in black.)*
- **Hill House, 1902–04.** A dark hall opens into white rooms stencilled with a geometric rose in pink, green and silver. Colour follows each room's use. *(visually checked: V&A O15888, the main bedroom north-wall elevation (watercolour and pencil on aged paper). Tall white panels about 1:3 are each topped by a 3×3 grid of small green squares (`#5C7E50`); a band of pink-mauve roses (faded to `#7B6B68`) runs above the fireplace; the rest is plain. Colour covers well under 5% of the elevation.)*
- **78 South Park Avenue dining room, 1905–06.** A dark rose-and-lattice room that sends every glance to the lit table. *(not obtained; unverified)*
- **White-painted table, 1902 (AIC 109927), added.** A white enamel oval table (`#E7E8E1` lit, `#C1C1C1` shade) with flat tapering legs. The only ornament is one small round inset medallion (a stylised grey-green and silver rose) where each leg group meets the top: ornament only at the nodes, about 1% of the object. It confirms the white register. *(visually checked: AIC scan)*

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (GSA north front): a long field with two regular runs of identical big gridded windows (4 + 4) flanking one small, asymmetric, dense central bay; the ornament is concentrated in thin ironwork at the window line.
- **Colour relations:** monochrome stone and dark glass; any expressive accent is black iron line, not colour.
- **Line and edge:** large flat planes and big rectilinear grids; the only curves are in small iron details and railings.
- **The procedure:** lay out a plain field; repeat one large gridded opening at a strict interval; break the symmetry only in one narrow central zone; add a few small linear ornaments along one line (the sill), never on the planes.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Charles Rennie Mackintosh.** *Raise tall plain bays in a strict repeat, spend all the ornament at one height and all the irregularity in one narrow bay.*
Process:
1. Choose the register for the whole room before anything else: all dark or all light, one near-neutral ground on 90% or more of it.
2. Divide the field into tall bays (1:2.3 to 1:3) with thin full-height verticals, and repeat the identical bay at a strict interval (for example 4 + 4). The bays stay plain.
3. Break the repeat in exactly one narrow bay (about 1/8 of the width), set off the axis: asymmetric, denser than the rest, holding the way in.
4. Put the ornament on one horizontal line: a small cluster of squares (3 × 3, about 1/8 of the bay's width) at the head of each tall member, at the same height in every bay, 1–5% of the area in total.
5. End a long straight with one tight curve or one small mark, at that line and nowhere else (straight to curve at least 10:1).
6. Choose one colour mode per room: either small notes at the nodes of step 4 (under 5% of the wall), or one object covered whole in a saturated tone (about a third of its own area, in a neutral frame). Never both.
7. Enclose with height: let the members beside a group stand 2–2.6× taller than what they flank, so the group becomes a room inside the room.
Why it works:
- A tall plain bay makes emptiness read as intended, and its height carries the eye up to the one place where something happens. Scatter the marks down the bay and the climb has no end.
- Against a strict repeat a single irregular bay is found without being marked (salience by oddness). If every bay varies, there is no entrance.
- Marks at one height in every bay join into a band across the whole wall (grouping by alignment), so a frieze exists with almost no ink. One saturated thing among neutrals gains by contrast; add scattered colour and it loses it.
Colour relations: one register; neutral ground ≥90%; line in the tone furthest from the ground; a second neutral, lighter than the line, for secondary emphasis; colour either as notes under 5% in two or three low-chroma hues, or one saturated object or surround (≈8% of a view); nothing in between.
Space and line: bays 1:2.3–1:3; identical repeat with one narrow off-axis exception; cluster of 3 × 3 at the head; one ornament line; long ruled verticals, one closed curve; flanking members 2–2.6× the height of what they enclose.
Type requirement: tall condensed capitals with straight stems and crossbars shifted off centre, the same move as a long vertical with one detail near the top; display and labels only, placed on the ornament line; reading text in a plain face in its own wide column, never squeezed into a bay.
Motion and interaction: within a room only the node responds (the cluster, the head of the member); the bays hold still. A marked or selected node changes shape or fill as well as tone, and its hit area is 44px or more however small it is drawn. Moving between a dark and a light room is the one large transition (≈500ms cross-fade; a hint, since the dark-to-light threshold was not seen).
Fails when: the content is wide (tables, maps, charts) and cannot be cut into tall bays; long text is set in 1:3 columns (the measure is too narrow to read); the screen fits a single bay, so there is no repeat to break; categories need several colours at once.
Signature (do not carry together): the stylised rose in a square · the ladder-backed high chair · condensed capitals with high crossbars and ligatures.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Mackintosh's procedure → a build-pipeline board in dark graphite (desktop, 1440 × 900, dense and technical).
1. *Register.* All dark: graphite (L 14) on 93% of the screen, lines at L 60, a second neutral at L 78.
2. *Bays.* Eight stage bays, each 150 × 420px (1:2.8), four on each side, divided by 2px verticals.
3. *Exception.* One narrow bay of 170px (12% of the width) for the release gate, starting at 44% of the width and so off the axis; it alone is dense (approvals, the one primary button, 48px high).
4. *Ornament line.* At 96px from the top of every bay sits a 3 × 3 cluster of 6px squares (22px across, about 1/7 of the bay): the last nine runs. Passed is filled, failed is hollow with a cross, running is half filled. The nine clusters align into one band across the board; the whole cluster is one 48px target.
5. *Curve.* Each divider is 630px tall and ends at the top in one 12px hook that holds the stage name (about 50:1).
6. *Colour.* The object mode: the running stage's body, 150 × 140px (a third of its bay), is filled in one acid lime inside neutral dividers. Nothing else is coloured.
7. *Enclosure.* The dividers stand 1.5× taller than the bay content here (2× was not possible at 900px), enough to make each stage a stall.
- *Type.* Stage names in condensed capitals on the ornament line; logs in a plain monospace in a separate 720px drawer.
- *Motion.* Opening a stage cross-fades the dark board to a light log room in 500ms; on hover only the cluster brightens.
Result: a graphite engineering board with one lime block. No rose, no white room, no chair. What survived is the tall repeated bay, ornament on one line, one irregular bay and one saturated object.

## Space & composition
- **Verticality as structure.** Elements run about 2.3:1 to 3:1 tall (Argyle chair 2.3:1, Hill House panels ~3:1). 5:1 was not seen. Chair backs rise well above head height and window mullions run full height. Treat height as the main axis and width as secondary.
- **Rooms inside rooms.** Tall chair backs and screens, not walls, break open fields into enclosed bays.
- **The square as module.** Small squares (8–12px in UI) appear only at the edges and ends of tall members, like beads at the top of a post. In the Hill House bedroom they sit as 3×3 green grids at the top of each tall panel.
- **Quiet field, then sculpture.** Most surfaces are plain. Ornament gathers at a few nodes (entrance, oriel window, headrail).
- **Thresholds** *(unverified: the dark-to-light threshold itself was not seen in the photos checked)*. The dark hall gives way to the white room. Movement through the space is designed as a change of register.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

One register per room or object. Dark: stained-oak brown with no bright accent. White: enamel with jewels under 5% across a wall (Hill House elevation). The Willow pieces show a second rule: saturated colour can cover a whole object if it is the only coloured thing (a purple-velvet chair, a violet-tile surround), and the frame stays silver, black or white. ## Form, line & typography
His lettering uses tall, condensed capitals with stems as straight as chair posts. Crossbars sit unusually high or low, so letters such as E, H and A read off-centre. Bowls and counters become stretched ovals, and letters often nest into ligatures. The line language mixes long ruled verticals with a single tight curve at the end, like a stem with a closed bud. The rose is the same move on a small scale: concentric arcs closed inside a square. ITC Rennie Mackintosh (1996) has capitals only, plus many ligatures: a display system, not a text face.

Free fonts that fit: **Federo** for condensed Deco-geometric display, **Poiret One** for hairline caps with oval bowls, and **Josefin Sans** for body and labels, since its geometric shapes have a tall, elegant cap height.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- Regular vertical repetition (chair backs, mullions) broken by one event. Motion settles like posts: 8–16px vertical translations, no bounce, `cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.1,1)`.
- Draw the frame first (stroke-dashoffset on lattice lines), then fade in the rose 150ms later.
- Hover lights a small "glass inset" (a 6px square turning from silver to pink) and does not lift the whole card.

## UI translations
1. Build the layout on narrow, tall columns with a 1:2.5–1:3 width-to-height ratio. Hero panels and cards should be portrait, never landscape.
2. Group related content with "high-back" dividers: 2px vertical rules that run 1.5× taller than the content they enclose, ending in a 3×3 grid of 4px squares.
3. Pick one register per screen, either dark (`#524237` ground, a single lit focal card) or white (`#E7E8E1` ground). Never mix the two in one view.
4. Keep pink and purple below 5% of the screen's pixels: square insets, a selected state, one icon. One object may be fully saturated (a purple or violet-blue card) if it is the only coloured thing, held by a silver, black or white frame. Use silver for every secondary highlight.
5. Set headings in condensed caps with letter-spacing of 0.08em and a line-height of 0.95, and use a ligature or nested letter in the logotype.
6. Use one stylised rose (concentric arcs in a square) as the only illustrative mark, and vary it slightly at each use, no two identical.
7. Replace the heavy nav bar with a 24px silver frieze strip along the top.
8. Put ornament only at nodes: the active tab, the primary CTA, the page title. Leave everything else plain.
9. On section change, crossfade from the dark register to the white one over 500ms. Each region of the app should feel like a different room. (A hint: the dark-to-light threshold was not seen, so this is unverified.)
10. In a dark-register list, dim everything except the hovered row, which gets a warm light, like candlelight on a dinner table.
11. Make cards end in an oval "headrail" cap at the top, with one small negative-space cutout as the brand mark.

## What this artist would reject
- Rounded, low, landscape cards in an even masonry grid that has no single focus.
- Ornament or gradient spread evenly over every surface.
- Mixing dark-mode and light-mode components on one screen.
- Saturated brand colour used across large areas for buttons, headers and backgrounds at once.
- Bouncy, playful spring motion.

## Signature prompt seed
Design the screen as a tall, narrow room in a single register: enamel white `#E7E8E1` or stained-oak brown `#524237`, never both. Group content with hairline vertical posts that rise past their content and end in small grids of squares. Put a silver frieze strip along the top. Leave most surfaces plain and gather ornament at one node, a stylised square rose in muted pink `#7B6B68` with a purple `#472B4E` shadow, covering under 5% of pixels. Headings are condensed caps with offset crossbars. Motion settles vertically, without bounce. Frame first, flower second.
