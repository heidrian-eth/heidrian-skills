# Gustav Klimt — Vienna Secession

## Essence
Klimt sets a small, softly modeled human zone (face, hands) inside a huge, flat field of metallic ornament. The pattern is not background but a second body that dresses and nearly swallows the figure. What makes it unmistakable is extreme flatness everywhere except one tender patch of naturalistic detail.

## Key works examined
- **Adele Bloch-Bauer I, 1903–07 (Neue Galerie, 140 × 140 cm).** Face, hair and hands are under 1/12 of the surface, and the rest is gold and silver leaf with gesso relief. A ratio of roughly 92% ornament to 8% human. *(visually checked: Neue Galerie scan. The face sits at 6–17% from the top, right of centre; gold/bronze tones cover ~78% of the pixels; the left ~40% is a flat speckled gold field; a green floor block (~2%) under a black-and-white check strip sits bottom left; red is only a few small squares.)*
- **The Kiss, 1908–09 (Belvedere, 180 × 180 cm).** Gold, silver and platinum leaf on a glazed brass-leaf ground flecked with metal. His robe is rectilinear and hers is circles. Pattern tells the two figures apart. *(visually checked: Belvedere scan. The background is a dark speckled bronze (`#786639`–`#8B7642`, ~57% of the pixels), and bright leaf gold (`#D7AE4E`, ~19%) belongs to the couple's robes. The meadow covers ~13%, red flowers ~1.4%, and the 'black' rectangles are green-black `#14291E`. Gold spirals appear in both robes.)*
- **Beethoven Frieze, 1902 (Secession, about 2.15 × 34.14 m).** Casein, gilt stucco, mirror shards and mother-of-pearl buttons. A narrative that runs around a room, with quiet stretches between climaxes. *(visually checked: secession.at wall photos. The left wall is ~88% bare off-white plaster (`#E3DED3`/`#D7D0C2`) with gold ~5%, drawn in fine contour lines; the end wall (Hostile Forces) is packed edge to edge in greys, browns and blue-violet hair, with one ultramarine robe ~1%; the right wall has ~22% gold.)*
- **Stoclet Frieze, 1905–11 (Palais Stoclet, with the Wiener Werkstätte).** Marble, enamel, gilded tiles and stones. Two facing Tree of Life panels bookend a geometric Knight panel. *(visually checked: the MAK working drawings MAL 226-2 Expectation and 226-8 Fulfillment, plus Eisler plate 16 (b/w) for the Knight panel. Expectation's robe is built from triangles, a third pattern language. In Fulfillment the man's robe mixes a black-and-white rectangle block with eye-ovals, so the two languages meet inside one garment. The ground is pale ochre paper with gold-ochre spirals.)*
- **Judith I, 1901 (Belvedere), added.** A 1:2 portrait. The frame's gilt header band (~11% of the height) carries 'JVDITH UND HOLOFERNES' in incised inscriptional capitals. Flesh is a large zone (~20% of the painted field by a pixel count), so the 8% rule does not hold for every work. *(visually checked: Belvedere scan)*
- **Square landscapes from 1899 on (e.g. Schloss Kammer on the Attersee).** Framed through a cardboard viewfinder with a square hole, then compressed into a skyless tapestry. *(visually checked: secondary source, artchive.com, Schloss Kammer on the Attersee III, 1910, Belvedere)* Seen: square (≈1:1.02); sky only as a sliver in one top corner (<1%); the façade is frontal, parallel to the picture plane and cropped on all four sides; three rounded tree masses overlap it and run off the top and sides. A level shoreline sits at ≈67% of the height, and the lower third is water repeating the façade's pale verticals as broken reflections. The whole surface is one even weave of small dabs (≈0.5–1% of the width) with no change of touch between tree, wall and water; tonal range is narrow (most of the surface within L* 30–56), the pale wall (≈7%) and the red roof (≈5%) the only departures.
- **San Vitale mosaics, Ravenna (visited Dec 1903).** The source of the icon-like gold ground.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Schloss Kammer III): square split by one level line at ≈2/3 height: an upper band of frontal, cropped architecture and foliage and a lower band of reflection; nothing recedes, every element is parallel to the frame. No focal point: three similar tree masses repeat across the width at roughly equal intervals.
- **Colour relations** (Schloss Kammer III): 5–6 muted tones of close value fill ≈88% of the surface; the lightest tone (pale wall) and the warmest (red roof) are each ≤7% and sit in bands at the top, then echo faintly in the water below. Contrast between neighbours is low everywhere; the hierarchy is by texture density, not by value.
- **Line and edge:** no lines at all; edges are where one dab colour gives way to another. Hard edges only on the few window frames.
- **The procedure:** crop a square tight enough to lose the sky; align everything to the frame; set one horizontal division at about 2/3; mirror the upper band into the lower as broken texture; render the entire field in one unit of mark so that density, not contrast, carries it; keep the lightest and warmest notes small and near the top.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Gustav Klimt.** *Flood the surface with intensity, then make the one calm, differently rendered patch the focus; separate things by a change of texture, not by lines.*
Process:
1. Fill the format: no empty ground. Build the field (55–80% of the area) from the content itself seen at texture scale, in one hue family and one fine grain, held in a value band narrow enough to fuse into a single tone at arm's length.
2. Raise one continuous silhouette for the main object: same hue, one clear value step brighter, about 20% of the area, tall, reaching near the top, with no outline.
3. Inside the silhouette, code kinds of content by texture grammar, not hue: one angular or gridded language, one round or organic language, and a shared filler motif running through both so they stay one body. Mix the two languages only where the content itself merges.
4. Reserve the focus: a patch ≤3% of the area (≤8% with its satellites) in the top fifth, rendered in the one mode used nowhere else (smooth, plain, low chroma, low detail). Never repeat that mode.
5. Pack the accents: one or two hues, ≤2% each, as clusters of small cells inside the pattern near the focus, never scattered and never on the field. Anchor the base with one band (10–15%) of a cooler contrasting hue in the finest grain, ended abruptly.
6. Over length, pulse the density: stretches 85–90% bare, carrying only thin drawing, then one section packed edge to edge.
7. Mode for content with no single subject: skip steps 2–4. Align everything parallel to the frame, set one level division at about 2/3 of the height, repeat the upper band below it as broken texture, and render the whole field in one size of mark, so that density of texture, not value contrast, carries the hierarchy; the lightest and the warmest tones stay ≤7% each, in bands near the top.
Why it works:
- Salience is difference from the surroundings. Where everything is rich, the smooth, pale, plain patch is the odd one and wins: emphasis by relief, so the focus can stay tiny and quiet.
- Fine detail at low contrast fuses into a tone. The field can hold a great deal of matter without competing, and a value step, or only a change of grain, is enough to make an edge. Half-merged things reward a second look.
- Each level of the nest changes exactly one variable (value, then texture grammar, then rendering mode), so the eye descends ground, object, kind, focus in order with no frame anywhere.
Colour relations: near-monochrome; one hue family covers 75–80% in two values (dark grainy ground ≈55% : bright object ≈20%); a near-black for the angular language, concentrated inside the object; cool base band 2–13%; accent clusters ≤2% each; the focus has the LOWEST chroma on the surface, so the peak of the hierarchy is the least saturated point.
Space and line: full bleed, no margins, no horizon; a format with no reading direction (near-square); object vertical and off-centre; focus 6–20% from the top; no drawn boundaries, edges are a value step (primary object) or a texture change at near-equal value (secondary); no shadows.
Type requirement: three textures that match the nest: a field face of even colour with no bold, which reads as grain; two label voices, one angular or tabular and one round, assigned by kind of content; the focal text alone in a softer, larger, generously leaded voice; display lettering sits inside a band as part of the pattern.
Motion and interaction: the surface is still and only light changes. Pointer, tilt or scroll passes a highlight over the field; arriving items brighten one cell at a time (≈120ms each, irregular 15–30ms offsets); the focus never animates and is never overlaid.
Fails when: the focus grows past about 1/5 (the field becomes a frame round a picture); the special mode is repeated (the field becomes wallpaper between cards); the field has high internal contrast or several hues (noise, and reading contrast fails); the content is sparse, so pattern has to be invented as decoration.
Signature (do not carry together): gold leaf · spiral, eye and check ornament · a pale naturalistic face in a square.

## Space & composition
- **The square is the default canvas.** Adele and The Kiss are 1:1 (measured from the scans: 1500×1504 and 1770×1772). Judith I is 1:2. The square removes the left-to-right pull, so the eye spirals inward.
- **Figures fill the field.** In The Kiss the couple is a single vertical bell shape that almost touches the top, above a flowered strip that ends abruptly like a cliff.
- **Merge, don't separate.** Adele's dress and chair dissolve into the background, and the edge shows only as a change of texture (spirals against speckle) at nearly the same value. In The Kiss the edge is a hard value step instead (bright robe against dark bronze), still with no outline.
- **Faces high.** Adele's face sits at 6–17% from the top, and the couple's faces in The Kiss at about 12–20%. The human zone belongs in the top fifth, not merely above centre.
- **Telescope cropping.** The landscapes have little or no horizon (Schloss Kammer III: no sky, but a level shoreline at ≈2/3 height with reflection below). Foliage runs off all four sides, giving even density and no focal point.
- **Frieze rhythm.** The Beethoven Frieze puts dense clusters of figures between long, nearly empty stretches of wall, so density pulses with the story.

## Form, line & typography
There are two main line languages, and each figure gets one. One is structural: upright rectangles, black-and-white checks and columns. The other is organic: circles, ovals, eye shapes and spirals. The Kiss assigns one to each figure, while gold spirals run through both robes as a shared filler. Stoclet adds triangles (Expectation), and Fulfillment mixes rectangles and eye-ovals inside one robe at the moment of union. Adele adds Egyptian eyes, Mycenaean whorls and her own initials worked into the motifs, so letters are ornament too. For type, use narrow inscriptional capitals for display, a soft serif for body and a circle-built geometric sans for labels. Suggested free fonts: **Marcellus SC** (inscriptional display), **Cormorant Garamond** (body), **Josefin Sans** (geometric labels and data).

## Rhythm, motion & time
The surface should shimmer, not slide. Gold leaf changes as the viewer moves, so the motion is light passing over a static pattern. Gesso-relief circles catch the light separately, which suggests a cell-by-cell glint. Pace long pages like the frieze: quiet stretches broken by dense, decorated climaxes. The human detail (a face, a hand) never animates.

## UI translations
1. **Make the hero a 1:1 square** that is 90% flat ornamental field. Put the one photographic "face" element in ≤3% of the area (8% with hands and shoulders), in the top fifth.
2. **Build two pattern tokens:** `--ornament-rect` (black/ivory rectangles) for structural surfaces and `--ornament-round` (radial-gradient circles and ovals) for personal content. Never use both on one element.
3. **Make edges out of density instead of borders.** A card repeats its parent's pattern at a tighter `background-size`, with no stroke and no shadow.
4. **Gold is a field, not a button.** Use dark bronze `#786639` as the large ground and bright `#D7AE4E` for the one primary object (about 20% of the area), with calls to action in ink black on gold. Coral goes on at most one element per screen.
5. **Shimmer on pointer.** Bind a gradient highlight to cursor or device-tilt position over the gold, with `mix-blend-mode: overlay`.
6. **Stagger the glint.** On load, brighten pattern circles one at a time (120ms each, with 15–30ms random delays), like light over gesso.
7. **Viewfinder crop for imagery.** Show galleries as tight square tiles with no horizon, texture running off all edges and no centred subject.
8. **Frieze scroll.** For onboarding, use a horizontal scroll-snap strip that alternates dense ornamented panels with sparse ones. The sparse panels are truly sparse: ~85–90% bare plaster ground with fine contour drawing.
9. **Bookend symmetry.** Lay out dashboards Stoclet-style: two mirrored organic panels with a compact geometric panel between them for the key metric.
10. **Keep the human zone untouched.** No overlays or animation on avatars or faces.

## What this artist would reject
- Neutral grey surfaces and empty white canvas as the default "calm."
- Drop shadows and elevation to separate layers, instead of shifts in pattern density.
- Wide 16:9 hero banners with a centred headline floating over a stock photo.
- Ornament scattered evenly as decoration instead of packed into one dense field.
- One generic icon set for everything, when structure and intimacy deserve different shape languages.
- Animating content itself (bouncing, sliding) instead of animating light on a still surface.
