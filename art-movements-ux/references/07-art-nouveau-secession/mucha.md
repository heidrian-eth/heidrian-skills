# Alphonse Mucha — Art Nouveau

## Essence
Mucha builds every image as a vertical shrine: one figure, a circular "halo" disc behind the head, a frame around her, and lettering locked into that frame as if cast from one piece. A single flowing outline governs everything (hair, smoke, drapery, tendrils), set against still geometric borders and filled with muted, chalky color. He treated ornament as a reusable, mass-printable system.

## Key works examined
- **Gismonda (Sarah Bernhardt), 1894–95**: 214 × 74 cm, almost 3:1, on two joined sheets. Extreme portrait format plus one halo anchors everything. *(visually checked: V&A scan, faded. The sheet measures 1:2.9. The 'GISMONDA' title band is ~8% of the height in navy mosaic letters; the halo is a horseshoe mosaic arch ~76% of the width, carrying 'SARAH BERNHARDT', with the head at ~22% from the top; the footer cartouche is ~15%. Gold ochre covers the robe, ~9–13% of the sheet.)*
- **Job (cigarette papers), 1896**: hair and smoke curl into the logo while a gold Byzantine zigzag border stays rigid. Curves are set against hard geometry, and the brand mark sits inside the illustration. *(visually checked: V&A 1897 and 1898 printings. The 1896 design's zigzag border band is ~4% of the width on a dusky lavender ground (`#766979`, ~13%); the 'JOB' letters fill the top third and stand directly behind the head as its halo; the hair is olive-brown gold. The 1898 poster sets a dusty-pink robe (~12–16%) inside a ring on a tiled monogram ground.)*
- **The Seasons, 1896**: four 103 × 54 cm panels (~1.9:1) with no text, Mucha's first series for the printer Champenois. One template, four variants. *(visually checked: AIC 97439, a later single-sheet version (1897) with four arch-topped panels of ~1:2.2. They share warm sepia/tan tones, carry no gold frames and no text, and each figure fills its panel top to bottom.)*
- **Rêverie, 1897–98**: a figure framed by a halo of pink flowers, turning the pages of a design book. The frame is the card. *(visually checked: V&A scan. The disc is ~95% of the poster width, the head ~26% from the top, the 'F. CHAMPENOIS' band ~13% of the height; the disc rim is a thin olive-bronze line, not gold; the corners outside the disc are flat sage green `#64684F` (~11%).)*
- **The Arts (1898) / The Precious Stones (1900)**: figures sit inside large discs, each with its own plant. A component library with theme slots. *(visually checked: Mucha Foundation series image of The Precious Stones, object 243; see 'One template, four colour keys' below.) *(The Arts visually checked: Mucha Foundation images of the four-panel series and Dance. The discs are not gold: each is an open sage-grey ring (#8C9078, ≈89% of the panel width in Dance) studded with ochre rosettes (#897B4A) around a pale straw disc, and the figure's hair and drapery break out over the ring. Each panel keeps its own plant (laurel, flowers) in the top corners and inside the ring. The head sits higher than in Gismonda, ≈18% from the top in Dance. The four panels share one template and one warm tan-salmon palette, with no text. The Precious Stones was obtained in a later pass, see above.)*
- **Documents Décoratifs, 1902**: 72 plates covering posters, wallpaper, friezes, stained glass, jewelry, furniture and lettering. In effect, a design-system handbook. *(visually checked: CMA 1969.237, plate 38. A poppy frieze repeats three times across the width on a dark brown ground, every shape is outlined in metallic gold, and a pattern swatch plus a corner-tile detail sit above it like spec sheets.)*

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Space** (Precious Stones): identical skeleton across four panels: disc top, figure middle, flower mass bottom; variants change only content and hue.
- **Colour relations:** shared muted ground and contour across the set; one key hue per panel, carried by the two largest soft areas (dress, flowers); the saturated parts sit in the lower half, the disc stays neutral.
- **The procedure:** fix one layout skeleton; vary a single key hue and one emblem (flower) per variant; keep frame, ground and line constant so the set reads as a family.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Alphonse Mucha.** *Build a fixed frame that does the pointing (one large quiet shape behind the focus, bands that hold the words), then change only one colour key and one emblem per variant.*
Process:
1. Fix a tall format (1:1.9–1:2.9) and stack it in bands that never move between variants: a title band (8–13% of the height; omitted in sets without text), the subject field, a foot band (up to 15%).
2. Place one large simple closed shape behind the subject: 75–95% of the width, touching or close to the top of the subject field, its centre on the subject's focal point, which sits 12–26% from the top. Make it the calmest area on the surface: lowest chroma, plain inside, with a regular border.
3. Let the subject break that shape in at least two places and run out toward the frame, so the shape reads as behind the subject and never as a box around it.
4. Oppose the two line kinds: the frame is still and regular (straight bands, a repeat about 4% of the width); the contents are drawn in one continuous line that is heavy on outer contours and light inside. Never make both fluid, or both rigid.
5. Hold every large fill at low chroma and close in value, and let one contour colour at the far end of the value scale do all the separating. Then give each variant one key hue, carried by its two largest soft areas, with the stronger share in the lower 40%; the shape from step 2, the ground and the contour stay the same in every variant.
6. Lock the words into the bands: letters widened or narrowed until the line fills its band exactly, drawn in the contour colour of step 5.
7. Pack detail round the shape and let it reach the frame; leave flat either the corners outside the shape (≈10%) or one blank zone beside the subject, not both.
Why it works:
- A complex subject seen against the largest calm area separates at once (figure and ground), and the concentric border leads the eye to the centre. Take the shape away and the subject sinks into the detail; make the shape the most intense thing and it competes with what it should present.
- With skeleton, ground and line constant, the one thing that differs is read as the message (grouping by likeness). Change two things per variant and the set stops being a set.
- A dark contour lets fills sit close in value without merging, so colour can stay quiet; a still frame against a moving line makes each legible by contrast of kind.
Colour relations: greyed throughout (large areas all low chroma; only small accents rise above); a pale warm-or-cool ground shared by the set; one dark contour hue; one key hue per variant on ≈25–40% of the area in two values; the backing shape neutral, paler inside than its border; an intense tone may cover the subject's main mass (≈10%) but never the backing shape.
Space and line: tall bands; backing shape 75–95% wide; focus 12–26% from the top; frame repeat ≈4% of the width; two line weights from one pen (outer ≈2–3× inner); no shadows.
Type requirement: a display face whose width can be fitted to a band without breaking (closed, rounded, even colour), used for short titles only; reading text in a plain face on the flat inside of the shape or the foot band, in the contour colour, at full reading contrast, never across detail.
Motion and interaction: the frame never moves. Changing variant swaps the key hue and the emblem in place (400–600ms cross-fade; instant under reduced motion); selection thickens the contour as well as tinting it; a variant is always named in its band, not told by hue alone.
Fails when: there is no single subject per view (tables, feeds, comparisons); the format is wide, so the backing shape cannot reach 75% of the width; titles are long or translated and cannot fill a band; many variants are shown small side by side, where greyed key hues cannot be told apart.
Signature (do not carry together): a figure with streaming hair before a halo · whiplash tendrils and flower borders · chalky lithograph tones on tan paper with hand-drawn lettering.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Mucha's procedure → a four-account banking overview in dark navy (phone, 390 × 844, one account per swipe).
1. *Bands.* Top band 8% (68px): account name. Subject field 77%. Foot band 15% (127px): two actions, each 56px high.
2. *Backing shape.* A circle 76% of the width (296px) directly under the top band, centre at 25% from the top, holding the balance. It is the calmest area: one step lighter than the ground (L 12 → L 17), no hue, with a 14px border of evenly spaced day ticks.
3. *Break.* The month's balance curve crosses the border twice and runs to both screen edges; the largest transaction's marker sits half on the border.
4. *Line kinds.* Bands and tick border are straight and regular (16px repeat, 4% of the width). The balance curve is one continuous line, 3px along its outer run, 1px for its inner comparisons.
5. *Colour.* All fills low chroma, within 8 L of each other; one pale contour (L 82) draws every edge (the dark-contour rule inverted). Key hue per account (dull teal, dull amber, dull violet, dull brick) on the area under the curve and on the category mass across the lower 40%. Circle, ground and contour identical on all four.
6. *Words.* Account name in capitals, width-fitted to the band (24–34px). Balance in a plain tabular face on the flat inside of the circle at 12:1.
7. *Density.* Transaction marks pack the lower field up to the frame; the two corners beside the circle stay flat.
- *Motion.* A swipe cross-fades key hue and lower mass in 450ms; bands, circle and contour do not move.
Result: a dark, numeric finance screen. No figure, no flowers, no pastel. What survived is the fixed skeleton, the neutral backing shape that the content breaks, and one variable per variant.

## Space & composition
- **Tall column.** Gismonda's width-to-height ratio is 1:2.9 (measured), and the AIC Seasons panels about 1:2.2: roughly a phone viewport made ceremonial.
- **One template, four colour keys** (The Precious Stones 1900, seen): four tall panels (≈1:2.3) share one layout: an ornamented ring-disc behind the head, touching the top, ≈85–95% of the panel width; the head at ≈12–16% from the top (higher than the 18–26% of the other works); a seated figure in the middle; the stone's flower massed across the lower ≈40%; a small title at the foot. Each panel is keyed to one hue family (orange Topaz, red Ruby, violet Amethyst, grey-green Emerald) carried by the dress and the flower, while ground, ring and contour stay the same muted tone.
- **Three-tier stack.** An ornamented title band sits at the top (8% in Gismonda, 13% in Rêverie), with the figure and halo below. Gismonda's footer is not thin: its theatre-name cartouche takes ~15% of the height.
- **The halo as the visual anchor.** A circle or arch behind the head pulls the eye to the face before anything else. It is big: 76% of the width in Gismonda, ≈85–95% in The Precious Stones, ≈89% in The Arts and ~95% in Rêverie (76–95% overall), with the head at 12–26% from the top (12–16% in The Precious Stones), above the upper third. In The Arts the ring is open and the figure overlaps it, so the frame never fully contains the content.
- **Uneven density.** Gismonda's background below the arch is mostly blank paper, with mosaic only in the arch and title. In Rêverie and Job, though, ornament runs right up to the frame, and only the corners outside the disc stay flat.
- **The frame is the layout.** Borders, arches and friezes enclose the content, and the lettering is built into them rather than floating on top.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

The logic is greyed, low-chroma colour (LCh chroma ≤30 for every large area; only small gold accents reach ~40) on a warm paper ground, held together by a dark olive-brown contour rather than by value contrast. The muted gold is not reserved for frames: it covers Gismonda's robe (~10%), outlines everything in Documents Décoratifs, and rims the halo only in some designs (Rêverie's rim is olive). The only blue seen is Gismonda's navy title lettering; The Arts' rings are a sage grey (#8C9078) carrying ochre-gold rosettes. Hexes are sampled from V&A, AIC and CMA scans of aged prints. Gismonda was printed in lavender, salmon, cobalt, light blue and olive on tan paper. Job's gold-on-purple suggests a deeper "night" variant.

## Form, line & typography
Mucha's line changes weight as it moves. It thickens on the outer contour and thins inside, and it ends in loops and coils. His lettering is hand-drawn: rounded, closed counters, flared or uncial-like strokes. The letters are stretched or squeezed to fill a band exactly, the way type fills a stone tablet,.

Fonts (Google Fonts): **Macondo Swash Caps** (display titles only, sparingly), **Marcellus** (flared caps for labels and nav), **Cormorant Garamond** (body).

## Rhythm, motion & time
Motion should feel like hair in water: slow, continuous, organic, never springy. Use easing like `cubic-bezier(.45,.05,.25,1)` and durations of 600–900 ms. Halos can rotate or "bloom" very slowly (a radial mask from 0 to 100% over about 800 ms) on load. Line ornaments should draw themselves with SVG `stroke-dashoffset`. Borders stay still; contents move. Theme by season or time of day, as in his series: fixed structure, swapped palette and flora.

## UI translations
1. Build hero screens as a vertical shrine: an ornamental title band (8–13% of height), a central subject with a halo behind the head at the 12–26% mark, and a footer cartouche up to 15% of height.
2. Put a big circle (`border-radius:50%`, or an arch as in Gismonda; 76–95% of the container width, thin olive or gold rim, pale straw fill with a floral or mosaic ring) behind avatars, product shots or the main stat, so the focal object reads as haloed. Alternatively, let the logo letters stand behind the subject as its halo (Job).
3. Make the title fill its band exactly: use `font-size` from `clamp()` plus letter-spacing so the word spans 100% of the container width, set on a tinted cartouche with a 1.5px sepia outline.
4. Set the page on `#E9DAC2` paper, not white. Keep every large colour greyed (LCh chroma ≤30). Gold goes on frames, the selected state and the main garment-like surface, never as a bright fill.
5. Outline cards and images with a variable-width contour (an SVG path or doubled border, 2px outside and 0.75px inside) instead of drop shadows.
6. Put rigid geometry (zigzag or tile borders made with `repeating-linear-gradient`) around fluid content. Never make both the frame and the content curvy.
7. Let one SVG "whiplash" vine grow out of the brand mark and wrap the section header, the way Job's smoke runs into the logo.
8. Concentrate ornament around the focal disc and let it reach the frame. Leave flat only the corners outside the halo (or, Gismonda-style, the plain paper below the arch).
9. Ship a token system like Documents Décoratifs. A small set of frame, halo, border, lettering and flora components plus 4 seasonal palette sets should let you assemble any screen.
10. On hover, draw a fine gold contour around the card (`stroke-dashoffset` over 700 ms) and very slightly widen the halo behind its thumbnail (scale 1 to 1.04).
11. On mobile, treat the 1:2.9 Gismonda ratio as the full-screen splash or onboarding format, one figure per screen.

## What this artist would reject
- Flat, borderless cards floating on white with generic drop shadows.
- Lettering placed on top of imagery without a container. In Mucha, type and frame are one object.
- Even, uniform ornament or icon sprinkles spread across the whole screen.
- High-chroma primary brand colors (pure #0000FF, neon CTAs).
- Bouncy, springy micro-interactions. He would prefer slow, flowing motion.

## Signature prompt seed
Design this app screen as a tall Mucha shrine on warm tan paper (#E9DAC2). An ornamented title cartouche spans the top band, its hand-lettered display type stretched to fill the width exactly. The central subject sits before a huge circular halo, nearly the full width, with a flower or mosaic ring and the head about a quarter of the way down. Hold everything with variable-weight sepia contours, not shadows. Use greyed lavender, dusty pink, sage and straw, with muted gold on frames, garments and selection. Rigid zigzag borders enclose fluid whiplash vines that grow out of the logo. Pack ornament around the halo, with flat corners outside it.
