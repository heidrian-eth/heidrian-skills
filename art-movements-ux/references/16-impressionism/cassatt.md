# Mary Cassatt — Impressionism

Conventions: positions are (x, y) as fractions of the picture or plate from the top left. L and C are CIELAB lightness and chroma. "Cluster" shares come from measure_palette.py (k-means); "patch" values are medians of a hand-placed box on one flat area; "polygon" shares are hand-traced outlines on the image, accurate to about ±2 points. Angles are pixel measurements, ±1–2°.

## Essence
Cassatt looks down and from close by at people absorbed in a small task, crops the room around them, and closes two or three bodies into one outline with a single point of contact that every line and gaze runs to. Flat or plainly modelled areas (skin, cloth, wall) are set hard against patterned ones (stripe, sprig, paper), so the pattern does the separating that shadow would normally do. In the colour prints she reduces this to a thin line, three to six flat tones and the bare paper, and reaches it by revising one fixed drawing through up to seventeen states.

## Detailed observations

**The Letter.** The plate is a tall 0.655 rectangle. Its left four fifths hold the woman, its right fifth (polygon 17.6%) a plain brown upright, the side of the writing desk, flush with the edge and running from y=0.10 to the bottom: a flat dark post that closes the sheet. A writing surface drops out of that post as an irregular quadrilateral. Its left edge climbs at 35° and its front edge is only 4° off horizontal, so it reads as a triangle wedged into the woman's lap. The blue of the desk (`#426476`, L41 C16) and the blue of the dress (patch `#486572`, L41 C13) are the same tone. Only a brown border about 3% of the plate wide separates furniture from body; take it away and they are one blue shape of about 42% of the plate. Three patterns share the sheet at three scales and three densities: sprigs on the wall (small green and brown, open, light ground), rosettes on the dress (ochre, larger, dark ground), fine vertical pleats on the blouse (line only). The flat parts are the face, the hands, the desk and the envelope. The envelope is bare paper (`#E3DEC9`, L88), a four-sided white at (0.45, 0.50) exactly where mouth, two hands and the downward gaze meet; a second, duller white sheet lies at (0.65, 0.75). The eyes are lowered, the face is cut by the envelope, and there is no shading anywhere on it. Only the hair is worked with dense parallel drypoint strokes, the one area of tonal build-up. Clusters: pale ground L80 34%, brown L36 25%, blues L37–49 22%, greens L61 4%, ochre L69 2%.

**The Bath: the drawing and eight impressions.** The drawing is a bare outline in black chalk, reversed left to right against the print, with several trial positions for the tub rim left visible. It was pressed into soft ground (the verso shows the transfer). Through eight impressions the contours of mother, child and tub barely move. What changes, in the order that the visible additions imply (my ordering, not the catalogue's): (1) line and hair only, plus one flat ochre shape for the whole dress (`#C4B177`, L73 C32) with no folds; (2) the same, darker (`#B09A5F`); (3) a much lighter yellow (`#D1C28F`, C28) with fold lines drawn in; (4) sprigs added, tub left as grey plate tone; (5) the background wiped dark, as a test of the figure light on dark; (6) background light again and the tub in a pale aqua (`#C3C5B3`, C10); (7) full strength: yellow `#E7D46B` (L85 C54), tub blue `#70959F`; (8) the edition, where yellow is pulled back to C44–45 and the tub to a grey-green (`#788C86`, L57 C8). So saturation is driven to a maximum and then reduced. In the final plate yellow measures 20.5% of the area, the mother-and-child mass 34% (polygon) and the tub 12%. The wall (L75 C12) and floor (L79 C12) differ by only 4 L and are told apart by one hairline at y=0.49, which stops behind the figure; on the left there is no line at all. Skin (L80–83) is barely lighter than the wall. The child is drawn in a thin red-brown line and the dress in a heavier black one. The tub is cut by the left edge. The mother leans from (0.43, 0.10) to a hand in the water at (0.22, 0.58), and the child is enclosed completely inside the yellow outline except one foot.

**In the Omnibus.** The drawing (recto) shows two women, a child and, at the right, a seated man with crossed legs and a top hat, heavily searched over. The soft-ground verso carries only the women, the child and the window lines: the man was dropped at the moment of transfer. In the print the space he occupied in the mirrored layout is simply an empty bench. One early colour impression has blank windows and an empty ceiling; the next adds the river, bridges and trees; the bench is teal in one proof, a clear blue in another and a muted grey-teal in the edition (patch `#354B4C`, L30). The three people make one mass of about 46% (polygon), with only slivers of bench showing between them: tan dress (`#BC8756`, L61 C37), pink dress (`#CFA289`, L70 C23), and bare-paper child (`#CDBAAC`, L77) overlapping both, so the undyed child is the seam. Behind them are horizontal bands: grey ceiling, a rail that rises 3.4° to the right, windows (y=0.18–0.38), sill, bench, kickboard, floor. The only tilted line of the box is the ceiling rail. The woman looks out of the right side; the nurse looks down at the child; the child looks out to the right as well. One diagonal, the furled umbrella, drops from (0.23, 0.56) to (0.28, 0.98). Two round registration holes are printed as black dots on the vertical centre line of the plate (x=0.50, y=0.01 and 0.985).

**The Coiffure.** A seated back at left, her frontal reflection at right. The mirror glass takes 24% of the plate (polygon) and the real body 16%. They are separated by one upright olive strip at x=0.27–0.37 that the head and hand overlap, so the two are joined. The mirror's lower edge falls 12° to the right and the wardrobe's base 23°, while every upright is vertical: a convergence without depth. Every colour in the mirror is the colour of the room, at lower strength. Stripes of the chair are hand-painted, unequal (widths vary about 1:3) and bend with the volume, so the stripe gives the chair its roundness without shading. Skin (`#F2D1A4`, L86 C27, L standard deviation 1.2) and skirt (`#F3E3C1`, L91, bare paper) are dead flat. 71% of the plate is above L75 and nothing is below L25. The wall paper and carpet are lift-ground shapes with irregular, brushed edges. At magnification the contours are single drypoint lines about 0.2–0.3 mm wide that swell and thin, start short and overlap at joints; the hair is the only place where strokes pile up into black, and it has reserved light streaks. The three impressions show the same drawing with a charcoal wardrobe and orange skin (`#665950`, `#C49F80`), then an ochre wardrobe, grey wall and pale skin, then a pink wall with red stripes. The drawing, reversed, has the head and arms already in place and no pattern whatever.

**The Fitting.** The white dress and its reflection stand as two pale columns at x=0.40–0.92. A kneeling seamstress, dark brown (`#5C4437`, L31, 19% of the plate, polygon), fills the lower left and is cut by the left and bottom edges. The wall is mustard (`#BFA574`) scattered with white scribbles, drawn by stopping out. Two stripe systems meet: pale grey-mauve on white (wide, L73–76 on L80, a step of 5–7 L) and near-black on brown (tight triple lines). The floor line slopes down to the right from y=0.53. The fitted woman looks down, the reflection turns away and the seamstress shows the back of her head: no gaze is returned.

**Afternoon Tea Party.** Two women lean in from opposite sides, and their hands meet at (0.47, 0.53), holding a plate and a cup. One cobalt (`#3B687F`, L42 C20) appears in six places and nowhere else: bonnet, cup, plate, and three cups on the tray, the last cut off by the right edge. Together it is 2.7–3.7% of the plate, the darkest saturated note besides a black cape (`#5E5A50`, L38, 13%). The cups have fine gold edges; the Met records gold paint applied by brush. The state proofs show the sequence: an outline proof with a grey cape only, a deep-toned colour trial (skirt L39), a very pale trial of the same shapes, and the edition, which takes the skirt to L78 olive and adds a coral curtain (`#F29B78`, C43, about 4%).

**Woman Bathing, Maternal Caress, Mother's Kiss, The Lamp.** Woman Bathing sets one unmodelled yellow back (`#EED9A5`, L standard deviation 0.8, the flattest patch measured) above a skirt of green, pink and paper stripes; wall and floor are both blue, joined with no skirting except one grey wedge at the right; a face is never shown and the mirror holds only hair. In Maternal Caress the child's back and the mother's hand are the same colour (`#EFC494`, `#EFC394`, L82 C31; Mother's Kiss does the same, `#D4C6AC` and `#D4C5AB`) and their hands and cheek overlap, so limb ownership takes a second look; the mother's and the child's heads touch at (0.55, 0.23). Mother's Kiss leaves 51% of the plate (polygon) as an empty wall above and beside the pair (pale lilac-blue by eye, measured near-neutral `#BCB7AE`, C5, in a warm photograph), and the pair is pushed to the lower left; the child's back, the mother's forearms and the cheek form one line. The Lamp repeats one lampshade and one blue vase in a dark mirror at a smaller size, and the fan repeats the semicircle of the shade at the opposite corner.

**The Child's Bath.** A 0.655 upright. The eye looks down at about 35°: the basin's rim measures an ellipse 775 × 410 px (32° of tilt) and the water in it 555 × 350 px (39°), though the bowl's outer wall and the jug are drawn nearly from the side. There is no horizon; the floor rises until it meets a chest at y≈0.33 and a wall at y≈0.22. Figures plus basin cover about 57% (polygon). The two dark heads touch at (0.50, 0.23), both at `#1A232D`-like L13, and both gazes run down to the hand washing the foot at (0.28, 0.77): 0.55 of the picture height of empty-of-event space between them, filled by two parallel legs and two parallel arms. The mother's left arm wraps the child and her hand sits on its hip, so that arms and legs, hand and knee, hand and foot, make a closed loop. Patterns surround the plain child: floral wall paper, painted chest, stripes in white, green and mauve (`#949EA4`, `#5D6F78`, `#888193`: equal chroma 5–11, L 46–65), rust carpet (`#5F3424`). The child's torso (`#B8A799`, L70 C10) is the one large unpatterned area in a picture whose brightest whites only reach L72 (`#ACB1B5`, the same value for towel and jug). Nothing is pure white: 1.6% of the pixels exceed L75. The jug is cut by the bottom edge at the right front, in the viewer's space.

**The Boating Party.** 1.31:1. The horizon is 6–7% from the top, so the picture is nearly all water and boat seen from above. The rower's back (polygon 25%, `#171B24`, L10 C7) is the darkest, flattest, nearest thing, and the only one with a turned-away face. The mother and child (16%, polygon) sit in a triangle of the yellow-green boat (`#838950`, L55 C32). Yellow-green covers 13.5% of the canvas and dark blue (L<40) 53%; pink, the child's dress, only 2.0%. Three edges run in toward the mother and child at about (0.45–0.50, 0.30–0.45): the sail's free edge falls at 58°, the oar rises at 24°, the rower's arm at about 19°; the curved gunwale rings the group like a basin. The sail, the sky-side corner and the thwarts are flat shapes with hard edges. Water is broken by short strokes; jacket, boat and sail are nearly textureless (jacket L standard deviation 1.3). Gazes: the mother looks right, past the man; the child looks out past him, the rower looks at them. The hand, the oar and the mother's hand meet at (0.45, 0.43) without touching the child. The oar and the sail leave the frame; the man's far side is cut. Scholars read the figures as drawing attention to the child by diagonals, and separate the man by tone (Wikipedia, citing Pollock and Mathews).

**Little Girl in a Blue Armchair.** 1.49:1. Teal-blue is 60% of the canvas (mask), in three lightnesses: `#589CA2` L60 lit, `#23747E` L45, `#0C5B64` L35 for the farther seats. Four seats ring an empty brown floor (`#4D5244`, L34, about 14%) that opens as a wedge between them; every seat is cut by an edge or overlapped. The girl (polygon 17%) sits low, her head at (0.72, 0.22), leaning back with one arm behind her head and her gaze running down left to the dog on the opposite chair at (0.12, 0.45). White dress (`#C5C6C0`, L80) and plaid sash make her the one thing not blue. Pattern on the chairs is a scatter of short single strokes in pink, green and black; red-pink touches are only 0.2%. The face is smoothly modelled; the upholstery next to it is wet, broad strokes with visible direction. Infrared work at the NGA, reported by several sources, finds Degas's hand in the background and an altered wall boundary; a letter by Cassatt confirms his help.

**In the Loge, Pearl Necklace, The Loge, The Tea.** In the Loge the woman is about half the canvas (polygon 50.5%), black on black (62% of pixels under L25), with the only strong lights on the face, the gloved hand, a balcony (`#C6AA83`) and a pink collar. She looks left along a level line at y≈0.31 to the edge; a man in the tier opposite, his head about a quarter the height of hers, leans at (0.20, 0.17) with glasses aimed at her. The balcony sweeps as two curves from the left and meets the raised arm at 0.35–0.5 x. In Pearl Necklace the balcony, chandelier and crowd at right and left are the mirror behind her, and her own bare back appears at the left: the mirror lowers sharpness and contrast, gold and violet-brown clusters replace the dress colours. Shadow on the face is a mauve-grey (`#9E7B7D`, L54). In The Loge a fan covers the lower face of the rear figure and its radius echoes the balcony curves. In The Tea the guest's cup covers nose and mouth, saucer lifted; the wall's red stripes are about 5% of the width apart and set a beat the figures interrupt; the silver tray (0.51–0.95 across) gets the sharpest lights and faces stay plain.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Two or three bodies, one outline** (Child's Bath 57%, Omnibus 46%, The Bath 34%, Maternal Caress, Kiss, Boating Party 16%). Bodies overlap with no background between them; the seams are contact points (cheek, hand on hip, hand on foot), and the skin of one runs into the other's in the same measured colour (Caress, Kiss: difference under 1 L).
- **One point of contact, aimed at by gaze and line** (Child's Bath: foot at (0.28, 0.77); Tea Party: plate at (0.47, 0.53); Letter: envelope at (0.45, 0.50); Boating Party: child, with three lines at 58°, 24°, 19°; Omnibus: the child). It is off the geometric centre by 3–25% and is a small, light, or uniquely coloured thing.
- **Looking is shown as an act, never met** (In the Loge, Fitting, Letter, Child's Bath, Woman Bathing, Coiffure). No measured picture has the main figure look at the viewer; gazes go down, out of frame, or into a mirror. Someone else's look (the man in the Loge, the rower, the nurse) runs back into the picture.
- **The face is covered by the act** (Letter: envelope; Tea: cup; The Loge: fan; In the Loge: opera glasses; Coiffure and Woman Bathing: turned away; Boating Party's man: cap and back). The covering object carries the meaning.
- **Plain against patterned, never both** (all ten prints, Child's Bath, Blue Armchair). Skin, and at least one large shape, is close to dead flat (patch L standard deviation 0.8–1.5 for skin in Coiffure, Woman Bathing, Caress and Kiss, 3–5 for aquatint cloth); next to it sits a busy stripe, sprig or scribble. The pattern marks where one shape stops; no shadow does. Stripes also bend to show roundness (Coiffure chair, Child's Bath skirt).
- **Pattern at two or three scales in one sheet** (Letter: sprig, rosette, pleat; Fitting: wide-pale versus tight-dark stripe; Coiffure: paper, stripe, carpet). Each has its own ground value, so they never tie.
- **High viewpoint, horizon cropped or lost** (Child's Bath ≈35° down, Boating Party horizon at 6–7%, Coiffure, Letter's lifted table, Blue Armchair floor). Ground becomes a patterned backdrop; things are tipped so their tops are visible, while some (basin wall, jug) are kept side-on.
- **Shear instead of recede** (Coiffure 12° and 23°, Letter 35° and 4°, Omnibus 3.4°, Fitting). Uprights stay vertical; only one set of edges slants, at different angles in each object.
- **Objects cut by the edge** (tub and jug, cups, oar, sail, four chairs, seamstress, armchair). Each edge cuts a thing that continues, on at least two sides.
- **A post or dark block closes one side** (Letter's desk 18%, Coiffure's wardrobe, Fitting's seamstress 19% at the foot, Boating Party's man 25%, Omnibus's grey ceiling). It is the darkest or plainest mass and never the subject.
- **The same line, many states** (Bath 17 states, Omnibus and Fitting 7, Caress 6, Coiffure and Tea and Kiss 5, Letter and Lamp 4, per the Met). Contour is settled in the drawing and transferred once; tone and colour are then tested: light-dark reversal, saturated-then-muted, with and without pattern. The final state is often less saturated than the trial (Bath yellow C54 to C45, tub C14 to C8; omnibus bench clear blue to L30 grey-teal).
- **Remove at the moment of transfer** (Omnibus: the man; Coiffure: no pattern in the drawing). Nothing in the drawing survives unless it is needed by the group.
- **Two or three plates** (Met records; Bath from two, most from three). Colour areas butt against each other and leave a hairline of paper; two pin holes on the centre line register the plates (Omnibus, Kiss, Lamp, Bath).
- **One saturated colour, rationed and repeated** (Tea Party cobalt 2.7–3.7% in six places; Boating Party pink 2%; Blue Armchair red touches 0.2%; Bath yellow 20%, the exception, carried by the mass).
- **White is paper, used as a shape** (Coiffure skirt L91, Letter envelope L88, Omnibus child L77). In the prints it is the lightest shape and not a highlight.
- **Reflection as second subject** (Coiffure 24% mirror, Fitting, Woman Bathing, Pearl Necklace, Lamp). The reflection shows what the body's pose hides, at a smaller size or lower strength, and its frame is overlapped by the body so that the two are one.
- **The generative rule:** choose an act between people or between a person and a thing; find its single point of contact; stand close and above; let bodies overlap into one outline that holds 35–55% of the frame; crop at least two objects on the edges; leave one shape empty and flat and dress the neighbours in pattern; put one dark plain block on an edge; keep colour in three to six flat tones with one rationed accent; draw the contour once, then vary only tone, pattern and colour until the weakest version is dropped. No gaze returns to the viewer.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Mary Cassatt.** *Find the one point where two parties meet, fuse them into a single cropped mass around it, tell regions apart by plain against textured at near-equal value, then freeze the outline and revise only tone through kept states.*
Process:
1. Name the act the view exists for and its ONE point of contact: the place where the two parties to it meet (a person and a thing, a request and its answer, two records). Put it 3–25% of the frame away from the geometric centre and keep it small, 1.5–4% of the area. Make it by leaving out: it is the bare ground showing through as a shape, the extreme value of the view, not something added on top.
2. Fuse the parties into one outline that holds 35–55% of the frame, with no ground between them. They overlap at the contact, and the surfaces that touch share one tone (under 1 L apart), so the seam is the contact itself. Empty ground lies behind or above the mass, up to half the frame, never between the parties. In a wide format the mass may fall to about 16% if a second plain mass of 25% or more balances it.
3. Stand close and above: no band at the top that sets a calm distance, and nothing complete with clear air round it. The frame cuts at least two things that continue beyond it, on at least two sides, and the mass itself touches or leaves the frame on one or two sides. Only what continues may be cut; the contact and the key figure are whole.
4. Close one side with one plain block flush with an edge, 18–25% of the frame, 30–45 L from the ground. It is the plainest mass, carries no texture and is never the subject.
5. Fix the outline now, once, and transfer it. Whatever the draft holds that the act does not need is removed at this moment, and its place is left empty, not refilled.
6. Separate by plain against textured, never by shadow or border. Where two neighbouring regions are within 4–7 L, leave one dead flat and give the other a regular fine texture (ruled rows, a repeat, running text). The part that carries the meaning is always the plain one. Use two or three textures per view, each at a different scale and on a different ground value, and never two textured regions side by side.
7. Colour in three to six flat tones: ground, one extreme, one or two mids, one accent. Keep the large neighbours low in chroma so that an accent at two to four times their chroma reads strong at 2–4% of the area. Relay the accent through three to six small places, one of them cut by the frame; never one place only, never a large one.
8. Spend the finish in one place per party: one dense, sharp, high-contrast mark that anchors it (its identifier). Everything else stays flat, drawn with a single line about 1/1000 of the short side, in two line colours assigned by kind, left open where shapes join.
9. Every pointer in the view (two or three edges at different angles, each secondary element's direction) runs to the contact; nothing addresses the viewer. If the subject hides a side that matters, show it once in a second view in the same tones at lower strength, with the subject overlapping the strip that divides them.
10. Revise by states, 4–17 of them, one variable each over the fixed outline: one tone for the whole mass, lighter and darker, with and without texture, light and dark reversed, chroma pushed to its maximum. Ship a state 15–40% below the strongest trial in chroma. Keep the states on two fixed register points so they can be laid side by side. A set of views shares one size class, one tone count and one state procedure.
Why it works:
- Overlap with no ground between is read as one object, so two parties become one thing with one event on it. A gap between them makes two objects and the eye alternates. Centred, the contact becomes a symbol; off-centre, the mass has a long side and a short side and the eye travels the long one to arrive.
- Texture segregates regions before value does. Two fields 4 L apart are told apart at once if one is ruled and the other flat, so shadow and border are freed and the whole view can sit in a narrow, light value band where a moderate colour reads as bright. Texture on the meaning-bearing part would hide it; the flat region is the odd one in a textured surround and is found first.
- A thing cut by the frame is read as continuing, so the view is an instant taken from something larger and near. A complete object with air round it is a specimen at a distance.
- With the outline fixed, two states differ by one variable and the difference is attributable. If outline and tone both move, nothing can be compared. Pushing chroma past the answer and coming back finds the least that still reads.
Colour relations: ground the largest share (a third to a half) and one extreme of value; the fused mass 35–55% in one or two mid tones that are under 1 L apart where parties touch; closing block 18–25%, 30–45 L from the ground, lowest in chroma; accent 2–4%, highest in chroma by a factor of two to four, in three to six places; large neighbours 4–7 L apart and separated by texture; contact shape in the ground's own value, 1.5–4%. Shade, where it is needed at all, is a second hue at mid value, never the dark extreme.
Space and line: upright frame near 2:3 to 4:5, or wide with the mass at about 16%; contact 3–25% off centre; two or more things cut on two or more sides; one edge closed by the block; no centred symmetry, the centre axis kept for the two register points; uprights stay vertical and depth, if shown, is one slanted edge per object at its own angle (3–35°); one line weight, about 1/1000 of the short side, two line colours.
Type requirement: an even-coloured text face of one weight, so a block of running text or a ruled list reads as a fine regular texture and can serve as one of the textured regions; one heavier cut reserved for the single dense mark per party. The key figure sits on a flat region, never on a textured one. Nothing is lettered across the mass's contact; credits, state number and count sit small at a lower corner outside the mass.
Motion and interaction: show the moment just before or just after contact. On approach (hover, focus, drag) the two parties close the last 4–8px over 150–250ms and hold; the press is the contact. A state change crossfades tone over 200–300ms while no outline moves. A scrubber or split view lays states side by side on the register points. No drift, no blur. Reduced motion: swap states at once and show the parties already in contact.
Fails when: the view has one party and no act (settings, a lone report); many equal peers (a grid has no single contact); the product must address the user directly (an alert, a destructive confirmation); the meaning-bearing content is itself the dense texture (a table that is the subject); the content must be seen whole (a chart with its axes, a map, a legal text); the frame is too small for a 35% mass plus a closing block.
Signature (do not carry together): an adult and a child closed in one outline and seen from above · flat unshaded skin beside hand-drawn stripes and sprigs on cream paper · a face hidden by a cup, a letter or a mirror.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Gate handover screen for a container terminal, rugged tablet held upright, 800 × 1280, near-black with one signal accent and a monospaced face, terse and industrial (the works are pale, domestic and hand-drawn).
1. *Act and contact.* The act is a declared manifest meeting the gate's own reading. The contact is the seal-number match: a slot 280 × 72px (2.0% of the screen) centred at (0.46, 0.57), 4% and 7% off the centre. It is a cut-out showing the ground (L 10), the darkest shape on the screen, with the matched number set in it in light ink.
2. *One mass.* The manifest panel (600 × 520px) and the gate-reading panel (520 × 320px) overlap by a 90px band that holds the slot; together 42% of the screen. Along the band both panels take one tone. The empty ground is above the mass, about 15% of the screen; none lies between the panels.
3. *Crop.* The manifest panel runs off the right edge, the previous handover is cut by the top edge and the next truck's card by the bottom edge. The slot and the reading are whole.
4. *Closing block.* A plain strip 152px wide down the left edge (19%), L 54, 44 L from the ground: gate, lane, operator, clock. No texture, no accent beyond one tag.
5. *Fix and remove.* The wireframe's driver photo and weather chip are not needed by the match and are dropped; their corner stays empty. The geometry is frozen here.
6. *Plain against textured.* Manifest panel L 30, reading panel L 34. The manifest is ruled rows at a 44px pitch (its text lines are the texture); the reading panel is dead flat and holds the measured weight. The queue card at the bottom is hatched at a 12px pitch on the ground value. No border, no shadow.
7. *Tones.* Five: ground, two mids, block, accent. The accent is about three times the chroma of the panels and totals 2.8% in five places: the lane tag, the tick on the matched row, the weight difference, the release control, and the next truck's tag, cut by the bottom edge. Each has a label or glyph.
8. *Finish.* One heavy mark per panel, the container number. Every other glyph is the regular weight; dividers are 1px (1/800 of the short side), light ink for declared values and a dimmer ink for measured ones.
9. *Pointers.* The matched row's rule, the reading panel's upper edge and the block's lane tag all end at the slot. No banner speaks to the operator; a mismatch changes the slot's tone and label.
10. *States.* Six kept on a compare page, registered on the top and bottom centre points: outline only; one tone for the whole mass; lighter mids; ground and block reversed; accent at full chroma; shipped, with the accent's chroma cut by a fifth. Waiting, matched, mismatch and released are tone states of the same outline.
- *Motion.* On scan the reading panel closes the last 8px to the manifest over 200ms and holds. Reduced motion shows them already joined.

Result: a dark, hard-edged gate tool with no pale ground, no figures and no hand-drawn mark. What survived is one contact made of bare ground, two parties fused round it, a crop on three sides, a plain block on one edge, flat against ruled at 4 L apart, a relayed accent, and tone revised over a fixed outline.

## Space & composition
- **Figure share:** main mass 34–57% of the frame in the prints and Child's Bath, around 16–17% in the wide pictures (armchair, boat), where a second large mass (a 25% rower, a 60% field of blue) balances it.
- **Format:** prints are tall and near 0.66–0.78 (Letter and Child's Bath both 0.655, Kiss 0.66, Fitting 0.68, Coiffure, Omnibus and Caress 0.73, Bath, Lamp and Tea 0.77–0.78). Landscape 1.31 and 1.49 for the two large group scenes.
- **Empty side:** Mother's Kiss leaves 51% plain, Blue Armchair 14% floor, In the Loge a plain brown wall right of centre. The empty part is behind or above and never between the bodies.
- **Contact points:** (0.47, 0.53), (0.45, 0.50), (0.28, 0.77), (0.50, 0.33). Heads, when there are two, touch or overlap: (0.50, 0.23), (0.55, 0.23), and Mother's Kiss.
- **Edges:** tub, jug, cups, oar, sail and chairs all cut. The main outline touches or leaves the frame on one or two sides (Child's Bath left, Fitting left and bottom, Loge right and bottom).
- **No centred symmetry.** The vertical centre is kept only for registration marks.

## Form, line & typography
- **Line:** one thin contour, 0.2–0.3 mm, uneven, swelling at overlaps, with open ends and doubled starts (Coiffure, zoom). It stops at the joint and is not closed for tidiness. Line colour is a second decision: red-brown for skin, black for cloth (Bath, Kiss).
- **Tone:** aquatint is flat grain with no gradient on form; gradient occurs only as plate-wide wipe (Omnibus tan and pink, Kiss wall). Edges of pattern are brushed or stopped out, so they wobble.
- **Hair is the only dense mark:** parallel strokes with reserved lights; the darkest, sharpest point of most prints and the cue that anchors a head.
- **Stripe is drawn by hand**, unequal (about 1:3), following the bulk. Sprigs are single dabs, repeated but not identical.
- **Handling contrast in oil:** smooth faces, broad wet touches for upholstery (Armchair detail), textureless flats for boat and jacket.
- **Lettering:** the prints carry a pencil inscription under the plate at the lower right ("Imprimée par l'artiste et M. Leroy", "25 épreuves", signature), small and off the plate, a colophon and not a title. The paintings are signed at a lower corner in a colour of the picture. Nothing is lettered inside the image.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- **Each work holds a held moment:** a lick of an envelope, a lifted cup, hair pinned, a wash cloth lowered, a pose between strokes of the oar. Motion is the moment before or after the contact.
- **Two beats at once:** figure group slow and large, stripes or sprigs fast and small (Tea wall at about 5% pitch; Coiffure stripes; skirt stripes of Child's Bath).
- **Series time:** the ten prints are one size-class of day, dressing, washing, writing, riding, tea, child, and share plate height (32–38 cm), two or three plates and an edition of 25. Within a print, time is the sequence of states, whose changes can be read by laying impressions side by side.
- **Eye path:** gaze leads to contact, then along arm or stripe back up to a head, and around again (Child's Bath loop). Reflections double the stop.

## UI translations
1. **One contact per view.** Find where the user's object and the system's answer meet and make that the single small target, 1.5–4% of the view and 3–25% off centre.
2. **Cut the contact out.** Render the contact as the ground showing through the panels and not as a raised button; it is the extreme value of the view.
3. **Fuse the pair.** Overlap the two related panels with no gutter and give the touching surfaces one tone; together they hold 35–55% of the view. Spare space goes above or behind, never between.
4. **Crop what continues.** Let at least two continuing elements (a list, a queue, a neighbouring item) run off two sides of the view; never crop the key figure or a chart.
5. **Close one edge with a plain block.** Put navigation or meta in one flat block of 18–25% on one edge, 30–45 L from the ground, with no texture.
6. **Ruled against flat instead of borders.** Where two regions are within 4–7 L, rule or hatch one and leave the other dead flat; the key figure sits on the flat one. Two or three textures per view, at different scales.
7. **Text is a texture.** A list or a paragraph in one even weight counts as a textured region; plan it as one and never put two side by side.
8. **Relay the accent.** Keep neighbours low in chroma and repeat one accent in three to six small labelled places totalling 2–4%, one of them cut by the frame.
9. **One dense mark per party.** Give each panel a single heavy, sharp identifier and keep everything else flat with 1px lines in two inks assigned by kind.
10. **Point inward.** Aim rules, edges and secondary elements at the contact; state changes appear at the contact, not in a banner that addresses the user.
11. **A second view for the hidden side.** If the object hides something that matters, show it once beside it in the same tones at lower strength, overlapped by the object.
12. **Freeze geometry, revise tone.** Lock the layout, then make 4–17 tone states changing one variable each; ship one 15–40% below the strongest in chroma, and keep the states comparable on fixed register points. Interface states (waiting, done, error) are tone states of the same outline.

## What this artist would reject
- A subject posed for the viewer and looking out to be liked.
- A complete room, object or body with clear air round it.
- Shadow used to separate two shapes that pattern or value could separate.
- Pattern on everything, or pattern on the part that carries the meaning.
- A first colour choice left unrevised; a finished thing that cannot show its trials.
- Two figures placed next to each other with a gap and no contact.
- A central, symmetrical motif, or a horizon that sets a calm distance.
- An accent colour in large, or in one place only.

## Signature prompt seed
Find the one place where the two parties to this view meet and make it a small cut-out of bare ground, off centre. Overlap the two parties into one mass of about half the view with no gap between them, and let the frame cut the things that continue. Close one edge with a plain flat block. Separate neighbouring regions by ruling one and leaving the other flat, at nearly the same value, with the key figure on the flat one. Use three to six flat tones and relay one accent through a few small places. Lock the layout, then revise tone only, keeping every state.
