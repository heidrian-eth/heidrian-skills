# Claude Monet — Impressionism

## Essence
Monet holds a plain motif and a viewpoint almost still and paints what changes: one condition of light that tints sky, object, ground and shadow together (he called it the *enveloppe*). The object stays recognisable by silhouette alone; nearly everything else (which part is lighter, which is warmer, where the edge is) is recalculated for each canvas. The picture is made of separate, visible touches whose direction follows the thing they belong to, and the result is judged against its sister canvases.

Single stacks stand left of centre (apex .31–.37) and fill 15.6–16.7% of the canvas, 43–47% of its width. Pairs put the large one right (.65–.77) with the small one at the other end, small : large area 1 : 3.5–4; Sunset, Snow overlaps them and is the exception (large apex .42). One large shape, at most two, and no people, animals or carts.

**End of Summer.** Evening light from behind right. The large stack's face is a dull rose-brown (`#937A70`, L53 C12). Its right contour is a band of red-orange touches only ≈25–40px (1.3–2% of the width) thick, `#9B745F` on average, with single touches at C42+; it has the same L as the face (52 vs 53), so the rim "lights" by chroma, not by brightness. The shadow on the stubble is `#89949E` (hue 259, blue) against sunlit stubble `#989D96` (hue 134): only 4 L darker. The house roofs and the tree line repeat the blue and the green in specks. Sky strokes run at about +31° (rising right), field strokes almost flat (−5°). The eye goes from the orange rim down to the blue shadow wedge, then along it left to the small stack.

**End of Day, Autumn.** Stacks and field differ by hue more than value: lower stack `#9B665E` (L48 C25 h34), field `#91847D` (L55 C7). The large stack is graded from olive at the tip to rose at the base inside one value (L51 to 48). The cool note is the far hill, `#68707B` (blue, L46), placed exactly behind the gap between the stacks. A dotted ring of pink-orange touches surrounds both outlines and spreads into the field, so there is no drawn edge.

**Sunset, Snow.** The upper right is orange (`#C5865E`, L61 C37 h57) and the field is violet-grey (`#9990AA`, L61 C15 h306): identical lightness, hues about 110° apart. Orange covers about 16% of the canvas (three clusters, 5.8 + 5.3 + 4.6%); half the canvas is one violet-grey cluster (`#908A9D`, 50%). The cast shadow is the most saturated cool in the picture: `#6C80AA`, C25 against 15 for lit snow, 8 L darker. The near-dark is confined to the stack (`#625457`, L37). Reddish strokes repeat the sky along the stack's left contour and in the lower-left snow. The small stack is half hidden behind the large one.

**Overcast Snow.** Dull light, no cast shadow, only a greenish dirty ring at the base. Stack `#75524A` (L38 C17 h36) against field `#B6B5BC` (L73 C4): the widest value step of the six (27 L between stack and rest). Cool colour is concentrated in one ribbon, the far hill (`#77799D`, C21). The sun is a pale greenish disc at (.41, .08) about the size of a house. About half the canvas (51%) has chroma under 8; greys are the main material.

**Stack of Wheat (1983.29).** Three kinds of stroke name three things: roof strokes slide down the cone (≈73° on the left slope, C9 mix of brown with violet-white snow), tree strokes are vertical, field strokes lie almost flat (170°, coherence 0.37). The dark base is `#5E4143`, L30, the deepest large area in the set. The field holds blue-violet, pale pink, brown and green commas; from afar it reads as lavender-grey `#A7A3B4`.

**Thaw, Sunset.** The sky is nearly grey (`#AFA8A6`, C3) except a yellow-orange strip above the hill (`#B7A37E`, C22), ≈7% of the height. That yellow returns as separate touches right and left of the stack and, as pink-orange, through the snow (`#A68D88`, h37). The shadow is neutral (`#877E80`, C4), 7 L darker. Its blue exists only as loose commas.

**Snow, Morning (Getty).** Sky, hill and field are within 7 L (77, 72, 79) and share hue 73–77: one warm veil. Only the two stacks fall below it (L44–45), their lit left edges orange-brown (`#875F43`, C26) and their snow pure blue-grey (`#828994`). Shadow `#9AA5B0`: 12 L below the snow and turned from hue 73 to 258. The shadow is the only large cool shape and it leads off the right edge.

**Impression, Sunrise.** Sun disc `#BC4D39`, L47, a=43, b=34, C55. The fog within a disc's width of it: `#64726F` and `#6A7474`, L47–48, C4–6. Difference in lightness: 0–1 L; difference in chroma: 50. The disc is 60px in 1920, 3.1% of the width and 0.10% of the area; sun plus its broken reflection are about 0.2%. It sits at (.61, .31); the reflection runs straight down under it as roughly 40 short horizontal dashes (by eye) whose gaps widen toward the viewer. Depth is a lightness ladder in one hue family (157–197°): dock and ships L41–43, far boat L40, middle boat L30, near boat L19. The three boats lie on a line falling right at about 23°. Values of the whole sit between 39 and 55 (5th–95th percentile), a 16 L range. The paint is thin and lets the canvas show; masts and cranes are single drags of the brush. The sky strokes run at about 22° against flat water strokes. No shoreline is completed.

**La Grenouillère.** The water occupies more than half the height and is built of horizontal bars (dominant angle 179°, coherence 0.60–0.69, the highest of any water measured) in four unmixed families: near-black green, ochre-green, pale blue, cream. Bar lengths grow from ≈30–40px far away to ≈60–110px near the viewer (≈1.5 to 5 cm on the canvas). Water contrast is large (L17–81 inside one region), and blacks are still used (2.7% below L20, 21% below L30). A small round island is the hub, placed at .44 across, with a horizontal gangway and plank fastening it left and right. The tree trunk rises through its middle and is mirrored by a wriggling dark column. The boat bottom left, the roofed barge right and the branches top left are all cut by the edge. Faces are blank; a figure is 3–6 strokes. The lettering on the barge is a few dark dabs, cropped and partly covered by a white towel.

**Gare Saint-Lazare (AIC).** A symmetrical gable frames the scene but its apex is at .38 of the width while the engine stands at .62, on either side of the centre. The engine is the darkest shape (`#33414C`, L27), straight under the biggest blue cloud. Steam is painted as two materials: white steam lit from outside (L81, `#C1CBCA`) and blue-grey steam indoors (L62, `#839B9E`), as curled separate touches with almost no common direction (coherence 0.10–0.16), against the ground's flat ones (0.60) and the ruled roof bars. Steam covers the roof's girders where they cross, so structure fades and comes back. The crowd is a band of dark dabs with a few red-brown heads. Whole-canvas hues lie only between 90° and 270° (green to blue); warm is dots. The middle band is the brightest and the emptiest.

**The Four Trees.** A near-square (1.008). Four trunks at 15, 37, 56 and 83% of the width: intervals of 22, 19 and 27%. They run off the top and off the bottom (as reflections); nowhere do we see a root or a crown. A dark bank crosses at 65–81% of the height; below it the trunks and the yellow trees repeat, softened, so the picture reads as one sheet with a band across it. Trunks go orange at the top left and blue-violet below; sky `#B5BEDE` (L77 C18) is violet-blue against yellow-pink foliage (touch C up to 24 but averaged C≈1: the pinks, yellows and blues cancel). The trunk strokes are the most strictly directed measured (87–91°, coherence 0.70–0.80).

**Poplars (tall, 7 trunks).** Trunk intervals as % of the width: 10, 19, 13, 9, 14, 15. The bank is at 77–85% of the height. Crowns grow from lower left to top right, cutting the top edge. Reflection repeats the trunks straight down. The same device as Four Trees, with the frame turned to portrait.

**Rouen Cathedral, two states.** In both, the portal's apex stands at about (.53–.54, .62–.64) and the spire at (.57–.62, 0–.01), with the gallery gable and the rose arch in the same relative places: the architecture is a fixed drawing cropped hard, with no whole tower, hardly any ground (bottom ≈3–4%) and sky in only a small wedge. In Sunlight the stone is the light thing (`#D2CAB6`, L81, warm h92) against a darker blue sky (`#93AEC9`, L70, h260); shadowed arches go orange and violet (`#B6A48D`, `#A2998E`) and stay high, L64–68. In the other canvas the sky is the light thing (`#DAE0E1`, L89, C2), the façade blue-grey (`#AAABCD` tower L75, rose arch `#6E8299` L54) and only slivers of peach (`#DBCFC4`, L84) catch the sun at the upper left. The two thus swap figure and ground: stone minus sky is +11 L in one and −10 to −35 L in the other. No line survives; vertical dabs (94°) make the stone, and the paint is a dry crust. Three figures at the bottom left in Sunlight, ≈4% of the height, give the scale.

**Houses of Parliament, three.** One silhouette. The Victoria Tower is 9–11% of the canvas width and placed at .42 (Sunset), .46 (AIC), .70 (Fog): the same drawing slid along. Water starts at 66–70% of the height; the building's reflection continues it down into the water in the same hue. Tower lightness is 50, 27 and 60 and the adjoining sky 68–76, 45–47 and 74: a constant interval of −14 to −26 L in three different keys. The tower's hue hardly moves (255–263, all blue) while its chroma goes 12, 16, 23 and the sky changes from yellow-pink to grey with an orange breach to pale lilac. In Sunset, pink-gold dashes ≈40–90px long (≈2–4 cm) cover the water but stop dead at the reflection's edge, which is blue. Vertical strokes on the building (90–94°, coherence 0.28–0.52), flat ones on the water (170–184°, 0.28–0.52), undirected scumble in the sky (0.04–0.15). Architecture has no windows, no cornice and no clock: just notches against the sky.

**Bridge, 1899, and Water Lilies, 1906.** Bridge: the bow of the bridge crosses the canvas at about 27–45% of the height and runs off both sides; the rest is foliage above, pads below. Vertical dabs in the foliage and its reflection and horizontal pads (angle 177°, coherence 0.44) share one plane; pad bands repeat every ≈8–10% of the height with the interval growing toward the viewer. Hues are 82% yellow-green (45–135°). Water Lilies: near-square (1.039). No bank and no sky; the sky is the blue patch in the middle (`#5286A4`, L54 C23) and the reflected willows are dull blue-green (`#58737D`). Pads lie in ≈8 loose horizontal clusters, some sliced by the left, right and top edges. In the lower middle, single pads are ≈110–160px wide (6–8% of the width) and in the top band whole clusters are only ≈40–60px high; distance is given only by that. The flowers are three dabs each (pink, red, violet) with touch chroma 40, about 1% of the area. Only 3% of the canvas has chroma under 8; there is no grey, yet L stays within 39–63.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Fix the drawing, vary the condition** (Stacks, seven; Rouen, two; Parliament, three). Band heights in the Stacks stay within 23–34 / 23–29 / 40–49%; the stack's eave stays at 63–66% of its height; Rouen's portal apex stays within ≈2% of the canvas; the Parliament tower is 9–11% wide in all three. What changes is colour, value order and the place of warm and cool.
- **Same frame size so canvases can be swapped** (Parliament ≈81 × 92 cm in all three; Rouen ≈100 × 66; Stacks 60–66 high, 92–101 wide). The Met notes that the London canvases were ordered in one non-standard size. The Getty essay reports more than a dozen Rouen canvases touched in one day.
- **Two or three fixed viewpoints, then crops and shifts** (Rouen: three rooms, each slightly more oblique; Parliament: one place, the tower moved from .42 to .70; Poplars: several cropping schemes). A series is a small set of viewpoints multiplied by conditions, not a free wander.
- **One light tints everything** (all the series). In Getty Morning sky, hill and field share hue 73–77 and 7 L. In the Rouen blue canvas 81% of the pixels with chroma above 8 lie in hue 225–315 (54% of all pixels are near-grey). In Four Trees the violet-blue sky, the water and the bank share hue 259–289. Whole-canvas mean chroma stays low (C1–15 in nearly every canvas) though touches reach C21–59.
- **Transposed keys, fixed intervals** (Parliament). Subject minus surroundings stays −14 to −26 L while the whole picture moves from L43 (fog) to L67 (pale blue). Object hue stays blue (255–263); only its chroma (12 → 23) and the air move.
- **Figure and ground may swap** (Rouen). Stone is 11 L lighter than the sky in Sunlight and 10 to 35 L darker in the blue canvas, with the same outline. Nothing about the object's lightness is constant; the silhouette is.
- **Opposed hues at equal lightness carry the event** (Impression: sun L47 / fog L47–48, chroma difference 50; Sunset, Snow: sky L61 / field L61, hues 110° apart; End of Summer: rim L52 / face L53). The eye reads light, although no lightness difference exists. Livingstone's account and a 2021 study say the same of the Impression sun.
- **Shadow is a shift in hue, small in value** (End of Summer 4 L; Thaw 7 L; Sunset, Snow 8 L; Getty 12 L). Its hue moves toward blue (258–280) and in Sunset, Snow it gains chroma (25 vs 15). Shadow is the complement of the light, not the darkened object.
- **Black is nearly absent after 1870** (every canvas measured of the 1890s: 0.0% of pixels below L20, except Bridge 0.7%; Grenouillère 1869: 2.7%; Impression 0.3%). Below L30: 0–4% in the series, against 21% in 1869 and 8% in Gare Saint-Lazare. Range is compressed: Getty 44–81, Impression 39–55, Water Lilies 39–63 (5th–95th percentile).
- **Touch direction says what the thing is** (every canvas). Sky +14° to +38°; field and water 170–184°, i.e. flat; buildings and trunks 87–94°; roofs of stacks follow the slope. Vertical strokes reach coherence 0.70–0.80 on poplar trunks, water 0.4–0.69, sky 0.04–0.3. Two directions per picture, three at most, sort the parts with no outline.
- **Opposed touches cancel at a distance** (Rouen West: mean touch chroma 9.5 drops to 6.7 when blurred, −30%; End of Summer −22%; Poplars −21%; Rouen Sunlight −17%). Impression, Parliament and Gare Saint-Lazare lose only 4–14%: they were mixed or scumbled, not divided. So optical mixture is a setting he turns up with the 1890s, not a constant.
- **Visible touch size is constant inside a zone and grows toward the viewer** (Grenouillère bars ≈1.5 cm far, ≈5 cm near; Parliament dashes 2–4 cm; Water Lilies pads 6–8% of the width near). Perry reported strokes about an inch apart and a quarter-inch thick in a first sitting.
- **Build-up in passes over the whole surface** (Perry: first pass over the whole canvas to fix the key, second closer together, subject emerging; AIC technical reports: work moved back and forth over the whole painting; Getty: thick crust plus scumbled veils added in the studio). No finished corner sits beside a bare one.
- **The layout is corrected as the light is** (AIC reports: Sunset, Snow stacks first set much farther apart; Overcast, a small stack painted out and the large one widened; Thaw, horizon height moved, perhaps several times). The frame is fixed for the viewer but was negotiated on the canvas.
- **The instant takes long** (letter to Geffroy, October 1890: instantaneity needs much work; Rouen: begun 1892–93, finished in 1894; London: completed at Giverny until 1904). Canvases stay open and are compared as a group; the group is shown as a group (15 Stacks in 1891, 20 Cathedrals in 1895).
- **Hard crop, incomplete object** (Grenouillère boats and roof; Poplars trunks; Rouen towers; Bridge ends; pads). Frame cuts at least two sides of the main thing, so it continues outside. Little is centred: anchors sit at .31–.46 or .61–.77.
- **Near-square formats and no horizon in the late work** (Four Trees 1.008, Water Lilies 1.039, Parliament ≈1.13). The plane tilts up until it fills the canvas and depth is only size of mark.
- **Reflection doubles the structure** (Impression sun column, Poplars, Parliament, Grenouillère trunk, Lilies). The upper half is repeated below in flatter, softer, broken touches; the axis is usually well below the middle (Poplars bank 65–81%, Parliament 66–70%); Impression's, at ≈46%, is the exception.
- **Generative rule:** choose one plain shape, one viewpoint and one frame; divide the frame in three bands; fix the drawing. For each state of the light set the overall key (mean L and mean hue), give the shadow the opposite hue and only 4–12 L less, put the event in two hues of equal L, let each zone have its own stroke direction, and repeat a little of the event's colour in all the other zones. Keep the states on the same frame and judge them together.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Claude Monet.** *Fix one frame, one viewpoint and one plain outline; for each condition re-key the whole view under one tint, hold the subject at a constant value interval from its surroundings, turn shade to the opposite hue a few L away, set the event at equal lightness in the opposing hue, and keep the states on the same frame as a series.*
Process:
1. Name the condition that varies (hour, period, load, environment) and fix everything that will not: ONE frame size for every state and one to three viewpoints, each a crop of the same layout. Divide the frame into three bands, 23–34 / 23–29 / 40–49% of the height, the largest nearest the user. In the frontal mode (frame between 1:1 and 1.13:1) drop the top band and let one surface fill the frame.
2. Place one plain subject, read by its outline alone, with no interior detail. Either a whole mass of 14–17% of the area and 43–47% of the width, centred at 0.31–0.37 or 0.65–0.77 across, its top docked on a band line within 3% of the height, with at most one partner a quarter of its area at the other end; or a mass cut by two edges of the frame. Its landmarks then stay within 2% of the frame in every state. The only move allowed between states is a slide along one axis at an unchanged width.
3. For each state set one key before any part: a mean lightness and one hue family. Pull every zone into it, so that 80% or more of the coloured area lies inside a 90° span of hue and the mean chroma stays low, with single small marks reaching four or more times the mean. Surfaces keep to a band 16–37 L wide. Both ends of the range stay unused by surfaces: no neutral dark, at most 4% of the area in the lowest fifth of lightness, at most 3% in the top tenth, and the lightest surface carries the tint.
4. Hold the subject 14–26 L from its surroundings and keep that interval in every state while the key moves by 20 L or more. The sign may flip from one state to another (subject lighter here, darker there); the outline identifies it, not its lightness. It is the only large area that leaves the band.
5. Show shade, and any second state of a surface, by hue first: turn it 110–180° from the key, only 4–12 L from its lit neighbour, at equal or higher chroma (up to 1.7 times). Never the same hue darkened, never a neutral grey.
6. Set the event (the thing this state is about) within 1 L of what surrounds it, in the hue opposed to the key or at 2.5–10 times the surrounding chroma, with no outline. Keep it to 0.1–2% of the area; one state in a set may let it reach about 16%. Where the subject faces the condition, the same rule gives a rim 1.3–2% of the width at the face's own lightness and about three times its chroma. Because the event has no value edge, its reading (number, label, glyph) stands beside it in ink.
7. Repeat the event's hue in every other zone as small separate marks, and gather the opposing hue of the key into one ribbon or one shape. No zone is left without a trace of the event.
8. Sort the zones by direction and not by borders: each zone's repeated unit (row, tick, bar, line of text) runs one way, two or three directions per view (flat for the ground band, upright for standing things, a third only for the top), and one zone may stay undirected. The unit keeps one size inside a zone and grows up to three times from the farthest zone to the nearest.
9. Build and load in passes over the whole frame: first every zone at its key value and coarse grain, then all of them tightened together. No finished region stands beside an empty one. Secondary things get three to six units each and no more; the finish goes to the subject's outline and to the relations of steps 4–6. The layout may still be corrected in these passes (a band line moved, a partner removed, the subject widened) and is then frozen for the set.
10. Keep every state on the same frame and show them as a set of 3–20, ordered by the condition or grouped by key. When the condition changes, switch to the state that matches it; do not repaint the current one.
Why it works:
- A tint that covers every zone is read as the light and discounted, so the things stay the same things under a new condition. Recolour one zone alone and it reads as a different object. With frame and outline fixed, whatever differs between two states belongs to the condition.
- Lightness is judged against the neighbour, not absolutely. A subject held at one interval keeps its weight in a pale key and a dark one, so re-keying never re-ranks the view; and because surfaces stay in a narrow band, that one interval is the only large value step and needs no outline.
- Position and edge are read mostly from lightness. A hue set at the lightness of its surroundings is seen but weakly located: it appears to glow without forming a hard shape, and it draws the eye at a thousandth of the area. Made lighter than its surroundings it becomes a second figure competing with the subject.
- Under a tinted light, shade appears as the opposing hue. Turning hue and keeping the value step small tells lit from unlit without spending the value range; a darkened shade would add a second ladder of darks and the subject would lose its rank.
- Units that share a direction group before any line is drawn, so two or three directions separate the zones and borders are not needed. An accent in one place reads as an object; the same hue returned in small amounts to every zone reads as one light on all of them.
Colour relations: one key family holds 80% or more of the coloured area at low mean chroma; surroundings (two bands) are the largest share and lie inside 7–12 L of each other; the subject is 14–26 L away and 14–17% of the area, the only large value step; shade is the opposing hue, 4–12 L from lit, at 1–1.7 times its chroma; the event is 0.1–2%, within 1 L of its surroundings, the highest chroma by 2.5–10 times; its hue returns as traces in every zone; the lightest surface is tinted and neither end of the lightness range is used by a surface.
Space and line: one frame for the whole set; three bands, the lowest the largest; one off-centre mass docked on a band line, or one mass cut by two edges; the empty side is the larger side; intervals between repeated uprights uneven (from 9 to 27% of the width) while their direction is strict; no outline and no divider anywhere: an edge is where direction and hue change; ruled straight lines only for things that are straight in fact; optionally the upper structure is repeated below a line at 65–81% of the height, flatter and at lower contrast.
Type requirement: text is the crisp exception to the narrow band. One sturdy, even-stroke face with open apertures (hairlines vanish on mid-value surfaces), in an ink that takes the hue opposed to the key and reaches 4.5:1 on the surface it sits on, even where that puts it beyond the surface band. One size per zone, largest in the nearest zone; lines of text run with their zone's direction and count as its unit. No text on the event, on the rim, or across a boundary between two hues of equal lightness. State name and time sit small in a lower corner in a hue taken from the view.
Motion and interaction: a change of condition re-keys every zone at once, on one duration and one curve, slower than a response to touch, and no outline moves. Hover, press and selection are the shade rule: a turn of hue with a 4–12 L step. A scrubber or a strip of small frames steps through the series on the fixed frame. Loading follows the passes of step 9. No blur, no grain, no drift, no shimmer. Reduced motion: swap the state at once.
Fails when: there is only one state (nothing varies, so the fixed frame buys nothing and the result is merely a tinted theme); the subject is dense small text or a table (mid-value surfaces cost contrast); the event must be found by shape, by a colour-blind user or as an alarm (equal lightness gives it no value contrast, so it cannot stand without its ink label); the layout must differ between states; the view holds many equal peers; the brand depends on black on white.
Signature (do not carry together): separate unblended touches of paint · a stack, façade or pond dissolved in a pale haze · a broken reflection under a soft, unfinished edge.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Cold-store monitor on a wall display, 1920 × 1080, graphite with a signal-orange accent and a monospaced face, terse and hard-edged (the works are pale, rural and hand-made).
1. *Condition and frame.* The condition is the shift (day, night, defrost, door open). One 1920 × 1080 frame for all of them, one viewpoint. Bands: site strip 280px (26%), chamber row 280px (26%), event log 520px (48%).
2. *Subject.* The chamber under watch: a plain block 860 × 386px (16% of the area, 45% of the width), centred at 0.34 across, its top 10px above the line between the first two bands. A second chamber of 430 × 193px (a quarter of the area) stands at 0.80. Neither has interior detail. Both keep their place in every state.
3. *Key.* Day: surfaces at L 56–68, mean L 62, a warm graphite family holding about 85% of the coloured area. Night: L 38–50, mean L 44, a cool family. No surface below L 20 or above L 90; the brand's near-black is used as text ink only.
4. *Interval.* The chamber block is L 42 by day and L 24 at night: 20 L below its surroundings in both, though the key has moved 18 L.
5. *Shade.* Acknowledged log rows and the chamber's idle half turn about 150° from the key's hue and step 6 L, at the same chroma. Nothing is greyed out.
6. *Event.* An open door: a 48px disc in the field just off the block's edge, at the field's own lightness (L 62 by day, L 44 at night) and about eight times its chroma, with twelve 48 × 6px dashes under it for the minutes open, their gaps widening downward. Together 0.25% of the screen. The temperature and the door's name stand beside it in ink.
7. *Echo.* The orange returns as 2px ticks on that chamber's log rows and as one tick in the site strip, about 0.3% in all. The opposing hue is gathered in one ribbon, the dock lane.
8. *Direction.* Log rows run flat, rack bars inside the chamber row stand upright, the site strip is left undirected. No dividers. Log rows are 28px high at the old end and 64px at the newest, with one type size per zone.
9. *Passes.* On load every band and both blocks appear at their key value in one frame, then rows and readings resolve everywhere together. No panel shows a skeleton beside a finished one.
10. *Series.* The last eight shifts sit in the site strip as 240 × 135px copies of the same frame, each in its own key. A change of shift swaps the state; selecting a copy swaps it back.
- *Motion.* The re-key runs over the whole frame at once in 900ms; no block moves. Reduced motion swaps at once.
- *Type.* One medium weight. Ink is L 12 on the day field (5.5:1), L 96 on the chamber block and on the night surfaces (5:1 or more), each tinted toward the hue opposed to the key.

Result: a hard, monospaced monitor with no haze and no soft edge. What survived is a fixed frame and outline, one key over every zone, a subject held 20 L away in both keys, shade as a turn of hue, an event that has colour and no value step, and the shifts kept side by side.

## Space & composition
- **Three bands, large field.** Sky 23–34%, middle band 23–29%, ground 40–49% (Stacks).
- **One mass, off-centre.** 14–17% of the canvas for the main mass; with a second, a 1 : 3.5–4 partner; the empty side is the larger side. Apex near the horizon line (±3% in five of seven).
- **Uneven intervals, strict direction.** Trunks at 22, 19, 27% (Four Trees) and 10, 19, 13, 9, 14, 15% (tall Poplars): irregular steps, identical vertical.
- **Crop.** Main object cut by at least one edge, in most by two. Sky is a small wedge (Rouen) or absent (Water Lilies).
- **Empty brightest centre** (Gare Saint-Lazare: open sky L74 in the middle, frame dark; Getty: pale field).
- **Event size.** Very small: Impression sun 0.10%, lily flowers ≈1%, yellow horizon strip in Thaw ≈7% of the height. Only Sunset, Snow lets the event be large, ≈16%.

Logic. (1) Each canvas is a family of 2–3 hues under one overall tint, with one opponent: blue-violet with orange, warm sand with blue, red-brown with blue-violet. (2) Mean chroma is low (C1–15); saturation is spent by single touches and by an event that rarely exceeds 1–2% of the area. (3) Shade and light are separated by hue first. (4) White is tinted: snow is lavender, pink or yellow-white, and pixels above L90 are at most 3.2%. (5) Black does not enter except for the early pictures. (6) The accent returns in minute amounts in every zone (Sunset, Snow red in the snow; Thaw yellow left and right).

## Form, line & typography
Form is silhouette plus stroke direction; nothing is contoured. Edges are rings of touches whose colour belongs to neither side (End of Day halo, End of Summer orange rim), or ragged notches (Parliament). Straight lines occur only where something is straight: the bridge arc, station roof bars, trunks. Letters appear once: the barge's sign in La Grenouillère, half hidden dabs that read as words only by context. Signature and date always sit in a lower corner in a colour taken from the canvas (red-brown, blue, grey). He keeps strokes in the same size range within a zone, so scale is legible; a figure can be three strokes and a head one.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- **Time is the axis of the series.** The same place at successive conditions; the eye compares canvases and sees what moved. Grouping may be by tonality instead of by hour (Getty: "blue" and "morning" groups overlap).
- **Method of switching.** When the light went, the canvas was put down for one that matched the new light (Getty: over a dozen a day at Rouen; NGS: the Poplars boat had slots to hold several).
- **Rhythm inside a canvas** is near-regular with irregular gaps: trunk intervals, pad bands every ≈8–10% widening toward the viewer, dashes of the reflection spreading downward.
- **Slow finishing of a fast effect.** First sitting sets the key over the whole; later passes tighten; the studio compares (letters 1890, 1893).
- **Flicker from equal lightness**: hues of equal L (Impression sun, Sunset sky) seem to vibrate because position information is weak. The eye drifts; nothing anchors it but silhouette.

## UI translations
1. **One frame, many conditions.** Decide which condition varies (hour, period, load, environment) and freeze the frame, the bands and the subject's outline for all of its states. Landmarks stay within 2% of the frame.
2. **Key the whole view.** Each state has one mean lightness and one hue family that tints every surface, including the lightest: 80% or more of the coloured area inside a 90° span of hue, low mean chroma.
3. **Constant interval, moving key.** Hold the main panel 14–26 L from its surroundings in every state and theme. The interval is the token; the absolute values are not.
4. **Narrow band for surfaces.** Keep surfaces inside 16–37 L and away from both ends of the range. Text ink is exempt and must reach 4.5:1.
5. **Shade by hue.** Selected, pressed, past and inactive surfaces turn 110–180° from the key and step only 4–12 L, at equal or higher chroma. Nothing is greyed or darkened in its own hue.
6. **An event with no value step.** Mark what the state is about with a hue opposed to the key at the lightness of its surroundings, 0.1–2% of the view, with no outline, and always with its reading beside it in ink.
7. **Light the facing edge.** A panel's side toward the condition takes a rim 1.3–2% of the width at the panel's own lightness and about three times its chroma.
8. **Return the accent to every zone.** Repeat the event's hue as small labelled marks in each other zone; gather its opponent in one ribbon.
9. **Direction instead of borders.** Give each zone one direction for its rows, ticks or bars, two or three directions per view, and remove dividers. Unit size is constant inside a zone and up to three times larger in the zone nearest the user.
10. **Dock or crop.** The main mass is 14–17% of the view, off-centre, with its top on a band line; or it is cut by two edges of the frame. It is never complete, centred and surrounded by margin.
11. **Whole-view passes.** Load the whole view at its key value first and refine every zone together. Secondary items get three to six units each.
12. **Show the set.** Keep 3–20 states as small copies of the same frame, ordered by the condition or grouped by key, and switch between them instead of animating one into another piecemeal.
13. **Sign in a corner.** State name and time sit small in a lower corner, in a hue taken from the view.

## What this artist would reject
- One canonical light for a thing: a neutral, "correct" illumination and a single state.
- Outlines and flat local colour filling them.
- Black or grey shadows; a shadow that is just the object's colour with less lightness.
- An accent louder than the air around it: the event shares the key of the scene.
- A complete, centred object with clear margins.
- Detail finished in one corner while the rest is blank.
- Ornament or narrative as the subject.

## Signature prompt seed
Fix one frame, three bands and one plain off-centre subject, and keep them identical in every state. Name the condition that varies and give each of its states one key: a mean lightness and one hue family that tints every surface, inside a narrow value band with no neutral dark and no pure white. Hold the subject at the same value interval from its surroundings in every key. Show shade and inactive states by turning to the opposite hue with a small value step. Mark the event in the opposing hue at the lightness of its surroundings, very small, with its reading beside it in crisp ink, and return a trace of that hue to every zone. Separate zones by the direction of their rows, not by borders. Show the states side by side on the same frame.
