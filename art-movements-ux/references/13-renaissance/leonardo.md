# Leonardo da Vinci — Renaissance

## Essence
Leonardo measures an object, builds it on a constructed scaffold (a square and circle, a vanishing point, a grid of axes), then shows it from several sides and in section on one sheet, with his mirror-written notes packed into the gaps around it. The drawing carries the argument and the text annotates it: neither is a caption to the other. The page is a working surface, not a composed layout. Views, notes, scale bars and letter keys accumulate around one dominant figure, and the construction lines are left visible.

## Key works examined
- **Vitruvian Man, c.1490 (pen and brown ink with wash over metalpoint, 34.4 × 24.5 cm, Gallerie dell'Accademia, Venice).** A figure in two poses inscribed in a square and a circle, with text above, a palm-ruled scale bar and text below. It teaches proportion as a stated module (palm, foot, cubit) drawn and written on the same sheet. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons photo by Luc Viatour, square/circle/centre positions, text-band shares, scale bar, palette)*
- **The Last Supper, c.1495–98 (tempera and oil on a gesso, pitch and mastic ground on plaster, 460 × 880 cm, Santa Maria delle Grazie, Milan).** One-point perspective whose orthogonals meet at Christ's head, with the apostles in four groups of three. It teaches a single construction point that is also the subject. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons high-res post-restoration image, vanishing point by overlaid orthogonals, tapestry interval ratios, window widths, group bounds, sampled colours)*
- **Perspective study for the Adoration of the Magi, c.1481 (metalpoint, pen and ink, wash, white heightening on prepared paper, ≈16.4 × 29 cm, Uffizi GDSU 436 E).** A ruled floor grid and architecture laid down first; horses and figures sketched over it, transparent. It teaches the scaffold drawn before the content and left visible under it. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons scan, vanishing-point position by eye, transversal spacing, palette)*
- **The skull sectioned, 1489 (pen and ink over traces of black chalk, 18.8 × 13.4 cm, Royal Collection RCIN 919057 recto).** Two views of one skull stacked in a column: cut open at three-quarter view, and sagittally sectioned inside a lettered grid of axes. It teaches coordinates as a way to locate a point inside an object. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons RCT scan converted from Adobe RGB, column/blank/text shares, palette)*
- **The fetus in the womb, c.1511 (red and traces of black chalk, pen and ink, wash, 30.4 × 22.0 cm, RCIN 919102 recto).** A uterus opened like a hinged shell around a fetus, with smaller repeats, enlarged details and boxed notes. It teaches the cutaway as the main figure and the detail as its satellite. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons RCT scan, sRGB-converted, figure share, satellite scale, boxed text, palette)*
- **The muscles of the shoulder, c.1510–11 (pen and ink with wash over black chalk, 29.2 × 19.8 cm, RCIN 919003, side titled "verso" on Commons).** Six views of the same shoulder turned around the body, two with the same old man's head. It teaches rotation of one object across a sheet. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons RCT scan, sRGB-converted, count of views, palette)*
- **The heart, bronchi and bronchial vessels, c.1511–13 (pen and ink on blue paper, 28.8 × 20.3 cm, RCIN 919071 recto).** An ox heart and bronchial tree with text columns that wrap the drawing's silhouette. It teaches text as a fill that takes the shape left by the image. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons RCT scan, column widths, text/drawing/blank shares, palette)*
- **Codex Leicester, sheet 2A, c.1506–10 (pen and ink on paper, page ≈29 × 22 cm per Wikipedia text, private collection).** Two pages on the moon, earthshine and light: text blocks, with diagrams in a right-hand margin column and one inline figure. It teaches the margin column as the image lane. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons "Vinci – Hammer 2A" scan, margin-column width, figure shares, line pitch; scan colour is boosted)*
- **Codex Arundel, ff.174v–175r, c.1480–1518 (pen and ink on paper, leaves mostly ≈22 × 16 cm, British Library Arundel MS 263).** A spread of optics text, a series of three equal circle diagrams, a column of eight drum-mechanism views, and a ringed diagram of the elements. It teaches the sequence of small views of one mechanism. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons BL scan, diagram sizes and counts, stain share, palette)*
- **Codex Atlanticus f.149 recto (formerly 53v), giant crossbow, c.1485–90 (pen and ink with wash, Biblioteca Ambrosiana, Milan).** One machine on a steep diagonal, with two state views of its trigger and a tiny human figure for scale. It teaches before/after views and a scale reference. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons detail scan, diagonal angle, span share, palette; sheet dimensions not found)*
- **Codex Atlanticus f.1069 recto, water-raising machines (pen and ink, Biblioteca Ambrosiana).** About ten machine vignettes on a spread, each self-contained with a 1–3-line caption, plus a pasted-on fragment from the album's assembly. It teaches a tray of independent object studies. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons scan, vignette count, text share, palette)*
- **Codex Atlanticus f.1 recto and Codex Leicester other sheets.** *(not obtained: not attempted after Wikimedia rate-limited original-file downloads; the Leicester, Arundel and Atlanticus sheets above stand for the codices)*

## Detailed observations

**Vitruvian Man.** The sheet is 1.40 tall to 1 wide. The square is a near-perfect square (1168 × 1158 px in a 1652-px-wide view) and takes 0.73 of the sheet width. The circle's diameter is 1.22× the square's side. Its bottom is tangent to the square's base, so the circle rises above the square by 0.22 of the side and overhangs each side by about 0.10. The two figures do not share a centre. The circle's centre sits exactly on the navel, and the square's centre falls at the root of the genitals, 0.108 of the side lower (I checked this with overlaid lines). The page stacks into three bands: top text ≈12% of the height (8 full lines plus 2 short ones, mirror-written, ragged on the left), figure zone ≈64%, bottom text ≈18% (1 centred line plus 11 lines). The top of the circle cuts into the lowest lines of the upper text, so the two do not keep apart. Directly under the square runs a ruled scale bar exactly as wide as the square. It has denser ticks at each end, where the finger subdivisions sit, and evenly spaced palm ticks between them; the text calls the man 24 palms. Contours are firm single pen lines. Shading is short horizontal hatching beside the legs and a few patches of hatching on the torso; there is no background tone. The warm cast of this photo (paper #F1CF97, L 85, C 33) is mostly the photograph and the aged paper. The ink measures #925B1E (L 44, C 46) and covers about 2% of the area. The eye starts at the face, which lies on the square's top band, then drops down the vertical axis to the two centres.

**The Last Supper.** Lines through the tops of the side tapestries cross at Christ's right temple, at about 0.49 of the painted width and just under half its height. Britannica places the vantage point about 4.6 m above the floor, and a nail hole is reported at that spot. The four tapestries on the left wall shrink from 207 to 107 to 74 to 52 px wide, each about 0.5–0.7 of the one before. The back wall is a third of the room's width and holds three openings. The central one (140 px) is about twice the width of the side ones (65–68 px), and its bright sky (#A8A7AA, L 69) sits directly behind Christ's head. That window is the only large light area at head height, so it acts as a halo without any drawn ring. Almost every head lies in one band along the horizon. The table runs 0.86 of the width. The twelve apostles fall into four tight clusters of three, two on each side, and a visible gap isolates Christ in the centre. Christ's body forms a broad triangle, with the head at the apex and the hands at the base corners, its sides at about 57° to the horizontal. The colour is dark and muted: tapestries #382518 (L 17), back wall #433932 (L 25), tablecloth #8D8786 (L 57, C 2), light right wall #A1958C (L 62). The two strongest notes are both on Christ: a warm red robe #976B58 (C 23) beside a grey-blue mantle #727377 (C 2 in this reproduction), the only red/blue pair centred on the axis. Across the whole field, nine clusters fall between L 16 and L 69, and none goes above C 19. The ceiling is coffered in a regular grid of about 6–7 bays (counted by eye) that recedes to the same point.

**Adoration perspective study (Uffizi 436 E).** The floor is a ruled grid. Its orthogonals fan out from a point right of centre, by eye at ≈0.58 of the width and just under half the height. Its transversals are ruled lines whose spacing closes from ≈25–30 px near the bottom edge to a few pixels at the horizon, about forty of them. A two-step platform runs across the foreground, and a stair of about twenty identical treads climbs diagonally from centre-left to the upper left. Arcades on the left repeat round arches of one module in perspective. All of this is fixed with a straightedge. Over it, freely and with no regard for the lines, he sketched rearing horses, figures and trees in pen and brown wash. The grid shows through them, so construction and content sit on one plane, with the construction never erased. Paper #E4CFB0 (L 84, C 18), ink #54412C (L 29); wash and line together cover ≈10% of the sheet below L −25 from the paper.

**The skull sectioned.** Both skulls share one column of about 48% of the sheet width, with its left edge at 0.44 of the width. The upper-left quarter of the sheet (≈22% of its area) is completely blank. The upper view is three-quarter, with the cranial vault sawn horizontally and the inside shown; a second cut runs vertically through the cap. The lower view is a sagittal section in strict profile, boxed by a rectangle of ruled verticals and horizontals with letter labels at their ends. The Royal Collection notes say the lines am and cb cross at the senso comune, at mid-height of the skull. The text is two short blocks (6 and 5 lines, ≈9% of the sheet) at the left, set level with the lower view, with their line ends pulled right toward the drawing. The skulls sit in a halo of straight parallel hatching at about 45°, running upper left to lower right (a left-hander's stroke). It is densest against the bone and fades out within ≈1/10 of the sheet width, so the object is lifted off the page by tone rather than outline. The palette is restrained: paper #E8DFCF (L 89, C 9), ink #967F64 (L 55) covering ≈5%, darkest strokes #897359 (L 50).

**The fetus in the womb.** One cutaway dominates. The uterus is shown cut open, with its front half swung back on the left like the lid of a box. It fills about 28% of the sheet, low and left of centre. Its inner wall is drawn as concentric shells with a serpentine band. The same banded wall is drawn again at the top right, enlarged and isolated as a curved strip. A smaller whole fetus-in-membranes (≈0.18 of the main figure's width) sits on the right as a second, schematic view. Below it the parts are pulled apart: placental pieces, a ring, and three opened membrane shells arranged like an exploded diagram. About a dozen text blocks of different widths fill the gaps. Two are boxed with hand-drawn frames, so a note becomes an object on the page. There is also a geometric diagram (a circle with lettered radii) at mid-right, and a small mechanical sketch at the lower right. Only the fetus's body has red chalk under the ink, #9A764B (L 52, C 30) against warm-grey ink lines #8A7247 (L 49, C 27) on near-neutral paper #DFDBD4 (L 88, C 4). That is the only warm-red area on the sheet. Its hatching follows the form, curving round the skull in concentric arcs, unlike the straight hatching of 1489.

**The muscles of the shoulder.** Six views of one shoulder cover the sheet, turned through front, side, top and back, at different depths of dissection. Twice the same aged, bald head shows the view's orientation (in profile at top centre, bowed at three-quarter lower right), so the head works as a compass for the rotation. One view at mid-left reduces the muscles to straight cords between bone attachments. The Royal Collection describes Leonardo's "thread diagrams" for this campaign and a note admitting that the density of information hurts legibility. Nine or so text blocks, each 4–10 lines, fill the gaps between views. They are never boxed, and their margins bend to the drawings. Paper #D6CFB3 (L 83, C 15), ink #6C613E (L 41) covering ≈12%, darkest #42381D (L 24).

**The heart, bronchi and bronchial vessels.** The drawing (heart plus branching bronchial tree) takes ≈25% of the sheet, running down the centre-left from top to about 70% of the height. Text takes ≈55% and blank ≈20%. The text does the wrapping. The left column's right edge steps inward as the bronchial tree widens, and between the tree and the right-hand block a column only 6–10 characters wide threads down the gap. Below the drawing a full-width block closes the page. On the right, two small diagrams (bronchial rings, a chain of cartilage) sit at mid-height in their own short text block. A single capital "A" keys the drawing to the notes. The blue paper has faded to a cool grey, #C1C5C1 (L 79, C 3); the ink is #5F543D (L 36, C 15) and covers ≈15%, the heaviest coverage of any sheet here.

**Codex Leicester, sheet 2A.** The text sits as justified-looking blocks with ragged ends, about 25 px line pitch in a 1307-px-tall scan. Paragraphs are separated by gaps of 1.5–2 line pitches and sometimes by a ruled stroke. On the left page a separate margin column on the right (≈17% of the page width) holds eight small diagrams stacked vertically: triangles, a circle in a triangle, a cratered moon, concentric rings, half-lit spheres. Between them run very narrow text columns (2–5 words wide). On the right page the main column runs almost full width, and diagrams sit in its right margin (two pairs of tangent-cylinder diagrams). One wide inline figure shows sun, moon and earth with the light rays ruled straight; it spans ≈0.69 of the text width and sits between paragraphs. A shaded moon at the bottom right is the only heavily toned area, and a narrow text column wraps its right side. Drawings take ≈10–14% of the right page and ≈6% of the left; text takes about 60%; the rest is blank. Paragraphs open with a large capital "A" or a drop mark. This scan's colour is boosted: paper #FEFCD7, ink #AD6F24 (C 53). These values belong to the scan and are not reliable as colour.

**Codex Arundel, ff.174v–175r.** The left page holds a construction diagram at the top left: crossing ruled lines, an hourglass of rays with lettered points. Below it runs a narrow text column ≈19 characters wide, and a main column sits to the right. Paragraphs are closed by short ruled lines and some are struck through. At the foot, three circle diagrams of equal diameter (≈75 px) sit in a row with even gaps: a circle with an inscribed triangle, with a vertical line of dots, and with eight radii. They read as one sequence of states. On the right page eight small views of one drum mechanism are stacked in a single column, from its side, its end, cut open and with the gear exposed, each 110–170 px tall and lettered a, b. Their 2–3-line captions are set to the left. At the top right sit four concentric rings labelled for earth, water, air and fire. Long tube instruments run horizontally across the lower half. The paper is a cool near-white, #E1DED7 (L 89, C 4). The ink is pale, #908068 (L 54, C 15), and covers only ≈3%. A water stain at the lower right (#C9AC83 and #AA8A65) covers ≈10%, more than the ink.

**Codex Atlanticus f.149r (giant crossbow).** The stock runs from the top centre to the lower right at ≈62° to the horizontal. The bow spans 93% of the sheet width. Two side views of the trigger block stand at the left, identical in width and stacked: one locked, one released by the hammer blow, each with a two-line caption underneath. They form a before/after pair. A soldier about 1/6 of the stock's length stands on the machine, the only scale reference. One text block (8 lines) sits at the right, and a gear detail with a short caption at the lower right. Text ≈10% of the sheet. Every surface is modelled with parallel hatching and a light wash for the ground shadow. Paper #F2DBB3 (L 88, C 23, warm scan), ink #866537 (L 45) covering ≈10%, darkest #4F2F0F (L 23).

**Codex Atlanticus f.1069r (water-raising machines).** About ten machines sit as separate vignettes across a two-page spread with a central fold: an Archimedean screw in a frame, a tub with two large toothed wheels, a rope-driven water wheel pulling from a tower, a pump with weights. Each is drawn as one oblique perspective view, some cut away to show the inside, with a 1–3-line caption above or beside it. Text is ≈8% of the area. Pairs of toothed wheels and cylindrical tubs repeat across the sheet as recurring units. A man's head in profile is doodled at the left, and a pasted paper fragment at the lower left (a paler rectangle) records Pompeo Leoni's later assembly of the album. Paper #CBB989 (L 76, C 27), ink #705D3B (L 41), with ink about 3% of the measured area.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Construct first, then draw over the construction, and never erase it** (Vitruvian Man, Adoration study, Last Supper, skull). The square and circle, the palm scale, the floor grid of ≈40 transversals, the nail-point vanishing point and the lettered axes are drawn before or under the content and stay visible. In the Adoration study the figures are transparent over the grid.
- **Two systems with offset centres** (Vitruvian Man). Circle centre at the navel, square centre at the genitals, 0.108 of the side apart. The circle is 1.22× the square, shares its base and rises above it. The figures do not share one centre.
- **One construction point is the subject** (Last Supper). All orthogonals converge on Christ's right temple, which is backed by the only bright opening (L 69, twice the width of its neighbours) and isolated by a gap between the four groups of three.
- **Diminishing intervals by a steady ratio** (Last Supper tapestries 207 : 107 : 74 : 52, each ≈0.5–0.7 of the last; Adoration transversals closing from ≈30 px to a few px). Depth is shown by intervals that shrink steadily, not by tone.
- **One dominant figure plus satellites at a fixed lower scale** (fetus 28% of the sheet, satellite ≈0.18 of its width; heart ≈25%; crossbow 93% span with two side details; skull, two equal views). A sheet has one main view and several smaller ones that explain it: a section, an enlarged band, a schematic repeat, a before/after pair.
- **Several views of one object on one sheet** (shoulder: six views and a head as orientation key; Arundel: eight drum views in one column; skull: oblique and sagittal; crossbow: locked and released). The object is turned, cut, opened or pulled apart, and each view is drawn at its own consistent scale.
- **Cut, open and pull apart** (fetus: uterus hinged open, membranes exploded into three shells; skull: vault sawn off, sagittal half; Atlanticus: machines cut away). Inside is shown by removing the part in front, not by transparency.
- **Text fills what the drawing leaves** (heart: a 6–10-character column threading a gap, left column stepping in; Leicester: 2–5-word columns between margin diagrams; fetus: a dozen blocks of different widths). Text width is set by the space left, not by a grid. Text share runs from ≈8–10% on machine and skull sheets to ≈55–60% on heart and Leicester pages.
- **The image lane is a margin column** (Leicester: right-hand column ≈17% of the page width holding eight stacked diagrams; Arundel: a column of eight drum views). Small diagrams stack vertically beside the running text, and an occasional wide figure (≈0.7 of the text width) breaks into the text between paragraphs.
- **Letters key image to text** (skull: am, cb, rm, hf on axes; Arundel drums a, b; heart "A"; Leicester capital "A" paragraph marks). Connection is by shared letters, not by leader lines.
- **Boundaries by ruled stroke or drawn box, never a panel** (Leicester and Arundel close paragraphs with a short rule; the fetus sheet boxes two notes with hand frames). Nothing is set on a filled ground.
- **Tone lifts the object off the page** (skull: 45° hatching halo fading within ≈1/10 of the width; crossbow ground wash; Leicester shaded moon the only dense tone). Background is otherwise the bare sheet.
- **One material, one ink, one accent** (fetus: red chalk under the fetus body only, C 30 against paper C 4; Last Supper: the single red/blue pair on Christ, C 23 vs C 2, in a field of nine clusters all under C 19). Colour is almost absent; where it appears, it marks the subject.
- **Blank is left where nothing is needed** (skull: 22% of the sheet in the upper left; Vitruvian Man: the figure zone has no background tone). Blank is not distributed evenly. It collects where the drawing faces away.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Leonardo da Vinci.** *Rule the measuring scaffold first and leave it showing; give one object a dominant view, with its cut, turned and changed-state views around it at a fixed lower scale; let the notes take the shape of the gaps and tie them to the parts by shared letters; put the one colour on the subject only.*
Process:
1. Rule the scaffold before any content, from the object's own measure: a unit named in the object's terms, a bar of that unit exactly as wide as the main frame (coarse ticks evenly spaced along it, fine subdivisions only in its two end units), and either a frame of axes with a letter at each end or one point that every receding line meets. Draw it straight, in the line value, and never remove it. Content is drawn freely over it and does not snap to it.
2. If two measures frame the same object, draw both frames on one shared base and let their centres fall where the measures put them. Do not force them to coincide: mark both centres. (In the one measured case the larger frame is 1.22× the smaller, so the centres sit 0.11 of the smaller side apart.)
3. Put the subject on the scaffold's point: every converging line meets on it. Give it the one large area of the extreme value at its own level, about twice the width of the areas beside it, with no ring or outline. Leave a visible gap around it and gather its peers in clusters of three, the same number on each side.
4. Show recession (in space, time or rank) by intervals that shrink at one steady ratio: each step 0.5–0.7 of the one before, four steps or more, all aimed at the point from step 3. Value stays out of it.
5. Give the object ONE dominant view of 25–30% of the format, off-centre (low and to one side). A single long object may instead cross the format on a diagonal near 60°, spanning 90% or more of the width.
6. Add 2–8 further views of the same object, each 0.15–0.25 of the dominant view's width, each made by one operation: cut it, open it on a hinge, pull it apart, turn it, change its state, enlarge one band of it, or reduce it to straight cords between its fixed points. The inside is shown by removing what is in front, never by transparency. A run of one operation is a row or column of equal size and even gaps (3 in a row, up to 8 in a column of constant width); two states are a pair of equal width, stacked, each with a two-line caption. Repeat one small orienting mark on every turned view, and place one referent of known size (about 1/6 of the object's length) on the dominant view.
7. Pour the notes into what the views leave. A block takes the width of its gap, down to 6–10 characters or 2–5 words, and its edge steps in and out with the outline beside it; no column grid sets it. Notes take 8–10% of an object-led view and 55–60% of a text-led one. A text-led view moves the small views into one side lane of about 17% of the width, stacked, and lets one wide figure (about 0.7 of the text width) break in between paragraphs.
8. Tie part to note by a shared letter: the same letter on the part and at the head of its note, with no leader line. Close a paragraph with a gap of 1.5–2 lines or a short ruled stroke. A note that must stand as an object gets a drawn frame, two at most per view. Nothing sits on a filled panel.
9. Work in one ground and one line value, 35–55 L apart, with marks covering 3–15% of the area. Spend the one chromatic colour on the subject alone, at about 1% of the area; where a second chromatic note is needed it sits on the same subject, opposite in temperature.
10. Let the blank collect: about 20% of the format stays empty in one quadrant, on the side the dominant view faces away from. Do not even out the margins.
Why it works:
- Scale reference: a scaffold and a unit bar left on the view let the reader check every size against a stated measure. Remove them and the sizes are assertions; the reader has to trust the drawing.
- Good continuation: lines that all aim at one point send the eye there with no help from size or colour. Move the subject off the point and the lines aim at nothing, so a second focus appears.
- Depth from a gradient of intervals: steps shrinking by one ratio read as recession on a flat surface. Equal steps read as a flat list, and a tone ramp does the same job only by adding a third value.
- Grouping by likeness with rank by size: views at one lower scale read as accounts of the dominant view. Make them its equals and the reader sees several objects and no explanation; drop them and one view has to stand for an object it cannot show whole.
- Proximity and figure against ground: a note shaped by the gap beside a part sits nearest that part, and its stepped edge repeats the outline. Set the same notes in a regular column and they move away, so leader lines have to bring them back across the figure.
- Identity instead of connection: a shared letter joins part and note without a mark between them. A leader is one more line among the contours, and ten of them hide the object.
- Salience by oddness: one chromatic area in a two-value field is found before anything is read. Spread the colour and it stops meaning "this is the subject".
- Visual weight: blank gathered on one side gives the view a direction and a place to grow. Equal margins make a framed picture that looks finished.
Colour relations: the ground is the largest role (a third or more as one flat area) and low in chroma; one line value does all the drawing and writing, 35–55 L from the ground, at 3–15% coverage; chroma stays in the lower third of the scale for both. One chromatic colour, about 1% of the area, the highest chroma on the view, on the subject only; a second, opposite in temperature, only on that same subject. The single largest area of the extreme value stands behind the subject. A stain, a warning or a status the case brings takes the subject colour or stays in the line value. Not carried: the hatched or washed tone that rounds objects in the works; depth here comes from step 4 and from the cut views.
Space and line: one dominant view at 25–30%; further views at 0.15–0.25 of its width; runs of 3–8 equal views; side lane about 17%; wide figure about 0.7 of the text width; note width down to 6–10 characters; intervals stepping by 0.5–0.7; blank about 20% in one quadrant; unit bar the width of the main frame; two frames on one base with centres apart. Two kinds of line on every view: ruled for the scaffold, free for the content, both in the one line value.
Type requirement: a face that stays legible in a measure of 6–10 characters at a small size (compact widths, clear a/b/c/m and numerals), set ragged at both ends, one size and one weight for all notes, line pitch about 2% of the view's height; single key letters that read at that size on a drawing; one larger initial to open a paragraph. Rank comes from position beside a view, not from a scale of sizes.
Motion and interaction: the view assembles in the order it was made (scaffold, dominant view, further views, notes), as cuts. The scaffold can be switched off and on. Choosing a letter marks the part and its note together. Choosing a smaller view exchanges it with the dominant one in place, the scales swapping. A turned run steps through its views with the orienting mark turning too; a state pair toggles; a pulled-apart view goes from whole to parts and back. A receding series scrolls with its intervals closing by the same ratio. A superseded note is struck through and stays; a new one takes the next free gap. Reduced motion: every change is a direct swap.
Fails when: there is no single object (a feed, unrelated peers), so nothing is dominant; the object has one meaningful view; the content is continuous prose with no figure; the format is so narrow that the gaps fall under 6 characters; the layout must re-flow freely, so no note keeps its fitted shape; note length is unknown in advance; more than about a dozen note blocks or 15% mark coverage, where density starts to cost legibility.
Signature (do not carry together): brown ink and parallel hatching on aged paper · small handwriting packed around anatomical or machine drawings · a body inscribed in a circle and a square, or a room in perspective closing on a central figure.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Review of one slow request in an API service, desktop 1440 × 900, mid teal ground with a near-white line value and one yellow signal, a geometric sans, terse and operational (the works are pale, brown-lined and hand-written).
1. *Scaffold.* The unit is 100ms, the request's own measure, drawn at 50px. A unit bar 600px wide (12 units, the latency budget) runs under the main frame: even ticks every 50px, 10ms subdivisions only in the first and last unit. A time axis and a service axis are ruled across the frame with a letter at each end.
2. *Two frames.* The budget frame is 600px wide, the measured frame 732px (1,464ms, 1.22×). They share the left edge, the request's start. Their midpoints are 66px apart (0.11 of 600) and both are marked.
3. *Subject.* The slow hop sits where the ruled lines meet (step 4). Behind it stands the one near-white area, 135 × 96px, twice the width of the hops beside it, with no outline. A 24px gap separates it from the other six hops, which sit in two clusters of three, one on each side.
4. *Recession.* Above the current trace (96px tall) the four previous deploys repeat it at 58, 35, 21 and 13px (each 0.6 of the last), every one scaled about the slow hop's position, so the ruled lines joining matching hops all pass through that point.
5. *Dominant view.* The trace frame is 732 × 480px (27% of the screen) at x 96–828, y 324–804, low and left.
6. *Further views.* Five, each 146px wide (0.2): the slow hop cut open into its spans; one five-second band of queue depth enlarged; the path reduced to straight cords between services; and the config before and after the deploy as a stacked pair with two-line captions. A healthy cached request, 100px long (about 1/7 of the trace), lies on the main view as the size referent.
7. *Notes.* Seven blocks of 4–10 lines at an 18px pitch (2% of 900) fill the gaps between the views. The narrowest, between two small views, is 10 characters wide. The block left of the cut-open hop steps in twice to follow its outline. Notes take about 12% of the screen.
8. *Letters.* Hops are lettered a–g on the trace and each note opens with its letter; no leader lines. Paragraphs end with a 24px ruled stroke. Two notes are framed: the decision taken and the open question.
9. *Colour.* Ground L 40, line value L 88 (a step of 48), lines and text about 9% of the screen. Yellow fills the slow hop's own bar only, 12,960px² (1%), with its duration written beside it.
10. *Blank.* The upper left, 720 × 324px (18%), stays empty: the trace faces right, toward the deploy that caused it.
- *Motion.* Scaffold, trace, small views and notes appear in that order as cuts. Clicking the cut-open hop exchanges it with the trace. Clicking "c" marks hop c and its note together.
- *Type.* One size and one weight for every note, ragged; rank comes from which view a note sits beside.

Result: a flat, teal, sans-serif incident review that does not read as a notebook page. What survived is a visible scaffold with a stated unit, a subject on the point the lines aim at, one dominant view with smaller cut and state views, notes fitted to the gaps and keyed by letter, and a single colour on the subject.

## Space & composition
- **Three stacked bands on a measured sheet.** Vitruvian Man: text 12%, figure 64%, text 18% of the height on a 1.40 : 1 sheet. Figure width 0.73 of the sheet.
- **Central axis with offset centres.** A vertical axis carries the figure. Two nested reference shapes share a base but not a centre (offset 0.108 of the side).
- **One-point perspective anchored on the subject.** Vanishing point at ≈0.49 width and just under half the height in the Last Supper, at Christ's head. Off-centre at ≈0.58 width in the Adoration study.
- **Groups of three around an isolated centre.** Twelve figures in four clusters of three, two per side, with a gap around the centre figure.
- **Dominant figure off-centre, satellites around it.** The main view takes 25–30% of the sheet, low left (fetus) or centre-left (heart). Satellites run 0.15–0.25 of its size, toward the right and the top.
- **Column of views.** Two to eight views stacked vertically in one column of constant width (skull 48% of the sheet width; Arundel drums).
- **Margin image lane.** Diagrams in a right-hand column of ≈17% of the page width, with text in the remaining ≈83%.
- **Diagonal for the machine.** The single large machine set at ≈60° across the sheet, spanning over 90% of the width.
- **Uneven void.** Blank space collects in one quadrant (≈22% in the skull sheet) rather than as even margins.

## Form, line & typography
- **Line.** Firm single pen contours for outlines (Vitruvian Man), ruled straight lines for construction (rays, grids, axes, scale bar), and free lines for living content drawn over the ruled ones. The ruled and the free coexist on every constructed sheet.
- **Hatching.** Straight parallel hatching at ≈45°, upper left to lower right, in the 1489 skull and the machine drawings. By c.1510 it becomes hatching that curves round the form (fetus head, shoulder muscles). Bambach describes the early manner as "exact, engraving-like parallel hatching."
- **Script.** Mirror-written, right to left, in a small, even, slightly forward-leaning hand. The line pitch is about 2% of the page height. Lines are ragged at both ends, and paragraphs are closed by a short rule or a gap of 1.5–2 lines.
- **Text block shapes.** Rectangular when there is room. Narrowed to 2–10 words when it threads between drawings. Occasionally boxed by a hand-drawn frame. Notes are linked to the image by letters (a, b, c, m) placed at the ends of lines and on parts.
- **Initials.** A large capital "A" or a drop mark at the head of a paragraph or keyed to a drawing (Leicester, heart).
- **Figure language.** Section, cutaway, hinged opening, exploded shells, two states side by side, the same object rotated through four to six views, and a tiny human figure for scale.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- **Sequence as states.** Three equal circles in a row (Arundel), locked and released trigger (crossbow), and six turns of a shoulder each show a step of change on one static sheet.
- **Rotation.** The shoulder sheet reads as the body turning, with the repeated head showing the direction.
- **Disassembly.** The fetus sheet moves from whole (opened shell) to parts (exploded membranes, placental pieces), and the skull from oblique cut to strict section.
- **Diminishing intervals.** Recession at a steady 0.5–0.7 step (tapestries) or a closing grid (Adoration). Depth is built as rhythm.
- **Gesture radiating from a still centre.** In the Last Supper the groups of three move and turn while the central figure holds still at the construction point.
- **Accumulation over time.** Notes and drawings are added in different inks and densities. Some paragraphs are struck through, a later stain covers ≈10% of one spread, and a fragment is pasted into an album page. The sheet records layers of work.

## UI translations
1. **Scaffold on show.** Draw the measuring grid, axes or guide lines in the line value under the content and leave them visible; offer a switch, not an erased final state.
2. **A unit bar under the figure.** Put a scale bar exactly as wide as the main figure directly beneath it, named in the content's own unit, with fine subdivisions only at its two ends.
3. **Two frames, two centres.** When two measures frame one object (budget and actual, plan and result), draw both on a shared base and mark both centres apart.
4. **Subject on the point.** Aim every guide line at the subject and back it with the only large area of the extreme value, twice the width of its neighbours; no ring, no outline.
5. **Threes around a gap.** Gather peer items in clusters of three, equal in number on each side of an isolated centre.
6. **Recede by ratio.** Show older, deeper or lower-ranked items at intervals of 0.5–0.7 of the previous one, with no change of tone.
7. **One main view, small satellites.** One view takes 25–30% of the screen, off-centre; 2–8 more views of the same object sit around it at 0.15–0.25 of its width.
8. **A view per operation.** Each satellite cuts, opens, pulls apart, turns, enlarges or simplifies the object, or shows it in another state; insides are shown by removal, never by transparency.
9. **State pairs and runs.** Two states are an equal-width stacked pair with two-line captions; a sequence is a row or column of equal views with even gaps.
10. **An orienting mark and a referent.** Repeat one small mark on every turned view to show its direction, and put one thing of known size on the main view.
11. **Notes fill the gaps.** Size each note block to the space beside the part it explains, down to 6–10 characters, and step its edge along the figure's outline.
12. **A side lane for small figures.** In a text-led view, stack small figures in one lane of about 17% of the width and let one wide figure (about 0.7 of the text width) sit between paragraphs.
13. **Letters, not leaders.** Put the same letter on a part and at the head of its note; draw no callout lines.
14. **Rules and frames, no panels.** End a paragraph with a short ruled stroke or a 1.5–2 line gap; frame at most two notes; fill nothing.
15. **One colour on the subject.** One ground and one line value 35–55 L apart; one chromatic colour at about 1% of the area, on the subject only, with a label.
16. **Uneven blank.** Leave about 20% of the screen empty in one quadrant, away from where the main view faces.
17. **Strike, don't delete.** Superseded notes stay, struck through; new ones take the next free gap.

## What this artist would reject
- A picture with a caption under it, where the text only labels and the drawing only decorates.
- A single view as the whole account of an object, with no section, no second side and no state change.
- Hidden construction: grids, axes and measuring units erased once the content is placed.
- Centres made to coincide for neatness when the measured object puts them apart.
- Colour spread across everything. Colour appears only where it marks the subject.
- Even, symmetric margins all round. Blank collects where the drawing faces away.
- Leader lines and callout boxes cluttering the figure, where a shared letter would do.

## Signature prompt seed
Rule the measuring scaffold first (a unit bar as wide as the main figure, lettered axes or one point that all guide lines meet) and leave it visible under freely placed content. Put the subject on that point, backed by the only large area of the extreme value. Give the object one dominant view at 25–30% of the screen, off-centre, and surround it with 2–8 smaller views at about a fifth of its width, each one cut, opened, turned, pulled apart or in another state. Show recession by intervals shrinking 0.5–0.7 per step, with no change of tone. Fit the notes to the gaps beside the parts they explain and key them with shared letters, with no callout lines and no filled panels. Use one ground, one line value and one colour, on the subject only. Let about a fifth of the screen stay blank on one side.
