# Piero della Francesca — Renaissance

## Essence
Piero built pictures the way he drew in his treatise: he fixed an axis, a horizon and a measuring unit first, then placed still figures and pale buildings on that frame so precisely that the whole picture reads as one constructed solid. His light is even, high-key and cool, and his colour sits mostly in a near-neutral band; a few saturated areas are spaced across that band at deliberate intervals. In his drawings, every point is numbered and labelled, and the words, figures and tables share the page as parts of one demonstration.

## Key works examined
- **Baptism of Christ, c. 1437–45 (egg tempera on poplar, 167 × 116 cm, National Gallery London).** A round-topped panel whose centre line runs through the dove, the bowl, the hands and the feet. It teaches one dominant vertical axis inside a simple geometric outline. *(visually checked: Web Gallery of Art image (NG photo); measured arch springing, dove position, trunk position, palette)*
- **Flagellation of Christ, c. 1455–60 (oil and tempera on panel, ≈58.4 × 81.5 cm, Galleria Nazionale delle Marche, Urbino).** Two scenes on one floor, divided by a column. It teaches a measured split of the field, scale contrast and a single shared horizon. *(visually checked: WGA image; measured column position, figure heights, palette, chroma distribution)*
- **Montefeltro (Brera) Altarpiece, 1472–74 (tempera and oil on panel, 251 × 172 cm, Pinacoteca di Brera, Milan).** A ring of figures under a coffered apse, with an egg hanging on the centre line. It teaches isocephaly (all heads at one height), symmetry and a near-neutral ground. *(visually checked: WGA image; measured egg position, head band, neutral share, palette)*
- **Diptych of Federico da Montefeltro and Battista Sforza, front, c. 1465–75 (oil on panel, 47 × 33 cm each, Uffizi, Florence).** Two profiles face each other across a continuous low landscape. It teaches paired panels with one shared horizon and one saturated block. *(visually checked: WGA images, unframed and in the Uffizi frame; measured horizon, sky gradient, face and red samples, palette)*
- **Diptych reverse: Triumphs with inscriptions (same panels).** Each side has a picture field above a painted parapet with four lines of Roman capitals. It teaches how text and image divide one panel. *(visually checked: WGA image; measured field heights, text line pitch, palette)*
- **Legend of the True Cross: Finding and Recognition of the Cross, c. 1452–66 (fresco, 356 × 747 cm, San Francesco, Arezzo).** Two episodes in one wide band, split by a temple façade. It teaches a wide frieze format with a head-height band and a geometric façade used as an index. *(visually checked: WGA image; palette, split position, head band)*
- **Legend of the True Cross: Dream of Constantine (fresco, 329 × 190 cm, Arezzo).** A conical tent with its pole on the centre line, lit from the angel at the upper left. It teaches one axis and a single directional light at night. *(visually checked: WGA image; axis position, palette)*
- **Legend of the True Cross: Exaltation of the Cross (lunette, fresco, 390 × 747 cm, Arezzo).** Two groups leave an empty middle where only a thin tree stands. It teaches the interval as the subject. *(visually checked: WGA image; gap width, palette)*
- **Cappella Maggiore, San Francesco, Arezzo, overall view.** Side walls stacked in three registers: lunette, then two rectangles. It teaches a tiered system of fields. *(visually checked: WGA photograph; register structure, by eye)*
- **Resurrection, c. 1460–65 (fresco and tempera, 225 × 200 cm, Museo Civico, Sansepolcro).** A frontal figure on the axis, framed by painted columns, above four sleeping soldiers. It teaches a triangle on an axis and a horizontal ledge as a dividing line. *(visually checked: WGA image; axis, ledge position, palette)*
- **Nativity, c. 1470–85 (oil on poplar, 124 × 123 cm, National Gallery London).** A near-square panel with a frieze of five singing angels in a row. It teaches repetition with small variation, and a pale ground. *(visually checked: WGA image; palette, chroma distribution)*
- **De prospectiva pingendi, c. 1474–82 (autograph MS Parm. 1576, Biblioteca Palatina, Parma; pages reproduced in the open-access critical edition by C. Gizzi, Ca' Foscari 2016).** Projected heads, a coffered dome, prisms; each drawing is built from ruled lines, lettered stations and number tables. It teaches the annotated construction drawing. *(visually checked: Gizzi edition plates 38 (c. 20v), 45 (c. 30r), 55 (c. 43r), 65 (c. 59v), 69 (c. 66v), 79 (c. 82r); measured ink coverage, text/diagram shares, station intervals, paper and ink colours)*
- **De prospectiva pingendi, 1899 Winterberg edition (Internet Archive).** *(not used for visual evidence: its diagrams are the editor's redrawings, and the foldout plates are not digitised)*
- **Madonna del Parto, Senigallia Madonna, Polyptych of the Misericordia, Ideal City (Urbino).** *(downloaded but not analysed beyond a palette run; the Ideal City is not securely Piero's, so it was dropped)*

## Detailed observations

**Baptism of Christ.** The panel is a rectangle under a semicircle. In the 940 × 1300 px image the arch springs at y ≈ 450–470 px, so the arch's radius is half the width (≈470 px) and the rectangle below is ≈0.88 of the width high, slightly less than a square. The dove, wings spread horizontally (≈260 px wide, ≈0.28 W), sits at x ≈ 0.50 W, y ≈ 0.35 H, which is on the springing line at the centre of the arch's circle. Directly below it on the same vertical are the bowl of water, Christ's joined hands (x ≈ 465 px), his navel and his feet. A second vertical, the pale walnut trunk, stands at x ≈ 0.30 W and runs from the foliage to the bottom edge, splitting off the three angels as a separate group on the left. The trunk (#C1B3A5) and Christ's torso (#BDA78E) are almost the same light value (L ≈ 70–74), so the trunk reads as a second, abstract body. Colour below the arch: warm pale ground and flesh (#CCBAAE, 21%, L 77, C 9; #A58E78, 20%, L 60, C 16), dark olive foliage (#352A1C, 16%, L 18; #68553D, 15%), cool pale sky (#B5B8CE, 13%, L 76, C 11). The only saturated areas are John's orange-brown robe (#974E2A, 9%, L 42, C 43) and a deep blue angel robe (#282E4F, 5%). For the whole image, 63% of pixels have chroma under 15 and under 4% exceed 40. The light is flat daylight from the left, with almost no cast shadow. The river stops at Christ's feet, and in the middle distance a second figure undressing repeats Christ's pale vertical, smaller. There is no drama of gesture: everyone is still.

**Flagellation.** The field (≈1.39 : 1) is split by the shaft of a fluted column. The shaft spans x ≈ 0.55–0.61 W, so its right edge falls at ≈0.61 W, close to the golden section, and its centre at ≈0.58 W. Left of it is a deep coffered portico; right of it is an open piazza. The two zones are scaled very differently. Christ, bound to a column at x ≈ 0.36 W, is ≈0.34 H tall; the three foreground men on the right are ≈0.75 H tall, about 2.2 times larger. Yet published reconstructions (Longo, cited by Gentili et al.) find that the knees of all the figures, near and far, lie on the horizon, at about 60 cm eye height. One low horizon, in this image ≈0.72 H down, binds both scenes. The floor is a measured grid: a band of brick-red paving with white marble strips in the foreground and a black-and-white patterned pavement under Christ, which reconstructions read as a square tile with a circle. Light on the two scenes comes from opposite sides (from the right in the portico, from the left outside). Colour: a pale field of marble and warm stone (#EEE8D8, 23%, L 91, C 9; #C6AC83, 28%, L 72, C 25), red-brown in pavement and cloaks (#924E33, 21%, L 40, C 39), dark accents (#37291C, 12%, L 19), and a thin band of pale blue sky (#B2C9EE, 4%, L 80, C 19) confined to the top-right corner. This is Piero's most chromatic panel in the sample (11% of pixels C > 40), but its median lightness is high (L 65). An inscription "OPVS PETRI DEBVRGO S[AN]C[T]I SEPVLCRI" runs along the base step of the throne as a single line of small capitals, a quiet signature placed on the architecture. The eye is led first to the large figures on the right, then along the floor lines back to the small lit body on the left.

**Montefeltro (Brera) Altarpiece.** A tall field (1.46 : 1). Architecture fills the top ≈49%: a barrel-vaulted bay with coffers in five tiers of rosettes, ending in a scallop-shell half-dome. From the shell an egg hangs on a thread. Its centre is at x ≈ 0.50 W, y ≈ 0.34 H, exactly above the Virgin's head on the vertical axis. The heads of the standing saints, angels and Virgin all fall within one band at y ≈ 0.49–0.54 H (≈5% of the height): strict isocephaly. They form a shallow semicircle, echoing the apse. Only the kneeling donor drops below it (head at ≈0.66 H), at the lower right in armour. The Child lies across the Virgin's lap, the one horizontal body. Colour is the most restrained of all the works: 66% of pixels are near-neutral (RGB spread < 8%), and 78% have chroma under 15. Clusters: mid grey stone #7A7471 (22%, L 49, C 3), pale grey #D2CCC6 (19%, L 83, C 4), warm grey #B5ADA4 (19%, L 70, C 6), dark grey-brown #564842 (15%), a brown/flesh #946246 (13%, C 30), near-black blue #25262A (12%) for the Virgin's mantle. Saturation appears only in small separated patches: the red cloak at far right, the red rug, a blue robe at left. The light is even and comes from the upper left, casting soft shadows inside the shell.

**Diptych, front.** Two panels (each 47 × 33 cm, ≈1.42 : 1) with strict profiles facing each other. Sky and landscape run continuously behind both, with the horizon at ≈0.59 H in each. The eyes are aligned on one line (y ≈ 0.34–0.35 H). The profile silhouette is drawn as one clean contour against the sky; the busts are cut by the bottom edge, so the figures fill the lower ≈60% and the sky the upper part. The sky darkens upward: sampled in a strip, it goes from L ≈ 80–86 near the horizon (#C7CAAC to #D2DAC4) to L ≈ 43–46 at the top (#5F667B), a ≈40 L gradient. Colour roles are split between the two panels: Battista is pale (face #CBBA82, L 76; WGA photo is yellowed), dressed in black with gold brocade; Federico is dark-skinned (face #8B6132, L 45) in a block of saturated vermilion (#781910, L 26, C 51; 13% of the pair). That red is the only strong colour in the pair. In the Uffizi frame the gilt frame takes more area than the paintings.

**Diptych, reverse (Triumphs).** Each panel is divided horizontally into a picture field (top ≈64% of the panel height) and a painted marble parapet carrying the inscription (≈29%), with a moulded cornice between them (≈5%). The inscription is four lines of evenly spaced Roman capitals, set flush left at a left margin of ≈6% of the panel width, ragged right, each line closed by a mid-height point (·). Measured in the 850 px-high image, the cap height is ≈15 px and the line pitch ≈29 px, so the space between lines is about one cap height. The text block is ≈12% of the panel height and sits in the upper half of the parapet, leaving its lower half as empty stone. The scenes above use a very high horizon (≈0.39 of the picture field), so the land spreads like a map. Each chariot moves inward toward the gap between the two panels, and the two sides balance: white horses on the left, unicorns on the right. Colour is almost entirely low chroma: 75% of pixels C < 15 and 0.7% C > 40. Sky #D1DAC6 (19%, L 86), grey-olive land #6D6751 (21%), pale stone #B3B299 (29%), small dark accents #292214 (17%, including the black gap).

**Finding and Recognition of the True Cross (Arezzo).** A wide frieze (≈2.1 : 1) in which two episodes share one band. The left half shows a hill town and three crosses dug from a field. The right half is a street before a temple façade (x ≈ 0.49–0.76 W), a flat front of geometric marble panels: a central roundel, two smaller roundels and a pediment with a disc. The figures stand in one row; their heads occupy a band at y ≈ 0.50–0.65 H, again nearly isocephalic. The long cross beams make repeated diagonals that cut across this horizontal band. Colour (fresco, partly lost): plaster and pale stone #CABAA8 (27%, L 77, C 11), grey-brown #7B6E5E (20%), ochre #B79978 (19%), red-brown #8B5131 (13%, C 36), grey-blue #9699A3 (9%). Losses in the plaster show as bare light patches; the original sky was azurite blue and is now patchy.

**Dream of Constantine (Arezzo).** A tall field (1.73 : 1). The tent is a cone with its pole exactly on the centre line (x ≈ 0.50 W), its apex just below the top edge. The scene is divided into three horizontal bands: the cone (top ≈35%), the hanging curtains (middle), the bed and figures (bottom ≈40%). The only light source is the angel diving in at the upper left; the inside of the tent is a dark triangle (#392120, 23% of the image, L 16). Ochre curtains (#CC9D62, 7%, C 39) and a pale bed are the lit shapes. Two guards stand at the left and right edges as vertical brackets.

**Exaltation of the Cross (Arezzo lunette).** Two groups of figures stand at the left and right feet of the pointed arch. Between them is a gap of ≈10% of the width with nothing but bare ground and a thin tree whose trunk is almost on the centre line. The sky takes over half of the lunette area. Colour: pale sky and plaster #D2C3B9 (26%, L 80), grey #A3978B (27%), red-brown robes #753D23 (11%, C 33). The decorated border is by an earlier painter, not Piero.

**Cappella Maggiore, overall.** Each side wall carries three stacked fields: a lunette at the top and two rectangles below, separated by painted cornices. The window wall carries narrower upright fields on either side of the window. Together the walls form a grid of fields read in rows, not one continuous picture.

**Resurrection (Sansepolcro).** The painted frame (two fluted columns, an entablature, a base with a partly lost inscription) encloses a nearly square scene. Christ is frontal, on the vertical axis (x ≈ 0.50 W), his head forming the apex of a triangle whose base is the four soldiers. The front edge of the sarcophagus is one horizontal ledge at ≈0.59 H that separates the sleeping zone from the waking zone. Trees at the left are bare and those at the right are in leaf. Colour: dark land #423A32 (24%), grey-olive #69685B (23%), flesh and pink #D1A578 (18%, C 32); greens and violets in the soldiers' clothing below the ledge, only pink and flesh above it.

**Nativity.** A near-square panel (124 × 123 cm). Five angels stand in a row with similar heights and spacing, two of them playing lutes whose round bodies repeat; small differences in head angle and mouth vary the rhythm. A broken shed gives a flat, slanted roof plane. The ground is a large empty area of pale earth (#DBB285, 29%, L 75, C 29) with sparse plants, and a pale cool sky (#D7D7E0, 18%, L 86, C 3). The Virgin's blue-black mantle (#1A1314, 13%) is the darkest mass. The surface is worn (losses from old cleaning), which flattens some areas further.

**De prospectiva pingendi (Parma MS, autograph).** The plates are pen drawings in brown ink on pale buff paper. The ink is a dark warm brown (#6D5133 to #654E32, L ≈ 35–38) and covers only 0.4–1.4% of a drawing page; construction lines are ruled in a lighter rust-brown just above the paper tone, and text from the verso shows through as a faint grey field. Paper: #F0E0C2 to #F5E4C6 (L ≈ 88–91, C ≈ 15–18), aged and foxed at the edges (#D3B791 tints). Page types observed:
- *c. 59v (heads, III.8a).* A profile head and a frontal head sit side by side at the top (≈0.03–0.35 H). Below them, two horizontal sections of the head are drawn as closed concentric rings, each ring punctuated by small numbered points, with radiating lines from a centre point. The drawing occupies the right 63% of the page (x 0.29–0.92); the left 29% is empty margin. Long ruled verticals drop from the heads through both sections, so the views are tied to each other by shared lines.
- *c. 66v (frontal head, III.8e).* One frontal head on a central axis labelled m. On each side are seven ruled verticals labelled A, B, C, D, E, F, G(H), spaced at an equal interval of ≈5% of the page width. Beside each vertical runs a tall column of tiny numbers. Below, a table of rows labelled A to GH lists the measurements. Picture, labels and table occupy one page and one grid. Ink coverage is 0.4%: most of the page is empty paper.
- *c. 82r (coffered dome, III.9e).* A hemispherical dome drawn as about five tiers of square coffers that shrink toward the top, set on a baseline lettered A–P symmetrically outward from the centre. Below it, 16 rows of numbers form a table that fills about half the page. Ink coverage 1.4%.
- *c. 43r (prism, III.5b).* The upper ≈56% of the page is a justified block of handwritten text, with lettered points (A, B, C, … DV, PQ, KL) referred to inline. The lower ≈36% is the diagram: a plan on the left and its perspective on the right, connected by long ruled lines to a distant eye point off to the upper right. Text and drawing share one column and one margin.
- *c. 20v and c. 30r (plans and floors, II.5, II.11).* Square plans subdivided into grids, with a vanishing apex above; long converging lines across nearly the whole page width.
Absent on all pages: shading, colour wash, decoration, freehand sketching. Every mark is either a ruled line, a contour that passes through numbered points, a letter or a number.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Axis first** (Baptism, Brera, Dream of Constantine, Resurrection, MS c. 66v). The main vertical is fixed at the exact centre (x ≈ 0.50 W) and several key elements are stacked on it: dove, bowl, hands, feet in the Baptism; egg above the Virgin's head in the Brera altarpiece; tent pole in the Dream; Christ in the Resurrection; line m in the frontal head drawing.
- **One second vertical, off centre** (Baptism, Flagellation, Exaltation). A secondary vertical divides the field: the walnut trunk at ≈0.30 W, the column with its right edge at ≈0.61 W, the thin tree in the gap of the Exaltation. It separates groups rather than holding a figure.
- **A single horizon binds unequal scales** (Flagellation, diptych). In the Flagellation, figures 0.34 H and 0.75 H tall share one horizon at knee height (≈60 cm). In the diptych, two separate panels share one horizon at ≈0.59 H, and the eye lines are aligned.
- **Heads in one band** (Brera, Finding/Recognition, Nativity). Standing heads fall within a band ≈5% of the height (Brera 0.49–0.54 H; Arezzo 0.50–0.65 H in a much wider field). Only one figure breaks the band, by kneeling.
- **Simple outline fields, stacked** (Baptism, Arezzo chapel, Triumphs). Fields are rectangles, semicircle-topped rectangles and lunettes; the chapel stacks three per wall. In the Triumphs one panel is split into image ≈64%, cornice ≈5%, text ≈29%.
- **Near-neutral ground, few saturated islands** (all paintings). In 8 of the 10 paintings measured, 46–78% of pixels have chroma under 15 and only 0.7–5.4% exceed C 40 (the Flagellation and the diptych front are the exceptions, at 11–14%). The saturated areas are separated, usually one cloak or block each: Federico's red, John's robe, the right-hand cloak in the Brera altarpiece.
- **High-key, even light** (Flagellation median L 65, Triumphs L 65, Nativity L 67). Pale stone, plaster and sky make up a third to nearly half of each picture (L > 70 in 28–45% of pixels in most works). Shadows are short and soft. The one night scene (Dream of Constantine) uses one source and one dark mass.
- **Interval as content** (Exaltation, Triumphs, diptych). A measured empty gap separates two groups or panels and carries the meaning: the ≈10% gap in the Exaltation, the black gap between the diptych panels that both chariots move toward.
- **Repetition with slight variation** (Nativity, Brera, Arezzo). Rows of similar heads and bodies, similar but not identical in angle and dress; coffers in tiers that diminish regularly.
- **The drawing is a labelled demonstration** (De prospectiva). Stations are lettered, intervals are equal, measurements go into tables, and the text cites the letters. Ink stays under ≈1.5% of the page; the drawing keeps an empty margin of about a third of the page.
- **The generative rule:** fix the axis, the horizon and a unit before anything else; set the outline of the field as a simple geometric figure; stack the main elements on the axis and add one off-centre divider; line up the heads (or the key elements) in one narrow band; fill most of the area with pale, low-chroma surfaces in even light; place a few saturated areas, separated from each other; leave a measured empty interval between groups; and in drawings, letter every station, keep intervals equal and put the numbers in a table on the same page.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Piero della Francesca.** *Fix an exact centre axis, one datum line and one unit before any content; stack the subject on the axis and split the field once, off-centre, with a divider that holds nothing; hang items of unequal scale from the datum and hold their key parts in one narrow band; leave a measured empty interval between groups; letter every station and tabulate it in the same view.*
Process:
1. Fix the frame before any content. The field is one simple figure (a rectangle, a rectangle under a half circle, a near-square); several fields stack as up to three registers divided by plain bands. Then fix three things inside it: a vertical axis at exactly 0.50 of the width, one horizontal datum, and one unit of about 5% of the width. The datum sits 0.59–0.72 of the way down for a view the user stands in front of, and near 0.39 for an overview that should spread out like a map.
2. Stack the subject on the axis: the 3–4 elements that define it, one above the other, each centred exactly, the topmost at about 0.34 of the height. Nothing else in the view is centred.
3. Split the field once with a second vertical, 0.30–0.42 of the width in from one edge. It holds no content; it separates groups. The two zones may differ in scale by about 2.2× and may show two states or two moments of the same thing.
4. Hang every item in both zones from the datum by the same point of the item, whatever its size. Where the view is split into separate panels, the datum continues at one height across them, and so does the line of their key parts.
5. Hold the key part of every peer in one band about 5% of the height (up to 15% in a field wider than 2:1). Peers repeat with small differences of interior, never of height. Exactly one item leaves the band, by dropping 12–15% of the height, and it is the one that differs in kind.
6. Leave a measured empty interval between groups, about 10% of the width, holding at most one thin mark. Whatever moves or faces, moves or faces toward it. Keep about a third of the view as one unbroken empty area.
7. Fill 46–78% of the area with near-neutral surfaces close to the ground in value, evenly lit, with no modelling of any element. Strong colour is under 5% of the view (13% at the limit), as separate blocks that never touch, one per group.
8. Where words and picture share a field, divide it by job: picture about 64%, a plain band about 5%, text zone about 29%. The text takes the upper half of its zone (about 12% of the field), flush left at a margin of 6% of the width, and the rest of the zone stays empty. Where the words explain a drawing, the text goes above (about 56%) and the drawing below (about 36%), in one column with one margin.
9. Letter the stations. Every point the text refers to carries a letter beside it; stations stand at one equal interval (the unit), counted outward from the axis; the measures go into a table in the same view whose rows carry the same letters; the text cites the letters. In such a view the marks cover under 1.5% of the surface.
Why it works:
- Scale reference: items of very different size hung from one line by the same point read as the same kind of thing at different distance or rank. Give each zone its own line and they become two unrelated pictures; equalise their sizes and the difference in rank is lost.
- Visual weight: an exact centre stack is the only symmetric thing in the view, so it holds still while everything else is read against it. Shift it by a few percent and it reads as an error; add a second centred column and the two compete.
- Figure and ground: a divider that holds nothing, set off-centre, makes two unequal zones without a hierarchy of boxes, and a measured gap between groups reads as a thing in its own right, which the two sides are then related across. Centre the divider and it competes with the axis; close the gap and the groups merge into one crowd.
- Grouping by likeness and salience by oddness: key parts on one band fuse into a row, so the one that drops is found without colour or size. Let every item find its own height and the row is a scatter in which nothing is odd.
- Good continuation: a letter that appears beside the point, at the head of its table row and in the sentence is one object seen three times, so the eye moves between picture, number and text without a leader line or a legend.
Colour relations: ground and near-ground surfaces take most of the area at low chroma (46–78% under chroma 15); a third to nearly half of the view sits at the ground's end of the value scale; the middle and far values are also low in chroma; strong colour (chroma about 36–52) is the rarest thing, at least 30 L from the ground, in separated blocks, one per group, usually under 5% in total; one hue may appear at both ends of the value scale but not in the middle. In a construction view, one mark value at 0.4–1.4% of the surface and a second, barely above the ground, for construction lines.
Space and line: axis at 0.50 W; top of the stack at 0.34 H; divider 0.30–0.42 W from an edge; datum 0.59–0.72 H (0.39 for overview); key-part band 5% of H; one exception 12–15% lower; interval 10% of W; zone scales up to 2.2 : 1; stations at 5% of W; fields 0.88 : 1 to 2.1 : 1; contours closed and continuous; construction lines ruled and allowed to run past the object to the edge.
Type requirement: two textures only. Display lines in letters of one height, evenly spaced, flush left, with the gap between lines equal to the letter height, and each line closed by one small mark. Running text as one even justified column that shares its margin with the drawing. Station letters and figures are the same small size, sit beside the line they name, and use tabular figures; a signature or credit is one small line set on an existing edge of the structure, never floating.
Motion and interaction: nothing in the view travels; a change of state is a cut or a fade of 120–200ms in place, and the band, the datum and the axis never move. Two states are shown side by side across the divider, not as an animation between them. Pointing at a table row marks its station and its letter in the text together, and the reverse. On resize, stations re-count at the unit and the axis stays at 0.50. Reduced motion: cuts only.
Fails when: there is no single subject to stack, so the axis stands empty; an axis stack over a row of equal tiles is the median centred hero (the off-centre divider and the unequal zones are what prevent it); items have no common point to hang from (free text of uneven length, a feed); the width cannot hold a divider, an interval and a third of empty area (narrow phone screens with dense content); the data needs many category colours; the view must look busy or fast.
Signature (do not carry together): still, pale figures in cool even daylight · a chequered floor and coffered classical architecture in strict perspective · a strict profile head against a low distant landscape.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Bike-share operations console, desktop 1440 × 900, near-black with one electric magenta, brisk and technical (the works are pale, slow and devotional).
1. *Frame.* One rectangle. Axis at x = 720px. Datum at y = 558px (0.62 H). Unit 48px (3.3% of the width, under the 5% of the works because ten stations must fit).
2. *Axis stack.* Four elements centred on x = 720 and on nothing else: the city-wide count of free bikes at y = 306px (0.34 H), the target for the hour below it, the datum tick, and the time of the last sync at the bottom. No other element is centred.
3. *Divider.* One 2px vertical at x = 432px (0.30 W), full height, carrying nothing. Left of it: the three trucks, drawn large. Right of it: the districts, drawn small. The left zone is what is happening now and the right zone is the forecast for the next two hours.
4. *Datum.* Truck load columns (420px tall) and district columns (190px tall, 2.2× smaller) all stand on y = 558 by their zero line.
5. *Band.* The three truck names sit in one band 45px high (5% H) at y = 96–141. The truck that is out of service drops 120px (13% H): it is the one that differs in kind, and it also carries the words "out of service".
6. *Interval.* Trucks end at x = 336 and districts begin at x = 480: a 144px gap (10% W) containing only the divider. From x = 1008 to the right edge (30% of the width) the view is empty.
7. *Colour.* Ground L 12; columns, rules and text are greys with chroma under 8, about 97% of the view. Magenta appears as two blocks that do not touch: the truck now dispatched (≈1.4%) and the one district forecast to run empty (≈0.5%), each also labelled in words. No shadows and no gradients.
8. *Words and picture.* Above the datum is the picture (62%); a plain band 45px high (5%) follows; the table zone takes the rest. The table uses its upper 110px (12% H), flush left at x = 86px (6% W), with the lower part left empty.
9. *Stations.* Ten district columns, five each side of the axis at 48px, lettered A–E outward on the left and F–K outward on the right, the letter beside each column's foot. The table has rows A–K (stock, forecast, deficit) in tabular figures with an 11px letter height and a 22px line pitch. The status sentence cites them: "E and H run empty before 18:00; truck 2 is on E."
- *Motion.* A new forecast replaces column heights with a 150ms fade in place. Pointing at row H marks column H and the letter H in the sentence.

Result: a dark, sharp console that does not read as a fresco or a treatise page. What survived is an exact axis stack, one empty off-centre divider, one datum under two scales, one band with one exception, a measured gap, and letters shared by picture, table and text.

## Space & composition
- **Centre axis, stacked.** Key elements at x ≈ 0.50 W, stacked vertically (Baptism: dove at 0.35 H, hands, feet; Brera: egg at 0.34 H above the Virgin's head).
- **Off-centre divider.** One vertical at ≈0.30 W (Baptism trunk) or ≈0.58–0.61 W (Flagellation column) splits groups.
- **Low or shared horizon.** Flagellation horizon ≈0.72 H at knee height; diptych horizon ≈0.59 H in both panels; Triumphs horizon high, ≈0.39 of the picture field, for a map view.
- **Isocephaly.** Heads in a band ≈5% of the height (Brera).
- **Field outlines.** Semicircle on near-square (Baptism, rectangle ≈0.88 W high); wide friezes ≈2.1 : 1 (Arezzo); near-square (Nativity, Resurrection); tall 1.46–1.73 : 1 (Brera, Dream).
- **Text and image division.** Triumphs: picture 64%, cornice 5%, text 29%, text block 12% of the panel. Treatise c. 43r: text 56% above, diagram 36% below.
- **Empty margins.** Drawing pages keep about a third of the page empty; the Nativity foreground is a broad empty floor.

## Form, line & typography
Contours are clean, continuous and closed: profiles against sky, the cone of the tent, the oval of a head, all defined by an edge without hatching. Volumes are simple solids (cylinder columns, cone tent, egg, hemisphere dome, the head as a stack of sections). In the treatise, curves are built by joining numbered points; straight construction lines are ruled and run far beyond the object. Lettering appears in two forms: monumental Roman capitals with even spacing and an interpunct ending each line (Triumph inscriptions; the signature line in the Flagellation), and the treatise's small humanist hand, set as a justified column, with capital letters as labels for points (A, B, … PQ, KL) and numbers set in columns and tables. Labels sit next to the line they name and are as small as the numbers.

## Rhythm, motion & time
Figures stand still. Actions are frozen at their quietest moment (water about to be poured, a lash raised but not falling). Rhythm comes from rows of near-identical uprights (angels, saints, columns, coffers) at nearly regular spacing, interrupted once by a gap or a kneeling figure. Time is split spatially: the Flagellation places two moments on one floor, lit from opposite sides; the Finding and Recognition puts two episodes in one frieze; the Resurrection separates sleep below the ledge from waking above it; bare trees on the left and leafy trees on the right. The diptych's chariots move slowly inward toward the central gap.

## UI translations
1. **Axis, datum and unit before content.** Set a centre line at exactly half the width, one horizontal datum 0.59–0.72 of the way down, and a unit of about 5% of the width; size and place everything else from these three.
2. **Stack the subject on the centre.** Put the three or four elements that define the screen one above the other on the centre line, the top one at about a third of the height; centre nothing else.
3. **One empty divider, off-centre.** Split the screen once at 0.30–0.42 of the width with a rule or a plain column that carries no content, and let the two zones differ in scale by up to about 2.2×.
4. **One baseline under two scales.** Stand large and small items on the same datum by the same point, so a detail panel and an overview read as one scene; carry the datum across separate panels at one height.
5. **Heads in a band.** Align the titles or key values of peer items in one band about 5% of the height; drop exactly one, the item that differs in kind, by 12–15%.
6. **The gap as a component.** Keep an empty interval of about 10% of the width between two groups, with at most one hairline in it, and orient both groups toward it.
7. **Quiet field, separated accents.** Keep 46–78% of the area near-neutral and close to the ground in value, with no shadows or modelling; give each group at most one saturated block, never touching another, under 5% in total.
8. **Picture over inscription.** In a card or panel that pairs an image with words, give the image about 64%, a plain band 5% and the text zone 29%; set the words flush left at a 6% margin in the upper half of their zone, with the line gap equal to the letter height.
9. **Lettered stations with a table in view.** Mark each referenced point with a letter at one equal interval from the centre line, list the measures in a table on the same screen under the same letters, and cite the letters in the text; no leader lines, no separate legend.
10. **Two moments, one field.** Show before and after, or now and next, as two zones on one datum either side of the divider, each still, in place of an animated transition.
11. **Stillness.** State changes are cuts or 120–200ms fades in place; the axis, the datum and the band never move.

## What this artist would reject
- Gesture, motion blur and emotional exaggeration; every figure is calm and still.
- Centres left unmarked or approximate; his axes are exact.
- Many saturated colours side by side; strong colour is rare and separated.
- Dark, contrasty dramatic light (except one night scene, with a single source).
- Ornament and hatching in the drawings; only ruled lines, points, letters and numbers.
- Text set apart from the image it explains; in the treatise the letters in the text name the points in the drawing.
- Crowded fields; there is always a broad empty floor, sky or margin.

## Signature prompt seed
Before placing any content, fix a centre line at exactly half the width, one horizontal datum about three fifths of the way down and a unit of about 5% of the width. Stack the few elements that define the subject on the centre line and centre nothing else. Split the field once, off-centre, with a divider that carries nothing, and stand items of unequal scale on the same datum either side of it. Hold the key parts of peer items in one narrow band and let exactly one drop out of it. Leave a measured empty gap between groups and about a third of the view empty. Keep most of the area near-neutral, flat and evenly lit, with a few separated blocks of strong colour, one per group. Letter every referenced point at equal intervals, tabulate the measures in the same view under the same letters, and let the text cite them. Nothing moves; states change in place.
