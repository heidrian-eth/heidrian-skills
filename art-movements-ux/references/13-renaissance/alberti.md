# Leon Battista Alberti — Renaissance

## Essence
Alberti built by measure first and by look second. A whole is fixed as a simple figure (a square, a window, a man three braccia tall), and every part is a whole-number fraction of it, stated so exactly that it can be rebuilt from words and numbers alone. That is why his treatises carry no pictures. On his buildings the measure stays visible as a skin of inlaid modules, stripes and incised joints laid over the structure, and one large opening (a round window, a triumphal arch) is set on the central axis.

## Key works examined
- **Façade of Santa Maria Novella, Florence, upper storey and frame c. 1456–70 (white Carrara marble and green serpentine/"verde di Prato" over an older Gothic lower register; inscription dated 1470; patron Giovanni Rucellai).** Teaches the square as master module, halved and quartered, and how a new order is laid over an inherited one. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons "Florence, Santa Maria Novella, facade.jpg", frontal 1920×1280; measured square overlay, bay intervals, stripe widths, attic module count, colour)*
- **Tempio Malatestiano, Rimini, exterior shell c. 1450–60, unfinished (marble/limestone casing around a Gothic church; patron Sigismondo Malatesta).** Teaches the triumphal arch as a façade, and a deep three-arch rhythm with an unfinished top left showing. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons "Rimini Tempio Malatestiano Esterno Facciata 1.jpg" and "Tempio malatestiano (cropped).jpg", both oblique; measured colour, bay rhythm by eye)*
- **Sant'Andrea, Mantua, façade designed 1470, begun 1472 (brick with stucco and stone dressings).** Teaches one giant opening framed by a giant order, with the small order inside it, the whole fitting in a square. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons "Sant Andrea straight.jpg", perspective-corrected frontal 540×717, plus the line engraving "Façade of Sant' Andrea, Mantua (Character of Renaissance Architecture).png" and an oblique photo; measured square overlay, bay intervals, colour)*
- **Palazzo Rucellai, Florence, façade c. 1446–51 or after 1461 (pietra forte veneer, executed probably with Bernardo Rossellino).** Teaches stacked orders on a flat wall, a grid of pilasters and entablatures drawn on incised ashlar, and an unfinished edge. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons "Palazzo Rucellai 2018.jpg", oblique from street level; counted bays and window parts, colour; storey heights not measurable because of the upward camera tilt)*
- **Tempietto of the Holy Sepulchre, Rucellai Chapel, San Pancrazio, Florence, c. 1467 (white and black/green marble, inlaid roundels).** Teaches a small object wrapped in a panel grid, one roundel per panel, with an inscription band of roman capitals. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons "Alberti Santo Sepulcro 01.JPG"; panel ratio, band widths, colour)*
- **San Sebastiano, Mantua, begun 1460 (stucco on brick; façade incomplete, the outer staircases are 20th-century additions).** Teaches a broken pediment and an almost bare wall, with the order reduced to thin flat strips. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons "San Sebastiano, Mantua, Front Facade.jpg", frontal; by eye only)*
- **De re aedificatoria, written c. 1443–52, editio princeps Florence: Nicolaus Laurentii, 1485 (incunable, ten books, no illustrations).** Teaches architecture transmitted only as text and number; the page is a text block with a blank space left for a hand-painted initial. *(visually checked: Internet Archive scan of the Getty Research Institute copy, id leonisbaptistaea00albe, pages n12, n13, n200; measured text-block ratio, area share, margins, line counts, paper colour)*
- **De re aedificatoria, illuminated manuscript, c. 1485–90 (Olomouc, Státní Archiv, Cod. Lat. C. O. 330).** A later illuminator's frame around Alberti's plain text, useful as the contrast case. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons "Leon Battista Alberti, De re aedificatoria.jpg"; measured text, frame and initial area shares and colour)*
- **De pictura / Della pittura, 1435 (Latin) and 1436 (Tuscan, dedicated to Brunelleschi), three books, no illustrations.** Teaches the picture as an "open window", the man of three braccia, the centric point, and the veil of threads squared into a grid. *(not obtained as an image: Alberti's manuscripts carry no figures; the 1540 Basel editio princeps was not found on e-rara. Construction read from the text, as reported by Ross Anderson, Drawing Matter)*
- **De statua, c. 1440s, in the 1804 Milan edition "Della pittura e della statua" (engraved plate p. 149).** A later engraver's picture of Alberti's measuring frame with graduated rulers and letter labels; not Alberti's own image. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons "Alberti - Della pittura e della statua, Milano, 1804 (page 149 crop).jpg"; layout by eye)*
- **Descriptio urbis Romae, c. 1450 (text plus 16 tables of polar coordinates for about 175 points; no map).** Teaches an image sent as a list of numbers to be redrawn by the reader with a "horizon" disc and a "radius" ruler. *(not obtained as an image: there is no drawing to obtain; read through Mario Carpo's essay)*
- **Self-Portrait plaquette, c. 1435 (bronze, oval, National Gallery of Art, Washington, Kress Collection; about 20 × 13.5 cm, from memory, as the NGA page refused the fetch).** Teaches profile, a single emblem (the winged eye) and a short capital inscription on one plain field. *(visually checked: NGA open-access image via Wikimedia Commons "Leon Battista Alberti, Self-Portrait, c. 1435, NGA 43845.jpg"; measured oval ratio, element positions, colour)*

## Detailed observations

**Santa Maria Novella.** Frontal photo, façade spans x≈340–1560 px (1222 px) and stands ≈1190 px from pavement to pediment apex: height/width ≈0.97–1.0, so the whole sits in a square (the residual is ground level and lens). A horizontal at half that square's height (y≈622) falls exactly on the top cornice of the attic band: the lower half is a 2:1 rectangle (two squares side by side) and the upper storey, between its outer pilasters (x≈668–1245, 577 px, 0.47 of the width; 632 px or 0.52 including the pediment cornice overhang), rises ≈595 px to the apex: a square of half the side, a quarter of the area. Museo Galileo states the same one-to-two scheme. Vertical bays: the lower storey's four green half-columns sit at x≈418, 820, 1078, 1480, giving bays of 402 : 258 : 402 px (side bays ≈1.56 × the central portal bay, near 3:2). The upper storey's pilasters at x≈683, 828, 1083, 1228 give 145 : 255 : 145; the upper central bay lines up with the lower portal bay to within 5 px on both sides, so one vertical channel runs from the door to the apex and holds the portal, the oculus and the pediment sun. The attic band is a row of 15 inlaid squares at a pitch of ≈71.6 px (≈1/17 of the width), each square ≈46 px with a small roundel at its centre. The upper pilasters are striped green and white in bands of 10 ± 1 px each, exactly 1:1, 13 dark bands up a 285 px shaft; the stripe is the smallest unit on the façade, ≈1/120 of the width. The upper side bays are divided into 2 × 3 rectangular panels (≈52 × 92 px), each with one centred star inlay. The round window (glass ≈175 px across, frame ≈225 px, 0.18 of the width) is the only curve in the upper storey apart from the two volutes, whose disc (≈125 px) is ≈0.55 of the oculus frame. The volutes fill the triangles between the narrow upper and wide lower storey; nothing else in the façade is diagonal except the pediment. The inscription runs the whole width of the upper frieze in spaced roman capitals that fill ≈55–60% of the band height, with the date MCCCCLXX at the right end. Colour (lower storey crop, k=9): white marble `#DACEBE` L83 C10 21% plus its weathered tones `#BDB4A4`/`#A49885` (L63–73) 30%; green serpentine reads near-neutral dark `#54534A` L35 C5 and `#33322D` L20 C3 ≈15%; the half-column shafts `#505349`; the pink marble strips around the portal `#AA907C` L62 C16. Upper storey (k=5): white `#D3C6B4` 40%, green-black `#424139` L27 20%; oculus glass `#54656C` L42 C7, the only cool hue. Chroma never exceeds ≈16 anywhere on the façade: the whole thing is a two-value drawing, light L≈80 against dark L≈20–35, with grey-white weathering between. Absent: figures in the upper storey, any diagonal grid, any colour stronger than a faded pink.

**Tempio Malatestiano.** The façade is three arches on a high plinth: a deep central entrance arch (≈1.5 × the width of each blind side arch by eye in the oblique views) flanked by two shallow blind arches, divided by four fluted half-columns standing on separate pedestals. A roundel (an oculus framed in a moulded ring, several filled with dark porphyry or serpentine discs, one red) sits in each spandrel. The upper storey is unfinished: rough pilaster stumps and a raw brick gable show above the cornice. The inscription runs along the frieze in spaced roman capitals. Inside the central arch a lunette holds a geometric inlay of diamonds and circles in coloured stone (`#7A5837` warm brown, near-black purple `#130D14`). Colour of the stone in overcast light (k=5, façade crop): `#979298` L61 C3 49%, `#766C6B` L46 23%, weathering stains `#514239`: a cool, almost achromatic pale grey, with colour only in the inlaid discs. The facade wall is otherwise plain: the arches are blind and smooth ashlar; the jointing is the only texture.

**Sant'Andrea, Mantua.** In the corrected frontal photo, the façade from the top of the plinth (y≈1230 at 1080-px scale) to the pediment apex (y≈205) is ≈1025 px tall and ≈1014 px wide between the outer plinths: a square again (ratio ≈1.01; the source image is perspective-corrected, so treat as approximate). Four giant pilasters (capitals at x≈135, 310, 785, 990) make bays of 175 : 475 : 205 px, so the central bay is ≈2.5 × a side bay and ≈0.47 of the width. The central arch opening (≈405 px, 0.40 of the width) rises to ≈0.72 of the façade height. The giant pilasters run ≈0.6 of the height; a smaller order of fluted columns (≈440 px tall, ≈0.43) stands inside the arch carrying the impost, so two scales of the same order are nested. Each side bay stacks three openings: a door (bottom), a round-headed niche (middle) and a window (top), each smaller than the arch and centred in its bay. Above the pediment a vaulted hood (the "ombrellone") repeats the arch motif a third time at a smaller scale. Colour (sunlit side bay): stucco `#CEC9BD` L81 C7, shadowed `#A59C8A`/`#706C60`, painted frieze traces `#61451D` C26; across the whole façade the vault interior goes as dark as `#24261B` L14, so the single opening is the darkest and largest area on the front (≈17% of the crop). Light: the arch is a deep shadow framed by a pale flat wall.

**Palazzo Rucellai.** Seven bays are visible at the upper floors, each with one two-light window under a round arch of radiating voussoirs, a small oculus in the tympanum and a slim colonnette as mullion: every window is identical. The right edge ends in rough toothing, the bay left unbuilt. Three storeys carry three different pilaster orders (Tuscan, a variant Ionic, Corinthian), each with its own full entablature, so the wall is a grid of verticals and horizontals with windows in the cells. The ashlar is flat and incised: joints are drawn into a smooth plane, and the block lengths vary (no two joints line up between courses), so the grid of the orders and the random coursing sit on two different layers. The ground floor has small square grilled windows high in the wall and a bench base with a diamond (opus reticulatum) pattern. The two doors sit in bays three and six, not on a central axis. The storeys read shorter going up, but the upward-tilted photo exaggerates this, so I did not measure a ratio. Colour (sunlit, k=5): pietra forte `#D4BD92` L78 C25 / `#C5AF87` L72 C23, shadowed stone `#947F65`, window voids `#1C1611` L8 ≈16–24%: one warm ochre material, the voids the only dark.

**Tempietto of the Holy Sepulchre.** A small apsed shrine wrapped in a grid of flat panels: three rows of panels per bay, divided by black bands ≈1/10 of the panel width, each panel nearly square (≈0.9 in the foreshortened view) and holding one centred inlaid roundel ≈0.47 of the panel width, each roundel a different star or rosette. Fluted pilasters mark the corners. A frieze of large spaced roman capitals runs round the top; the letters fill ≈2/3 of the band. Colour (panel crop): white marble `#A39078`–`#A7998D` under warm light L≈61–64, black bands `#161411` L7 ≈26%. Pure two-value contrast; the variety is only in the roundel patterns.

**San Sebastiano.** Frontally: one wide, almost plain stucco wall with a pediment broken in the middle by a tall arched window and a broken entablature; the order is reduced to thin flat strips. Five openings across the lower level, square-headed in the centre three and arched at the ends. Most of the wall is blank, peeling plaster (by eye ≈60% of the façade face). Absent: ornament on the main field.

**De re aedificatoria, 1485.** Each page is one column of roman type, 34–36 lines on a full page (n200), text block width/height ≈0.60–0.62 (close to 3:5), covering ≈44–47% of the scanned page. Margins: inner (gutter) ≈8–10% of the page width, outer ≈26–30%, top ≈8–10%, bottom ≈20%: the block sits high and toward the spine, the classic ratio inner < top < outer < bottom. On n12 the book opens with a centred all-caps heading in two lines ("LEONIS BAPTISTE ALBERTI DE LINEAMENTIS LIBER PRIMVS"), then a hanging indent: the first eight lines are pushed right by ≈30% of the block width, leaving an empty square with a small printed guide letter "d" for an illuminator who never came. The same page lists the ten books by title in one running sentence, the table of contents set as prose. No illustration on any page I examined; the type is a roman with long s and ligatures, spaced evenly, with a few abbreviation marks. Paper: `#EDEBD4` L92 C12 and `#F2EED1` 70% of the page, ink `#AFB099`/`#CACBB4` as averaged with paper; a warm aged off-white.

**De re aedificatoria, Olomouc manuscript.** The same text dressed by an illuminator: the near-square text block (width/height 1.07) takes only ≈22% of the page and ≈37% of the decorated frame, which itself covers ≈61% of the page. The frame is dense scrolling foliage, portrait medallions and putti on a dark blue ground; the initial M with a portrait of a young man holding a compass is ≈24% of the text block. Colour: border `#A9805E` gold L57 C26, `#7D4735` red-brown C30, `#2D262C` dark blue-black, cream `#E9DBBD`; text area reproduced near white `#FEFEFC` (overexposed copy). This is the reverse of the printed book: here the image eats the page, in the printed edition the text owns it.

**De pictura (text).** As retold by Ross Anderson from the Tuscan text: draw a rectangle of right angles and treat it as an open window; fix the height of the largest figure and divide it into three braccia; mark off braccia along the base line; place the centric point directly above the centre of the base at three braccia; join the base divisions to it; find the receding transversals from a distance point off to the side, outside the frame, and check them with a diagonal; the result is a chequerboard of square braccia on which figures and buildings are placed. Elsewhere in Book II, the veil (velo) is a thin cloth of fine threads squared into parallel cells, set between the eye and the object so that each cell's contents can be copied into the matching cell on a squared panel. The units are the human body and the square tile; the grid is both a measuring device and a guide for placement. The work is divided into three parts: outline (circumscription), the joining of planes into bodies and bodies into a scene (composition), and the reception of light.

**De statua plate (1804).** A later engraving of Alberti's measuring idea: a rectangular frame with two graduated vertical rulers and a graduated base, a profile head placed inside, every point lettered (A, B, C, D, F, G, H, I, K). The scales run on the edges, the subject in the field, the letters outside the field next to the edge they name. It is a diagram of measurement, not a portrait.

**Descriptio urbis Romae.** No image: a short text explaining two instruments (a graduated disc and a graduated rotating ruler) and 16 tables listing about 175 points of Rome as angle-and-distance pairs. The reader rebuilds the map. Carpo's essay reports that none of Alberti's treatises on perspective, architecture or sculpture were illustrated, because hand-copied pictures degrade and numbers do not.

**Self-Portrait plaquette.** An oval about 0.68 as wide as tall (1110 × 1635 px in the NGA image), almost filled by a left-facing profile head whose eye sits at ≈(0.20, 0.30) of the oval's bounding box. Behind the neck at ≈(0.14, 0.70) is a small winged eye, his emblem; down the right edge, set vertically, the capitals "L. BAP." with point stops. One material, one plane: `#3B342B` L21 and `#4F463E` L31 bronze (71% of the field), highlights `#ADAAA9` L72 6%, on a black velvet ground. No border, no background scene.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **The whole first, as a square** (Santa Maria Novella, Sant'Andrea). Both façades are as tall as they are wide within ≈1–3%. In Santa Maria Novella the square is halved horizontally exactly at the attic cornice (y 622 of 12–1232), and the upper storey is a square of half the side; in Sant'Andrea the giant order runs ≈0.6 of the square's height and the arch ≈0.72.
- **One axis, one big opening** (Santa Maria Novella, Sant'Andrea, Tempio, San Sebastiano). The central bay is distinct in width (SMN 258 vs 402 lower, 255 vs 145 upper; Sant'Andrea 475 vs 175–205) and carries the single largest void: the portal, oculus (0.18 of the width) and pediment disc stacked in one channel in Florence; an arch 0.40 wide and 0.72 high in Mantua. Upper and lower central bays line up within 5 px.
- **Halving and repeating small modules** (SMN attic 15 squares at 1/17 of width; pilaster stripes 1:1 at ≈1/120; Tempietto panels in rows of three with one roundel each). Each level of the façade has its own module, and the smaller ones nest inside the larger cells; no module is free-sized.
- **Grid laid on the wall as a separate layer** (Palazzo Rucellai, Santa Maria Novella, Tempietto). The orders, panels and inlays are a flat drawn grid; the structure behind is not shown. On Rucellai the pilaster grid and the random ashlar joints are two independent layers.
- **One part repeated identically, the singular part distinguished** (Rucellai's seven identical windows; SMN's 15 identical attic squares vs one oculus; Tempietto roundels same size, each a different pattern). Repetition holds the size; the variation is inside the cell.
- **Two values, low chroma** (SMN light L≈80 vs dark L≈20–35, chroma ≤16; Tempietto white vs black L7; Tempio pale grey C≈3; Rucellai one ochre C≈23–25 with dark voids). Colour stronger than C≈30 appears only in small inlaid discs (Tempio) or in another hand's illumination (Olomouc).
- **Inscription as structure** (SMN frieze across the full upper width, Tempio frieze, Tempietto band, plaquette "L. BAP."). Roman capitals, evenly spaced, filling ≈55–67% of their band height, placed in a horizontal (or, on the plaquette, vertical) band of their own, never over an image.
- **The text carries the design; pictures are left out on purpose** (De re aedificatoria 1485, De pictura, Descriptio urbis Romae). Text blocks ≈0.6 wide/tall at ≈45% of the page; numbers in tables (175 points) replace a map; the only picture spaces are blanks for illuminators. When an illuminator does add images (Olomouc), the text falls to 22% of the page.
- **Unfinished edges left visible** (Rucellai's toothed right edge, Tempio's raw upper storey, San Sebastiano's bare wall). The system is clear enough that a missing bay or storey reads as missing, not as a different design.
- **The generative rule:** fix the whole as a simple figure (square or window); divide it by halves into a lower 2:1 and an upper 1:1; put one large opening on the central axis and stack the other singular elements on that axis; fill the remaining field with one repeated module per level, each a whole fraction of the level above; draw the grid of orders and bands as a flat layer in two values; write the name and date in spaced capitals in a band of their own; state the whole in numbers so it can be rebuilt without a picture.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Leon Battista Alberti.** *Fix the whole as one simple figure and divide it only by halving; stack every singular thing in one channel that lines up through all registers; fill the rest with one repeated module per level, each a whole fraction of the whole; draw that grid as a flat second-value layer over whatever lies beneath, and state it in numbers so the view can be rebuilt without the picture.*
Process:
1. Fix the whole before any content: one simple figure (a square, sides equal within 1–3%, or the frame treated as a window). Take the unit from the content, not from the screen: the height of the tallest item divided by three, marked off along the base. Every later size is a whole count or a whole fraction of this figure.
2. Divide by halving only. Cut the whole at half its height, and let the cut land on the edge of a band, not in a gap. The lower register is then 2:1. The upper register is a figure of the same shape at half the side (a quarter of the area) standing on the cut; the two leftover quarters stay plain field. Sizes exist at 1, 1/2 and 1/4, with nothing between.
3. Give each register three bays, the middle one plainly unlike its neighbours (side to centre between 3:2 and 1:2.5, never 1:1). The middle bays of all registers share their left and right edges within 0.5% of the width. That channel holds every singular element, one per register, stacked.
4. Put the ONE large opening in the channel: 0.18–0.40 of the whole's width and up to 0.72 of its height, the single largest area of the extreme value. If a second frame is needed, nest the same form inside the opening at about 0.7 of the outer one. Nothing in a side bay is as large as the opening.
5. Fill what remains with one repeated module per level, each a whole fraction of the whole (about 1/17 for a band of cells, about 1/120 for the finest 1:1 alternation, which fuses into a mid tone at reading distance). A band that crosses the channel has an odd count (15). The mark in a cell takes 0.47–0.64 of the cell; a divider is about 1/10 of it. Cells never change size: what varies is the interior of the cell.
6. Draw the grid as its own flat layer in the second value, coplanar with the field, with no modelling and no cast shadow. Whatever lies beneath (inherited layout, content of uneven length) keeps its own rhythm and is not snapped to the grid. When an older part must stay, lay the new order over it and align only the channel.
7. Put the name and the date in a band of their own that spans the register: one line of even-height letters, evenly spaced, filling 55–67% of the band's height, never over content. A short label may run along a side edge. Scales and reference letters go on the edges, outside the field, beside the edge they name.
8. State the view in numbers: a text and a table (a pair of coordinates per point) from which the view can be redrawn. The text is set first and owns its page (about 45%); ornament around a text never takes more area than the text. Where a number is more exact than a picture, leave the picture out.
9. Leave what is not built visibly missing: the cell keeps its place and its dividers, the interior shows the bare layer beneath. Never re-flow to close the gap.
Why it works:
- Scale reference: a half is the one fraction the eye checks without measuring, so a whole cut only by halves can be verified at a glance and every part is located against the whole. A cut at 0.45 or 0.6 reads as a choice and has to be explained.
- Good continuation: one channel with shared edges gives the eye a single path through all registers, so the singular things read as one sequence. Shift one register by 2–3% and they become unrelated stacks.
- Grouping by likeness and salience by oddness: equal cells fuse into a texture, so one opening of another size wins without colour. Varying the interior of a cell rewards the near view and leaves the far view unchanged; varying its size breaks the texture and competes with the opening.
- Layers told apart by regularity: a regular drawn grid over an irregular bed reads as two things without any shadow, so the order can be laid on content that was not made for it.
- Completion: in a regular system the eye predicts the absent cell, so a gap reads as "not yet". In a free layout the same gap reads as a different design, or as an error.
- Numbers survive copying: a view stated as counts and coordinates is the same view at every size and in every renderer, and can be read aloud. A picture is exact only at the size it was drawn.
Colour relations: two values do all the work, 45–75 L apart; the field value takes about twice the area of the line value (40% to 20% where both are counted; dividers up to 26% of a panelled zone); chroma on both stays at or below about 16, or one warm field up to about 25 if it is the only material, and then the second value appears only in the openings (16–24%). The main opening is the extreme value. Chroma above about 26 exists only as small marks inside cells or inside the opening, and one hue opposite in temperature to the field may appear at about 2%, in the opening. A signal the case brings takes one of those two places.
Space and line: whole 1:1; registers 2:1 below and 1:1 at half the side above; three bays, side to centre 3:2 to 1:2.5; channel edges within 0.5%; opening 0.18–0.40 of the width, to 0.72 of the height; modules about 1/17 and 1/120; cell mark 0.47–0.64; divider 1/10; lines straight and orthogonal; the only curves are whole circles and half circles; blank field is allowed up to about 60% of a face.
Type requirement: for bands, letters of one height (capitals or lining numerals) with even spacing at 55–67% of the band; for text, one even column in a block about 3:5, set high and toward the inner edge (inner margin 8–10%, top 8–10%, bottom about 20%, outer 26–30%), headings centred above it in at most two lines, tabular numerals for the tables, and one reserved blank square (about 30% of the block's width, eight lines deep) where something will be added later.
Motion and interaction: the view assembles in the order of its construction (whole, halving, channel, opening, modules, bands), each stage a cut 80–120ms after the last; points from the table plot one by one in table order. On resize the whole keeps its figure and the modules re-count in whole numbers, never stretch. The grid layer can be switched off to show the bed beneath. Selecting a cell swaps its two values; the cell does not grow or lift. A missing cell fills in place. Table row and plotted point highlight together. Reduced motion: the finished state at once.
Fails when: there is no single principal item, so the channel stands empty; the three bays come out equal (a centred item over three equal tiles is the median hero and feature row); the content is a feed with no fixed whole; sizes are live, so no module keeps a whole fraction; the content is photographic and no number can replace it; meaning needs more than two values and two small accents; the width cannot hold three bays.
Signature (do not carry together): light stone inlaid with dark stripes, panels and roundels · a gabled front with a round window or one great arch on the axis, joined by scrolls · a frieze of spaced roman capitals with the patron's name and the year.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Sleep tracker, night summary, phone 390 × 844, deep indigo ground with one pale value and a coral signal, a rounded sans, soft and private (the works are pale, public, cut in stone).
1. *Whole.* One square 360 × 360 at x 15–375. The unit is the tallest item, the score numeral at 72px, divided by three: 24px. The square is 15 units a side.
2. *Halving.* The cut is at 180px and lands on the top edge of the 24px band of nights. Below it a 360 × 180 register (2:1). Above it a 180 × 180 register at x 105–285; the two 90px quarters beside it stay plain ground. The steps are drawn as divisions inside the one square, so no stepped outline appears.
3. *Channel.* Lower bays 135 : 90 : 135 (3:2:3). Upper bays 45 : 90 : 45. Both middle bays run x 150–240, so the edges agree exactly.
4. *Opening.* In the upper middle bay, one opening 72 × 130 (0.2 of the width, 0.72 of its register), the lightest area on the screen, holding last night's score. Inside it a second frame of the same shape at 50 × 91 (0.7) holds the time asleep.
5. *Modules.* The band on the cut: the last 15 nights as 15 cells at a 24px pitch (1/15), each mark 15px (0.64), each interior that night's own pattern of stages. The lower side bays: under the band, 2 × 3 panels of 45 × 78 with 4.5px dividers (1/10), one centred numeral per panel at 21px (0.47). The left bay is last night, the right bay the 15-night mean, slot for slot.
6. *Layer.* The minute-by-minute movement trace runs behind the lower register at its own irregular rhythm, 6 L off the ground. The grid is drawn flat over it in the pale value and does not snap to it.
7. *Band.* Under the square, one 24px band across the full 360: the date and the total, in numerals and capitals 15px high (0.63), evenly spaced. Hour marks sit outside the square along its lower edge.
8. *Numbers.* Below the band, the 15 nights as a table of bedtime, duration and score in tabular numerals. The square can be redrawn from it, and a screen reader reads it in place of the figure.
9. *Missing.* A new user with 9 nights sees 6 cells with their dividers and no interior. The band keeps 15 places.
- *Colour.* Ground L 16, pale value L 78 (a step of 62), both under chroma 16; the ground takes about twice the area of the pale lines. Coral appears only inside the opening, about 2% of the square, when the score is low, with a word beside it.
- *Motion.* Square, cut, channel, opening, cells and band appear in that order, 100ms apart. Tapping a night swaps the two values in its cell and marks its table row.

Result: a dark, soft-lettered night summary that does not read as a building front. What survived is one figure cut by halves, one aligned channel with the only large opening, a module per level with variation inside fixed cells, a flat grid over an independent bed, and a table that rebuilds the view.

## Space & composition
- **Square whole, half and quarter.** Total ≈1:1; lower register 2:1; upper register 1:1 at half the side (SMN). Sant'Andrea: square whole with a central bay ≈0.47 of the width.
- **Tripartite bays with a wider centre.** Side : centre : side = 402 : 258 : 402 lower and 145 : 255 : 145 upper at SMN; 175 : 475 : 205 at Sant'Andrea; three arches at the Tempio.
- **Vertical alignment between registers.** The central bay of each level lines up with the one below to within ≈0.5% of the width.
- **Nested scales.** Giant order + small order (Sant'Andrea); façade square → attic squares at 1/17 → stripes at 1/120 (SMN).
- **Page.** Text block ≈3:5 at ≈45% of the page, placed high and to the spine, outer margin ≈3× inner, bottom ≈2× top (1485 De re).
- **Void.** Plain fields are common: San Sebastiano's wall, the Tempio's blind arches, the plaquette's field.

## Form, line & typography
Line is straight and orthogonal: pilasters, entablatures, panel bands, joints. Curves are few and whole: full circles (oculi, roundels, discs) and semicircular arches; the only diagonals are pediments and the two SMN volutes. Edges are flat and inlaid, not modelled: stripes and panels are coplanar with the wall. Letterforms are inscriptional roman capitals with even spacing, used in full-width bands (SMN, Tempio, Tempietto) and as a short vertical label on the plaquette; in print, an early roman type with long s, ligatures and abbreviation marks, set in one even column, headings in centred capitals, initials left as blank squares with guide letters. The De statua plate (later) labels every point with a capital letter outside the figure next to the edge it names.

## Rhythm, motion & time
Rhythm is regular and stated: 15 equal attic squares, 1:1 stripes, seven identical windows, three panels per bay. Variation sits inside fixed cells (each roundel or star different, each cell the same size). The eye moves up the central axis (door → oculus or arch → pediment disc) and then sideways along the bands and inscriptions. Time shows as layering and incompleteness: SMN lays a new storey over a fourteenth-century lower register; Tempio wraps an older church and stops at the cornice; Rucellai stops one bay short; Sant'Andrea took 328 years. The Descriptio is a procedure in time: the reader plots 175 points one by one to make the image appear.

## UI translations
1. **One figure, halved.** Fix the main view as a square (or one stated figure) and place every division at 1/2 or 1/4 of it; print the scheme in the kit.
2. **Unit from the content.** Set the base unit as a third of the tallest item's height and count the grid in those units, so the grid is scaled to what it holds.
3. **One aligned channel.** Stack the title mark, the principal figure and the primary action in a single middle bay whose edges agree in every register within 0.5% of the width.
4. **Unequal bays.** Make the middle bay 1.5–2.5 times wider or narrower than each side bay; three equal columns are out.
5. **One large opening.** Give the channel a single opening 0.18–0.40 of the view's width, the only large area of the extreme value, and nest a second frame of the same form inside it at about 0.7.
6. **A module per level.** Fill each band with identical cells that are a whole fraction of the width (15 cells in a band, 2 × 3 panels in a bay); change the interior of a cell, never its size.
7. **Grid as an overlay.** Draw dividers as a flat layer in the second value over content that keeps its own rhythm; let the user switch the layer off.
8. **Two values.** Carry structure with one light and one dark value 45–75 L apart and chroma at or below about 16; keep stronger colour to small marks inside cells or inside the opening (about 2%), always with a label.
9. **Label bands.** Set the name and the date as one evenly spaced line of even-height letters filling 55–67% of a full-width band of its own.
10. **Scales on the edges.** Put axis ticks and reference letters outside the field, beside the edge they name.
11. **Ship the numbers.** Publish each figure with the table that rebuilds it, and use the table as the accessible alternative; where a number is more exact than a picture, show the number.
12. **Text owns its page.** Set reading text as one column in a block about 3:5 at about 45% of the page, high and toward the inner edge; ornament never takes more area than the text.
13. **Missing reads as missing.** Keep empty or pending cells in place with their dividers and no interior.
14. **Build in construction order.** Reveal the whole, the halving, the channel, the opening, the modules and the bands in that order, as cuts; reduced motion shows the finished state.

## What this artist would reject
- A shape or spacing not derived from the whole (free-sized blocks with no ratio to the frame).
- An off-axis main opening, or several equally large openings competing.
- Decoration that covers the text area (the Olomouc border is the other hand's taste, not his).
- Pictures where a number or a description would be more exact.
- Saturated colour across large areas; colour used where a light/dark contrast would do.
- Hidden structure: a grid that cannot be read or rebuilt from its stated measures.

## Signature prompt seed
Fix the main view as one square and divide it only by halving: a 2:1 register below, a half-size square above. Give each register three bays with a middle bay clearly unlike the sides, and align the middle bays edge to edge so one channel runs through the view; stack every singular element there and give it the one large opening. Fill the rest with identical cells that are whole fractions of the width, varying only what is inside each cell. Draw the grid as a flat layer in a second value over content that keeps its own rhythm, with no shading. Use two values, low in chroma, and keep stronger colour to small labelled marks inside cells or the opening. Set the name and date in an evenly spaced band of their own, scales outside the field. Publish the table of numbers that rebuilds the view, and leave unfinished cells visibly empty in place.
