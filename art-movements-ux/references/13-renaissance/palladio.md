# Andrea Palladio — Renaissance

## Essence
Palladio made every building out of a short list of whole-number rooms (a circle, a square, and five named rectangles from 4:3 to 2:1), mirrored about one axis, and then published each one as a single woodcut page: the plan above, the elevation below at the same scale and width, and the dimension of every room written as a bare number inside the room itself. The page and the building follow one rule: a temple front marks the centre, low repeated arcades run out to the sides, and the drawing records measures, not effects.

## Detailed observations

**Title page (1570).** The whole page is a woodcut building: two pairs of fluted Corinthian columns at left and right, broken pediments with reclining trumpeters on top, a seated crowned figure (REGINA VIRTUS) in the upper centre, and allegorical women standing in front of the columns. The title is set in a plain rectangular tablet spanning ≈0.27–0.66 of the page width and sitting between ≈0.30 and 0.47 of the height, a quiet field of capitals and italic inside the busiest image in the book. Letterforms: spaced roman capitals for the title line (IQVATTROLIBRI is set almost solid), small capitals for "DELL'ARCHITETTVRA," italic lower case for the descriptive lines, roman capitals again for "CON PRIVILEGI." Four type styles, one centred axis. The imprint sits in a second tablet in the base. Hatching is dense everywhere except inside the two tablets, which are nearly bare paper: the text sits in the calmest area of the page. This is the only plate in the book that is pictorial rather than measured.

**Book I, Tuscan arcade (p. 18).** One full arch between two piers, entablature across the top, and below the ground line the piers in plan section, filled with dense diagonal hatching. The plan section has the same widths as the elevation above it, directly underneath. The opening, measured to the arch crown, is ≈585 px wide and ≈910 px high, h:w ≈1.56. The dimensions are written in modules and minutes ("MO 7 M 40" vertical, "MO 6 M 25" horizontal), placed in the empty void of the arch on its centre axis; vertical dimensions are set rotated 90° and read up the member, horizontal ones run across. Tiny arrowheads or ticks mark where a span ends; there are no extension lines beyond the object. Pier labels ("MO 1," "MO 7," "26") sit inside the pier faces. Ink covers 3.9% of the page (pixels below grey 100). Light falls only as hatching on cut sections and on the soffit of the arch; the elevation surfaces stay white.

**Book I, ch. XXI–XXIII (pp. 52–53).** Chapter XXI (text page, no figure) gives the rule in seven parts: rooms are round (rarely), square, the diagonal of the square in length (√2:1), a square and a third (4:3), a square and a half (3:2), a square and two thirds (5:3), or two squares (2:1). The same paragraph requires rooms on the right to equal those on the left. Chapter XXIII (p. 53) gives vault heights by means: for a room 12 × 6 feet, add and halve, so 9 (arithmetic); for 9 × 4, multiply and take the root, so 6, "la sesquialtera" (geometric). The page is ≈80% justified text, a single column with a 6-line woodcut initial; two small geometric diagrams (a rectangle with points a–f, and a semicircle on a split base) sit in the right ≈40% of the column, with the text wrapped beside them. Ink 7.6%, the densest page examined. The diagrams are built from lines and letters only: no hatching, no number, no ornament.

**Villa Rotonda, plate Book II p. 19.** Plan above, half elevation and half section below, on one centre line (x ≈570 of 1139 px). The plan's extent is x 56–1068 px and the elevation's 56–1072: the two drawings have the same width to within 0.4%, so one scale governs both. The square core runs x 305–814, 509 px of 1012, i.e. exactly half the overall span, the other half going to the four porticoes and their stairs. The round hall is about half the core (by eye). Room sizes are written as "P. 26," "P. 15," "P. 11," "P. 30," "P. 12," "P. 6" (P = piedi) inside each room; the four corner suites are identical (26 × 15 large rooms, 15 × 11 small ones), mirrored on both axes; 26:15 ≈1.73 and 15:11 ≈1.36, close to but not exactly the √3 and 4:3 of the canon. Walls are drawn as double lines with diagonal hatch; stairs as long runs of parallel lines; each portico has 6 column dots (4 free plus 2 at the ends). The plan's top portico breaks the four-line text block at the head of the page: the type stops around it. In the lower drawing, the left half shows the outside (statues on the pediment and parapet, rectangular windows filled with hatch), the right half the section (dome shells, floor levels, a barrel-vaulted basement arcade of 3 bays). Ink 3.3%. In the photo the block is a light render (lit wall #B6A997, L 70) under a dark terracotta dome and roof (#33221B to #4C3025, L 15–23), against a saturated sky #205B9F (L 38, C 42, 41% of the full frame); the portico interior is warm in shade (#9F7A5A, L 54, C 25). There are 6 Ionic columns per front, all four fronts the same.

**Villa Emo, plate Book II p. 55.** Text block at the top, y ≈30–250 of 1600 (≈14% of the page), 7 lines, running heading in spaced capitals "S E C O N D O." Then a wide blank band (ghost of the verso showing through), the plan at y ≈820–1060, the elevation at y ≈1290–1470. Shared width again: plan x 56–984, elevation 41–982. In elevation the block is ≈21% of the total width (≈200 of 940 px) and ≈1.7× the height of the wings; each wing is a run of 11 identical round arches, ending in a small square dovecote tower. ## Process and relations (observed)
- **A short list of room shapes, named by ratio** (Book I ch. XXI; Rotonda; Emo; Barbaro). Seven shapes only: circle, square, √2:1, 4:3, 3:2, 5:3, 2:1. Plates name rooms by number (Rotonda 26 × 15, 15 × 11, a 30-foot round hall; Emo 16 and 27; Barbaro 16, 12, 20, 18, 32). The plated numbers come near the canon rather than always hitting it (26:15 ≈1.73, 15:11 ≈1.36).
- **Dependent dimensions are derived, not chosen** (Book I ch. XXIII). Vault height is the arithmetic mean of length and width (12 × 6 → 9) or the geometric mean (9 × 4 → 6); the page shows the construction as a diagram with lettered points beside the text.
- **Plan over elevation at one width** (Rotonda 56–1068 vs 56–1072 px; Emo 56–984 vs 41–982; Barbaro 113–905 vs 102–902; Tuscan arch, plan section directly under the piers). One centre line, one scale, the two views stacked with a gap and no frame between them (only the Basilica plates frame their drawings).
- **The number lives inside the thing it measures** (all plates). Room sizes sit as bare numerals in the middle of rooms; heights run vertically up the column or void; arrowed dimension lines appear only in the Basilica detail. No leader lines, keys or legends on the villa plates; a few letter keys (A, B, C) on the text pages.
- **Halves that differ only by role** (Rotonda: half elevation, half section on one plate; San Giorgio: stone face, brick body). Symmetry is kept in the outline while each half carries different information.
- **A raised centre, long even arms** (Emo: block ≈21% of width, ≈1.7× wing height, 11 equal arches per wing; Barbaro: central block, 5 arches per wing, pavilions at the ends; Basilica: 9 equal bays). The centre is a temple front; the sides repeat a single unit at a constant interval and end in a small terminal element.
- **One front at several sizes** (San Giorgio, 2 interlocked fronts; Redentore, 3 nested pediments; Rotonda, 4 identical porticoes). Hierarchy is made by repeating the same pediment at different scales on one axis, not by adding a new form.
- **Light is spent on voids** (Basilica p. 43, openings hatched dark; Tuscan arch, plan sections hatched; Rotonda, windows filled). Solids stay paper-white. In the buildings, the same split: pale stone L 70–90 against openings L 9–22.
- **Sparse ink** (villa plates 2.5–3.3% ink; Basilica 5.3%; text page 7.6%; title page much denser). The measured drawing carries less ink than the text page beside it.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Andrea Palladio.** *Draw every size from one short list of whole-number ratios and derive the rest as means; show one object as two views stacked on one axis at one scale and width; write each measure inside the thing it measures; spend tone on openings only.*
Process:
1. Fix the ratio list before any layout: at most seven shapes for every region (1:1, 4:3, 3:2, 5:3, 2:1, the diagonal of the square √2:1, and one round form, the last two rarely). Count sides in whole units of one module. A region may miss its ratio by up to about 4% to stay on whole units; it may not sit between two ratios.
2. Derive every dependent size from the two it sits between, never by eye: the arithmetic mean (12 and 6 give 9) or the geometric mean (9 and 4 give 6). Third dimensions, gaps and middle sizes all come this way.
3. Set one axis and mirror the slots about it: equal count and equal sizes on both sides. Because the outline is then known from one half, the two halves may carry different information about the same object (its outside and its inside, the finished face and the body behind it).
4. Show the object twice, stacked on that axis at one scale: the view from above first and larger (3–4× the height of the second on an upright format), the view from the front below it. Their left and right edges agree within 1–2%. A gap separates them, no frame. The explanatory text sits first, at the full measure, in 14–22% of the height.
5. Give the run one odd unit and even arms: the odd unit takes about 1/5 of the width and about 1.7× the height of the rest; each arm repeats one unit 5–11 times at a constant interval and stops at a small terminal unit. Only the odd unit and the ends vary. Where the object spreads on two axes, the core takes half the span.
6. Rank by rescaling, not by inventing: the form that marks the odd unit returns at 2–3 sizes on the same axis, the smaller ones nested in or beside the larger. No level of importance gets a form of its own.
7. Spend tone on what is open or cut, nowhere else: solid surfaces keep the ground's value; openings and cut sections take the value furthest from it (a step of 48–80 L). Marks cover 2.5–5.5% of a drawn view, less than the text block beside it (about 8%).
8. Write each measure inside the thing it measures: a bare numeral, with its unit abbreviated or stated once in the text, in the middle of the region; a length reads along the member it belongs to. No legend, no leader line, no key. A tick marks where a span ends; arrowed lines appear only in an enlarged detail.
Why it works:
- Grouping by likeness: regions cut from one short list of ratios are seen as kin at any size, so a screen of twenty panels reads as a few families. Sizes chosen by eye make twenty one-offs, and nothing says which belong together.
- Correspondence by alignment: two views at one scale and one width let the eye drop a vertical from a feature in one to the same feature in the other. Change the scale or shift the axis and every feature needs a label to be found again.
- Proximity: a number in the middle of the empty region it describes cannot be assigned to the wrong part and costs no area, since that space was unused. Move it to a legend and each reading becomes a search and a trip back.
- Salience by oddness: a constant interval fuses the arms into one texture, so a single unit of another size wins without colour. Vary the interval and the arms start competing with the centre.
- Figure and ground as a code: tone that always means "open or cut" is read as a category. Tone used to round a solid is an effect, and it buries the numbers.
Colour relations: the ground is the lightest and largest role (60% or more) and low in chroma; one dark mark value does all the drawing at 2.5–5.5% coverage; solids equal the ground; openings are the extreme value, 48–80 L from the solids; a finished face may be 40 L lighter than the body it is applied to, at a third to a half of its chroma, on one side only. The evidence holds no signal colour: one the case brings takes an existing role (the opening tone or the axis), never a new one.
Space and line: seven ratios; means for dependents; mirrored slots; two views sharing edges within 1–2%; text 14–22%, gap 4–5%, first view 3–4× the second; odd unit ≈1/5 of the width and ≈1.7× the height; arms of 5–11 equal units with a terminal; core = half the span on two axes; the principal opening ≈0.45 of its bay; one line weight; hatch only between the double lines of a cut and in openings.
Type requirement: numerals that stay legible at the smallest size inside a region, with fractions and short unit abbreviations; rank by style within one family (spaced capitals for running heads, small capitals, italic for description, upright for text) on one axis, at most four styles, with sizes taken from step 2; body text as one full-measure column that a figure may interrupt (a figure may take about 40% of the column while text runs beside it).
Motion and interaction: the two views are linked, so a pan, zoom or selection in one moves the other by the same amount and marks the same extent; a region that grows jumps from one listed ratio to the next (4:3, 3:2, 5:3, 2:1) and its dependents re-derive as means; a mirrored half switches role in place while the outline holds; a measure appears in its region on hover or focus, never in a floating tooltip; the foot of each view names the next. Reduced motion: every change is a direct swap.
Fails when: the content has no mirror pair (a conversation, one long text with a side note); the arms have fewer than 5 units a side (a centre between two or three equal tiles is the median hero and feature row); regions are too small to hold their own numbers, so legends return; the object has no second view worth stacking; sizes are live and unstable, so no region keeps a ratio; the width is too narrow for both arms.
Signature (do not carry together): a gabled, columned front on a tall centre with low arcaded wings on one base line · black woodcut line with diagonal hatching on cream paper · spaced roman capitals, pictorial initials and round-arched bays.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Deploy review screen for an operations team, desktop 1440 × 900, dark charcoal ground, one pale mark value, a monospaced family, terse and technical (the works are pale, printed and ceremonial).
1. *Ratios.* Module 16px. Three shapes only: the cell 48 × 64 (3:4), the summary tile 96 × 128 (3:4) and the deploy block 240 × 320 (3:4). Nothing on the screen has another proportion except the text block, which runs the full measure.
2. *Means.* The lower view's height is the arithmetic mean of one cell and one arm: (64 + 192) / 2 = 128px.
3. *Axis and mirror.* The axis is the deploy moment, at x = 720. Eleven five-minute columns before it, eleven after, all 48px. The left half shows the old version and the right half the new one: same slots, different role.
4. *Two views.* Text first, 144px (16%), then a 40px gap (4.4%). Upper view: three services × 22 intervals as cells. Lower view: total error rate over the same 22 intervals. Both run x 72–1368, no frame between them. The upper is 2.5× the lower, under the 3–4× of an upright page because the screen is landscape.
5. *Odd unit and arms.* The deploy block is 240 of 1296px (18.5%) and 320px against arms of 192px (1.67×). Each arm ends in one terminal cell that states the window's start or end time. The arms are centred on the block's mid-height, not seated on a common base line, so the outline is not a building.
6. *Rescale.* The 3:4 upright rectangle marks rank at three sizes on the axis: cell, summary tile in the lower view, deploy block. The alert state gets no new shape.
7. *Tone.* Cells keep the ground's value (L 18). Intervals in which a service returned nothing are the openings: filled with the extreme value (L 88, a step of 70 L). Marks cover about 5% of the two views.
8. *Measures.* Each cell holds its p95 latency as a bare numeral; the unit is stated once in the text block. The block's height is labelled along its own side with the deploy's duration. No legend.
- *Motion.* Focusing a column in the upper view marks the same x in the lower one. Opening a service steps its cells from 3:4 to 2:3 and the lower view re-derives its height.
- *Type.* One family, four styles: spaced capitals for the running head, italic for the description, upright for text, tabular numerals in the cells.

Result: a dark console of numbered cells that does not read as an architectural plate. What survived is one ratio family, derived dimensions, two views on shared edges, an odd unit against even arms, tone as the code for "nothing here", and numbers inside their regions.

## Space & composition
- **Page stack.** Villa plates read top to bottom: text 14–22% of the page height, a 4–5% blank gap, plan 45–50%, elevation 12–15%, signature line. The text spans the full measure of the page; the drawings are centred on the page axis.
- **Shared width.** Plan and elevation sit on the same centre line and the same left and right edges (within 1–2%).
- **Half core.** Rotonda's square core is exactly half the overall span (509 of 1012 px); the four porticoes take the other half.
- **Centre and arms.** In elevation the centre block is about a fifth of the width (Emo 21%), and the arms are long runs of one unit (11, 9 or 5 arches).
- **Bay ratio.** In the serliana the arch takes ≈0.45 of the 22-foot bay (10 feet); the rest is split between the two side openings and columns.
- **Voids as figure.** On the large plates the openings are the darkest areas; in photos the step from stone to opening is 48–80 L.
- **Wrapped figures on text pages.** On the proportion pages, diagrams take the right ≈40% of the column for 15–20 lines while text continues beside them.

## Form, line & typography
- **Line.** Woodcut lines of near-uniform weight; walls are double lines with diagonal hatching between; stairs are runs of parallel lines; columns in plan are solid dots; curves (hemicycles, domes, the Basilica roof) are single unbroken arcs.
- **Lettering on plates.** Numbers and abbreviations ("P. 30," "MO 7 M 40," "PI 50") are cut into the block in a small roman, sometimes rotated to read along the member.
- **Book type.** Roman text in a single justified column, italic for descriptive lines, spaced roman capitals for running heads ("S E C O N D O") and chapter titles, large pictorial woodcut initials 5–6 lines deep, signatures and catchwords at the foot. Four styles on the title page, all on one axis.
- **Architectural form.** The temple front (columns plus a triangular pediment), the serliana bay, the arch on piers, the round hall: a small set of forms, repeated and rescaled.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- **Even intervals.** Emo's 11 + 11 arches and the Basilica's 9 bays per storey are drawn at a constant interval; variation appears only at the centre and the ends.
- **A to B to A.** Every villa reads arm, centre, arm; every facade reads low front, high front, low front.
- **Sequence through the book.** Orders in Book I, houses in Book II, public works in Book III, temples in Book IV: each plate a self-contained page, the next building introduced by the catchword "LA SEGVENTE" (the following) at the foot.
- **Built over decades.** The Rotonda was finished after Palladio's death; San Giorgio's facade in 1610, 30 years after it. The plates show the rule, and the built works sometimes differ from them.

## UI translations
1. **A ratio list as a token set.** Declare seven aspect ratios (1:1, √2:1, 4:3, 3:2, 5:3, 2:1 and one round form) and let no panel, image or cell use another.
2. **Means for the in-between sizes.** Derive a row height, gap or middle type size as the arithmetic or geometric mean of its two neighbours (12 and 6 give 9; 9 and 4 give 6), and print the rule in the kit.
3. **Two views on one width.** Put a map-like view above and a profile view below, at one scale, with identical left and right edges and a gap between them, so a column in one is found directly under itself in the other.
4. **Label inside the region.** Write a value as a bare numeral in the middle of the area it measures, with the unit stated once; drop the legend and the leader lines.
5. **Half and half.** On a mirrored outline, let the left half show one role and the right half another (summary and detail, outside and inside) without changing the outline.
6. **One odd unit, even arms.** Give the principal item about a fifth of the row and about 1.7× the height, and run 5–11 identical units at a constant interval on each side, each run ending in a small terminal unit.
7. **Core at half.** Where a view extends on two axes, the central region takes half of the overall span and the approaches take the other half.
8. **Rank by size of one form.** Mark importance by repeating the same form at two or three sizes on one axis, not by a new shape per level.
9. **Tone marks what is open.** Leave solid surfaces at the ground's value and fill only empty, missing or cut regions with the opposite extreme (48–80 L away), as flat tone or hatch, never as a gradient.
10. **Sparse marks.** Keep the marks of a diagram to 2.5–5.5% of its area, below the density of the text beside it.
11. **Figures interrupt the text.** A diagram may break into the text block or take about 40% of the column while the text runs beside it.
12. **Name the next.** End each view with the name of the one that follows, set at the foot.

## What this artist would reject
- A room or panel size picked by eye with no ratio behind it.
- Asymmetry about the main axis: the text requires equal rooms on both sides.
- Dimensions in a separate legend or key when they can sit inside the shape.
- Plan and elevation drawn at different scales or on different axes.
- Ornament or shading on solid surfaces; on the plates hatching marks cuts and voids only.
- A new form for each level of importance where the same front at another size would do.

## Signature prompt seed
Choose every proportion from one short list of whole-number ratios (1:1, 4:3, 3:2, 5:3, 2:1) and derive every in-between size as the mean of its two neighbours. Mirror the slots about a single axis, then let the two halves show different aspects of the same thing. Show the subject twice, a map-like view above and a profile below, at one scale with identical left and right edges. Give one unit about a fifth of the width and 1.7× the height, flanked by long runs of one repeated unit at a constant interval. Mark rank by the same form at two or three sizes. Keep solid surfaces at the ground's value and put tone only on what is open or missing. Write each number inside the region it measures; use no legend.
