# Albrecht Dürer — Renaissance

## Essence
Dürer builds things from a stated measure and then shows the measure. A letter is drawn inside a square whose side sets every stroke (stems ≈1/10 of the side). A body is ruled into labelled horizontal slices, and a room is constructed toward a point. The construction is printed beside, under or through the finished form. His signature is itself a constructed letter pair, the AD monogram, set on a small dated tablet that he places like a label at the edge of the scene.

## Key works examined
- **Underweysung der Messung (Instruction in Measurement), 1525 (book, woodcut illustrations, letterpress in Schwabacher, ≈31 × 21 cm; Getty Research Institute copy, Internet Archive; Met copies 349058 and 41.48.3).** A geometry manual for craftsmen covering curves and spirals, tilings, constructed Roman capitals and textura, polyhedra nets, and perspective devices. It teaches construction shown next to the result. *(visually checked: Internet Archive scan vnderweysungderm00drer, all 192 pages as a contact sheet, 13 pages at full size; measured stem/height ratios, page bands, ink cover, inscription line heights, palette)*
- **Constructed Roman alphabet (Underweysung, Book III, 1525).** Each capital sits in a square with lettered corners a, b, c, d. It teaches one module that sets every stroke. *(visually checked: IA pages n116 B/C, n121 K/L, n130 A–D summary; measured stem 26 px on a 250 px letter)*
- **Constructed textura (Underweysung, Book III, 1525).** Blackletter built from stacked squares capped by lozenges (squares turned 45°). It teaches a whole alphabet from one square. *(visually checked: IA pages n138 construction, n139 finished alphabet; module and gap measured)*
- **Tower inscription diagram (Underweysung, Book III, 1525).** Line heights grow upward so that every line looks the same size from one eye point. It teaches size set by viewing angle. *(visually checked: IA page n113; nine line heights measured)*
- **Polyhedra nets (Underweysung, Book IV, 1525).** Unfolded solids printed flat to be cut out and folded. It teaches a solid shown as its flat net. *(visually checked: IA pages n149 truncated cube, n152 and contact sheet n144–n156; ink cover measured)*
- **Draughtsman with a lute, 1525 (woodcut, ≈13 × 18 cm, last plate of the Underweysung; Met 17.37.313 impression).** Two men transfer a lute onto a hinged panel with a string. It teaches the device shown working, with its monogram and date on the wall. *(visually checked: IA page n182, plus n181 draughtsman with a sighting post; block-to-page area measured)*
- **Vier Bücher von menschlicher Proportion (Four Books on Human Proportion), 1528 (book, woodcuts; Glasgow School of Art Library copy, Internet Archive; Met 31.54.106–109 leaves).** Bodies are ruled into named landmark slices with measurements written on every slice. It teaches the annotated figure and the variant set. *(visually checked: IA scan hierinnsindbegri00dure, 124 pages as a contact sheet, 9 pages at full size; measured head:body, label count, monogram, palette)*
- **AD monogram (title page of the Proportion, 1528, and on the prints).** An A whose crossbar and top bar make a frame, with a D inside. It teaches a mark built like the letters. *(visually checked: IA page n004; bbox and stroke widths measured; positions measured on six works)*
- **Melencolia I, 1514 (engraving, 24.1 × 18.7 cm, Met 43.106.1).** A seated winged figure surrounded by measuring tools: a polyhedron, a sphere, a ladder, scales, an hourglass, a 4 × 4 magic square. It teaches instruments as the subject. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons, Met image DP820348; palette, ink cover)*
- **The Rhinoceros, 1515 (woodcut, image 21.3 × 29.5 cm; NGA impression viewed, Met 19.73.159 for size).** An animal drawn from a written report and a sketch, with a five-line text band above. It teaches a text header joined to a full-bleed image. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons, NGA image 47903; text-band share, palette, ink cover)*
- **Saint Jerome in His Study, 1514 (engraving, 24.6 × 18.9 cm, Met 19.73.68).** A constructed interior with an off-centre vanishing point and light falling through bullseye panes. It teaches calm, measured space. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons, Met image DP820349; palette, ink cover, rough vanishing point)*
- **Adam and Eve, 1504 (engraving, ≈25 × 20 cm, Met).** Two ideal nudes built from proportion studies against a near-black forest, with a hanging signature tablet. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons, Met image DT646; palette, head:body, tablet position)*
- **Knight, Death and the Devil, 1513 (engraving, plate 24.3 × 18.8 cm, Met 43.106.2).** *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons, Met image DP102226; tablet position only)*
- **Self-Portrait at 28 ("in fur coat"), 1500 (oil on lime panel, 67.1 × 48.9 cm, Alte Pinakothek, Munich).** A strictly frontal face, flanked at eye level by the monogram (left) and a gold Latin inscription (right). It is the one colour work here. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons high-resolution file; palette, placement)*
- **Great Piece of Turf, 1503 (watercolour and gouache, ≈40.3 × 31.1 cm, Albertina, Vienna).** A clump of grasses seen from ground level. A colour control for the observed, unconstructed side of his work. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons, Google Art Project file; palette)*

## Detailed observations
**Underweysung, the constructed capitals (pages n116, n121, n130).** Every capital sits in a ruled square (≈255 × 255 px at the scan size) with corners lettered a b c d and internal points e, f, g, h, i. The finished letter matches the square's height: the K is 250 px tall and the square 255 px. The broad stem measures 26 px, 1/9.6 of the letter height, which matches the stated rule of one tenth of the side ("ein zehenteyl", read on the page). The thin strokes run 10–13 px (≈1/20–1/25), and a secondary source gives a range of 1/30 to 1/10. Serifs are arcs of small circles drawn inside the square's corners, and the circles are visible on the construction. B fills 0.73 of its square's width. C and D are ruled circles plus a diagonal from a to d. Page layouts vary but follow one logic. On n116 the two finished B's form a band across the top (rows 132–385 of 1598, 16% of the page height), the paragraph of instruction takes 36% (rows 400–978), and the two C construction squares sit below (21%, rows 1050–1392). On n121 the construction square sits on the left, with the finished K, K or L to its right at the same height and on the same baseline. On n130 one column of four construction squares runs down the left, each followed by three finished variants in a row: a 1 : 3 ratio of rule to result, with all four rows equal in height. A one-line header on n130 states that capitals are made one third larger than the text line ("eyns dritteyls grosser"). Ink covers about 10% of these pages. The paper reads as an aged warm cream, `#E2CD99` (L 83, C 29), on 69% of the page. Nothing is boxed except the construction squares; the letters float on bare paper.

**Underweysung, textura (n138, n139).** The construction plate draws each lowercase letter as a column of square cells with a lozenge (the same square turned 45°) at the top and at the bottom. The i is about three cells between its lozenge caps, and n is two such columns. The white gap between the two stems of n is ≈0.65 of a stem (45 px against 70 px in the enlarged crop). Curved letters (a, z, s) borrow one arc each; everything else is straight. Letters are labelled in small script inside their cells. The finished alphabet on the facing page prints in four rows (a–h, i–p, q–v, w–z), black from edge to edge. Ink rises to 25% of the page, the densest page of the book, with `#080903` (L 3) on 17.5%. The same square serves as module, stem width, gap and cap.

**Underweysung, tower inscription (n113).** A 14-line paragraph fills the top third. Below it, an elevation of a pointed tower carries a nine-line Roman inscription, "DAS·W GOTES BLEIBT / EWIGLICH DIS·WORT IST·CRISTVS / ALLER·CRIST GLAVBIGEN HEIL". To its right is the construction: rays from an eye point c, a quarter arc divided into equal parts and numbered 1–17, and parallels carried back to the wall. Measured line heights from top to bottom are 46, 37, 39 | 28, 26, 23 | 22, 23, 21 px, so the top line is ≈2.1× the bottom line. The lines come in three groups of three with a blank line between groups. The text says the upper letters are made larger so they read like the lower ones. The size of each line comes from equal angles at the eye, not equal heights on the wall.

**Underweysung, nets and tilings (n149, n152, n062–n068).** The truncated cube net (n149) lays six octagons in a cross, with eight triangles hinged at the corners. It takes ≈65% of the page height (rows 130–1175 of 1598), centred, under a figure number "36", with a two-line caption and one decorated initial at the bottom. Ink cover is 1.5%, the emptiest page measured. Line weight is uniform, there is no shading, and the edges are single thin rules. The tilings (n062–n068) repeat one tile in rectangles and circles, often two or three samples per page, each a different arrangement of the same shapes.

**Draughtsman with a lute (n182).** The woodcut block measures ≈1075 × 783 px (1.37 : 1) and covers ≈45% of the page area. Three lines of text sit above it, and below it a closing paragraph set as an inverted triangle (each line shorter, centred) ends in the colophon "Gedruckt zu Nüremberg. Im 1525 Jar." The image is a stage box: a back wall of vertical boards, two side doorways, and a table seen from the front. The working line, a taut string, runs from a pulley on the wall through the frame to the lute and is the only diagonal in the upper half. The monogram and "1525" sit on the back wall just above the left man's head, at 0.43 across and 0.08 down within the block. The companion plate n181 (a man drawing a seated sitter through a sighting post and glass) uses the same layout: picture above (≈44% of the page), then a heading "Ein andre meynung" ("another method") and a block of instructions.

**Proportion 1528, title page (n004).** About eight lines of blackletter title sit at the top, and a large AD monogram stands in the centre: the A's outer box is 373 × 354 px (1.05 : 1, nearly square), 36% of the page width. The A has a flat top bar that overhangs both legs and a crossbar at ≈0.22 of its height. The left leg is thin (≈10–13 px) and the right leg thick (≈25 px), the same thick–thin stress as his Roman A. The D stands under the crossbar with a 22–24 px stem and fills about 0.58 of the A's height. Old paper is `#B79761` (L 64, C 34), foxed and darker than the 1525 copy. Ink takes 3% of the page.

**Proportion 1528, ruled figures (n072, n112).** A woman in side view and front view stands on one shared baseline under one shared top rule. Between the views runs a column of ≈27 landmark names, from "Scheytel" (crown) through "Kinn", "Halsgrüblein", "Nabel" and "Mitten im kny" down to "Soln" (soles). Each name sits level with the horizontal line it names across the figure. Every slice carries its measure as a stacked figure (numerator over unit signs). On the left, nested brackets in up to five columns group the slices into larger spans (head, torso, thigh, shin), like an indented outline. Head height to body height is 175 : 1285 px ≈ 1 : 7.3 here and 180 : 1285 ≈ 1 : 7.1 on n112. On n112 a single front figure raises one arm to touch a top rule, and a large compass arc swings from fingertip to foot, closing the figure into a circle segment. The arc and the rules are printed in the same weight as the contour. Ink cover is 4–8%, and paper is `#DCC595`/`#D5BB89` (L 77–81, C 27–29).

**Proportion, Book III, varying the rule (n160, n168, n186).** On n160 a square is redrawn as a trapezoid, narrow at the front and wide at the back, and the head grid inside it deforms with it: two pairs of drawings, rule on the left and result on the right. On n168 six profile heads appear in a 2 × 3 grid, each inside a square of the same size (≈280 px). The heads differ only because the horizontal division lines are moved or the face line is tilted: one frame with six outcomes. On n186 the device he calls the "Felscher" (falsifier) has ≈27 lines fanning from a vertex b to a baseline a–c. The landmark intervals are transferred through it, so a whole column of measurements can be stretched or compressed in one consistent way. The labels "Unden" and "Oben" are inverted here.

**Melencolia I, 1514.** The plate is dense: ink below the threshold covers 53% of the image. Six measured clusters run from `#4C4741` (L 30) to `#D7C5AC` (L 80). The mid-darks L 30–47 take 64%, and only 7% reaches the paper tone. The light is staged: the lit planes are the polyhedron's top face, the sphere, the figure's skirt and the radiant burst over the sea. The objects are laid out as a catalogue of measuring tools. A 4 × 4 number square sits in a ruled frame high on the right wall, under a bell, beside an hourglass and a pair of scales. The bottom row of the square reads 4 15 14 1, and its middle two cells give the date 1514. A ladder rises at ≈70° from the horizontal (by eye) and leaves the frame at the top, as do the building's edges. The title "MELENCOLIA I" is set in Roman capitals on a banner carried by a bat in the upper left. Monogram and date sit on the step at the lower right (≈0.95 across, 0.80 down).

**The Rhinoceros, 1515.** A five-line blackletter text band runs across the full width at the top and takes ≈10% of the sheet height (rows 0–155 of 1508), separated from the image by the block line. The animal fills the frame. It spans ≈97% of the width, the tail is cut by the left border, the horn almost touches the right, and the feet stand within ≈8% of the bottom edge. The title "1515 / RHINOCERVS / AD" sits in the only open area, the upper right sky (≈0.70–0.94 across, 0.12–0.26 down), in Roman capitals with the monogram centred below. The hide is broken into plates, and each plate is filled with a different repeated motif (rings, scales, rivet dots, wavy lines), like a set of samples. Ink cover is 21%, against 44–53% for the engravings. Paper is `#EFDFC7` (L 89, C 13) on 30.5%, and the rest is mid-tone line texture (L 47–81).

**Saint Jerome in His Study, 1514.** Room-depth perspective: the ceiling boards, bench and window embrasure recede toward a point right of centre, roughly 0.65 across and 0.3 down by eye (rough, from two orthogonals). The saint sits small (his head ≈ 0.63, 0.47) at the far table. The foreground band is a step with the sleeping lion and dog across the bottom 30%. Light enters through bullseye panes on the left and lays patterned patches on the embrasure. Ink cover is 44%. Clusters run from L 34 to L 76, with L 41–56 on 64%. A small dated tablet with the monogram lies on the floor at the right (≈0.91, 0.75).

**Adam and Eve, 1504.** Two pale nudes stand against a near-black forest: `#333432` (L 21) plus `#4B4A45` (L 32) cover 41.5% of the image, while the figure light `#E8DED0` (L 88) takes 14.5%. Adam's head is ≈1/7.5 of his height (160 : 1200 px), and his figure takes 74% of the plate height. The bodies follow the proportion construction he was then studying. A tablet reading "ALBERTVS DVRER NORICVS FACIEBAT" with monogram and 1504 hangs from a branch at ≈0.23 across, 0.23 down, beside Adam's head.

**Self-Portrait, 1500.** The only colour work here, and its colour is mostly near-black. `#0C0C0A` (L 4) covers 45.6% of the panel and `#1C1106` (L 7) another 20%. The warm values step up through `#321B08` (L 13), `#4A2607` (L 20, C 28) and `#644019` (L 31, C 33) to the lit hair, fur and flesh `#AA7D4B` (L 56, C 35), at only 4.2%. The face is strictly frontal and centred, with the nose on the vertical midline (0.50 across) and the eyes at 0.24 down. At exactly that eye line two labels flank the head: on the left the date 1500 above the AD monogram (≈0.10 across), and on the right a four-line gold Latin inscription (≈0.85 across). The light comes from the upper left, and the right cheek falls into shadow. The hand is raised to the fur at the lower centre.

**Great Piece of Turf, 1503 (control).** Bare paper `#E7D8CC` (L 86) covers the top ≈40% and 39% of the whole area. The eye level is at the ground, so the grass stems rise against the empty sheet. Measured greens: `#556851` (L 41, C 16), `#7C896A` (L 56, C 18), `#B1B195` (L 72, C 15), with earth `#695E4D` (L 41) at the base. Even in colour, chroma stays at or below 18. There is no construction: this is the observed pole of his practice.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **One module sets every part** (constructed capitals, textura, Proportion figures). The Roman square sets a stem of ≈1/10 (measured 1/9.6), thin strokes of ≈1/20–1/30, and serif circles inside the corners. The textura square is the stem, its 45° turn is the cap, and the counter is ≈0.65 of it. The body is ruled into ≈27 named slices, with the head at 1/7.1–1/7.5 of the height.
- **Rule shown next to the result** (n121, n130, n160, n138/n139, n182). The construction square sits at the left at the result's height (n121), one construction column feeds three finished variants (n130, 1 : 3), the rule and the deformed result are printed as pairs (n160), the construction plate faces the finished alphabet (n138/n139), and the device is drawn in use (n182). The construction lines are printed at the same weight as the result.
- **Text, image and rule share one page in stacked bands** (n116: 16% / 36% / 21% of the page height; n182: text, then a block of ≈45% of the area, then an inverted-triangle colophon; the Rhinoceros: a 10% text band over a 90% image). Columns are full width, the blocks are left to the paper, and there is no frame other than the block line.
- **Labels sit level with what they name** (n072: ≈27 landmark names aligned to their rules; nested brackets in up to five levels; letter construction points a–i). Annotation is in-line and lettered rather than keyed to a legend.
- **Variants from one frame** (n130: three versions per letter; n168: six heads in identical squares; n160 and n186: one transform applied to a whole set). Variation comes from moving the internal division lines, not from changing the frame.
- **Size set by the viewer's angle** (n113: top line ≈2.1× the bottom line, from equal angles at an eye point c; the lute and sighting-post woodcuts make the same point mechanically).
- **The maker's mark as a dated tablet at the edge** (positions: Melencolia 0.95/0.80, Jerome 0.91/0.75, Knight ≈0.12/0.90, Adam and Eve 0.23/0.23, Rhinoceros 0.83/0.23, Self-Portrait 0.10/0.24, lute plate 0.43/0.08). The mark is always small (the A is ≈1.05 : 1), always paired with a date, and set in an open area at a corner or on a wall.
- **Density as a deliberate scale** (ink cover: polyhedron net 1.5%, construction plates 4–10%, the Rhinoceros woodcut 21%, the textura specimen 25%, engravings 44–53%). Teaching pages are nearly empty; the narrative plates are dense.
- **Low chroma throughout** (prints C 1–15 on paper; aged book paper C 27–34 is age; even the painted self-portrait peaks at C 35 on 4% of the area; Turf greens C ≤ 18).

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Albrecht Dürer.** *Declare one square module and take every stroke, gap and size from it; print the rule beside its result at the same height and line weight, one rule feeding a row of outcomes that differ only in where their inner divisions fall; name every division level with its line; sign and date each view in its open corner.*
Process:
1. Declare one square module before anything is drawn, and take every other measure from its side: the heavy stroke 1/10 of it, the light stroke 1/20–1/30 (a half to a third of the heavy one), display text 4/3 of running text. In a compact system the module is itself the stroke: its 45° turn ends a stroke, and the gap between two strokes is about 0.65 of one. No size enters that is not a stated fraction of the side.
2. Rule the principal object across at its own real landmarks, top to bottom: unequal intervals, as many as the object has (up to about 27), each one a named division with its measure. Every view of the object stands on one shared base line under one shared top rule, so a division runs through all of them at one height.
3. Print the rule beside its result. The construction (the module frame with its points lettered in the order they are made) sits on the left; the finished form sits on the right at the same height and on the same base line. One construction feeds up to three results (1 : 3), and the rows are equal in height. Rule and result are drawn in one line weight. Only the construction frame has an outline; results stand on bare ground.
4. Make variants inside identical frames (2 × 3, or 4 rows × 3). Between variants move only the inner division lines, or tilt one line; never the frame. To change a whole set, pass every interval through one transform (a fan of lines from a single point, or one frame redrawn as a trapezoid), so all of them stretch together. Do not adjust them one by one.
5. Name each division where it is. One column of names runs between or beside the views, each name level with the line it names; each interval carries its own measure as a numeral. On the opposite side, nested brackets (up to five levels) gather intervals into larger spans, as an indented outline does. No legend, no key, no leader to a distant note.
6. Stack what remains in full-width bands with nothing boxed: the result, the instruction and the construction (measured at 16%, 36% and 21% of the height, with margins of 8% above and 13% below); or a text band of about 10% over an image that takes the rest; or text, a figure of about 45% of the area, text. A full-bleed figure may be cut by the frame, and its title goes into its one open area.
7. Where parts of a view lie at different distances or angles from the viewer, size them by equal angles at the eye, not by equal heights on the surface. Over nine lines the farthest is about 2.1× the nearest, stepped in three groups of three with one blank line between groups.
8. Choose the density by the view's job and hold it: marks cover 1.5–10% of a view that teaches or measures and 21–25% of a specimen that shows a finished set. Emphasis is a value step or a denser mark, not a hue. (Above 25% the evidence is tonal modelling of scenes, which is not carried.)
9. Stamp every view with who made it and when: one small mark (near square, about 1.05 : 1) paired with a date, drawn with the same heavy and light strokes as the lettering, set in an open area within a quarter of the format from an edge. Never centred, never over the subject, never absent.
Why it works:
- Scale reference: when every stroke and gap is a stated fraction of one side, a reader can check any part against the whole, and parts made at different times agree. Sizes set one by one have to be trusted singly.
- Correspondence by alignment: a construction and its result at one height on one base line let the eye carry a point straight across from the rule to the form. Delete the construction and the form is an assertion; draw it lighter and it reads as decoration that could go; lay it over the result and both are harder to read.
- Proximity and good continuation: a name level with its line is found by following the line. Move the names to a key and each reading is a search and a trip back; the brackets give the hierarchy without a single box.
- Grouping by likeness, salience by oddness: identical frames fuse into a set, so the only thing left to see is where the inner lines moved. Change the frame as well and the eye compares frames. One transform over all intervals keeps their order and ratios, so the family stays recognisable.
- Size constancy: lines sized by equal angles look equal from where the viewer stands. Equal heights make the far lines look smaller, and smaller reads as less important.
- Figure and ground: a small mark in the one open area is found without competing with the subject, and a date fixes which state of the rule the view shows.
Colour relations: the ground is the lightest and largest role, about 70% of a working view and as little as 30% under a full-bleed figure; one mark value about 80 L away does all the drawing; chroma stays at or below about 15 on both. Rank is carried by density of marks and by value steps. The evidence holds no signal colour: where the case brings one it takes the smallest share (about 4%, the share of the highest-chroma note in the one painted work) and sits on the subject, never on the construction.
Space and line: one square module; strokes 1/10 and 1/20–1/30; gap 0.65 of a stroke; rule to results 1 : 3 at equal height; variants in equal frames, 2 × 3 or 4 × 3; up to about 27 named intervals and five bracket levels; bands 16 / 36 / 21% or 10 / 90%, or a figure at 45% of the area; margins 8% above and 13% below; far to near 2.1 : 1 by equal angles; mark about 1.05 : 1 within a quarter of the format from an edge; straight rules and compass arcs only on a working view; one line weight for rule and result.
Type requirement: letters that visibly come from the module, with a heavy stem near 1/10 of their height and light strokes a half to a third of it; two contrasting styles with two fixed jobs (one dense and even for running text, one open and formal for titles and inscriptions), the second 4/3 the size of the first; numerals that can stack as fractions; single letters legible at the smallest size for construction points.
Motion and interaction: the construction replays in the order of its lettered points, one cut per point (80–120ms apart), and the result appears last, already aligned. Dragging an inner division line redraws the result beside it at once; one control drives the shared transform, so every interval moves together and the names travel with their lines. Focusing a name marks its line across every view; focusing a line marks its name. The construction is never hidden behind a toggle. Reduced motion: the finished state with the construction in place.
Fails when: the content has no rule to show (photographs, free prose, a feed); the audience wants only the result, so the construction is noise (a checkout); the view is too narrow to hold rule and result side by side at one height; divisions fall closer together than one line of text, so level names collide; variants need different frames; the object has no fixed landmarks, or they change faster than they can be named.
Signature (do not carry together): letters or bodies inside ruled squares with corners lettered a b c d · a monogram on a small dated tablet · dense black cross-hatched line with blackletter text on aged cream paper.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Storage pricing calculator for a consumer cloud service, desktop 1440 × 900, deep violet ground, one pale lilac mark value, a rounded geometric sans, friendly (the works are black line on paper, scholarly and austere).
1. *Module.* A 40px square. Heavy rule 4px (1/10), light rule 2px (1/20). Adjacent bars stand 26px apart (0.65 of the module). Running text 15px, titles 20px (4/3).
2. *Divisions.* The monthly bill is a bar 280px tall, ruled across at its six real components (storage, transfer, requests, support, tax, credit), so the intervals are unequal. "This month" and "next month" stand on one base line under one top rule.
3. *Rule beside result.* In the lowest band a construction frame 189 × 189px on the left shows the tariff as a stepped line with its break points lettered a to d in the order the price is worked out. To its right stand three finished bills (light, typical and peak use) at the same 189px height on the same base line: 1 : 3. The tariff and the bills share the 2px line. Only the construction frame has an outline.
4. *Variants.* The three bills are identical frames; only their division lines sit at different heights. One "growth" control passes all six intervals through one scale, so the components stretch together.
5. *Names.* A column of the six component names runs between the two bars, each level with its line, with the amount written on each slice. Brackets on the left gather the slices into "usage" and "fixed", and those two into "total": three levels. No colour key.
6. *Bands.* Margins 72px above (8%) and 117px below (13%). Total band 144px (16%), annotated bill band 324px (36%), construction band 189px (21%), with two 27px gaps. No panel has a border or a fill.
7. *Angle.* Not used: one reader at one distance, so all lines keep their size.
8. *Density.* Marks stay under 10% of the screen. The bill's largest component is told from the rest by a denser fill of 2px rules, not by a second colour.
9. *Mark.* The tariff's version number and effective date sit as a 42 × 40px mark in the open upper right of the bill band (0.92 across, 0.20 down), drawn in the 4px and 2px strokes.
- *Motion.* Opening the screen plots the tariff's points a to d as four cuts 100ms apart, then the three bills. Dragging a break point redraws all three bills at once.
- *Type.* The rounded sans in two styles: regular for text, spaced capitals at 20px for the band titles.

Result: a violet, friendly calculator that does not read as a printed treatise page. What survived is one module behind every size, the rule printed beside three outcomes at one height, variants that differ only in their division lines, names level with what they name, and a dated mark in the open corner.

## Space & composition
- Teaching pages are stacked full-width bands: finished form, instruction, construction. Margins run ≈8% at the top and ≈13% at the bottom on n116, and nothing is boxed except the construction squares.
- The construction sits beside the result at the same height (left = rule, right = result), or in a left column feeding a row of variants (1 : 3).
- The scene plates fill the frame to the edge: the rhinoceros spans 97% of the width and is cut by the border, and the Melencolia ladder exits the top. The title goes into the one open area (upper right).
- Measured space: Jerome's room recedes to a point right of centre, and the figure is small and far away; the foreground is a strip across the bottom 30%.
- Variants are tiled in identical frames (2 × 3 heads, 4 rows × 3 letters).

## Form, line & typography
- Roman capitals constructed in a square: broad stem ≈1/10 of the height (measured 26/250), thin ≈1/20–1/30, serifs as corner arcs, B ≈0.73 as wide as tall. Thick and thin follow pen stress (the A's left leg thin, right leg thick, also in the monogram).
- Textura from one square: stem = one square column, caps = the same square turned 45°, counters ≈0.65 of a stem. Curves are allowed only on a, s and z.
- Text is set in Schwabacher blackletter, with Roman capitals reserved for inscriptions and titles (RHINOCERVS, MELENCOLIA, the tower text). There are two scripts with two jobs.
- Capitals are one third larger than the text (stated on n130).
- Line is uniform and thin on the construction pages, with no hatching; cross-hatching and dot-and-lozenge engraving are kept for the scene plates.
- Annotation is small lettered points (a, b, c, d …), stacked numeric fractions and landmark words, always set level with the feature.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- Repetition runs in counted steps: equal angular divisions numbered 1–17 (n113), ≈27 body slices, 4 rows × 3 variants, 2 × 3 heads.
- Sequences progress: line heights in three groups of three steps (46 → 21 px), and the spirals and curves of Book I unfold point by numbered point.
- The time marks are concrete: the date sits with the monogram on every work, and Melencolia shows an hourglass, a bell and a date hidden in the number square.
- Process as sequence: the instruction paragraph reads step by step ("Darnach …", "afterwards"), and the drawing labels the points in the order of construction.

## UI translations
1. **One module, stated.** Pick one square and publish every stroke, gap and type size as a fraction of its side (heavy rule 1/10, light rule 1/20–1/30, gap 0.65 of a stroke, display text 4/3 of body).
2. **Rule beside result.** Next to any computed or generated value, show the construction that produced it, at the same height and on the same base line, with its points lettered in order.
3. **One rule, three outcomes.** Let one construction feed a row of up to three results (1 : 3) in rows of equal height, so the reader sees the rule and its range together.
4. **Same weight for rule and result.** Draw the construction in the same line weight as the finished form; set it apart by its frame and its letters only.
5. **Box only the construction.** Results, text and figures stand on bare ground; the one outlined frame on a view is the module frame.
6. **Names level with lines.** Label a divided object in one column beside it, each name level with the division it names and each interval carrying its own numeral; no legend.
7. **Brackets for hierarchy.** Group divisions into spans with nested brackets on one side (up to five levels) instead of nested cards.
8. **Variants in equal frames.** Show options in a 2 × 3 or 4 × 3 grid of identical frames in which only the inner division lines differ.
9. **One transform for the set.** Give the user a single control that stretches every interval through the same transform, and let names travel with their lines.
10. **Stacked bands.** Lay a working view out as full-width bands for result, instruction and construction (about 16%, 36% and 21% of the height) with margins of 8% above and 13% below.
11. **Size by angle.** On surfaces seen at a slant or from afar (wall displays, tall scrolling columns read from one position), size lines by equal angles at the eye: the farthest about 2.1× the nearest, in groups of three.
12. **Density by job.** Hold marks to 1.5–10% of a view that teaches or measures and 21–25% of a specimen view; raise emphasis with a value step or denser marks, not a hue.
13. **Maker and date.** Stamp every view with a small near-square mark and a date (author, version, time of the data) in an open area near an edge.
14. **Replay the construction.** Reveal the lettered points in order as cuts, the result last; reduced motion shows the finished state with the construction in place.

## What this artist would reject
- A form with no stated rule: a letter or body drawn freehand without its square, slices or numbers.
- Hiding the construction: guidelines deleted from the final page, or a measure kept off the page in a separate legend.
- Labels detached from what they name (keys, colour legends, tooltips far from the feature).
- Uniform sizing that ignores the viewing angle (the same cap height at the top of a tower as at the bottom).
- Decorative frames and boxes around every element; he boxes only the construction square.
- Hue used for emphasis where a value step or a denser hatch would serve.
- An anonymous or undated work: the AD and the year are always there.

## Signature prompt seed
Declare one square module and take every stroke, gap and type size from its side: heavy rules a tenth of it, light rules a half to a third of that, display text a third larger than body. Rule the main object across at its real landmarks and set each name in one column level with its line, with nested brackets for the larger spans and no legend. Print the construction beside its result at the same height and line weight, one construction feeding a row of three outcomes. Show variants in identical frames where only the inner division lines move, and change a whole set through one shared transform. Stack result, instruction and construction as full-width bands with nothing boxed except the construction frame. Size lines by equal angles at the viewer's eye where the distance varies. Work in one ground and one mark value with low chroma, keep working views sparse, and carry emphasis by value or density. Put a small maker's mark and a date in the open corner of every view. No shading or modelled depth.
