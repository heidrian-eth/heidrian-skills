# Mary Ellen Bute — Visual Music

## Essence
Bute picks a few plain figures (a ring, a rod, a stair, one triangle, one curve), lights them against black or one flat colour, and makes a known piece of music unfold in them: one figure per event, added, repeated, trailed or swept along a fixed axis. The figure's form stays constant while its number, size, position and light change on a schedule counted in frames. Synchronisation is selective. Measured frame by frame, accents are hit to within one frame in short passages and left alone in others, where light, slow drift and dissolves carry the phrase.

## Detailed observations

**Rhythm in Light.** The ground is black or near black: `#0F0F0F`–`#191919` takes 72% of the lattice frame at 2:04 and whites stop at `#777777` (L 52, 2.7%) in this transfer. There is no figure drawn in line. Everything is a solid: bracelets, paper pyramids, ping-pong balls, a card lattice, cellophane, an egg-beater, sparklers. The opening passage (1:38–1:50) runs as a clear series. A cluster of four pyramids is replaced by rings that enter along the left and the top, in strings of 4–6 overlapping copies. The rings close to one stack, then one ring alone, 0.45 of the frame width, centred and completely still for about 1.0 s (1:43.9–1:44.9). It is then cut to 8 small rings, each 0.05–0.15 wide, which drift out from the centre and grow. One object is shown as many, then one, then many. Pyramids rise from the bottom edge, not from the centre. At 3:10.4 the tip climbs from 0.81 to 0.36 of frame height in 1.47 s in near equal steps (74 px of 240 per second, 0.31 frame-height/s); a second pair at 4:10.4 climbs at 0.28. Both are constant speed, with no ease. Exits are by fade, by defocus to grey blur (3:08–3:10 the edge softens, then L falls to black in about 0.7 s), or by a cut. Full-film density in 10 s blocks: luma 10–12% for the first minute, then swings 10 / 32 / 10 / 48 / 15%; the brightest block (48%, white blur filling the frame at 4:00) is followed within 3 s by one ball on black (under 2% of the frame). 52 hard changes in 195 s, median gap 0.8 s, longest unbroken passage 18.7 s. Black or nearly black frames 11%. Sync: 33% of motion peaks fall within 40 ms of a sound onset against 28% by chance, so accents are not systematically hit; the flyer's claim of a mathematical relation is not visible at event level in this transfer. The grouping of sections to phrases is visible, the hits are not. Published accounts agree with the frames on the materials (Bute herself lists pyramids and ping-pong balls).

**Synchromy No. 2.** The darkest film of the set: `#030303` takes 83% of the staircase frame, 33% of all frames are under 6% luma, and 10 s blocks sit between 3 and 15% luma throughout. Motion energy is also the lowest (0.1–1.1%, blocks). Figures are glints: bent tubes of light, glass colanders seen as dotted ellipses, stepped solids, a plaster Venus head, calla lilies, mirrored symmetric arrangements in 2 to 4 parts. The key passage is a staircase (2:56–3:16). It starts as one lit slab at the bottom of the frame, left of centre, and a slab is added on top at 176.6, 176.9, 178.1, 179.6, 181.1, 182.9, 185.3, 188.3, 190.4 and 193.1 s. The gaps are 1.2, 1.5, 1.5, 1.8, 2.4, 3.0, 2.1 and 2.7 s, so the build slows by about half as it goes up. Each slab is about 0.055 of frame height and 0.30 wide, shifted to the right, so the stack bends into an S. Each arrives as a light fade of 0.6–0.9 s, left part first, and never moves again; the whole stack sinks and turns a few pixels as it grows. Lit area climbs from 1% to 18% in 16 s in an almost straight line. While it finishes, a second family (thin light blades, 1–2% each) begins at the top corners at 3:03.6, before the first is complete. In the opening, light rods form for 0.3–0.6 s, slide and dissolve; a burst of blades (1:45.3–1:49.9) grows from 5 marks to about 14, then loses them one by one, in 4.6 s. Only 17 hard changes in 238 s (median gap 3.4 s, longest 57 s): passages are joined by fades and overlays. Sync is absent at event level: 23% of motion peaks within 40 ms against 25% by chance.

**Dada.** Pale grey solids (`#A3A1A2`, L 66) on black that takes 98% of a typical frame. One figure type per section, each at about 0.25–0.5 of frame width: rod, ring, ring with 8 rays, striped rod (baton), four triangles in a square, square frames that leave trails, cubes, a ladder with a ball, a string-covered ball. The measured pattern is snap and hold. From 0:22.4 to 0:24.8 the image holds for 0.50, 0.47, 0.57 and 0.57 s with a change in 1–4 frames between (held frames are 14% of the whole clip). The rod swings like a needle for 1.0–1.3 s, then three rods lie crossed and dead still for 0.8 s. The ring jumps between three tilts with no in-between, then eight rays snap on around it in one frame (0:24.7), hold 0.8 s, and only then tip in parallel as one body through five angles of about 0.2 s each. The baton appears at 0:26.7, 60 ms before the strongest onset of the passage (26.76). Motion peaks within 40 ms of an onset: 30% against 20% by chance (2.5 standard deviations), the clearest sync of the black-and-white films. The music's fastest pulse is 0.267 s; three of those make the 0.80 s waltz bar. Middle section (0:35–0:52): motion energy doubles (6–9% against 2–4%) as trails of 4–6 copies fill the frame, then drops back to single cubes. The whole film runs figure, multiplied figure, figure.

**Parabola.** One curve, cut in sheet and wire, repeated into fans, shells, fountains and cones: 10 to 25 copies in a stack, each rotated a fixed few degrees. Ground `#020202`, 78% of the frame; lit edges up to `#C3C3C3` (L 79, 2.8%). Sculpture area at the start of the first section is 8–13% of the frame, later 15–25%. Three light set-ups recur: rim light on black (only edges seen), a soft oval spot on the back wall with the sculpture in front as a silhouette, and a cast shadow of the fan behind the fan. The movement is mostly rotation and light travel. At 4:18.4–4:22 the sculpture does not move at all while the lit ribs fill in, from 6 to all 12, and drain again: three passes in 3.6 s, about 1.2 s each. At 1:40–1:53 a fan closes to a single edge and reopens, and the shadow behind it opens as the solid closes (7 s out, 7 s back, the same path reversed, with a dwell at the turn of about 1 s). Speeds are low: motion energy in 5 s blocks is 2–4% except one loud block at 3:25 (8.5%). Luma in 10 s blocks moves 4–28%. Hard changes 98 in 439 s, but many come in pairs inside fast light moves; the longest unbroken passage is 40 s. Slow envelopes agree with the music: correlation of the 2 s smoothed sound flux and motion is 0.48 at zero lag, 0.51 with the sound one second later, the highest of the long films; fast sync is at chance (27% against 24%). So the loud blocks move more; the beats are not hit. CVM disputes the credit: Boyd's sculpture is the subject, and CVM calls his co-director credit false.

**Escape.** The plot is geometric. A red outline triangle is shut behind black lines over drifting blue light, and at the end the lines go away. Ground: blues `#001774`, `#23407B`, `#004298` (L 17–30, C 38–60), 55–85% of the frame; lines `#020C00` (L 2), 8–27%; triangle `#852D20`–`#B8452A`-range, 2% as outline. The introduction is a list of births, each a straight line in time. A vertical bar grows up from the bottom at 0.38 frame-height/s (0:55.6–0:57.2). It then widens from 0.03 to 0.92 of the frame width in 0.9 s at a constant rate (about 1 frame-width/s) and turns the frame black. The black opens as 2, then 4, then 8 slots, a new outer pair about every 0.4 s, each slot 0.1 wide and growing in height, so the profile at any instant is a symmetric staircase. Horizontal bars then arrive one by one, from the bottom up, against the four notes of the descending phrase: onsets at 65.00, 65.62, 66.30 and 67.10 s; full-width hairlines appear at 64.8, 65.3, 66.0 and 66.8 and reach full thickness one tenth of a second later. The image leads the sound by 100–300 ms each time, and the direction is the opposite of the pitch. The bars stay. The main section (1:35–1:47) repeats a fixed cycle. A triangle strikes a trail of 10–18 copies along a diagonal, then along the other diagonal, then straight up, then a single triangle zooms towards the viewer, while the grid behind rotates. Trail passes last 1.0–1.5 s, the leading edge moving 0.6 frame-height/s at constant speed; each ends by a 1-frame reset (jumps at 95.47, 96.97, 101.07, 102.71). The zoom grows from 0.24 to 0.59 of the frame width in 1.7 s, about 5.5% per 0.1 s: a constant ratio, so it accelerates in size. The second time the same zoom takes 2.4 s (about 4% per 0.1 s), a stretched repeat. In the late section (3:05–3:19) the triangle spins one third of a turn per 0.4 s and shrinks from 0.65 to 0.23 of the frame width along a spiral in 4.4 s; the bars bend into arcs that slide through each other in 8 s, then snap to a spiral with a 0.3 s dissolve. Two motions with two tempos share the frame: slow lines, pulsing triangle. Events within 40 ms: 25% against 23%, at chance; only within 80 ms is there a small excess (49% against 42%). Silences of the toccata (1:01.8–1:02.6, 1:04.6–1:05.0, 1:09.6–1:10) are not black: the light keeps drifting and lines fade.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **The score comes first and is converted to a plan.** Her stated method is to plot the music and use it as counterpart or springboard (Schillinger studies, the Polka Graph chart). Visible results: Spook Sport's 12 wedges for 12 strokes, Escape's 4 bars for 4 notes, Tarantella's seat per pitch, Color Rhapsodie's 15-frame cell.
- **One event, one figure, and only that.** In Tarantella the disc exists for the note and is gone in 0.10–0.20 s; in Escape each note leaves one bar; in Spook Sport each chime leaves one wedge. Two readings of one idea: the note that vanishes and the note that stays.
- **Accumulate or flash, never both for the same voice.** Staircase (Synchromy No. 2), bars (Escape), wedges (Spook Sport) add and stay; discs, zigzags and square (Tarantella), red flash (Color Rhapsodie) come and go. Sustained music builds; struck music flashes.
- **A still carrier and a moving accent.** Red oval with zigzags, red line with discs, magenta band with square, eye opening with hooks, grid with triangle. The carrier takes 30–99% of the frame and hardly moves; the accent is 0.5–6%.
- **The same thing multiplied, then reduced to one.** Rhythm in Light rings (4–6 copies, stack, one ring, eight), Escape's triangle trail then one triangle, Dada's rod to rays and back, Parabola's 10–25 ribs closing to a single edge.
- **The system is a position rule.** Trails step along a fixed axis (diagonal, other diagonal, vertical) in Escape; seats alternate in Tarantella; stairs step by equal rise and drift to the right in Synchromy No. 2; fans repeat a curve by a fixed angle in Parabola. Only count, axis, size and light vary.
- **Constant speed entrances.** Pyramids 0.28–0.31 frame-height/s, Escape's bar 0.38, trails 0.6, black wipe 1.0 frame-width/s: all straight lines in time. Acceleration appears only as constant-ratio scale (triangle zoom, Rhapsodie decay) and as shortened intervals.
- **Crescendo by peak or by rate, not by smoother motion.** Tarantella's square peaks 2.1, 3.2, 4.6, 6.1% at unchanged beat; discs halve their spacing; Rhapsodie peaks 33, 43, 76% and then cuts the cell from 15 to 8 to 4 frames; Synchromy's stair slows down instead as the aria broadens.
- **Two clocks in one frame.** Abstronic hook 0.2 s, ellipses 0.83 s; Escape slow lines against a pulsing triangle; Parabola object still, light moving; Mood Contrasts 3.5 s figure with 0.4–0.8 s petals.
- **Sync is spent, not spread.** Over 3-minute windows hits are near chance in Rhythm in Light, Synchromy No. 2, Parabola, Escape and Abstronic #2, modest in Spook Sport and clearly above in Dada, Tarantella, Color Rhapsodie and Abstronic #1. Where the music is percussive and simple she hits frames; she chose Copland and Gillis because their rhythm was simple and sharp, and judged a Bach arrangement too intricate.
- **Lead rather than lag.** Where an offset exists the image is early: 100–300 ms in Escape's bars, 30–120 ms in Spook Sport, 60 ms in Dada's baton, 170 ms for Rhapsodie's upbeat. No film shows a steady image lag.
- **Light does the moving.** Parabola ribs fill and drain on a still sculpture; Escape's caustic light drifts through every silence; Synchromy's slabs fade in instead of sliding; Rhythm in Light defocuses to leave.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Mary Ellen Bute.** *List the events first; give each kind one plain mark at a fixed seat on a still carrier; a mark either stays (count) or flashes (acknowledge), never both; time to the frame only sparse, sharp events and carry the rest by slow light; raise emphasis by peak or rate; close on one held figure.*
Process:
1. Write the event list before any drawing. Name every kind of event the view will show, its expected rate, and whether it is struck (an instant: a press, a receipt, a refusal) or sustained (something that builds: steps done, items in). Keep 1–3 kinds per view. The list fixes every count that follows: n events of a kind get n marks, no more. If the list changes, every later step is redone.
2. Set the carrier. One field or one large plain form takes 30–99% of the view and does not move, reflow or scroll while events arrive; one ground value per section. All marks together stay small against it: 0.5–6% of the view for a single voice, up to about 20% when a build is complete.
3. Give each kind one plain mark and a seat rule. The mark's form never changes; only its count, size, position and light may. Position follows a rule set here: 2–4 fixed seats that alternate and carry the event's attribute (which source, which outcome), or equal steps along one fixed axis from an edge (each step about 0.05 of the view's height).
4. Decide for each kind: it stays or it flashes, never both. A sustained kind accumulates: each event adds one unit at the next seat, lit by a fade of 0.6–0.9 s, and the unit never moves again. A struck kind flashes: on in one frame, alive 100–200 ms, off in one frame, with the seat empty for at least 150 ms before it can fire again (in an interface, at least 400 ms between two flashes at one seat).
5. Spend exact timing; do not spread it. Only kinds that are sparse and sharp (gaps of 0.25 s or more) are timed to the event: the mark lands within 50 ms, and where there is an offset the mark is early (30–300 ms), never late. Every other stream gets no per-event mark: one slow property of the carrier (its light or its lit share) follows the level averaged over about 2 s.
6. Use three kinds of change and no others: a hard on or off in one frame; travel or growth at constant speed from an edge (0.3–1.0 of the view's dimension per second), with a reset in one frame; and a change of light on a thing that stays put (0.6–0.9 s to arrive, about 1.2 s to fill a set of 12). Scale grows or decays by a constant ratio (4–6% per 0.1 s up; a short hold, then about a fifth lost per 40 ms down). If a second clock is needed, put it on the carrier at about 4 times the mark's interval, linear both ways and at zero on each fourth beat; never a third.
7. Raise emphasis by peak or by rate, at an unchanged beat: each peak about 1.3–1.5 times the last in area, or the interval halved (1, 1/2, 1/4) for the close. To broaden instead, stretch the interval to about double across 8 steps. Never add smoothness or speed to the motion itself.
8. Compose density over the whole sequence. Inside a section it grows only by adding copies of the same mark, in a near straight line. At a section break it drops at once to one figure on the ground (under 2%). The fullest passage sits 60–90% of the way through and is followed by a short close on one figure, complete and held still 0.5–1.0 s. When nothing is happening, the last complete figure stays, still; the view is never blank and never idling.
Why it works:
- One event, one mark at a known seat makes the picture countable: the number of marks is the number of events, read without a numeral, and the seat says which kind before the form is resolved. A mark that both stays and flashes cannot be read as either a state or an event. Multiply the kinds and the seats stop being learnable within two or three repeats.
- Salience by oddness in time: on a carrier that does not move, a mark of half a percent alive for a tenth of a second is seen. Move the carrier and every mark has to grow to compete; equalise carrier and mark and the view becomes texture.
- A hard onset has an instant; an eased one has none, so it cannot be on time. Constant speed makes position a reading of elapsed time (halfway along is halfway through), which an ease hides. A mark that arrives with or just before its event is taken as the event itself; one that arrives after is read as lag.
- Adaptation: marks on every event become a steady flicker and coincide with the events no more often than chance. Exact hits read as caused only against passages that do not hit, and a peak reads as louder only against the previous peak on the same beat.
- After the fullest passage one small figure on an empty ground is the strongest contrast available, so the close needs no added effect. A complete figure held still reads as "done"; a blank reads as "broken".
Colour relations: one ground at an end of the value scale, 70–99% of the view in sparse sections and changed only by a cut at a section break; at most 2–3 inks per section; the highest chroma is the smallest thing (the struck mark, 0.5–1%) and sits on the ground furthest from it in value; a carrier form may take about 30% in a strong hue if it never moves; third colours come only from the overlap of two inks already present; swapping ground and figure is one accent, used once in a sequence. Seat and form carry the meaning, colour only repeats it.
Space and line: three placements only: dead centre, growing from one edge, or stepping along one diagonal; a single figure 0.25–0.5 of the view's width, copies 0.05–0.15; accumulated units of equal rise with a constant sideways offset; 10–25 copies when a figure is multiplied, then back to one; one line weight per view; mirror symmetry for the full ensemble only, asymmetry for a single voice; depth by overlap and stepped size.
Type requirement: words are events of the staying kind. A label appears at its seat with its event, on in one step, and remains; text never flashes and never travels. Before anything moves, a legend introduces each mark once with its meaning. One weight; counts in tabular figures that change by whole steps, never rolling.
Motion and interaction: struck feedback on within 50 ms of the input, alive 100–200 ms, at most one flash per seat per 400 ms and never above 3 per second in the view, on marks under 6% of the view; staying units fade in over 600–900 ms, linear; entrances at constant speed (a full-height travel in 1–3 s); a hold of 500–1000 ms on every completed figure; linear, step or constant-ratio only, no ease-in-out, no overshoot; a section break is a cut. The slow clock runs only while work runs and stops with it. Where a result is not yet confirmed, the flash may lead (it answers the input) and the staying unit waits for the confirmation. Reduced motion: a struck mark appears at its seat in one step and stays until the next event of its kind replaces it; staying units appear whole; travel becomes placement at the end position; the slow clock stops at its mean; peaks keep their sizes. Count, seat, order and the held close are unchanged.
Fails when: events come faster than about 3 a second or cannot be counted (a log tail, tick-rate prices), where only the slow level is left; nothing discrete happens (long reading, a form being filled), where nothing should move; more than 3 kinds must be shown at once; the magnitude of each event must be read exactly (money, health), which a mark cannot carry without a figure beside it; the act is irreversible and a leading mark would claim a result not yet true; the view is too small to keep a still carrier around the marks.
Signature (do not carry together): lit rings, stairs and pyramids on a black void · a red outline triangle behind a black grid on drifting blue · oscilloscope ribbons timed to a well-known piece of music.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Bute's procedure → a packing-station tablet in a warehouse (landscape, 1024 × 768), pale grey ground, one brand orange, a plain sans. No black, no sound, nothing that dances.
1. *Event list.* Three kinds. Scan accepted: struck, set by the packer's hand, about one every 1.5–2 s. Scan refused: struck, rare. Order line complete: sustained, exactly 12 in this order. Network packets arrive many times a second and are not on the list.
2. *Carrier.* The order panel, 640 × 768 (62.5% of the view), holds 12 rows of 64px and does not scroll, reorder or reflow while the order is packed. The right column (384px) is empty ground.
3. *Marks and seats.* A completed line is one slab, 64 × 56px (0.46% of the view), at the left end of its row; rows fill from the bottom edge upward, one row (0.083 of the height) per step. A scan is one disc, 72px across (0.52%), in the right column: the upper seat for accepted, the lower seat for refused, where it is a bar of the same area.
4. *Stay or flash.* Slabs stay: each fades from the ground value to its own in 700ms, linear, and never moves; 12 slabs are 5.5% of the view. Discs flash: on in one frame, 150ms, off; a seat cannot fire again for 400ms, and scans that arrive faster only step the count.
5. *Exact timing.* The disc fires within 50ms of the scanner's local decode, ahead of the stock server's answer; the slab waits for that answer. Packets get no mark: a hairline under the right column takes the upload level averaged over 2 s.
6. *Change.* Nothing slides. The only travel is the hairline, at constant speed. The refused bar comes with a text line beside its seat that stays until dismissed.
7. *Emphasis.* For the last three lines the disc is 72, 88 and 106px across (area × 1.5 each time) on the same 150ms life. No other change marks the approach to the end.
8. *Density and close.* Slabs build in a straight line from 0.5% to 5.5%. When the twelfth lands, the full stack holds still for 800ms, then the view cuts to one mark alone on the ground: the box label, 240 × 96px (2.9%), still until the next order is opened.
- *Colour.* Ground about 92% at the light end; slabs a neutral two value steps darker; the orange only on the disc, the smallest thing and the highest chroma; the refused bar in the platform's error colour, with its label.
- *Type.* One weight. Each row's quantity is a tabular figure that steps by whole units. A three-line legend (slab, disc, bar) shows before the first scan.
- *Reduced motion.* The disc appears in its seat and stays until the next scan replaces it; slabs appear whole; the hairline moves in steps of a tenth. The count, the seats and the held close are the same.
Result: a pale, quiet packing screen that is still most of the time. No void, no triangle, no ribbon, no music. What survived is the event list, one mark per event at a fixed seat, the split between what stays and what flashes, exact timing spent on the scan alone, emphasis by peak, and the held close on one figure.

## Space & composition
- Figures sit in three places: dead centre (ring, triangle, disc, eye), growing from the bottom edge (pyramids, stairs, bar, fountain), or along one diagonal (Escape trails, Tarantella band and wedges).
- Empty ground dominates: figure area 0.5–2% (Dada, Tarantella trill, Spook Sport midnight), 2–6% (Abstronic lines), 8–25% (Parabola), and only brief full-frame events (white blur in Rhythm in Light, colour floods in Tarantella and Rhapsodie).
- Depth is made by overlap and size in steps, not perspective drawing, except in Rhapsodie's corridor, Abstronic's road of tiles and Mood Contrasts' chequerboard, each a one-point view with the figure at the vanishing point.
- Containers: oval spot (Parabola, Tarantella), eye shape (opening titles, Abstronic), grid (Escape). The eye with a soundtrack stripe for a pupil is the series emblem of "Seeing Sound".
- Mirror symmetry is used for tutti (Synchromy No. 2, Spook Sport green dance, Mood Contrasts); asymmetry for single voices.

## Form, line & typography
- The vocabulary is small and named: ring, rod, pyramid, stair, parabola, triangle, grid, disc, zigzag, wedge, sine. One film takes one to three.
- Line weight is constant inside a film (Escape's bars, Tarantella's trill line), or swells along its length (Spook Sport, Tarantella's zigzags, drawn with thick and thin ends). Oscilloscope lines are doubled by a pale edge.
- Titles are part of the films. Thin, geometric capitals with staggered indents in Rhythm in Light (1934); rounded, inline display caps in Escape, orange on blue, letters drifting in; hand-lettered red and green in Spook Sport, the first letter in the second colour; "The End" in script. Early titles explain: the film is "a pictorial accompaniment in abstract forms".
- Words are timed: Tarantella's title is cut by two blue blades, and a dictionary page picks out in white the words that define the dance.

**Cuts.** Median gap between hard changes: 0.8 s (Rhythm in Light), 0.87 (Tarantella), 1.9 (Parabola), 2.3 (Spook Sport), 3.4 (Synchromy No. 2), 5.2 (Escape). Flash films cut fast; light films dissolve. In Tarantella most of the changes are figure flashes on a held ground, so a true shot lasts 7–30 s (about 5 large cuts per minute, measure_time.py: 16 in 180 s).

**Stillness and black.** Held frames: Tarantella 24%, Synchromy No. 2 15%, Spook Sport 11%, Dada 14%, others 4–5%. Dark frames (under 6% luma): Spook Sport 68%, Synchromy No. 2 33%, Parabola 23%, the rest 9–11%. Stillness is a figure held whole (ring, rays, oval), rarely a black gap.

**Density.** Grows by adding copies and voices inside one scene (Synchromy area 1 to 18% in 16 s; Rhapsodie peak 33 to 76%; Dada motion 3 to 9%), and drops at once at a section break to one figure on black. The loudest part of a film is placed 60–90% of the way through and followed by a short single-figure close: Spook Sport's motion peak at about 6:55–7:05 of 7:54, Rhythm in Light's two peaks at 3:25 and in the last 10 s (cellophane and sparklers) before the end title.

## UI translations
1. **Feedback:** Answer a discrete input with one small mark (a disc 0.09 of the width, `#006BA7` on `#070516`, under 1% of the view) that is on within 50ms, lives 100–200ms and goes off hard. Do not fade it and do not let it settle.
2. **Feedback:** Give the mark two fixed seats and alternate them, or one seat per outcome, as Tarantella seats its disc by pitch. The place tells the kind before the shape is read.
3. **Progress:** Build a count as a stack: one slab per completed step, 0.055 of the height, lit by a 0.6–0.9 s fade and never moved again (Synchromy No. 2's stair, Spook Sport's 12 wedges for 12 strokes). No bar that slides, no percentage that rolls.
4. **Progress:** Let a longer process broaden: intervals of 1.2 s at the start may stretch to 2.7 s near the end without any change of animation.
5. **Loading:** Keep the object still and move the light: fill a fixed set of 12 ribs from 6 to 12 and drain it, about 1.2 s per pass (Parabola), at most while work runs. Under reduced motion show the set half lit and still.
6. **Transitions:** Enter from the bottom edge at constant speed (0.3–0.4 of the height per second) or wipe at 1.0 of the width per second; leave by a cut, a fade of 0.7 s, or a one-frame reset. No ease-in-out and no overshoot.
7. **Transitions:** Change section by a cut, with one ground colour per section. Between sections that share a ground, dissolve.
8. **State change:** Snap between 3–5 prepared poses and hold each 0.5 s (Dada); do not tween between states that have no meaningful in-between.
9. **Data in motion:** For a busy stream draw no per-event mark. Drive the carrier's lit share from the level smoothed over 2 s (Parabola's envelope, r 0.48) and keep exact marks for the few sharp events.
10. **Data in motion:** Two clocks at most: a mark that changes every 0.2 s over a ground that swells and empties every 0.83 s, linear, reaching zero on each fourth beat (Abstronic).
11. **Emphasis:** Make the next peak bigger, not faster: 2.1, 3.2, 4.6, 6.1% of the view on an unchanged beat (Tarantella's square); for a finale halve the cell, 15 to 8 to 4 frames (Color Rhapsodie).
12. **Stillness:** After a group of events hold the complete figure for 1.0 s with nothing moving (the lone ring, 0.45 of the width, in Rhythm in Light). An idle screen shows that figure, not a blank and not an idle loop.
13. **Colour:** Black or one flat ground at 70–99%; two inks (`#A40104`, `#089752`) and the cream where they overlap; the struck mark is the highest chroma and the smallest area.
14. **Onboarding:** List the cast before the action: each mark shown once with its name and colour, then the place and the time (Spook Sport's opening).
15. **Accessibility:** The measured zigzag flashes run at about 3.3 per second on 30% of the frame; do not copy that rate. Keep flashes at or under 3 per second, on marks under 6% of the view, and give every flash a staying form under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## What this artist would reject
- Motion that follows every beat equally: her films are mostly at chance, and the hits come in blocks.
- Eased slide-ins and settling overshoots: entrances are constant speed, hard on and off, or a light fade.
- Several unrelated figure types in one scene: each section has one, with a carrier.
- Spectacle that replaces the figure: she called color-organ results amorphous and wanted controlled drawing.
- Figures that imitate nature: her 1956 text puts direct sensation ahead of story and imitation (Escape and Spook Sport, with their small plots, are exceptions she named).
- Sound added afterwards: the music is chosen first, known to the audience, and short.
- Slow dissolves between sections where the music cuts; a constant, decorative background movement with no job.
