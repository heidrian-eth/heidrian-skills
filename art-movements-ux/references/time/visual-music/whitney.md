# John Whitney — Visual Music

## Essence
Whitney gives a set of identical elements one rule of motion and a different whole-number rate each (1, 2, 3 … n), then lets the set run. The picture scatters into disorder and falls back into order each time the rates line up at a simple fraction, so tension and release come from the arithmetic and not from drawn events. The rule stays visible: one start, one path, steady speed, sine turns, and a short moment of resolution between long stretches of drift.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **One rule, n rates** (Arabesque 360 points at rates 1–360; Permutations 281; Matrix 24 squares on one path; Catalog 16 copies; Matrix III 16–24 outlines). Every element obeys the same path and direction; only its number changes. Nothing drifts.
- **Order is brief and scatter is long** (Permutations 22 s of scatter, then 2.4 s to firm into a rose; Arabesque orders of 0.06–0.13 s in the fast first loop, 1–3.5 s at the slowed thirds; Permutations' later flower-and-star passages about 2 s in every 6.25).
- **Order falls on simple fractions of one long period** (Arabesque section four: thirds at 13.0 and 26.6 s, fifths at 8 s, quarters at 10 and 20 s of 40; section three: five ellipses at each fifth; Whitney describes nodes at eighths, quarters and halves).
- **Constant speed on a path, sine at its ends** (Matrix I 0.31 width per second constant; Permutations ring 0.218 height per second; Catalog rings sine, 0.12 height per second through centre; Arabesque ellipse eases out to rest). Nothing accelerates except the Vertigo figures, whose doubling time shortens from 4 s to 2 s.
- **Loops of fixed length** (Film 1 3.29 s; Experiments 2.0 s; Arabesque 4.00 s; Catalog dot arms 3.8 s as 1.5 + 2.3; Permutations ring colour about 4.2 s). Repeats are identical; change comes from superposition or from a hue cycle of another length (Arabesque 12 s of hue over 4 s of shape).
- **A canon of entries at a fixed step** (Arabesque 1.33 s, six entries; Matrix I 0.25 s behind per square; Vertigo figures handed over on about a 10–15 s stagger, 1 s overlap; film note: a 15 s sequence begun every 5 s in Film 4).
- **A new figure is born in the hole of the old** (Vertigo: eight figures; Permutations single point; Catalog ring inner hole; Arabesque circle redrawn at half size after the fade). No figure is wiped; it is passed or grown past the edge.
- **Symmetry is a mirror of the same motion** (Arabesque top and bottom borders, X and Y flips; Matrix I path; Film 4 pairs; Catalog 16-fold).
- **Colour moves slowly or snaps, never both at once** (Arabesque hue wheel across 4 s; Matrix ground fades of 1–2 s; Permutations ring swaps white/red hard every 4.2 s; Catalog strobe at 0.167 s is the one exception). Figure hue is single per layer.
- **Sound shares the grid, seldom the hit** (Film 1 and 4: a common 0.82 / 0.41 / 0.62 s unit, with lags of −200 to +440 ms to onsets; Arabesque's first opening lands on a pluck; otherwise Arabesque and Vertigo: structure only; Permutations, Matrix III, Experiments: none).
- **Black is the stage** (Arabesque 100% dark frames, lit area 1–4%; Permutations 7%, Matrix I 10%, Film 1 23%, Film 4 28% fully black; Catalog 1%).
- **The generative pattern seen:** choose a small unit and a path; number the copies; advance each at its number; watch for the fractions where they fall into order; hold or slow a little there; start a second copy a fixed step later; let the loop end on its own start.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**John Whitney.** *Give every member of a set the same rule of motion and a whole-number rate of its own on one shared period, so that order is the brief moment when the rates agree at a simple fraction: drift between, slow and dwell there, enter copies at a fixed step, and start each new thing in the gap the last one leaves.*
Process:
1. Fix the set and its one rule. Take 16–360 identical members (below about 12 nothing can resolve) and give them one start, one path and one direction. A member has a number, its rank in the set, and nothing else of its own: no hand placement, no private easing, no drift outside the rule. If the rule changes, every later step is redone.
2. Number the rates on one period. Fix one long period (24–40s when it plays by itself) and let member k complete k laps of the path in it, or, as a chain, follow the leader k fixed steps behind (0.25s each, about 0.08 of the view apart). Rates are whole numbers only. The whole state is then a function of one number, the phase, which a clock, a real process or the user's hand can drive forward or back.
3. Choose the fractions before drawing anything. At a half, a third, a quarter or a fifth of the period the members whose rates share that divisor coincide, and the set shows as many groups as the denominator (three at each third, five at each fifth; rates of 3 : 4 : 5 resolve in turn inside one period). Decide which fractions the view will use and what each one means. A fraction with no meaning is passed without a stop.
4. Proportion order to drift. Order takes a tenth to a third of the time and drift takes the rest. Left alone an order lasts 60–130ms, too short to read, so slow the phase toward each chosen fraction and dwell there for 1–3.5s, nearly still for 1s of it; an order may take up to 2.4s to firm. The first order is given away at the start: the aligned set, small and still, for at least 0.5s before it opens.
5. Fix the speed laws, one per kind of motion. Along a path: constant speed (0.2–0.3 of the view per second for the fastest member). Where a motion reverses: sine, fastest in the middle and nought at each end. Settling to rest: ease out over about 0.6s, each step half to two thirds of the last. A figure that leaves by growing: each doubling quicker than the last. Nothing eases in and out along a path.
6. Loop exactly and vary by phase. A short loop (2–4s) repeats to the frame. Change comes from a second cycle of another length laid over it (one colour cycle to three shape loops), never from editing a repeat. One property is in transition at a time: colour either slides over a whole loop or a 1–2s fade, or it snaps while the shape moves on, and a snap recurs no more than once a second.
7. Add voices as copies. A second, third, up to sixth voice is the same motion entered a fixed step later, the step a simple fraction of the loop (a third: 1.33s in 4s). Each voice takes one hue and sits slightly off the others' centre. An answer is the mirror of the motion, never a new motion.
8. Succeed without a cut. The next figure starts small (0.07–0.2 of the view's height) in the gap at the centre of the one that is leaving; the two overlap for about a tenth of the shorter life; the old one leaves by growing past the edge or closing to a point. The edge is a seam: what leaves one side enters the opposite side.
9. Share the outer clock's grid, not its hits. Where another process runs beside the picture (a score, a refresh cycle, a job), give the loop that process's period or a 3 : 2 division of it. Let single moments coincide only at the opening and at section marks, within 30–170ms of the cue. Between them the amount of motion follows the amount of activity over seconds, not each event. Blanks between sections shorten (to about a half, then level) while the outer process runs on.
10. Write the still form: the set at each chosen fraction, drawn as its groups, with the fraction stated in figures. If the view says nothing when shown only at its fractions, the fractions were chosen wrongly (step 3).
Why it works:
- Members that share a position or a direction are grouped as one (proximity, common fate). With whole-number rates the groups form and dissolve by themselves, so an event appears that nobody drew and the eye reads it as the set agreeing. Equalise the rates and the set is one rigid object with no event. Take the rates off whole numbers and it never returns: noise.
- The long drift sets what the eye expects (adaptation), so order is salient by oddness. Keep order on screen all the time and it is a static ornament; multiply mirrors and it is a kaleidoscope. Remove the dwell and order passes under the threshold of reading.
- Constant speed is read as a law, so attention goes to the relations between members and not to each one's start and stop. A sine at a reversal is what steady turning looks like from the side, so the motion reads as continuing, not as braking.
- An exact repeat lets the viewer predict; a second cycle of unequal length makes every repeat differ in one property only, which is then read as the content.
- Copies entered at a fixed step show one motion at several ages at once, so the viewer sees where each will be next: time laid out as space.
- The eye already rests in the gap at the centre of the leaving figure, so a birth there needs no search, and the overlap keeps one continuous object (persistence). A cut would force the viewer to find the subject again.
- A shared period binds two streams into one tempo without claiming that each mark is caused by each event. A hit on every event claims a cause, and the claim fails the first time one is late.
Colour relations: one ground, a dark value or one flat hue per section, on 80–97% of the view; lines and points light 1–8% of it; the figure has the highest chroma and the ground almost none; one saturated hue per layer, and several hues at once (up to six) only as one per voice; hue belongs to time, either travelling round the wheel on a cycle three times the shape loop or fixed for a whole section; hues of line work stay apart where they cross, and only solid translucent fields mix; a ground change is one fade of 1–2s at a section mark.
Space and line: one centred field about three quarters of the view's short side with a gap of 0.05–0.4 at its centre, or fixed bands (about a quarter of the width each, gaps of 0.06) whose content moves while the band stays; members are the smallest mark that can be seen (a point, a hairline, a thin outline) at one constant weight, so density comes from count and from convergence, never from thickness; size is graded by position on the path only (up to 1 : 8); symmetry is a mirror of the same motion; the edge wraps.
Type requirement: text is not a member of the set. It stands still at a fixed edge in one weight while the set passes beside or behind it, and it never rides a moving member. Rank and fraction are stated in figures ("3 of 5", "week 6 of 12"), so the family needs tabular figures and true fractions or a clear slash. Lettering may join the set only as a title mark that nobody has to read.
Motion and interaction: long period 24–40s; loop 2–4s, exact; entry step a third of the loop or 0.25s along a chain; dwell 1–3.5s; opening hold 0.5s or more; settle 0.6s ease-out; path motion linear, reversals on a sine; ground fade 1–2s; overlap of old and new about a tenth of the shorter life. The phase is the one control: bind it to real progress, to scroll or to a drag, and the set runs forward and back without new animation. Short loops run only while a process runs or the user drives them; anything idle moves on the long period (30s or more) or not at all. A snap happens at most once a second and a whole-ground snap marks a section only; no strobing, no full-field flashes. Reduced motion: show the set only at its chosen fractions, stepping from one order to the next in a single change with the fraction in figures; voices become still copies set side by side at their step; drift is not shown. Order, fractions, voices and succession all remain.
Fails when: the set has fewer than about 12 members, or they are unequal, or their rank means nothing, so the rates are arbitrary; no period exists (an open-ended wait has no fractions to resolve at); the user must read or press the members while they move; the state must be legible at every instant (monitoring, tables under comparison), since drift says nothing; each event needs its own confirmation, which a shared grid does not give; long reading, where nothing should move.
Signature (do not carry together): fine points or hairlines blooming into rosettes and looped curves at the centre of a black field · one saturated hue per layer turning through the spectrum · a droning or plucked score running under the pattern.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Maintenance planner for one machine, phone 390 × 844, warm paper ground, one brand ink, a serif for headings, no sound, nothing radial (the films are black, centred, abstract and set to music). Sixteen service tasks recur every 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 or 12 weeks, all counted from the day the machine was commissioned.
1. *Set and rule.* Sixteen rows of 40px under a 96px header. Each row has a still label (132px) and a 210px hairline track with one 6px marker. The rule for every marker: travel left to right across its track once per interval. All sixteen start at the left end.
2. *Rates and period.* The period is the 12-week cycle. A weekly task laps 12 times in it, a fortnightly one 6, then 4, 3, 2 and 1 (four, four, two, three, two and one tasks; rows sorted by rate). The phase is the week number, driven by a slider or by today's date.
3. *Fractions.* Thirds (weeks 4 and 8): rates 12, 6 and 3 coincide, 11 of 16 tasks due. Half (week 6): rates 12, 6, 4 and 2, 12 of 16 due. Whole (week 12): all 16. Each is a service day. Quarters (weeks 3 and 9) bring only 6 and are passed without a stop.
4. *Order and drift.* "Preview cycle" plays a week in 2.0s, 24s in all, and dwells 1.5s at weeks 4 and 8, 2.5s at week 6 and 3.5s at week 12: 9s of order in 33s (27%). Before it starts the sixteen markers stand in one column at the left for 0.5s.
5. *Speed laws.* Every marker moves at constant speed: the weekly ones 105px a second (0.27 of the width), the 12-week one under 9px a second. Into a dwell the phase slows over 0.6s, each step about 0.6 of the last. Nothing reverses on this screen, so no sine is used.
6. *Loop and phase.* A week is one exact 2.0s loop of the top four rows. At a dwell the labels of the due rows snap from regular to medium weight while the markers are still, and snap back when the phase moves on; weight never changes while a marker travels.
7. *Voices.* A second machine, commissioned four weeks later (a third of the cycle), is the same motion entered one step late: a second marker in each row, 4px lower, in its own hue.
8. *Succession.* Tapping a row opens the task sheet from the marker's own 6px spot; it grows over 400ms past the edges of the list, which stays visible for the first 40ms. A marker that reaches the right end re-enters at the left.
9. *Outer clock.* The preview starts on the press. After that it keeps its own 2.0s grid and does not answer each task with a tick; live, a marker moves only as the calendar does.
10. *Still form.* With reduced motion the preview is four still frames (weeks 4, 6, 8 and 12) stepped by a Next button; the due markers stand in a column at the right end and the header reads "Week 6 of 12 · 12 of 16 due". The slider moves a week at a time.
- *Colour.* Paper keeps about 98% of the screen; tracks and markers take about 1%; the marker carries the brand ink at full chroma, the second machine one other hue, and nothing else is coloured.
- *Type.* Labels in one weight at the left edge; week and counts in tabular figures.

Result: a quiet paper-coloured table whose dots fall into one column on service days and scatter between them. No black field, no rosette, no spectrum, nothing to hear. What survived is one rule with whole-number rates, order at chosen fractions with a dwell, one voice entered a third of the period late, and a still form made of the fractions alone.

## Space & composition
- Centred radial fields of 0.75 of the frame height (Permutations, Catalog, Vertigo rosettes) with a hole of 0.05–0.4 at the centre; the hole is where the next figure starts.
- Off-centre cause: Matrix I and Arabesque put motion on a path that leaves and re-enters the frame, wrapping left edge to right (modulo), so the frame edge is a seam and not a wall.
- Vertical stacking with translation: Arabesque's later copies are displaced slightly above or below; canon layers never share a centre exactly.
- Arabesque columns: three bands of about 0.25 of the width each with black gaps of 0.06 between; the band does not move, its content does.
- Type in Vertigo holds the right or the two edges; figures pass behind it. Catalog and Permutations use captions only in title cards.
- Ground colour as a scene: Matrix I keeps one flat ground per section (blue, green, brown, red, violet, navy), 90–97% of the frame once its shading clusters are added, with figures as 1–3 hue outlines.
- Figure-to-ground share: lit line area 1–8% (Arabesque, Permutations, Matrix III), 7–25% for solid Catalog shapes, up to 80% for the strobe and veil fields.

## Form, line & typography
- Elements are the smallest possible: dots (Permutations, Arabesque, Catalog arms), thin closed outlines (Matrix I, III), straight rods left as ghosts (Experiments), bare dots joined into curves by the eye (Arabesque at fractions).
- Line weight is constant, about 1–2 px in 480 lines; density is made by count, not thickness. In Vertigo the strands crowd at the spiral's edge, so bright areas are where lines converge.
- Shapes follow the rule: triangle, square, hexagon, circle, all equal within a set and graded only in size (Matrix I 0.05–0.4 of the width by position).
- Solid, soft-edged cutouts appear only in the Film Exercises, and later veils in Catalog.
- Type: Vertigo outlined slab-serif capitals in warm grey, widely spaced; Catalog's own title is a heap of overprinted letters used as artwork; Matrix III title in outline slab capitals overprinted on a triangle; Permutations end card in spaced slab capitals, white on black. Titles are type with the same rotation or multiplication as the figures where they are used as artwork, else static.

## UI translations
1. **Data in motion:** Give every item in a set the same path and item k the rate k on one period (*Arabesque*: 360 points at rates 1–360); alignment at a half, third or quarter of the period is then the event, with no extra animation.
2. **Progress:** Show a known wait as a set resolving at fractions: three groups at each third, five at each fifth of a 40s period, each readable for 1–3.5s (*Arabesque* section four), with the fraction also in figures.
3. **Loading:** For a wait with a known period, loop exactly at 2.0s or 4.0s (*Experiments in Motion Graphics*, *Arabesque*) and change one thing on a longer cycle: hue once round every three loops (12s over 4s).
4. **Easing:** Linear along a path (*Matrix I*: 0.31 of the width per second, the same on every bend); sine where a value turns back (*Catalog* rings: fastest at centre, nought at each end); ease-out over 0.6s only when something comes to rest (*Arabesque* ellipse). Never `ease-in-out` on a path.
5. **Stagger:** Enter list items as a chain on one path, each 0.25s and 0.08 of the width behind the last (*Matrix I*), or as a canon at a third of the loop (1.33s in 4s, six entries, *Arabesque*); equal steps, not a curve.
6. **Transition:** Start the incoming view small, 0.07–0.2 of the height, in the centre gap of the outgoing one, overlap the two briefly and let the old one grow past the edge (*Vertigo* titles: eight figures, no cut in 105s).
7. **Emphasis by timing:** Let scatter run long and order briefly: about 22s of drift, 2.4s to firm (*Permutations*). In a product, shorten both and keep the proportion at a tenth to a third.
8. **Opening:** Hold the aligned set small and still for at least 0.5s, then open it (*Arabesque* circle before the first pluck).
9. **Colour change:** Either a slow slide (ground fade of 1–2s between sections, *Matrix I*) or a hard swap on the loop boundary (ring white to red every 4.2s, *Permutations*); not both on one element at once.
10. **Layers:** One hue per layer on a ground that keeps 80–97% of the view, lines at 1–8%: `#D2000A` points on `#000000` (*Arabesque*), `#B3DEC7` lines on `#0B5346` (*Experiments*).
11. **Scrubbing:** Bind the phase to scroll or drag, so the whole set runs forward and back from one number; the 281 points of *Permutations* differ only in radius and rate.
12. **Rhythm with a background process:** Match the loop to the process's period (*Film Exercise 1*: 3.29s picture and sound loop) or divide one bar by 3 against 2 (*Film Exercise 4*: 0.41s against 0.62s); do not fire a mark on every event.
13. **Density:** Let the amount of motion follow the amount of activity over several seconds (*Arabesque*: loudest music with the densest picture, r 0.51), and drop it before each new section.
14. **Stillness:** Between sections show nothing while the process continues, and shorten the blank each time: 16.4s, 8.6s, 7.5s (*Film Exercise 1*).
15. **Wrap:** Treat the edge as a seam: what leaves on the right enters on the left (*Arabesque*, *Matrix I*), so a cycle needs no reset.
16. **Accessibility:** Do not carry the *Catalog* strobe (ground swapped every 0.167s, three colour cycles a second over most of the frame) or the *Film Exercises*' full-field flashes: they sit at or over the limit of 3 flashes a second (WCAG 2.3.1). Keep snaps to one a second and off the whole ground.
17. **Accessibility:** Under `prefers-reduced-motion` show only the ordered states, one step per fraction, with the fraction in figures; canon voices become still copies side by side. Short loops stop when the process stops; idle motion uses the 30s-or-longer period or none.
18. **Accessibility:** Never put a label or a control on a moving member; text holds an edge and the set passes behind it (*Vertigo* titles).

## What this artist would reject
- Motion that is only added to a picture: he calls such stasis an outmoded habit of static media.
- Elements placed by hand or drifting at random: every point obeys a rule and none moves aimlessly.
- Doubling of voices and symmetry for its own sake: he warns against kaleidoscope doubling.
- Assembling a static image piece by piece: he says it builds stasis.
- Music illustrated note by note with moving shapes: he sought structure common to both.
- A cut or edge as the source of surprise when a smooth resolve would do (the one jump in Arabesque he defends as a percussive exception).

## Signature prompt seed
Design the screen as a Whitney set. On a black ground (`#000000`) that keeps nine tenths of the view, place a few hundred identical points on one hidden path in a centred field three quarters of the height, with a gap at its centre. Give point k the rate k on one 40s period and move every point at constant speed, sine where it turns. Let the set scatter, then slow and dwell 1–3.5s where it resolves into three, four or five figures at the thirds, quarters and fifths. Loop shapes at exactly 4s and turn the single line hue (`#D2000A` onward round the wheel) once every 12s. Enter up to six copies 1.33s apart, each in its own hue. Start each new figure small in the gap of the old. Type stays still at one edge. No cuts, no strobe, no full-field flashes; with reduced motion show only the resolved figures and their fractions.
