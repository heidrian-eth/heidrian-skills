# Len Lye — Visual Music

## Essence
Lye makes motion directly by hand on the film strip: painted, combed, stamped, scratched or stencilled. A line is never still, because every frame is a new gesture over the same simple figure. One figure type and one ground are held for a short phrase, the figure is shaken or slid across the frame while everything else stays constant, and then the ground is swapped in a single frame. Image and music meet at phrase joins and rests and by instrument-to-shape pairing, with no measurable note-by-note lock; the steel sculptures do the same with a strip of metal, loading slowly, snapping, then ringing.

## Detailed observations

**Reliability first: what could not be measured.** The two public transfers of *A Colour Box* differ in speed (image offset between them shrinks from 1.96 s at 45 s to 0.75 s at 145 s). Their sound offsets shrink too, from about 2.2 s to about 1.0 s, but not by the same amount: sound sits 0.15–0.3 s differently against the picture in the two files. For *Free Radicals* the two files disagree by about 1.0–1.2 s in the first half and 0.6–0.7 s in the second. Frame-exact sync claims for these films therefore cannot be made from public copies. The tool's shot counts also fail on direct film: *A Colour Box* returns 511 "cuts" (median 0.16 s) in the sharp file and 7 in the blended official one, because nearly every frame differs. For the direct films I use phrases read from the sheets instead.

**A Colour Box.** The film is a series of passages of roughly 2–10 s, each with one way of marking. The titles last to about 28.5 s; abstract painting runs 28.5–124 s; stencilled lettering 124–158 s; a reprise and title to 181 s. Ribbon passage, 36–42 s: four white ribbons (each 4–8% of the width) on ultramarine, then one ribbon (13–15% of the width) on magenta. The ribbon enters at the right edge (x 0.97 at 38.20 s), travels left to x 0.46 by 38.64 s (about 1.2 widths/s, slowing at the end), then shakes: from 38.72 to 39.76 s its middle jumps between x 0.31 and 0.64 from one drawn frame to the next, bending as an S, a C or a straight bar. At 39.80 it disappears and only the magenta ground stays, 0.5 s, broken by a dark comb-scraped wedge that enters from the right for 3–5 frames. At 40.36 the ribbon comes back straight and glides from x 0.45 to 0.58 in 0.45 s (about 0.3 widths/s); it holds still at 0.57–0.58 until 40.92, then shakes again. So the same line has two states, glide and shake, with an empty ground between, about 0.6–1 s each. The ribbon width does not change while it moves. The line edges carry a darker red halo where wet dye pools, and fine streaks of the brush run inside the white. On the official copy the ribbon reads cream on brown-mauve, so hue is not trustworthy there.
Comb passage, 72–80 s: the figure class stays the same (7–25 parallel white lines, wavy or slanted, sometimes crossed into a net) while the ground is replaced by a single-frame switch: dark green, red at 72.80, blue 73.44, magenta 74.04, orange 74.64, red 75.24, lilac 75.72, black 76.08, red 76.80, blue 77.28, magenta 77.76, pale blue 78.84, then blue 79.28, red 79.44, black 79.68, white 79.92. The gaps are 0.64, 0.60, 0.60, 0.60, 0.48, 0.36, 0.72, 0.48, 0.48, 1.08, 0.44, 0.16, 0.24, 0.24 s: steady near 0.6 s, then crowding before the passage changes to blots. Adjacent grounds never share a hue. A dark round blob (half to two-thirds of the frame height) pops in for 2–6 frames at 74.28, 75.48, 77.28, 78.48, 79.56 s, and is gone. The sound sheet shows loud low notes (about 110 Hz) at 71.0, 72.1, 74.0, 75.35, 76.15, 78.25, 79.4 s. Four of the five blobs follow a low note by 0.1–0.3 s (median about 0.2 s); the blob at 77.28 has no low note and the note at 76.15 has no blob. The delay is the same size as the 0.15–0.3 s A/V disagreement between the two transfers, so a blob per low note in the original is plausible but not proven.
Lettering, 140–155 s: one price line on a plain warm pink, letter height 0.19–0.24 of the frame. "3 lbs." appears at 140.6 s, sits near x 0.16–0.23 for 0.8 s while creeping (0.1 widths/s), then runs out right at 0.6–1.5 widths/s. "for" enters from the right at about 0.5 widths/s and leaves left at 0.75–1.2. "6d." creeps from x 0.17 to 0.27 over 0.6 s, flipping colour from teal to magenta between 143.2 and 143.4, then runs out at 0.55–0.85 widths/s. The rule is dwell, then dart, and it accelerates on the way out only. Each price lives about 1.7–2.2 s; the next overlaps the old one by 0.4–0.6 s, and the two are in different colours so they can cross. Whether the words sit on the beat is not resolved. The film keeps the lowest motion of its length here (12–14/255 mean change per frame in 120–140 s, 4–10 in some blocks) against 35–48 in the painted middle, so it calms down while it must be read and wakes up again for the coda.
Colour: the sharp transfer is strongly boosted and cropped. Magenta ground `#BC0081` L 41 C 72; red `#B80020` L 39 C 74; blue `#16125E`–`#394CAC`; green `#00AA84` L 62 C 47; lilac `#8A7FD0`; yellow `#FECC56` L 84 C 63; lines `#EBF4FD`. The chroma of every ground lies between 45 and 90; white lines are 10–20% of the frame in ribbon passages, about 17–30% in comb passages; blob passages (80, 88.5 s) reverse it, white ground 39–79% with dark red or purple marks. The official copy shows the same shapes on cream and dull rose.

**Rainbow Dance.** Flat silhouettes cut from filmed action on painted or stencilled scenery, a figure of 0.5–0.65 of the frame height. From 19.7 to 26.2 s, while a hiker walks left across a hill landscape, the whole colour scheme of ground and figure swaps at 19.72, 20.88, 21.96, 23.00, 24.04, 25.12, 26.16 s: steps of 1.04–1.16 s, that is 26–29 frames, with the landscape outline fixed. Two ground schemes alternate (A dark green hills with grey-violet sky, B pale yellow map ground), and the figure takes red, yellow, blue, orange or black at its own 0.4–0.6 s changes inside each step. The sound in this edit has its own bass figure repeating every 1.30–1.32 s, so the 1.05 s clock does not divide it; the edit may not carry the original sound, so no sync verdict. A colour scheme in clip17 changes every 0.20 s from 23.96 to 28.4 s (22 successive cuts), so the clock can also run at 5 Hz.
Trail, 27.0–30.4 s: the walker leaps and a silhouette is left behind at each pose. Counts of copies: 1 (27.0), 3 (27.2), 4 (27.8), 5 (28.0–28.2), held complete for about 1 s. Copies are in separate flat colours (blue, yellow, dark blue, magenta, black) and overlap by a third to a half. Then they are taken away oldest first: the blue copy is gone by 29.4, the yellow by 29.6, the dark blue by 29.8, magenta by 30.2, one every 0.2–0.4 s, until only the live black figure remains. Growth takes about 1.2 s, hold 1 s, decay 0.9 s.
Colour: figures are single saturated flats on either scenic dark or near-white. Rainbow sector scene `#FFFBEE` 51%, blue `#0B3BBA` C 71 16%, pink `#FF3FC5` C 83 12%, red `#FF1D04` C 97 11%, yellow `#FFF428` C 79 4%. Trail scene: white 52%, sky `#73BED3` 15%, black 14%, blue 10%, yellow 5%, pink 3%. Sun scene: one hue `#0346D0` 54% with a red disc `#F20E00` 21% ringed in yellow-green. A transfer with black border; boosted.

**Trade Tattoo.** The film alternates photographed work, reduced to two-value silhouettes, with stencil fields. Median shot 0.56 s in the tool's reading, shots of 0.1–1.2 s in the sheet; 27% of listed shots are under 0.3 s. Sequence 68–76.5 s, read at 0.12 s: white bar stencils on black that swing from diagonal to chevron to opposite diagonal, changing every 0.12–0.36 s, for 2.4 s (68.0–70.4); then a man in cap waving, 1.3 s (70.5–71.8), the same green dash stencil printed only inside his dark coat, on and off every 0.24–0.36 s; bars again 0.6 s in light blue; a locomotive 1.0 s (72.4–73.4) against a flat yellow sky, the engine growing from about 0.25 of the frame height to past its top edge in 0.5 s; smoke 2 s; a plain rounded rectangle (about 0.5 of the width, 0.36 of the height) for 0.4–0.6 s at 73.8 and 75.6 s, white and almost still while the dot grid around it doubles and shrinks, then pink, tilted and crossing left to right at about 2.5 widths/s at 76.2–76.5. The rectangle returns as a stencilled envelope at 148 s. Pattern belongs to the silhouette: only the dark coat takes the stencil, the sky does not. Words arrive as broken, slightly misregistered letters on black ("RHYTHM" doubled at 100 s).
Colour: black `#121114`–`#151013` at 33–95% of most sampled frames (the yellow envelope shot is the exception, 2%); one saturated hue per shot: orange `#FF5500` C 91 23% (104 s), yellow `#FFFF48` C 84 31% (72.6 s, train sky) or `#F2E634` (148 s), red to brown 40–45% dark field with lens shapes (40 s), blue `#144065` with pink and white (11 s). A second hue is under 13%. Sound pulse 0.232 s / 129 per min is present, but this is an edit, so no offsets.

**Colour Flight.** Same vocabulary in a low-resolution, boosted transfer. 92–100 s, read at 0.12 s: a white vertical band (10–20% of the width), or two, drifts sideways over a ground of fine comb lines for 2.5 s; the comb pattern changes every 0.12–0.24 s (slant, wave, crossed) and the ground hue changes yellow, orange, green at about 0.3–0.5 s. From 95.0 s the same motion becomes 8–14 coloured vertical stripes (yellow, red, black, blue, white, green) that widen, narrow and shift by about a stripe width per 0.12 s. A small dark crescent (0.15–0.45 of the width) bounces up and down over them in 0.5–0.6 s cycles at 95.6–96.0, 97.4–97.8, 98.4–98.8, 99.3–99.7, 100.4 s, growing, then gone for 0.4–1 s. A black gap of about 0.7 s (96.3–97.0) falls in the middle of the stripes with only thin hatched verticals lit, then the stripes return at full. Median listed shot 0.48 s (quartiles 0.4–1.24). Colours: yellow `#FFCE0B` 28%, orange `#EE9B00` 28%, red `#FA541D` 13%, cyan `#A0F2F0` 9%, dark 13–23%; no neutral mid-grey.

**Swinging the Lambeth Walk.** Two separate line systems. Horizontal: one bright line of 5–8% of the frame height, wavy, jumping in height between frames, on a red-brown field. Vertical: thin parallel lines or wide stripes. 44–58 s against the sound sheet: a strummed chord at 44.30 s is answered by a switch to broad vertical stripes (measured image switch 44.23 s, 70 ms early); the plucked guitar notes that follow (44.8–46.6 s) show a single horizontal wavy line; the second strum at 46.87 s brings vertical stripes again (image 46.83, 40 ms early); a lone chord at 47.55 cuts to a pale horizontal line (47.60); the soft sustained note after 48.4–49.0 is followed by a blank pale screen with a faint ghost, 49.0–49.6 s; the violin or clarinet enters at 52.42 s and the screen switches to thin verticals on orange (52.40, 20 ms early), and stays with them until a wave raster at 55.0, for 0.3–0.4 s, about 0.4 s after a low hit at 54.55. Other strong rises with an image switch within 80 ms: 51.44 (−43 ms), 57.98 (−11), 66.13 (+70), 76.66 (−56), 79.66 (−59). These are picked from a list of about 60 rises; over the whole film the hit rate is 13% within 60 ms, equal to chance (best 23% at −0.16 s). So the changes of instrument at 44.3, 46.9 and 52.4 s are clear to the eye and tight, while the notes inside a phrase are not marked one by one. Line direction over the whole film: 63% of frames vertical-dominant, 16% horizontal; direction correlates with bass-band energy (0.32, chance spread 0.13) and with bass-minus-mid (0.23, chance spread 0.13, largest of the shuffles 0.18), so weak but above chance, in line with the published account (guitar: vibrating horizontal line; bass: stomping vertical). Longest horizontal stretches: 48–52, 96–106, 164–174 s. Image motion is calm: 4–20% change, below A Colour Box. Colour is nearly all lost: red-orange `#FF8C4F`–`#FF855A` 54% with pale yellow line `#FFDB80`; treat the hues as faded.

**Color Cry.** Photogram shadows of cloth, netting, beads and cut card on one or two colours. 60–74 s, read at 0.2 s: mauve netting (60.0) goes to near black within 0.5 s and back as pink grid; tiny triangles in red on black, re-dealt every frame-pair for 2 s; red-gold weave 2 s (63.4–65.4); a wide black lattice on hot pink 1.6 s (65.6–67.2); a black T on pale pink 2.4 s (67.3–69.7), and on orange 2.0 s (69.8–71.6); then red bead grid 0.8 s; a plaid that slides for 2 s. Within a held colour the cross or bar is re-placed at two heights alternately, each about 0.2 s (y 0.15 and 0.75–0.85): the same shadow flips up/down while the colour stays. The colour then changes by single frame. Quartiles of listed shots 0.5–2.8 s. Verticals (2–4 bars, 10–40% of the width) in the second half (100–170 s) are held for several seconds and mostly drift. Ground colours by frame: `#FDBA1E` 43% over red 29% (76 s); pink `#D59A86` (faded rose) 34% with two reds 38% (98 s); near black 44–77% at 64, 124 s; deep green `#002E07` 79% (152 s); orange `#F88718` 52% (184 s). Colours are warm by a wide margin and the film is cropped.

**Free Radicals.** White scratches on black, no halftone, 16 mm. Lit area: mean 4.7% (median 2.4%, 90th percentile 13.6%, max 53%) in the clear transfer, 7.3% in the other because of brighter glow. Phrases: 198 lit runs, median 0.23 s, quartiles 0.10–0.67, longest 11.8 s; 197 black runs, median 0.07 s, quartiles 0.03–0.17, longest 1.0 s; 14% of the time is fully black. Read at 0.1 s from 86.0 s: 0.2 s black; a burst of horizontal strokes; a wandering line that grows from 1 to 3 strokes over 0.6 s (86.3–86.9), changes shape every 0.1–0.2 s; black 88.2–88.3 (0.2 s); angular zigzag 0.5 s; black 0.2; a dense knot of 10–14 crossing strokes that peaks at 89.4 and thins to a single arc by 89.8, then black; a figure of 3–5 bent strokes 0.5 s; black; twisting column of 4–6 strokes growing 1.8 s (90.6–92.4), thinning at the end; black 0.2 s; one S-shaped drawing held and slightly wobbled for 0.5 s (92.7–93.1). Figures stay in one place and wobble: each frame is a redrawn version of the last. They do not travel; they twist, grow, thin. They start on one frame with one to three strokes, swell within 0.2–0.4 s, and leave by thinning over 0.2–0.4 s or in a single frame. Strip passages (0:30–1:00, 2:30, 2:50): 3–7 vertical lines, 1–3% of the width each, drifting sideways with one line thrust in. Density per 10 s: 13, 12, 10 (verticals) falls to 2–4 for most of the middle with returns to 7.9, 10.1 at 2:30 and 2:50. Both transfers agree on this shape. Sound has 4.4 detected onsets/s, so nearly everything is within 60 ms of one by chance; births after black lie no closer to onsets than chance (54% vs 53%; 43% vs 53%). With a lag scan the strongest agreement between big image changes and drum rises is 27–30% against 14% by chance, at −0.70 s in one file and +0.50 s in the other. Those two lags differ by 1.2 s, as the two transfers do, so a coupling is plausible, but not proven. A published account says the music was edited to match the picture; the official page says the images were synchronised to it. Colour: black `#000200` 55–92%, white to grey 10–25%; neutral, C ≤ 5.

**Trilogy (A Flip and Two Twisters).** Filmed on black with light on the back wall; three long steel bands hung from a ceiling, the two side bands spinning, the central loop hanging as a U. 8.5–12 s: side bands change pose every 0.12 s sample, so they turn about once per 0.36 s (about 2.8 poses repeat per second, read from the left band's dark straight pose at 8.86, 9.22, 9.46/9.82, 10.42, 10.78, 11.14 s, an upper-limit estimate because of 0.12 s sampling); the shadow twins are drawn as S curves. The central loop is slow: from 10 to 14.2 s its lower arc widens (shadow loop width 0.10 to 0.24 of the frame) with no visible turning; at 14.26–14.62 s (0.4 s) it creases and folds through itself, the base of the loop going flat and then upward; by 14.98 it stands fully inverted; from 15.1 to 15.9 (0.8 s) it springs back to a narrow U; then it rings: narrow at 15.9, wide at 16.5, narrow at 17.1, wide near 18.2, the swing shrinking, with only a small tremble after about 19 s. Load about 4.2 s, flip 0.4–0.7 s, recovery 0.8 s, visible ringing 3–4 s. The side bands slow and hang straight by 28 s. No sync to judge, sound unmeasured (audio absent for most of the clip).

**Blade.** Visitor video, hand-held: a steel strip (about 3% of the frame width edge-on, 17% broadside) and a ball on a rod at its left. The picture shows vibration at 5.1 and 10.2 Hz (the two strongest peaks above 1 Hz between 1 and 11 s); audio onsets every 0.10–0.13 s (median 0.128) are the strip striking the ball or rattling, as fast as 8 per second. At 4.0–4.6 s the edge-on strip shows a waist: its blur is wide above and below and narrow between, a standing wave. From 15 s it stands broadside and the motion is a fine shimmer of horizontal reflection bands with the strip's outline steady for 20 s, a sustained state, while early seconds are sparse and sharp. The ball on its rod is blurred by the same fast shake in most early frames. The published account names a hammering against a cork ball with light, sound and movement.

**Fountain, Firebush, Universe.** Fountain: many tens of thin rods from one base in a fan of roughly 90–120° (by eye); motion is a slow swivel, seen as a drift of highlights below 0.3 Hz (the camera pans, so no rate). Firebush sways at about 0.4–0.6 Hz with the rods' tips blurred. Lit in black rooms with one coloured spot per fountain (blue, green, red, white). Universe: a standing steel loop on a low base, rocking left and right and flattening, with a small ball hung above it; the picture's energy is below 0.35 Hz, but the camera is hand-held. The Getty essay describes the related Loop as rocked by electromagnets at each end.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **One figure class, many grounds** (A Colour Box 72–80 s, Colour Flight 92–100 s, Rainbow Dance 19.7–26 s). The figure type holds for 2–8 s while the ground switches in single frames: 0.48–0.72 s in A Colour Box, 1.04–1.16 s in Rainbow Dance. Grounds that touch in time never share a hue.
- **Two states of the same line: glide and shake, with an empty ground between** (A Colour Box 38.2–41.7 s: glide 0.4 s at 1.2 widths/s or 0.45 s at 0.3 widths/s, shake about 1 s with the line redrawn 0.2–0.3 widths away each frame, empty ground 0.5 s; Lambeth Walk: line and blank pale 0.6 s at 49.0; Colour Flight: 0.7 s black gap inside stripes). Width and colour of the line do not change during the shake.
- **Constant-rate travel, no easing, reversal in one frame** (Particles 0.76–0.92 heights/s; Tal Farlow 0.72 widths/s across and 0.36 widths/s in the V; A Colour Box first glide 1.2 widths/s with a slight slowing). Acceleration appears only in exits of words and in the physical works (A Colour Box prices: dwell at 0.1, out at 0.6–1.5 widths/s; Trilogy).
- **Abrupt birth, quick swell, thinning or cut-off death.** Free Radicals figures start with 1–3 strokes, swell in 0.2–0.4 s to as many as 14 and thin in 0.2–0.4 s; Particles rows swell in 0.1–0.3 s; Tal Farlow bars slide in from an edge or appear whole; ground colours and instrument changes are single-frame (A Colour Box, Lambeth Walk, Rainbow Dance). No fades were seen apart from this swelling and thinning of strokes.
- **Black or empty ground as breath** (Free Radicals 14% black in runs of median 0.07 s, p75 0.17; Particles 25%, median 0.08; Tal Farlow 7%, median 0.12–0.16; Colour Flight 0.7 s; Lambeth 0.6 s of pale; A Colour Box 0.5 s). Breaths are short and frequent, not long.
- **Phrases, not notes, meet the music** (Lambeth Walk: three instrument changes within 20–70 ms, image early; Tal Farlow: rests are black, density lags loudness by about 0.35 s; A Colour Box, Free Radicals, Particles, Colour Flight, Color Cry: onset-level agreement at chance, the automatic test and the sheets agree). Public copies of A Colour Box and Free Radicals cannot settle frame sync.
- **One instrument, one direction of line** (Lambeth Walk: chords and bass vertical, plucked notes horizontal; weak statistical support, direct observation at 44.23, 46.83, 52.40 s; Tal Farlow keeps verticals for strings).
- **Pattern belongs to the object, not to the frame** (Trade Tattoo: stencil printed only inside the coat, the train, the envelope; Rainbow Dance: pattern fills a silhouette at 12–16 s; A Colour Box: the comb wedge crosses the ribbon and changes colour only where it overlaps).
- **Trails are counted copies in separate colours and removed oldest first** (Rainbow Dance 27–30.4 s: up to 5 copies, 1.2 s in, 1 s hold, 0.2 s apart out).
- **Small stamp on a large moving ground** (A Colour Box blob 2–4 frames; Colour Flight crescent 0.5–0.6 s bounce; Trade Tattoo rectangle 0.35–0.6 s; Particles dots).
- **Density drops when words must be read** (A Colour Box 120–155 s: change 4–14/255 against 35–48 before and 22 after; Trade Tattoo and Colour Flight letter on near-plain ground).
- **Physical works: slow load, abrupt release, ringing** (Trilogy 4.2 s / 0.4–0.7 s / 3–4 s; Blade standing wave at 5 and 10 Hz; Universe sway under 0.35 Hz).
- **The generative pattern seen:** mark the strip frame by frame with one tool; hold a figure type while a fixed clock swaps the ground; give the figure a glide at constant rate or a shake; let it start abruptly and swell fast; interrupt with short empty beats; pair a line direction with an instrument or a phrase; leave a trail when an action needs to be seen; when letters come, slow down and let each dwell, then dart. Lye, quoted by the Getty essay: "there could be such a thing as composing motion."

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Len Lye.** *Cut the run into short phrases at the joins the process itself has; hold one figure and one ground through each phrase and change one of them in a single step; redraw a live thing in place and move a travelling one at constant speed; break often with short empty beats, and still nearly everything while words are read.*
Process:
1. Find the phrases before any form. List the joins the process has of its own accord: a source or stage changes, it pauses, it ends. A phrase runs 1–8s (a figure class holds 2–8s, a single statement 1.2–2.4s). Everything below is timed to these joins, and the events inside a phrase are not marked one by one. If the joins move, every later step is redone.
2. Lock one figure class and one ground per phrase. The figure is one kind of mark in one width and one colour: a band 4–15% of the view's width, or 1–7 hairlines of 1–3% each. The ground is one flat field. The figure covers 1–11% of the view on the resting ground and 10–30% on a coloured one, sits off-centre and does not reach all four edges.
3. At a join change one of the two, in a single step. Either the ground is replaced under a figure that stays (no blend, and the new ground differs in hue from the last), or the ground holds and the figure jumps to a second place or changes class. A ground lasts 0.5–1.1s on a steady clock in the works and the intervals shorten to a half or less just before a passage ends; in an interface the swap happens at a join only, and no more than once a second.
4. Give the figure kinds of motion, not degrees of speed. Going somewhere: a glide at constant speed (a slow class at 0.3–0.4 of the view per second, a fast class at 0.7–1.2), with no ease in or out and the turnaround in one step. Working: redrawn in place, every drawing different in bend and position and the same in width and colour (in the works 20–25 drawings a second, 0.15–0.33 of the width apart). Resting: straight and still, about 0.5s. A figure is in one state at a time.
5. Start whole and small, finish thin. A figure appears in one step in its least form (1–3 strokes), swells to full in 0.1–0.4s, and leaves by thinning over 0.2–0.4s or in one step. Nothing cross-fades into its successor and nothing assembles itself slowly.
6. Breathe. Show the ground alone between two states and at every pause of the process: 0.1–0.2s inside a phrase, 0.5–0.7s at a join, never longer than about 1s, and 7–25% of the running time in all. A pause in the process is the empty ground, never a frozen figure.
7. Meet the process at its joins and nowhere else. When the source or stage changes, the picture changes with the cause: up to 20–70ms ahead of it, never after. A rest shows the empty ground. Between joins the amount of marking follows the level of activity loosely, 0.3–0.4s behind it. Give each source one direction of line and keep it for the whole run.
8. Lay a path out as counted copies when an action has to be seen after it has passed. Leave one copy at each pose, up to 5, each in its own flat colour and overlapping the last by a third to a half. Build over about 1.2s, hold the full set for 1s, then remove oldest first, 0.2–0.4s apart, until only the live figure is left.
9. Quiet the view for words. When something must be read, bring the amount of change down to between a tenth and four tenths of the running level, on a plain ground. Show one to three words, or one figure and its unit, at a time. It dwells nearly still (creeping at 0.1 of the width per second), then leaves at 0.6–1.5 widths per second: the only accelerated move in the piece. The next may overlap it for about a quarter of its life, in a different colour.
10. For an action that gathers before it commits, use load, snap, ring: a slow visible load (the figure widens to about 2.4 times), a release in a tenth to a sixth of the load's time, a recovery a little longer than the release, then two to four swings, each smaller than the last, about 0.6s apart.
11. Compose the density of the whole run. Either rise to a plateau, rest where the words come and close on a short coda at about half the plateau; or open dense, run a long sparse middle at about a quarter of the opening and return twice, briefly, near the end. The busiest stretch of the process need not be the busiest stretch of the picture.
12. Write the still form, one frame per phrase. The ground states the phrase, with its name in words. The figure's shape states its condition: straight for resting or travelling, bent for working, absent for a pause. The copies state the count, with the figure beside them. If these frames do not tell the run, the joins were chosen wrongly (step 1).
Why it works:
- A figure that keeps its width and colour while the ground is replaced stays one object (grouping by likeness), so the change is read as a change in its situation and not as a new scene. Because the eye has adapted to the held ground, a one-step swap is salient with nothing added. Blend the two grounds and a third, mixed state appears that means nothing and smears the moment; change figure and ground together and the thread is cut.
- A drawing repeated exactly is predicted after one cycle and then ignored. Redrawing with small differences in bend and place keeps the figure odd enough to be watched, and holding width and colour keeps it the same thing. Vary the width or the colour too and it reads as several objects; loop it and it reads as a machine idling.
- Travel and agitation in place differ in kind, so the state is read without comparing speeds. The empty beat between them clears adaptation, and the second state is seen fresh.
- Constant speed reads as a law: the position can be predicted, so attention goes to what the thing is and where it is heading. An ease adds a small start and a small stop to every move. With every glide constant, the one acceleration (a word leaving, a release) is salient by oddness.
- A gap under about a fifth of a second is bridged by persistence, so it divides the stream into phrases without losing the viewer's place. A gap over a second reads as finished or broken.
- A claim of coincidence is made only where it can be kept. A change of source is the one event that both streams state clearly. When events come four to nine a second, a mark on each is indistinguishable from chance and too fast to read. Leading by a few hundredths of a second makes the picture read as part of the cause; trailing makes it read as an echo.
- Copies in separate colours stay separate figures where they overlap, so a movement in time is laid out in space and can be counted. Removing them oldest first restates the order and ends on the one that is live.
- Reading needs a steady fixation and motion elsewhere pulls it away. Since the view has been busy, the calm is itself the signal that words have arrived.
- A slow load builds expectation, the short release is salient against it, and the shrinking swings show the energy leaving, so the end is seen as settled and not as stopped.
Colour relations: one flat ground at a time. On a resting ground at one end of the value scale the figure takes 1–11% and the ground 85–99%; on a coloured ground the figure takes 10–30%. The figure is the extreme value in the view, opposite the resting ground, and has almost no chroma; the grounds carry the chroma, and successive grounds differ in hue and never blend. With a resting ground, one strong hue may hold 20–50% of a phrase and a second stays under 13%. Where shapes carry the colour, a neutral holds the larger share and colour stays under about a third. Counted copies take one flat colour each. The ground under words is pale and plain, about 80% of the view, with the words in two hues at 1–3%. No mid-value neutral sits between the extremes. Inverse mode: a light ground of 40–80% with the marks dark.
Space and line: one field edge to edge, marks running along one axis, the field divided into 1–14 parallel bands; a figure 10–60% of the width, off-centre, not touching all four edges; depth from overlap only; a pattern or texture belongs to the object it fills and stops at that object's edge, never at the frame's; a band 4–15% of the width, a hairline 1–3%, 7–25 lines when lines run parallel; from one drawing to the next a figure differs in bend and place, never in width.
Type requirement: words are objects, not lines of text: one to three words, or one figure and one unit, alone on a plain field. They need a sturdy face with open counters that holds at display size on a strong flat ground (letter height an eighth to a quarter of the field's height), in two colours so that successive words can cross. Running text takes no part: it sits outside the field, still.
Motion and interaction: glide linear at 0.3–0.4 or 0.7–1.2 of the view per second, reversal in one step; swell 0.1–0.4s; thinning 0.2–0.4s; empty beat 0.1–0.2s, 0.5–0.7s at a join, 1s at most; ground swap in one step with no fade; copies in over 1.2s, held 1s, out 0.2–0.4s apart; word dwell, then exit at 0.6–1.5 widths per second; load to release 6–10 : 1. In an interface a redraw is fired by a real tick of the process (a chunk, a sample, a line), at most 8 a second, on a figure under 5% of the view that never blinks off, and only while the process runs; a ground swap comes at a join only, at most once a second, on a bounded region or within about 10 L of the last ground, so it is a change of hue and not a flash; empty beats come no more than twice a second; a word stays until it has been read or acted on. Idle, nothing moves. The works' 0.2s colour clock and frame-rate redraw are not carried. Reduced motion: one still frame per phrase, changed in one step at the join; the working figure is drawn bent and still, the travelling one at its destination; the pause is the empty ground with its label; copies appear whole with their count and leave together; load and release are two still states. Phrase, state, pause and count all remain.
Fails when: the process has no joins (a steady stream, a monitor that never changes stage); a value must be read exactly at every instant, since a redrawn figure is noise against a scale; the user must read or press the figure while it moves; every event needs its own confirmation (typing, each tap), which phrase timing does not give; several independent processes share one view, so several figures would work at once; long reading; a view with no second ground distinct from the first.
Signature (do not carry together): white scratched lines trembling on black · full-chroma dye grounds swapped under brushed or combed white ribbons, with stencil lettering · a dance-band or drum record running under the picture.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Lye's procedure → a receipt-scanning flow in an expense app (phone, 390 × 844), pale sage and ink, one serif. No black, no saturated field, no sound, nothing hand-drawn.
1. *Phrases.* The process has four joins: page found, shutter, text read, saved. It also has a pause, while the user lays down the next page. Phrases last 1–6s. Single recognised words are not on the list.
2. *Figure and ground.* The figure is one ruled line across the page preview, 4px thick and 234px long (60% of the width), in the ink value, the darkest thing in the view. The preview (358 × 520px) is the field; the line is under 1% of it and sits left of centre.
3. *One change per join.* Two grounds, a sage and a warm paper tone, 6 L apart. The preview swaps from one to the other in one step at the shutter and back at "saved": two swaps per page, seconds apart. The line stays through the first swap.
4. *Kinds of motion.* Page found: the line glides from the top of the preview to the first row of text at 416px/s (0.8 of the height per second), linear, and stops in one step. Reading: at each line of text the recogniser returns (3–6 a second) the rule is redrawn at that line's height and tilted to its skew, always 4px and ink. Saved: straight and still.
5. *Birth and death.* The rule appears in one step at a third of its length, reaches full length in 200ms, and at "saved" thins from 4px to 1px over 300ms and is gone.
6. *Breath.* If no line of text returns for 0.5s the rule is removed and the paper ground stands alone, so a stall looks empty and not frozen. Between pages the ground is empty for 500ms.
7. *Joins.* The ground swaps on the shutter press itself, before the server has answered. The page counter follows the uploads about 0.3s behind.
8. *Counted copies.* Each captured page leaves an outline, offset so that it overlaps the last by a third, each in its own tint, up to 5 (after that only the figure "7 pages" grows). On save they leave oldest first, 250ms apart, until one remains.
9. *Words.* When the total is found everything stops. "48,20 EUR" stands alone on the paper ground, 96px high (0.18 of the field), until the user confirms it; then it leaves towards the list, accelerating, in 300ms.
10. *Load, snap, ring.* Deleting a page is a hold: the row's bar widens to 2.4 times over 1.2s, the row is gone in 150ms, and the rows below close up in 250ms with two small diminishing swings.
11. *Density.* A page's run is: glide, 2–6s of redraws, then the long still total. The list screen that follows has no motion at all.
- *Reduced motion.* The rule is drawn once per phrase: at the first row, tilted while reading, straight when saved. Grounds change in one step with the stage named in words. Copies appear whole beside "3 pages".
Result: a quiet sage scanning screen that is still whenever there is something to read. No black, no dye colours, no trembling scratches, no music. What survived is the phrase list, one figure held across a one-step change of ground, redrawing as the sign of work, constant-speed travel, the pause shown as empty ground, counted copies and the stop for words.

## Space & composition
The direct films fill the frame edge to edge with marks running along the strip's long axis. Vertical lines are easiest on film; they dominate (63% of Lambeth Walk frames vertical, nearly all of Tal Farlow and the strip passages of Free Radicals), so the frame is divided into 1–14 vertical bands. Scratch films put a figure of 10–60% of the width off-centre in a black frame: Free Radicals knots sit right of centre, twisting columns left of centre. Figures rarely reach all four edges: Free Radicals 2–5% lit, Particles under 1%, Tal Farlow 9–11%, Rainbow Dance and Trade Tattoo figures 0.4–0.65 of the height on a fixed landscape. Depth comes only from overlap: a wedge across a ribbon, a copy over a copy, a pattern inside a coat. Physical works use the dark room as the same empty ground: one lit strip or fan of rods, black around.

## Form, line & typography
Line is one of five tools, each with its own edge. A brush ribbon with streaks inside it, 4–15% of the width, uneven edges and a pooled halo. A comb scraped through wet colour: 7–25 parallel lines, each a steady spacing apart, wavy together. A scratch on black: jagged, hairy, varying in width 0.5–3%, broken where it lifts. A dot or screen stamp: hard-edged circles or squares in grids of 5–10 per row, often misregistered, with an oval flattening where the film is out of register. A stencil: lens, triangle, chevron, rectangle, envelope. Curves are S and C; corners turn up in zigzags and angular scratches. Figures differ from one drawing to the next in bend and place, not in stroke width.
Lettering is stencil-cut or scratched. A Colour Box prices: heavy rounded stencil with cut counters (the ink bridge shows), letter height 0.19–0.24 of the frame. GPO title cards: geometric sans in wide tracking (letters about 0.12 of the frame high, spaces as wide as the letters). Free Radicals title: crooked scratched capitals 0.3–0.5 of the frame high. Color Cry: brush script. Trade Tattoo: broken typewriter or slab capitals, double-exposed. Words sit on a plain field and move as objects; they are never set in lines of running text: one to three words, or one number and one unit, at a time.

## UI translations
1. **State change:** Keep the element and replace what is behind it in one step, with no cross-fade (*A Colour Box* 72–80s: one class of combed lines over twelve grounds, each swapped in a single frame). In a product do it at a real change of stage, once a second at most, on a bounded panel.
2. **Working state:** Show that something is live by redrawing it, not by looping it: same width, same colour, a new bend and place each time (*A Colour Box* ribbon, *Free Radicals* figures). Fire each redraw from a real tick of the process, at most 8 a second, on a mark under 5% of the view.
3. **Two motions for two meanings:** Glide for "going somewhere", redraw in place for "working", with 0.5s of bare ground between them (*A Colour Box* 38.2–41.7s). Do not express both with one motion at two speeds.
4. **Easing:** Linear travel and a one-step turnaround (*Particles in Space* 0.76–0.92 heights/s, *Tal Farlow* 0.72 widths/s and a V with no slowdown). Keep acceleration for the one thing that leaves after being read.
5. **Entrance and exit:** On in one step at least size, full within 0.1–0.4s, out by thinning in 0.2–0.4s (*Free Radicals* knots: 1–3 strokes to 14 and back). No slow assembly, no fade-and-slide.
6. **Loading:** Divide a wait into phrases of 1–8s by its own stages and give each one figure and one ground. Between stages show the ground alone for 0.5–0.7s (*Colour Flight* 0.7s gap, *Lambeth Walk* 0.6s of pale).
7. **Stall or pause:** When the process rests, clear the mark and leave the ground, for up to about 1s before saying so in words (*Tal Farlow*: rests in the guitar are black; no black run in the scratch films passes 1s).
8. **Feedback timing:** Change the picture with the cause, a few hundredths of a second early and never late (*Swinging the Lambeth Walk*: image 20–70ms ahead of three instrument changes). Do not mark every event inside a phrase: the films do not.
9. **Source to direction:** Give each source or channel one direction of line and keep it (*Lambeth Walk*: plucked notes a horizontal line, chords and bass verticals), with a label, since direction alone is a weak cue.
10. **Activity level:** Let the amount of marking follow the level of activity about 0.3–0.4s behind (*Tal Farlow*), not each sample.
11. **Progress and history:** Leave a copy at each completed pose, up to 5, each in its own flat colour, overlapping by a third to a half; remove oldest first, 0.2–0.4s apart (*Rainbow Dance* 27–30.4s). Add the count in figures.
12. **Messages:** One to three words, or one figure and its unit, alone on a plain ground; near-still while read, then out at speed (*A Colour Box* prices: creep at 0.1 widths/s, exit at 0.6–1.5). A message that matters stays until dismissed.
13. **When nothing should move:** Cut motion to a tenth to four tenths of the running level wherever there is text (*A Colour Box* 120–155s: 4–14 against 35–48). Idle screens are still.
14. **Hold to commit:** Load slowly, release in a tenth to a sixth of that time, then two to four shrinking swings (*Trilogy*: 4.2s load, 0.4–0.7s flip, 0.8s recovery, ringing 3–4s). In a product scale the whole figure down and keep the ratios.
15. **Pattern as state:** Clip a state pattern (pending, syncing) to the item it describes and move it with the item (*Trade Tattoo*: the stencil prints only inside the coat, the train, the envelope).
16. **Colour:** One flat ground at a time and the mark at the extreme value: `#EBF4FD` ribbon at 15% on magenta `#BC0081`, or scratch white `#C1C6C0` at 5% on `#000200` (*A Colour Box*, *Free Radicals*). The hues are from boosted and faded transfers: take the shares, not the swatches.
17. **Density over a session:** Dense opening, long sparse middle at about a quarter of it, two brief returns (*Free Radicals* ink per 10s: 13, 12, 10, then 2–4, then 8 and 10).
18. **Accessibility:** Do not carry the 0.2s colour clock (*Rainbow Dance*: 22 full-frame colour cuts at five a second), the single-frame black gaps of the scratch films or frame-rate redraw over a large area: they pass 3 flashes a second (WCAG 2.3.1). Swap a ground once a second at most and keep it a change of hue.
19. **Accessibility:** Under `prefers-reduced-motion` show one still frame per phrase: ground and label for the stage, the mark straight or bent for its state, bare ground for a pause, copies with their count. Never put text or a control on a mark that is being redrawn.

## What this artist would reject
Motion that is smooth because it is smoothed: there is no ease curve on a drawn line in these films. Cross-fades as the default change between grounds (ground swaps are single-frame). A figure that slowly assembles itself. A long empty hold: the longest black is about a second. Every note illustrated, or the picture locked to a metronome. A mark with no trace of the tool (a comb, a brush, a needle). Type set as paragraphs. A repeated figure with no change from one drawing to the next: where it repeats it is bent or placed differently. Colour as tint: grounds are full saturation or black.

## Signature prompt seed
Design the screen as a Lye strip. One flat ground at a time, black (`#000200`) at rest or a single full-chroma field (magenta `#BC0081`, ultramarine `#16125E`, red `#B80020`), with one white hand-drawn line (`#EBF4FD`), 4–15% of the width, off-centre. Hold the line and swap the ground in one step at each change of stage, never more than once a second. The line glides at constant speed when it travels and is redrawn with a new bend when it works; it appears in one step, swells in 0.3s and thins away. Leave bare ground for half a second between phrases. Set one to three words at a time in a heavy stencil face on a plain pale field, still while read, then gone fast. Under reduced motion show one still frame per phrase; no strobing, no cross-fades.
