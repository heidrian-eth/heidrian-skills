# Oskar Fischinger — Visual Music

## Essence
Fischinger plans a film on a time ruler before he draws it: the sound is cut into single frames, each note gets a place and a length, and a figure is then given a whole life inside that length. In the frames a figure is born at a seed or a hairline, reaches full size in 3–5 frames or appears full size in one frame, and then spends the rest of its time shrinking, flattening or cooling in colour until it is a thread, a dot or a faint residue. Many such lives overlap on a dark or single-hue ground, residues pile up, and at a few strong points the whole pile speaks at once.

## Detailed observations

**Studie Nr. 6.** White marks on black: the ground measures `#050505` (L 2) and marks `#D1D1D1` (L 84), no hue. Mean luma by 5 s block runs from 2.3% to 12.7% and about 81% of frames are darker than 8% luma. The film is drawn on ones: the 30 fps file repeats exactly every fifth frame, so 24 different pictures a second are left. There are no edits in the usual sense; the 42 "cuts" the tool reports are drawn jumps where a swelling stroke fills and leaves the frame in 2–4 frames (12.7–13.0 s, 63–64 s, 110–113 s). Block section, 44–59 s: white blocks, each 0.5–2% of the frame, are born low as a small trapezoid, rise, swell, then flatten into a horizontal hairline and go out high. One isolated block (47.21–47.63 s) was measured frame by frame: 0.19, 0.49, 0.95, 1.86, then 1.52, 0.80, 0.41, 0.15% of the frame. So it roughly doubles each frame on the way up and halves each frame on the way down, peak on the fourth picture, whole life about 0.4 s (10 file frames). The next block is already born (0.16–0.28%) while the last is at a hairline, so the field almost never empties. Blocks answer in mirrored pairs (two high, two low; one wide above, two narrow below), and group counts go 1, 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 10 between 47 and 49.5 s before a single bar 9.4% of the frame replaces them all (49.71 s), which falls to 1.3% in 4 frames. The next group starts 0.04 s later from 0.16%. Arc section, 10–17 s: crescents enter from the lower left, travel up and right on curved paths and leave the top edge; one crossing takes 0.6–0.9 s, about 1–1.5 frame heights per second, and figures turn to stay tangent to their path, with a blunt lit head and a feathered tail. A chevron at 16.6–17.0 s doubles in width roughly every 0.15 s and leaves by the top, accelerating. At 84–87 s about 12 seeds become about 45 short dashes, join as three-armed stars, then shrink back to dots: one shape change per 0.3 s while positions stay put. At 89–99 s there is one ring or disc at the centre, 5–20% of frame height, pulsing with long dark around it (luma 3.4–4.1%): the quiet before the densest passage. 109.8–115.7 s is the climax: crescents, long strokes and blocks replace each other every 0.8–1.4 s, with the new family already entering over the leaving one; coverage peaks at 12.7% luma per block. Image to sound: the soundtrack's pulse is 0.534 s with a half at 0.267 s and has about 5 onsets a second; motion energy averaged around the 243 strongest onsets is flat (z −0.00 to 0.07, chance ±0.08), so no per-note lock shows at the level of the whole frame.

**Studie Nr. 5.** A swarm of about 13 small hooks keeps to the left third, 0.3–1.4% of the frame in total, while a second group at the right changes shape continuously, 1.4–6.7%. The swarm rides up and down between 0.10 and 0.64 of the frame height, about 2.5–2.9 s per full trip (six to seven beats of the 0.418 s pulse), about 0.4 frame heights a second at mid travel, and its hooks flip between cup-up and cup-down as the direction turns. The right group goes, in 7 s: two arches → two parallel lines → six nested crescents → about 25 wavy dashes → 10 straight parallel diagonals (10.0 s, its densest, 6.7%) → the diagonals sink into one lens (10.6 s) → bell → six crescents → lines → six small dots, snapping to six overlapping rings in one 0.1 s step (12.0→12.1 s) and back in another (12.2→12.3 s) → lines. Shape states last 0.2–0.5 s; change is continuous redrawing, not dissolve. Sound pulse 0.418 s (143 per minute, strength 0.85, the clearest of all clips); motion near onsets is raised for a broad 0.25 s before and at the onset (z 0.35–0.40) and lower after it (0.18–0.25), but the chance range is ±0.20, so this is a lean, not a lock.

**Studie Nr. 7.** At 14 s about 21 thin dashes stand still, 1.0–1.3% of the frame, the residue of earlier groups. At 15.06 s they begin to thicken, at 15.10 they cover 4.6%, 15.14: 12.8%, 15.18: 18.2% as tilted diamonds, 15.23: 5.7%, 15.27: 2.8%, then about 1.5% as hairlines now on a diagonal. So: 4 frames up, 2 frames down, the shape returns turned by about 45°. The sound steps up 12 dB between 15.14 and 15.18 (onset detected 15.163 s): the swell starts about 60–100 ms before the sound and peaks on it (+20 ms). Later peaks: 15.68 s a travelling swell (lower half first, upper half 0.2 s later) that ends just as the next 8 dB step arrives at 15.85–15.89 s; a pair at 16.52 and 16.77 s, 0.25 s apart and nearly equal (11.9 and 11.7%). Dashes then bend into arcs, turn through every angle and are drawn to the right edge, ending as dots (17.69–17.73 s: 4.0% to 0.3% in one frame) and 0.12 s of full black, after which three tiny marks (0.2%) start the next build-up. From 18.2 to 25 s trios of blocks land one after another at different places, 0.3–0.5 s each, and each trio stays behind as three dashes, so the field fills from 3 to about 45 marks by 25 s. At 27–30 s the vocabulary switches to long curved strokes and crescents (motion energy rises from about 2 to 5). Averaged over all strong onsets there is no lock (z within ±0.15); sync is at phrase accents, not at every note.

**Studie Nr. 8.** First (5.0–6.0 s) a field of 14–51 seeds breathes: seed (1.9%) → split pair → wide bar (6.9% at 5.17 s, 9.0% at 5.58 s) → pair → hairlines (0.4%) in 0.33–0.42 s, with the count rising at each breath (about 22 → 39 → 52). Image peaks at 5.17, 5.58, 6.17, 6.67 s; loudness peaks at 5.25, 5.69, 6.13, 6.58 s and detected onsets 5.19, 5.63, 6.07, 6.53. The image runs from 80 ms early to 90 ms late against loudness, so it shares the beat but is not frame-exact in this upload. From 6.1 s large rectangles appear one at a time, cascading down a diagonal. One of them: an outline box in one frame, a level that fills it top to bottom in 3–4 frames, full for 2–3 frames (1.3–3.5% of the frame), then it collapses to its own base in 3–4 frames (share of a crop round the box: 22.4, 18.6, 12.4, 6.0, 3.0, 1.6, 1.1%, roughly halving) and stays as a bar of about 0.1%. Whole life 0.5 s; the next box opens as this one empties, about 0.25–0.3 s later, so two or three are alive. The bars do not go: 14 pieces at 9.2 s, 18 at 10.0, 23 at 10.5, 25 at 11.5, 32 at 12.5, 40 at 13.75, drifting by under 2% of the frame a second. At 13.2 s one box reaches 12% of the frame. At 15.0–15.3 s every bar swells together (7.9 → 10.8%, 25 merged shapes) as the sound reaches its loudest (−19.5 dB, 12 dB above the field's level), then the same marks tilt, bend and become wedges and diamonds, about 10% each time, pulsing at about 0.25 s until the cut to black.

**Kreise.** Opening: 25–30 overlapping discs of three hues and three size classes (about 8, 14 and 30% of frame height), overlaps printing as a fourth colour (red + green → cream, blue + green → cyan): additive, not transparent. Then a ground of 20 or so concentric arcs `#07048E` (L 16, C 85, about 32% with its darker edges) on near-black `#040103` (55%), centred below the frame. Cohorts of 7–13 green discs `#0B9661` are born as dots, all at once, and grow in step: radius per frame up by about ×1.10 for the 7-frame cohort (10.00–10.22 s) and by a constant step for the 12–13 frame ones (10.30–10.72 s; 10.84–11.34 s), reaching 4–7% of the frame, then vanish in 2 frames. Births at 9.97, 10.26, 10.82 s follow each other by 0.3–0.55 s. After 11.43 s the ground is left alone for 0.58 s (14 frames, motion 1.5). At 12.011 s, in one frame, the whole ground flips from blue to green `#0F8943` at double stroke weight, coverage 0.16 → 35%, a blue disc of a third of the frame shrinks to nothing over 0.6 s while 15 red-and-cream discs grow, and at 12.762 s it flips back in one frame: 18 frames, 0.75 s. At 14.83–15.71 s a cluster of six concentric red rings flashes on for 3 frames (low, full, low: 8.5, 11.1, 8.5%) and off for 3, four times, period 0.25 s; on the fifth turn it becomes six red discs that streak outward and settle. The flip at 12.01 s is not on a loud onset (nearest detected 12.26 s, 250 ms later): CVM's "synchronized" holds for phrase shape here, not for this hit. In the tape of the advertising version the ground flips again and again at 0.167 s intervals (32.4–34.5 s and 37.4–39.4 s), the middle (43–53 s) thins to one or two small rings on black, and the end (83–91 s) grows to the brightest frames (luma 32–48%) as the rings become lettering.

**Komposition in Blau.** A shallow stage of painted blue wall and reflecting blue floor, horizon at 0.6 of the height. Teardrop phrase: a green shape filling 57% of the upper field appears in 1–2 frames and shrinks towards its right-hand tip in 27 frames: equivalent diameter 0.98 → 0.04 of the field's height, −0.02 to −0.03 per frame early and −0.04 in the middle, almost a straight line. As it shrinks it warms: `#1D7A55` → `#69A263` (frame 7) → pale yellow `#A6B180` (frame 10, the brightest, at about two thirds size) → `#A5995A` → orange `#A27E47` → red-brown `#904A30` in the last 4 frames. The three passes measured have the same length to a frame: starts at 3.14, 4.33 and 5.50 s, that is 28 frames, 1.17 s. Meanwhile rows of rounded posts march through the floor plane left to right, a different formation on each pass. Block field (6.8–9.6 s): about 25 blocks rise out of a fixed plan. Height rises in three steps 18 → 32 → 44 → 54% of the lower field and drops back in one frame: a 4-frame cycle, 0.167 s; three strokes, then a slow fall on twos (54, 54, 43, 43, 32, 32, 18) and 6–9 still frames (17.5%), then four strokes and the same fall. Stroke tops at 6.95, 7.12, 7.28 and 7.95, 8.12, 8.29, 8.45 s against detected onsets at 6.94, 7.08, 7.22 and 8.00, 8.13, 8.26: offsets −50 to +65 ms, drifting, because image strokes are evenly 167 ms and the notes about 135 ms apart. Accent phrases match; notes do not. On the third phrase the blocks turn from green to orange in one frame. Stairs (9.7–29 s): colour bands slide in from below one at a time over the blue, 0.15–0.3 s each, held 0.3–1 s; blue cubes are then added by rows of 1, 2, 2, 2, 5, 6, 7 one row per 0.17–0.33 s; at 26.5 s the whole plane pushes slowly toward the viewer (cubes ×1.6 in 1.7 s).

**An Optical Poem.** Ground a flat saturated blue, `#0000B6` in this file (L 21, C 103; CVM warns that all YouTube copies are faded or distorted, and this one looks pushed, so use hue relations only). Opening: a red disc appears in a single frame at 58.5% of the frame (4.283 s), 95% (13.24 s) or 79% (16.859 s), dead centre, and then only shrinks: diameter in frame heights 0.996, 0.783, 0.635, 0.529, 0.453, 0.397, 0.353 at 0.5 s steps. The reciprocal of the diameter climbs by a constant 0.28–0.33 each half second (1.00, 1.28, 1.57, 1.89, 2.21, 2.52, 2.83; second disc 0.79, 1.12, 1.52, 1.86, 2.27, 2.66, 2.97, 3.32), which is exactly a disc moving straight away at constant speed: the ease-out is perspective, not a curve applied. It stays up to 8.9 s or so as a reduced disc of 6–7% before being covered. Third disc against sound: the previous note has decayed to −60 dB; the disc lands at 16.859 s, the sound attack climbs at 16.92–16.98 s: image 60–120 ms early. The disc at 13.2 s arrives on a long held tone with no attack, and the loud orchestral entry at 14.68 s changes nothing on screen. Around strong onsets in the first 36 s, motion shows one clear spike at −2 frames (z 0.56, chance ±0.12): hits are placed about 80 ms ahead. Secondary discs are born at 0.3–0.5 of frame height and shrink the same way to a third in about 2 s, tinted by order (cream first, then pink, violet, near-ground purple for low soft notes at 19.5 s and 24.5 s). In the fast part (36–60 s), three discs at a time re-strike about every 0.25–0.4 s, each with 4–5 frames where a cream core shows before the hue settles, while a pale arc as wide as the frame sweeps right to left below in 0.45 s and returns about every 0.6 s. Columns of three-ring targets (62–71 s) travel left while changing scale 1 : 3 as a wave moving down the column. Squares section (75–100 s) uses one hue only, turquoise on blue, 24 or so squares at once. Cuts: 22 detected in 180 s, median 4.3 s, longest 25 s, mostly disc attacks and ground changes. The final ground is wine red `#85003E` with several hundred fixed dots and only 6–20 moving discs on a spiral path. Luma per 5 s runs from 2.7 to 22%, quiet after each rise.

**Allegretto.** The ground is two offset families of concentric circles, so a moiré of thin lines (red on dark, `#AB2A1D`, `#6A0E06`) always moves outward. Frame luma in the opening falls to a minimum every fourth frame (values 88, 93, 106, 89, 70, 97, 106, 93, 64…; autocorrelation 0.70 at 0.167 s) as ring colour alternates cream, green, red: a ground flutter of 6 a second, against a sound pulse of 0.221 and 0.441 s (strength 0.77, 0.67). Foreground: cream lozenges split from one to two to about 12 in 1.2 s (7.5–8.7 s), then swell into touching rows and drop back to small singles every 0.2–0.3 s. A central disc (16–19 s) changes colour and diameter nearly every frame: cream 0.43 of frame height, red 0.49, navy ring 0.6. Seven small capsules fall through it in a fixed arc formation while turning end over end, from 0.31 to 0.66 of the height in 0.42 s, about 0.85 frame heights a second. Lozenges as large as the frame flip colour in full, about every 0.1 s at 12.4–12.9 s (red, blue-cream, green, magenta, green). Layer count seen: ground, moiré, disc or lozenge, small figures, overlay: four to five, as Moritz says. Density falls at the end to two or three shards on near-black (luma 24% at 5–15 s, 9% at 35–40 s, 4% at 40–45 s). Motion around strong onsets peaks at +1 frame (z 0.38, chance ±0.12): weak but present.

**Radio Dynamics.** Silent by design. 6–32 s is a strict metre: 10 frames (0.417 s) per cell, confirmed by the intervals between hard changes (10, 10, 10, 10, 10…, with 40-frame doubles where the break is soft). Cells rotate through four compositions: three horizontal bars; three diagonal bars; three vertical bars; one square. Each bar is born as a hairline and thickens by an equal step each frame (top bar about 1% → 17% of the frame height in 6–7 frames, read off the sheet) while the ground fades continuously to a nearer hue, so that figure and ground meet (yellow bar on a red ground going orange going yellow) at about frame 7 and the cell ends as one flat field, or the bars reappear in the reversed value. Then one frame of black or of the next ground: reset to hairline with no transition. 24–32 s: cells split 5 + 5 and 6 + 4. 32–47 s: the metre accelerates to flashes every 2–5 frames (gaps 2, 2, 3, 4, 4, 3…; median 4, 0.167 s; 6 a second), with mean frame difference about 80 of 255 every frame: full-field alternation of red, green, orange, a wall of 150–200 small bricks, then small blue-green checks. At 47.9 s it stops: two nested yellow-to-maroon discs sit cut by the left and right edges, and between them a small orange disc (0.10 of frame height) swells to 0.65 in 2.9 s while rising from 0.53 to 0.3 of the height and darkening orange → red → maroon → black; it covers, vanishes, and the seed is back: restarts at 48.0, 51.2, 54.3, 57.5 s, period 3.15 s, 75 frames. Motion energy there is 4 of 100, a fifth of the flicker section. So: 10-frame metre → 4-frame flicker → 75-frame breath. There are 20 s of slow after 15 s of fast. (The flicker section would breach common flash-safety limits; noted as fact.)

**An American March and its score.** In the trailer three layers run on black with faint blue stripes and stars: oblong bars (3 → 8 at once, 1–2% each), orange ellipses, a yellow pair. Colour on black: ground `#0C0C09` 77%, blue `#172343` 9%, yellow `#E2CE37` 6%, red `#F9493A` 4.5%. Every bar is born as a white or pale hairline, thickens and saturates (pale → teal → dark green; pale → orange → brown-red) over 8–12 frames and is replaced by its successor, pale and thin, on top of it. Hard changes come every 10–13 frames (mean 11.6, 0.48 s) and the sound pulse is 0.464–0.476 s (half 0.232); at 21.2–22.3 s they run every 3 frames. The score is on graph paper with a frame ruler at top and bottom, marked in seconds of film (red "2/1, 2/2, 2/3…"). Under the stave each note is drawn as a red triangle: full height at the attack and a straight taper to a point over its length, stacked where notes overlap, with a black loudness trace beneath them. Above, arrows mark when each visual object lives ("1st star", "2 star", "3 star" as spans that overlap). It is the same envelope as the frames: instant attack, straight decay, overlapping spans.

**Lumigraph.** Soft colour sheets (red `#C9210E` L 43 C 80, cyan `#098491`, cream-gold, pink) on near-black 23–37%. One edge of every form is razor sharp, straight or with a small bright cusp where a fingertip presses the latex, and the other edge dissolves over 5–15% of the frame. One to three forms at a time, at the rim, the middle kept dark. CVM: the camera was undercranked, so tempo is not evidence (the 12 changes a second are an artefact of that).

**Wax Experiments and Spirals.** Wax: slow swelling black or white disc inside streaming grey marbling; nothing is drawn from frame to frame, the picture is a solid cut slice by slice. The disc grows from 7% to the full frame in 3.5 s (7.4–11.1 s) and the next one starts from a dot: the first occurrence here of swell, swallow, restart from a seed. Spirals: two patterns turning against each other for the whole excerpt, only the interference moves.

**Where published accounts and frames disagree.** (1) Accounts of "precise synchronization": peri-onset averages are flat for Studien 6 and 7, weak for 5 and 8, and clearest in Optical Poem's opening and Allegretto. Individual accents do land within about 20–120 ms. He himself ranks rhythm coordination as "secondary", which fits. YouTube and archive files may shift sound by a frame or two. (2) filmportal's search summary says each Study took several hundred drawings; on-ones animation of 115 s needs about 2,700 exposures, so several hundred would require heavy re-use, which is not visible in Nr. 6. (3) Optical Poem is dated 1937 by CVM and 1938 elsewhere. (4) Kreise excerpt: title card 1933–34.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **A note has a shape in time: attack, then a long decay.** Studie 6 block 0.19 → 1.86% in 4 pictures, then halving each picture; Studie 8 box empties 22 → 1% in 8 frames after a 4-frame fill; Optical Poem disc full in one frame then 3.4–4 s of recession; Komposition teardrop full in 1–2 frames, 27 frames out; American March score, one full-height triangle tapering per note. Attack is 1–5 frames everywhere; decay is 2 to 80 times longer, with the exception of Studie 7's tutti (4 up, 2 down) and the block strokes (3 up, 1 down), which are percussive.
- **Birth from a seed or hairline, death to a hairline, dot or residue.** Dots (Kreise cohorts, Radio Dynamics disc), hairlines (Radio Dynamics bars, American March bars, Studie 7 dashes), outline that fills (Studie 8). Nothing fades in as a whole; opacity fades are rare (Kreise red rings' low-full-low is the nearest).
- **Spans overlap; the field seldom empties.** The next figure opens 0.04–0.3 s before the last is gone (Studie 6, Studie 8, American March). Full black occurs at sentence ends only: 0.12 s (Studie 7, 17.77–17.9 s), 0.15 s (Studie 6, 53.75 s), 0.17 s (Studie 6, 57.97–58.13 s).
- **Residue accumulates and then the whole field speaks once.** Studie 7: 3 → 21 dashes, then 1% → 18% in 4 frames. Studie 8: 14 → 40 bars over 4.5 s, then all swell. Komposition: cubes added by row (1, 2, 2, 2, 5, 6, 7) then the whole plane pushes forward. Each earlier mark keeps its place until that moment.
- **Form at three rates.** Beat (0.17–0.42 s), phrase (1.0–3.2 s), section (8–25 s). Radio Dynamics: 10 frames / 4 cells = 40 frames / about 26 s. Komposition: 4-frame stroke / 25–28-frame phrase / ABA of sections.
- **Two layers with different clocks.** Studie 5 swarm 2.5–2.9 s vs group 0.2–0.5 s; Allegretto ground flutter 0.167 s vs music 0.44 s vs capsule falls of about 1.2 s; Kreise still ground vs cohorts; Radio Dynamics' still side discs vs a 3.15 s central swell.
- **Small number of shape families, kept apart, then crossed.** Studie 6: crescents, strokes, blocks; they alternate by section and are cut together only in the last quarter (110–116 s). Optical Poem: discs, then targets, squares, capsules, triangles, then discs again.
- **Colour is a function of age or size.** Komposition teardrop green → yellow → orange → red-brown with shrinkage; Radio Dynamics disc orange → maroon with growth; American March pale → saturated → dark; Optical Poem tint by order of arrival and loudness.
- **The ground is an instrument.** Kreise ground flips blue ↔ green in one frame for 0.75 s; Radio Dynamics ground travels until figure and ground are equal; Allegretto ground rings change hue every few frames; Komposition's wall becomes colour bands.
- **Accents land early.** Studie 7 swell begins 60–100 ms before its sound and peaks +20 ms; Optical Poem discs 60–120 ms early, motion peak at −2 frames; Studie 5 motion leans before the beat. Never clearly late except Studie 8's third and fourth breaths (+40 to +90 ms) and Allegretto (+1 frame).
- **Motion laws are physical, not eased by taste.** Constant-speed recession (Optical Poem, 1/diameter linear in time); geometric growth and decay (Studie 6, Kreise small cohort); constant step (Radio Dynamics bars, Kreise large cohorts, teardrop). A phrase keeps one law from start to end, and resets in one frame.
- **Repetition is exact, variation is in what it carries.** Teardrop passes 28 frames each, three different post formations; Radio Dynamics' four compositions recur in the same order with new hues; Kreise cohorts repeat with new counts and positions.
- **Method stated.** He divided the record into single frames, "put it down on paper" and used the music as an architectural ground plan (CVM correspondence text, 1947 draft). Motion Painting drops this: camera exposed after each stroke for nine months, close sync abandoned, the picture "a free walk" beside the music.
- **The generative pattern seen:** lay out a frame ruler; mark each event's start and length as a wedge; give each wedge one figure with one growth law, one colour path and one home position; overlap wedges; let finished figures stay as thin residue; vary only which family and which layer carries the next phrase; spend the residue in one tutti; leave a short dark.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Oskar Fischinger.** *Rule the time before the form: give every event a start and a length on one ruler, open its figure from a seed in one to five units, let it decay in place many times longer on one law, overlap the next, keep a thin residue, and spend the residue in one unison per section before a short blank.*
Process:
1. Rule the time first. Fix one unit (about 40ms) and write every event the view can show as a span on one ruler: a start and a length, both in whole units. Nest three rates: a pulse of 4–10 units (170–420ms), a phrase of 4–7 pulses (1.0–3.2s), a section of 8–25s. An event whose end cannot be marked gets no motion. If the unit changes, every later step is redone.
2. Give each span one envelope, and make it lopsided: full strength within 1–5 units of its start (one unit for the strongest event), then a decay 2–80 times longer than the attack. The reversed envelope (3–4 units up, 1–2 down) is kept for step 7 and for nothing else.
3. Give each figure one home position and two small ends. It opens from a seed (a dot or a hairline, a tenth of its peak or less) at the place where it will live, and closes to a hairline or a dot at the same place. Nothing fades in or out as a whole. One state turns into another through that minimum (form, line, form), so the line is the state every form shares.
4. Bind size and colour to age by one law per phrase: an equal step each unit, a constant ratio each unit (halving or doubling), or a constant speed of recession (the reciprocal of the size grows by equal steps). The law holds from the first unit to the last and resets in one unit. Colour walks one way along a path of 3–5 stops as the figure ages, starting at the path's lightest or most saturated end. A phrase that recurs keeps its length to the unit; only what it carries changes.
5. Overlap the spans and keep the residue. The next figure opens 40–300ms before the last has closed, so the field does not empty inside a phrase. A finished figure stays at its home as a thin still mark, a tenth to a thirtieth of its peak, drifting less than 2% of the view per second. The count of marks (from 3 up to 20–45 in a section) is the record of what has happened.
6. Run two clocks. One layer cycles at least 5 times slower than the other (2.5–3.2s against 0.2–0.5s) and the two never share a period. The ground is a third voice: it may change whole in one unit to mark a section and holds the new state for at least 750ms.
7. Spend the residue once per section. After the quietest stretch, all the marks swell together (to 10–18% of the view in 4 units, back in 2), then nothing moves for 120–170ms, then the next section starts from about three seeds. The largest unison comes in the last quarter. Up to that point about three families of form take turns and do not mix.
8. Lead the accent. Start an attack 60–120ms before the moment it marks, so the peak and the moment coincide. In an interface the attack starts at the input, not at the reply.
9. Write the still form of every event before animating it: where the figure sits, which stop of its colour path states its age, and what mark it leaves. If an event has no still form it has no span, and it is cut.
Why it works:
- An abrupt onset is found by the eye without search (salience by transient), and a slow decay leaves time to see what arrived and where it went. Equalise the two (ease in, ease out) and the peak falls mid-way, so the event has no moment and is felt late. Reverse them and it reads as a departure.
- A figure that opens and closes at one place is read as one object with a life (object persistence), so a change of state is seen as the same thing changing. A whole-view fade has no location until it is half there, and it wipes the record. Remove the residue and the user has to remember the count.
- Parts that move together are grouped as one (common fate). After a stretch in which every mark kept its own time, a single unison reads as one event made of all of them. Multiply the unison and the field becomes one object that blinks; the parts can no longer be told apart.
- Two layers at clearly different rates separate without shadow or overlap, and the slow one gives the fast one a reference. With equal periods they fuse into one pattern.
- A change that begins at its cause, within about a tenth of a second, is bound to it. One that begins after is read as a separate, later event: lag.
- A law and a length that repeat exactly set the rate the eye expects (adaptation), so any difference in a repeat is read as content. Durations tuned one by one have to be read again each time.
- Colour bound to age puts elapsed time into a single still frame, which is what lets the whole logic survive with motion off.
Colour relations: the ground is one dark value or one flat hue on 67–85% of the view; figures average 1–4% and peak at 10–18%; a figure stands 25–80 L from the ground, except where meeting the ground is the event; one opposed pair of hues with a shared middle that both can travel to; one hue family per section; the attack takes the path's lightest or most saturated stop and age moves it one way (darker, cooler or warmer, chosen once); where two figures overlap the overlap is a planned third colour; the residue takes the path's quietest stop and about 1–2.5% of the view in sum.
Space and line: figures are small in a large ground; the centre is kept for the stillest or the strongest event; a lead sits off-centre, near 0.3 of the width, and answers mirror about the vertical axis; position carries order (each successor one step along a diagonal, or rising as it lives); depth comes from size alone (1 down to 0.3); figures may cross the edge of the field; about three families of form per section, with the hairline as their common minimum; a leading edge is crisp, and a trailing edge may be soft to show direction; anything near the size of the view lasts 10 units or less.
Type requirement: text is never a figure. It arrives whole in one unit, holds its size and its place, and shows age by a step of weight or tone, so the family needs at least three such steps (new, settled, residue). Figures are tabular, so a changing number does not move its neighbours from their home positions. Every span is also stated in words or digits (a count, a time, "3 of 20"), because the ruler has to be readable without the motion.
Motion and interaction: unit about 40ms; attack 40–200ms with no easing (a hard step, or an equal step per unit); decay 80ms–4s on the phrase's law (linear, halving per unit, or reciprocal), never ease-in-out; pulse 170–420ms; phrase 1.0–3.2s, repeated to the unit; overlap 40–300ms; unison 160ms up and 80ms down, once per section; blank 120–170ms; a whole-ground change holds 750ms or more. Feedback starts on press and peaks on the reply. Events that arrive faster than the pulse are gathered into one figure per pulse. No part of the view passes from light to dark more than 3 times a second, and the ground never alternates. Nothing loops while no event is in progress. Reduced motion: every figure appears in one step at its residue size at its home, age is read from the colour stop and the written count, and the unison becomes one change of tone on the marks plus a line of text; order, overlap of spans, residue and the single summing moment all remain.
Fails when: an event's end cannot be known (an open-ended wait has no span, so only the slow clock and a plain statement of time are honest); the view has no events (long reading, reference tables), where nothing should move; events outrun the pulse for a whole section; fewer than about three events fall in a section, so there is no residue to spend and the unison is empty show; a message must stay until it is acknowledged (errors, destructive confirmations), since decay would remove it; the record must not be visible to others on a shared screen.
Signature (do not carry together): pale hand-drawn crescents and hairlines swelling on black · saturated discs and concentric rings pulsing on a flat blue ground · every figure struck to the notes of an orchestral score.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Invoice import review, desktop 1280 × 800, warm paper ground, one brand green, a serif for headings, no sound (the films are dark, saturated, abstract and set to music). Twenty invoices are checked by the server one at a time.
1. *Ruler.* Unit 40ms. Pulse 400ms (10 units), one invoice per pulse. Phrase 2.0s (5 pulses), so the table is ruled in groups of five rows. Section 8s (20 rows). Events on the ruler: row checked (520ms), row failed (3.24s), group closed (one unit), file closed (240ms and a 160ms blank). Waiting for the upload itself has no known end, so it gets no figure, only the text "Uploading".
2. *Envelope.* A checked row's band is full in 40ms and decays over 480ms (12 times longer). A failed row decays over 3.2s (80 times).
3. *Home and ends.* Each row is 1232 × 32px (3.9% of the screen). Its figure is a tint behind the row's text: it opens from a 1px hairline on the row's lower edge and closes to the same hairline. The text never moves, fades or changes size.
4. *Law and colour.* The band's height halves every 80ms: 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1px. Its colour walks four stops, from the brand green at full chroma at the attack to the grey of the table rules at the hairline. Every checked row takes exactly 520ms.
5. *Overlap and residue.* A row's decay (480ms) outlasts the pulse (400ms), so the next band opens 80ms before the last is a hairline. Each hairline stays: 1232px², 0.12% of the screen, a thirty-second of the band. After 20 rows the rules cover 2.4%. A failed row leaves a 3px rule, a cross icon and the words "2 errors".
6. *Two clocks.* Fast: the row bands, 400ms. Slow: a rule in the left margin beside each group of five grows by a fifth of the group's height with each checked row and, when the group closes, becomes the text "5 of 5" in one unit: 2.0s, five times slower. The ground does not change; this view has one section.
7. *Unison.* After the twentieth row all 20 hairlines thicken together from 1 to 6px in 160ms (2.4% to 14.4% of the screen) and return in 80ms. Nothing moves for 160ms. Then the summary line, "20 checked, 2 need attention", appears whole in one unit. This happens once.
8. *Lead.* On pressing "Check", the first row's hairline is drawn at once; the server's first reply comes about 100ms later and the band peaks on it.
9. *Still form.* With reduced motion each rule appears in one step with its reply. The newest rule is green, the three before it step toward grey, and the margin text counts "3 of 5". The unison is replaced by the summary line. No band is drawn.
- *Type.* Amounts in tabular figures. A row's text is medium weight while its band is alive and regular once it is a rule; group counts are in the small label size.

Result: a quiet paper-coloured table that checks itself row by row and sums up once. No black ground, no discs, no rhythm to hear. What survived is the ruler, the lopsided envelope, the home position, the residue as the progress record, two clocks, and one unison followed by a blank.

## Space & composition
- Figures are small against a large dark or flat ground: mean white coverage 1–4% (Studie 5: 2.9%, Studie 7: 1.9% mean, 90th percentile 4.4%; Studie 8: 3.6%, 90th percentile 6.8%), peaks 10–18%. Optical Poem 67–85% ground; American March 77% black.
- Position carries pitch-like and phrase-like order: Studie 8 boxes step down a diagonal left to right; Studie 6 blocks climb as they live; Optical Poem chains lie on a diagonal.
- Answers are mirrored about the vertical axis (Studie 6 48–51 s; Radio Dynamics side discs) while single leads sit off centre (Studie 5 swarm at x 0.27–0.35).
- Depth comes from size alone: Optical Poem discs at 1.0 → 0.3, targets 3 : 1 along a column, Komposition's floor rows larger toward the viewer. No shading except photographed light.
- Figures cross the frame edge freely: strokes leave at the top (Studie 6), Radio Dynamics side discs are cut by the edges, the Optical Poem arc is wider than the frame.
- Centre is reserved for the stillest or the strongest event (Studie 6 ring, Optical Poem attack disc, Allegretto disc, Radio Dynamics seed). The Lumigraph does the reverse and keeps the centre dark.

## Form, line & typography
- Families: crescent or hook with a blunt lit head and feathered tail; tapered stroke; trapezoid block with a ragged lower edge; hairline; disc; concentric target; ring; lozenge; capsule; teardrop. About three per section.
- One figure is redrawn into the next through a minimum: block → line → block; crescent → line → crescent; disc → dot. A line is the common state of every family in the Studies.
- Charcoal grain at trailing edges gives direction of travel; the leading edge is crisp. Colour films use crisp edges all round; the Lumigraph one crisp, one soft.
- Orientation follows path or beat: hooks flip with direction (Studie 5), dashes turn 45° per accent (Studie 7).
- Same-size siblings: 3 (Studie 7, Optical Poem), 6 (Studie 5 rings, Kreise rings), 7 (Allegretto capsules), 13 (Studie 5 hooks).
- Ornament Sound strips: rows of teeth, waves and lens shapes as seen, used as the sound itself. He states that flat figures sound soft and sharp deep ones loud.
- Titles only: upright grotesque capitals and lower case, white on black, centred (Studie 6 title card); Kreise's advertising ending turns rings into script lettering in motion.

## UI translations
1. **Timing plan:** Before any component, draw a ruler in 40ms units and mark each event as a wedge, full height at its start and tapering to a point at its end, as in the *American March* score; stack wedges where events overlap.
2. **Envelope:** Press feedback is full in one unit and decays over 0.4–0.5s; a major arrival is full in one unit and recedes for 3–4s (*Optical Poem* disc). Never `ease-in-out`.
3. **Easing:** Use one law per transition and write it as keyframes: halving per 40ms step (*Studie 6* block), an equal step per unit (*Radio Dynamics* bars), or scale = 1 / (1 + kt) with k near 0.6 per second for recession (*Optical Poem*: 1.0 to 0.35 in 3s). Reset with `steps(1)`.
4. **Birth and death:** A panel opens from a 1px line along one of its own edges and closes back to it; a toast collapses toward its base to a hairline (*Studie 8* box: 4 units to fill, 8 to empty). No whole-element opacity fades.
5. **State change:** Morph through the line: block to hairline to block in two or three units, so the user sees one thing change (*Studie 6*, *Studie 7*).
6. **Progress:** Each finished step leaves a bar about 0.1% of the view at the place where it happened (*Studie 8*: 14 bars grow to 40). The row of bars is the progress indicator; there is no separate track.
7. **Completion:** When the task ends, every bar thickens together once (1% to 10–18% of the view in 160ms, back in 80ms), followed by 120–170ms of stillness (*Studie 7*).
8. **Loading:** A known wait is one seed that swells on a constant step and restarts from the seed, 3.15s per turn (*Radio Dynamics* disc), beside a stated time; an unknown wait shows text only.
9. **Layers:** Run a slow layer (2.5–3s) under a fast one (0.2–0.5s) and keep their periods unequal (*Studie 5* swarm and group).
10. **Colour by age:** New items take the lightest stop and darken or cool as they age: pale `#A6B180`, then orange `#A27E47`, then red-brown `#904A30` (*Komposition in Blau* teardrop), on a ground that keeps 67–85% of the view.
11. **Ground change:** A change of mode replaces the whole ground in one unit and holds at least 0.75s (*Kreise*: `#07048E` lines to `#0F8943`). It does not alternate.
12. **Lead:** Start a visual hit on pointer-down, 60–120ms ahead of the confirmed result, so the peak meets the reply.
13. **Repetition:** A recurring transition has one length to the unit (28 units for each teardrop pass); vary what it carries, not how long it takes.
14. **Stillness:** After a dense passage hold one small figure, 5–20% of the height, at the centre of a quiet field (*Studie 6* ring), and leave 6–9 still units between phrases.
15. **Accessibility:** Do not carry the 4-unit full-field alternation of *Radio Dynamics* or the *Kreise* advertising flips: six a second breaks the limit of 3 flashes a second (WCAG 2.3.1). Under `prefers-reduced-motion` figures appear at residue size in one step, age is shown by colour stop and count, and the unison is one tone change with a line of text.
16. **Accessibility:** Residue and age never carry meaning alone; each bar has a text or icon equivalent, and a message that needs an answer does not decay.

## What this artist would reject
- Music illustrated one to one: he wrote that the films are no illustrations of music, and rhythm match is secondary to new ways of motion (CVM correspondence).
- Motion of unmarked length: every event in the score has a start and an end on the frame ruler.
- Fading a whole picture in and out: figures are born from hairlines or seeds and end in them.
- Figures that all change at the same moment all the time: unison is spent once per section.
- Wiping the slate between events when a thin residue can stay.
- Symmetrical ease-in-out: frames show instant or 4-frame attacks with long decays, or constant-rate growth with a one-frame reset.
- Committee-made work and photographed realism (My Statements are in My Work; True Creation).
- Large figures at rest: anything near the full frame lasts 1–10 frames.

## Signature prompt seed
Design the screen as a Fischinger score. First draw a ruler in 40ms units and give every event a wedge: full at its start, tapering to a point. On a near-black ground (`#050505`) or one flat blue (`#1A4370`) that keeps about three quarters of the view, each figure opens from a hairline or a dot at its own place, is full within one to five units, then shrinks for many times longer by halving or by an equal step, cooling from pale yellow (`#A6B180`) through orange to red-brown (`#904A30`) as it ages. Overlap each figure with the next and leave a thin bar behind for each. Run a slow layer under a fast one. Once per section let every bar swell together, then hold 150ms of stillness. Start hits 80ms before the moment they mark. No ease-in-out, no whole-screen fades, no flicker; with reduced motion show the bars, the colour stops and the counts.
