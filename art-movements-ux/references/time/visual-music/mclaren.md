# Norman McLaren — Visual Music

## Essence
McLaren builds each film on one self-set technical limit (one tool, one mark, one operation), then puts everything into what changes between one frame and the next. Music is the script: it is measured note by note in frames before the images are made, or the sound is itself drawn, so the smallest image event is one frame (42 ms) and the largest is a musical section. Marks hit at full strength on the note and then decay, black and stillness count as rests, and a simple generating rule (a split every 15 s, a delayed copy, a stripe per cycle of sound) produces the density.

## Key works examined
All clips are the National Film Board of Canada's own YouTube uploads, 23.976 fps. Shares of frame come from thresholded frames at native rate (240 × 180 or 480 × 360). "Window" times are positions in the upload, including the NFB logo.

- **Dots, 1940 (pen and ink on clear 35 mm, picture and sound both drawn; 2:30 upload, about 1:20 of action; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P_DAzhQgTXA).** One figure per sound: full size at once, then a decay. *(measured: clip01, 62–142 s frame by frame: figure count, area, position, births against onsets; frame sheets 65–71 s and 129–132 s, sound sheets 62–82 s and 129–132 s; colour of 3 frames)*
- **Boogie-Doodle, 1940 (drawn on film to Albert Ammons' piano; 3:21; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fZs5kwNwVs0).** A barrier that crosses the frame once per bar is the bar line. *(measured: clip09, 54–194 s: bar sweeps, counts, ground changes; sheet 75–78 s; colour of 4 frames)*

- **Lines Vertical, 1960 (with Evelyn Lambart; lines ruled on black leader; music by Maurice Blackburn; 6:05; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_k7B5J9XYNo).** One line becomes many by splitting on a fixed clock. *(measured: clip08, 45–345 s: line count, width, coverage, positions, speeds, ground changes; colour of 6 frames)*

**How far the sync figures can be trusted.** These uploads carry the sound late. Synchromy fixes the size of the error, because by its maker's own account the picture *is* the soundtrack. In that upload each image best matches the sound 3–4 frames later (125–165 ms): at the right lag every picture–sound pitch pair fits one constant, and at lag 0 they do not. Dots shows the same constant delay (median 143 ms over its 15 solo beats, 110 ms over all matched flashes). The two slower Begone and Blinkity passages show 65–110 ms. All offsets below are raw readings. Read them as "constant lead of the image, most or all of it the transfer". The informative number is the spread, not the mean.

## Detailed observations

**Dots.** One flat red field fills the frame; the figures are blue blobs with a thin pale-blue rim. From 62 to 78 s there is one sound per second (onsets 62.19, 63.19, 64.19 … 78.19) and exactly one dot per sound, alone. Every dot is born at its largest in a single frame. There is no growth-in. Measured at 64.04 s: area 0.88% of frame, then 0.49, 0.40, 0.33, 0.31 on successive frames. Over ten solo dots, half the area is gone after 1–3 frames (median 2, ≈83 ms), 90% after 7–17 frames (typically 8, ≈330 ms), and the last speck dies at frame 19–22 of 24. That leaves 2–6 empty frames before the next birth. The curve is a decay: a plunge, then a long thin tail, the same shape as the drawn click's own envelope in the sound sheet. The dot also drifts while it shrinks: about 0.03–0.09 of the frame net over its life when the sounds fall in pitch (64–69 s), up to 0.24–0.32 from 72 s, about half of it in the first 5 frames. The hand shows as a wobble: path length is 1–7 times the net move. Pitch and place agree in this opening. The sounds fall from about 1.7 kHz to 100 Hz between 62 and 70 s while the dot's height goes 0.17, 0.28, 0.48, 0.66, 0.80, 0.87 of the frame (top to bottom), and its birth area goes 0.88 → 1.9%. The ninth beat, the lowest and longest sound (about 0.35 s against 0.1 s), is answered by a four-armed star of 11.4% of the frame (39 × 53% of width and height), about 10 times any dot before it. The sequence then repeats and ends on 12.2% at 78.06 s. Later, size and pitch or loudness hardly correlate (Spearman −0.20 with spectral centroid, +0.10 with dB, n ≈ 255), so the mapping is strict for the theme and free afterwards. Births per figure overall: median area 1.4%, 10–90% range 0.45–4.5%, maximum 12.2%. The frame is almost always nearly empty: mean coverage 0.2% (62–70 s), 1.0% (78–86 s), 2.4% (94–102 s), 2.8% (134–142 s); median figure count 1, then 3–5, peak 34. Of the clear births, 88% have an onset at the constant lag (chance 41%). At 129.6–131.6 s a swarm of about 33 dots swells out of one point. From 130.17 s two different swarms are drawn on alternate frames (one on even frames, one on odd), so the eye holds both expanding rings at once: one is out at the edge while the next is still a speck. Each ring goes from a speck to beyond the frame edge in about 0.6–0.8 s, with its dots growing as they spread, so each figure still reads as one attack. There are no cuts after the title; the ground never changes.

**Boogie-Doodle.** Two actors: a solid blue disc (about 1–2% of frame, white crescent on its left, black on its right, a misregistered two-dye print) and a black calligraphic line figure (heart, loop, spiral, wing). Ground: red `#B02207` for all but two passages. Vertical diagonal bars cross the frame and part the two. I found 116 bar events between 55 and 194 s. Through the first 20 bars the interval between them is 1.25–1.34 s (median 1.29 s), which is four beats of the measured 0.325 s pulse: one bar line per musical bar, all through. Early bars enter near the figure and cross 0.07–0.14 of the width per frame (1.7–3.4 frame widths/s), out in 4–8 frames. From 78 s they enter alternately from left and right edge (0.05, 0.96, 0.09, 0.93 …), move 0.06–0.09 of the width per frame, and stay on screen 10–14 frames. The disc and the line react to the bar. In 75.71–76.0 s three bars sweep left in 5 frames, the heart is dragged left with them and the disc is thrown right. Then the heart chases back across in about 8 frames, the disc shrinks to a speck in 5–6 frames, and swells again over about 14. Figures change by redrawing the same outline (heart → two lobes → rabbit ears) over 6–12 frames, without a cut. Twice the colour pair is swapped (red line on blue) for exactly four bars, 93.9–99.2 s and 125.4–130.5 s (5.3 and 5.1 s; starts 31.5 s apart, ≈24 bars). Three of the four switches pass through 1–3 black frames. Figure count rises from 2–4 (54–134 s) to 5–5.5 (154–194 s), coverage 3.6 → 7.6%. Held frames: none (median change 1.8% every frame).

**Begone Dull Care.** Three parts. *I (about 40–208 s, fast).* Full-frame painted textures (measured `#CABDB3` cream with `#C22335` and `#B1445B` blots; `#A31512` red and `#8E7281` mauve) alternate with engraved marks on black. The ground flips between dark and light 82 times in 150 s, median spacing 0.25 s but clustered: a burst of 4–6 flips within half a second, then seconds without. 40% of the flips fall within 50 ms of a sound onset at lag 0–40 ms, against about 20% at any other lag: two-fifths are struck on the note, the rest run free. In 116.0–117.6 s columns of short red, white and green dashes on black hold each tilt for 4–6 frames (0.17–0.25 s), change tilt within one frame, and close in from the two edges until they cross and weave. At 117.6 s the weave is cut off within 2 frames and replaced by full-frame red and cream paint for 1.3 s in one piece. In paint passages the film strip slides as one band through many frames, so each picture is a different patch of the same texture; frame difference averages 8.7% and reaches 19–25%. *II (about 209–328 s, slow).* Black, with hair-thin white verticals (1.3–2.9% of width) and specks. Coverage mean 0.86%, median 0.2%, 27% of frames empty, longest black 5.2 s; at most 7 full-height lines and 21 specks. Half the frame-to-frame changes are invisible (<0.15%). A line stands for a sustained or resting sound, a speck for a short note. The best-measured gesture: a single line stands at x = 0.246 through a silence (297.4–297.7 s), then moves 0.247, 0.251, 0.259, 0.277, 0.303, 0.389, 0.523 on seven successive frames. Each step is 1.5–3.3 times the last, pure acceleration, and the line snaps apart at 297.97–298.05 s, on the piano entry at 297.99 s. The seven following notes (298.49, 298.72, 298.95, 299.17, 299.41, 299.62, 299.88; 0.22–0.23 s apart) each get one speck-with-tail, 2 frames long, then 3–5 black frames; births 298.17, .38, .59, .80, 299.05, .26, .51 s, spaced 0.21–0.25 s. They walk left across the frame (x ≈ 0.57, 0.63, 0.49, 0.37, 0.11, 0.04 on their brightest frames) as the run goes on and land at the left edge where the next line grows in. Then a bunch of lines gathers at the left edge while the piano thickens (300.0–304.3 s) and vanishes together between 304.25 and 304.5 s, at the next single note (304.56 s). *III (about 330–467 s, very fast).* Pulse of brush strokes. At 440 s a 17-frame cycle (0.71 s; autocorrelation peaks at 12, 15, 17 frames) repeats: a black stroke whips from left edge to right edge in 3–4 frames, an orange wash bursts over about half the frame in 1 frame and thins out over 6, a red stroke drops in at the fifth frame after it and lasts 3, then 5–8 almost white frames. At 345–455 s the change per frame has median 13%, with no held frames (4%). The slow part has almost no colour.

**Neighbours.** Live action in full daylight: green lawn `#566B56`, `#849271` 25–60% of frame, two men in pale blue and white shirts, painted house-fronts, an orange flower of under 1% that carries the plot. 77 cuts, median shot 3.28 s, longest 37.5 s at the start; shots shorten as the fight grows (about 2–3 s each at 344–438 s) and the last seven titles change every 1.25 s. Motion energy per block is 1–2% at the start, 4–9% in the meeting, 11–18% in the fight (345–410 s), 1–2% in the burial. In 142–148 s the two men circle each other on fixed feet. Their bodies slide on the lawn, and they swap sides about every 0.8 s (19–20 frames) at first and every 0.5–0.6 s (12–14 frames) later, an accelerating round, with a leg kicked out at each pass and a handshake at the end (147.8 s). The steps between frames are nearly equal (median change 3.1%), so it reads as smooth but slippery: momentum and inertia are missing, not the pose. No held frames in any window. The drawn card sound (onsets 5–8 per second in fights, long silences between) does not correlate with frame change on a 1-frame scale (|r| ≤ 0.06). On a half-second scale it does follow in the later scenes (r 0.57–0.61 in 285–355 s, 0.39 in 395–420 s, though the best lag sits at the edge of the ±0.5 s tested): sound and action share a swell, not a hit. That fits McLaren's note that the music was made after the shooting, to a planned rhythm. Published accounts disagree on the sound: Wikipedia describes marks scratched on the film edge, while McLaren's notes say the first card set. I did not test the waveform.

**Blinkity Blank.** Black ground, engraved figures in white, crimson, gold and green, mostly 14% × 16% of the frame in size (10–90%: 3–45% wide, 4–49% tall). 36% of frames hold nothing visible (35–54% for other thresholds) and 70% of the 5 s blocks stay at or under 2.5% mean luma. The 10 s blocks range from 0% to 72% empty. Of 475 visible runs, 305 (64%) are a single frame, 39 are two, 53 are 13 frames or longer. Of 475 gaps, 161 are 1 frame, 150 are 2–3, 130 are 4–12, 34 are longer, the longest 4.6 s (at 261 s). McLaren's note says most frames carry nothing at all. That does not hold for the whole film as transferred: about a third to a half, depending on threshold and afterglow, with a majority empty only in 100–120 s, 200–210 s and 260–280 s (55–72%). Visible coverage: median 1.4%, 10–90% 0.16–7.4%, and rare flashes over 20%. At 78–79.4 s a crimson spiky ball sits at left while green-white fragments arrive from the right frame by frame. No figure keeps its shape for 2 frames; the ball is redrawn each time in a different pose within the same 10% patch, so it reads as alive. At 79.42–79.63 s the two merge into one long form (about a third of the width) for 4 frames, then part, the ball shrinking back over 8 frames. The same flare reappears as a single-frame double picture at 80.21, 80.46 and 80.88 s, each followed by 4–10 frames of near black. The chords sound at 79.57, 80.27, 80.53 and 80.97 s, so each flash leads by 60–90 ms, steadily. At 177.33–177.58 s a figure reaches out to a small square spiral, which bursts: coverage 7.4, 18, 21, 37% on four successive frames, 3.1% on the next, 0 after that. The loudest chord crest (177.5–177.7 s) covers the burst, and 20 blank frames (0.83 s) follow while the chord dies. (The largest flash of the film, 93% of the frame, falls in 260–280 s.) Over the whole window 47% of onsets have a flash at that constant lag (chance 26%) and 53% of flashes an onset (chance 30%). About half is struck on the note, and the rest is free. The only long contiguous passages (13 frames to 16 s) are where a character (mostly a bird) must be recognised, as at 176.0–177.5 s, when a line with two claws folds into a body over 24 frames. Frames with more than 10% coverage are 0–3% of the film until 220 s, then 13% (220–240 s) and 8–11% (260–305 s).

**Lines Vertical and Lines Horizontal.** The same film, measured twice. The two line-count curves correlate at 0.98 with a 46-frame (1.9 s) shift (Horizontal starts later in its upload), and the numbers agree: 1 line at 45 s, 2 at 53.7, 3 at 83.7, 4 at 98.7, 5 at 113.8, 6 at 129.0, 7–8 at 144.0 (Vertical; +2 s in Horizontal): a new line every 15.0–15.3 s after the second one (30 s for the third). Medians per 15 s window (Vertical): 1, 2, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10, 12, 11, 8, 10, 10, 10, 13, 15, 10, 3, 2. Peak 33 lines at 285–305 s, then 10, 3, 2 and one line at the end with "END / FIN" on either side. Coverage goes 1.7% → 6% → 14–17% → 29% (max 47%) → 1.8%. Line width 1.1–2.4% of the frame in Vertical (median 1.5%), 1.2–3.5% in Horizontal. Speed is constant on each path. In 45–75 s the lone Vertical line crosses at 0.09–0.13 frame widths/s (median 0.10) and the Horizontal line at 0.10–0.18 frame heights/s. At the edge it turns in one frame (positions 0.874, 0.883, 0.874: no slowing), with turn points at about 0.16 and 0.86 of the width. McLaren's own notes say the lines neither slow rising nor speed up falling, and this measures true. Speeds later vary by line: median 0.12–0.14, 90th percentile 0.16–0.26 frame/s; in the Vertical version thicker lines are faster (thin 0.12, thick 0.15 frame/s, r 0.23), as the notes claim; in the Horizontal measurement this relation vanished (r −0.02). Lines meet, merge and separate again: 8–12 parallel verticals spread evenly, then all gather into two bundles and one white slab (10–90% count range per 30 s: 5–11, 9–14). The ground is one slow tint: in Vertical a grey (`#484749`) through purple, near-black (`#160009`), then red (`#750000`), brown and back; in Horizontal blue (`#004ABD`) for 85 s, cyan, then within 4 s (130–134 s) lilac to peach, then tan (`#E5BCA0`) to magenta (`#B027B6` from 198 s), then within 13 s (293–307 s) magenta to orange (`#FB8B00`), and back to blue. Colour changes are gradual and few (10–17 in five minutes); two of the large ones start within a second of a new line (Horizontal 130.2 s, line six at 130.9; Vertical 144.0 s, lines seven and eight at 144.0). In the dense stretch the lines are black in Horizontal, while in Vertical they stay pale.

**Pas de deux.** Black, with white outlines of dancers (backlit, high-contrast printing). Mean luma by minute: 2.0, 0.4, 1.2, 1.2, 0.9, 1.6, 0.9, 1.6, 3.2, 5.7, 9.0, 5.8, 14.7, 5.4%. By mean luma 87% of frames are near black (under 8%), and there are only 2 detected hard cuts (638 s and the end title); scenes join by cross-fades. One woman at about 30% of frame height alone for the first minutes (coverage 1.4%, 60–180 s), motion 0.15% per frame, 45% of frames held. Two figures (one blurred, large; one small) with a mirrored pair at 300–306 s (coverage 2.5%). Then a couple with up to 11 ghosts (coverage 10%, peak 18% at 560–680 s). In 534–552 s the copies of one slow step fan out into 8–11 standing outlines across about 35% of the frame. At 534.5–537 s, a pause, they collapse into one crisp figure within about 0.75 s; movement starting again at 537.2 s fans them out until 548.5 s. At 742–745 s the chain of raised arms is 10–11 copies deep; the first copy of the new pose and the last of the old are about 2 s apart, so about 5 frames per step over 10 gaps. This agrees with the notes (average stagger 3, 4 or 5 frames; range 2–20; maximum 11 exposures). Motion is not image-to-sound: correlations of frame change with sound rise are 0.0 ± 0.05 and with loudness on a half-second scale −0.14 to 0.29. Images stay in the same place while the harp and pan-pipe texture moves; tempo comes from the dancer. Sound onsets go from 1.5–3 per second (first eight minutes) to 3.4–4.3 (last five). Coverage and motion grow together in one long crescendo, cut to black at 730–740 s, and then comes the largest picture of all (close figures, luma 15–32% at 740–765 s) before a fade to the last still pair. Published accounts give eleven exposures as the maximum, and one NFB archive note says sometimes more. I could count at most 11.

**Synchromy.** Picture = soundtrack. Before 88.7 s one blue column (`#0029ED` to `#0018E2`, light stripe `#0053F9`, 9% of width) on a blue field; then 2 columns, 5 by 154 s, 7 by 205 s, 9 by 238 s, then 11 filling the frame from 268 s. Each note is white, pink or orange blocks stacked in a column, on for 1 frame and absent for the rest of its slot. In 90–92 s each note's stripes show for exactly 1 frame in 4, so the note is drawn once every 167 ms. Spacing 4 frames in 252 of 369 notes in 58–128 s (8 frames 32 times, 2 frames 46). At the calibrated lag the number of stripes in the frame's height times 44.5 Hz is the sound's fundamental. Over 369 notes the match is far above any other ratio (harmonic score 1.27–1.57 at 44–44.5 Hz per stripe, against 0.3–0.8 for other ratios), and stripe width tracks loudness (Spearman 0.74–0.82). The wide block is the loud note, the narrow block the soft one: exactly the card-and-shutter method in the notes (72 cards, six octaves, shutter width = volume). Colour of the columns changes at phrase boundaries: 74 change events in 34–454 s, first 41 s apart (88.7, 129.8 s), then roughly 5–15 s, then steady 1.3–1.5 s (328–349 s), 2.0 s (350–374 s), 2.6 s, 3.4 s. Within a phrase the colour holds absolutely. At 430–433 s chord blocks appear on every second to fourth frame: about 1 frame on, 1–2 off, so 30–40% of frames show notes and the rest show plain blue columns. Dense and quiet alternate: the frame goes black at 135–141 s and 250–256 s.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **The sound is measured first, in frames, and the images are cut to it** (Begone Dull Care, Boogie-Doodle, Blinkity Blank, Synchromy: notes and dope sheets; Dots and Synchromy where sound is itself drawn). Evidence: bar lines every 1.25–1.34 s = 4 beats (Boogie); one dot per second, 15 of 15 beats (Dots); 369 notes matched to stripes (Synchromy).
- **Hit hard, decay fast, then rest.** The figure is full size on frame 1 and half gone by frame 2 (Dots, 83 ms); one-frame stripes for a 4-frame slot (Synchromy); 1-frame flashes, 64% of visible runs (Blinkity); a burst over half the frame in 1 frame, thinning over 6 (Begone III); a four-frame burst and then 20 blank frames (Blinkity 177 s). Nothing eases in.
- **The gap is a composed element.** Empty frames 36% (Blinkity), 27% (Begone II), 2–6 frames before every Dots birth; 70–75% of frames without stripes in Synchromy's single- and double-voice passages; Pas de deux averages under 8% luma in 87% of its frames. In the sparse passages the sound is silent there too (Dots 62–78 s; the Begone line standing through the rest at 297.4–297.7 s).
- **Two reaction types, chosen per line of the score.** Strict (position, size and time all follow a musical parameter: Dots' opening pitch fall, Synchromy's stripes) and loose (about half the onsets get a figure, the rest is free: Blinkity 47%, Begone I 40%). Never one mapping for everything: in Dots the strict mapping applies only to the theme, later ones drop it (size vs pitch −0.20).
- **One sustained sound, one line; one short note, one speck** (Begone II; Boogie: the bar as bar line, the disc and curve as the two voices). Musical duration is written as length on screen.
- **Acceleration exists only where something is about to break** (Begone II line: 0.247 → 0.523 in 7 frames, multiplicative). Otherwise motion is constant and ends abruptly (Lines, 0.10–0.15 frame/s, one-frame bounce; Boogie bars 0.06–0.14 frame width/frame).
- **Regular generating clock under free detail.** A new line every 15.0–15.3 s (Lines, both); bars at 1.29 s, colour swaps at whole 4-bar lengths (Boogie); colour per phrase (Synchromy, 1.3–3.4 s); stagger of 3–5 frames (Pas de deux).
- **Delay as a multiplier** (Pas de deux: identical motion exposed up to 11 times, each a few frames later; rest merges them in about 0.75 s). The trail has length only while the thing moves.
- **Interleave two figures on odd and even frames** to show two at once without overlap (Dots 130.17–131 s: two swarms; Synchromy chords on alternate frames).
- **Turning a system 90° re-colours and re-scores the same motion into a new film** (Lines Horizontal): count curves correlate 0.98; the notes claim the turn brings in gravity.
- **Accumulate, then reduce to one.** Counts 1 → 33 → 2 (Lines); 1 → 34 → 1 (Dots); coverage 0.4% → 14.7% (Pas de deux minute 2 to 13) then a still pair; 1 → 11 columns then black (Synchromy).
- **Stated method:** he says he sets technical and artistic limits first, takes the easiest of equal options, lets the music stand as the script, borrows musical forms (ABA for Begone, Hindu classical form for Lines and Synchromy), and improvises inside them.
- **The generative pattern seen:** choose one drawing act and one musical measure in frames; let every sound make one mark; give it full force on its frame and let it decay; leave the black between; keep colour steady within a phrase; let a slow clock multiply the marks; return to one.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Norman McLaren.** *Set one limit (one mark, one operation) and count the events in time units before drawing; land each mark whole on its event, let it decay front-first and leave its place empty; bind one slow line strictly and mark about half of the rest; move at constant speed, accelerating only into a break; multiply by one slow rule, then return to one.*
Process:
1. Set the limit. One mark and one operation for the whole view: appear and decay, travel along one axis, or copy with a delay. A second shape is allowed only for a second voice. Ground, weight and the section's colour are locked from here on, and all expression goes into what differs between one moment and the next. If the limit changes, every later step is redone.
2. Count the script before drawing anything. List the events with their times and lengths in units of about 40 ms. Choose two or three tempo levels at least 4 times apart (unit 40 ms : slot about 160 ms : cycle about 1.3 s : generation about 15 s : the whole sequence) and use nothing between them; every duration in the view is a whole multiple of the level below it.
3. Write duration as extent. A sustained state gets one line that runs 90% or more of the view's length, 1–3.5% of the short side thick, and stands exactly as long as the state does. An instant gets one small mark, 0.5–4.5% of the view (typically about 1.4%). The rarest, heaviest event gets a mark about 10 times the usual one, once or twice in a sequence.
4. Split the events into one strict line and a loose remainder. The strict line is slow (about one event a second while it is being learnt) and bound in everything: one mark per event with no exceptions, its position stepping along one axis with one attribute of the event and its size with another. Of all the other events only 40–50% get a mark, on time; the rest pass unmarked. Never use one mapping for everything.
5. Arrive whole, decay front-first, then rest. The mark is at full size and contrast on the event's own unit, with no grow-in. It loses about half in the first 1–2 units (80 ms), 90% by about 330 ms and its last trace by 0.8–0.9 s; the briefest kind is on for 1 unit of a 4-unit slot. Its place then stays empty for 2–6 units (80–250 ms) before anything else arrives there. In a sparse passage 25–45% of the time shows the ground alone, and a pause in the events is a pause on screen.
6. Use three motions and no others. Travel at constant speed along one axis (0.10–0.15 of the view per second when it idles; a crossing sweep is on screen 170–580 ms), turning within one unit at a fixed line about 0.15 in from each edge, with no slowing. A pose held 4–6 units and changed in one. Acceleration only for a thing about to end: from standing still, each step 1.5–3.3 times the last over 6–7 units, finishing on the event that replaces it.
7. Grow density by one rule, never by hand. Either a slow clock admits one new unit per fixed interval (about a twentieth of the sequence), or a moving thing leaves delayed copies of itself (3–5 units apart, at most 11, about 2 s of its past) that exist only while it moves and fold into one within about 0.75 s when it stops. Counts run from 1 to 10–35, coverage from under 2% to about 30%.
8. Compose the whole on the count. Begin with one unit; put the fullest passage 80–90% of the way through; clear the view completely for a beat before the largest picture; end on one unit, still. Colour holds inside a section and changes only at a section boundary, where the ground may retint over 1.5–13 s as a new unit arrives.
Why it works:
- An abrupt onset has an instant and takes the eye; a ramp has none, so an eased arrival cannot be on its event. A decay that spends half its strength at once clears the place, so the next arrival is read as a new event; fade it evenly and neighbours smear into a texture.
- Figure and ground in time: the empty interval is what separates two events and resets adaptation. Remove the gaps and the hits become a steady flicker that the eye stops reading. A view that rests when the events rest is trusted to mean something when it moves.
- One strict line teaches the code, and the loose remainder keeps it from turning into a meter. Bind everything and the viewer stops looking, because each mark is predicted; bind nothing and marks meet events no more often than chance. Measured: 88% of strict births match an event against 41% by chance, and loose passages sit near half.
- Constant speed makes position a reading of elapsed time and makes no false promise of arrival. Acceleration is then odd, so it is salient, and when it is kept for endings the viewer learns that something speeding up is about to be replaced.
- Units born from one rule are grouped by likeness and common fate, so thirty read as one growing thing, and the count is a clock. Copies that exist only during movement make their spread a reading of speed and their folding a reading of rest. After the fullest passage one unit on an empty ground is the strongest contrast left, so the close needs no effect.
Colour relations: one flat ground takes 80–98% of the view and sits at an end of the value or chroma scale; marks cover 0.2–7% in sparse passages and up to about 30% at the peak; 2–3 hues per section, chosen for the section and held, never per item; the mark is opposite the ground in value or in hue; the widest hue contrast and the highest chroma are kept for the fullest passage, the quiet ones stay in one hue family; ground changes are few (10–17 in five minutes) and gradual; one mode has no hue at all. A swap of ground and mark is allowed for a whole cycle group (4 cycles), entered through 1–3 empty units.
Space and line: no depth, no horizon, no container; marks in the middle third or anywhere in turn; lines and sweeps enter and leave at the edge and run the full extent; equal columns of about 9% of the width, 1 to 11 of them, each filled from one side in proportion to strength; mirror symmetry about the centre axis only while two voices speak together; 1–3 line weights between 1% and 3.5% of the short side, the thicker allowed to move faster; the small irregularities of the tool are kept.
Type requirement: words are the layer that stays. Each event that must be read later has one line of text that appears whole in one step and does not decay; the mark is the event, the text is the state. Labels stand on either side of a mark, never on it. One sturdy weight that holds on a full-strength ground at one-step appearance; figures tabular, changing by whole steps on the slot or the cycle, never rolling.
Motion and interaction: arrival in one frame on the input or the event; decay to half in 80 ms, to a tenth in about 330 ms, gone by 800–900 ms, on a curve that is steep first and thin after; 80–250 ms of empty place after it; at most 3 arrivals a second in the view, faster events being gathered into the next slot as one mark; poses held 170–250 ms and switched in one frame; a reacting neighbour recoils in about 230 ms and recovers in about 580 ms; travel linear, turns in one frame; the only accelerating curve is the exit of a thing being replaced, 250–300 ms, ending on the replacement's first frame; an outline may be redrawn into another over 250–500 ms without a cut; copies trail 125–210 ms apart and fold in 750 ms. No ease-out, no ease-in-out, no overshoot. Marks that alternate faster than 3 a second, or that cover more than a few percent of the view in one frame, are not carried. Reduced motion: a mark appears whole and is removed whole at the end of its life, or stays until the next of its kind replaces it; travel becomes placement at the end position; the accelerating exit becomes a one-step swap; copies are not drawn, and one still marker shows the previous position while the value is changing; the slow clock still admits one unit per interval, in whole steps. The strict line's positions and sizes, the empty intervals, the count and the return to one are unchanged.
Fails when: events that must each be acknowledged come faster than 3 a second (only the loose half and the text count are left); a state has to stay visible and be read at leisure with no room for a text line (a decaying mark carries an event, never a state); an alert must not go away until it is answered; nothing discrete happens (long reading, a form being filled), where nothing should move; the content needs several unlike marks at once; the view has to look full at every moment (a hero, a shop window), since a quarter to a half of the time is ground; exact magnitudes must be read from the mark alone.
Signature (do not carry together): hand-scratched figures that flash for single frames on black · ruled lines splitting and regrouping on one flat saturated field · white ghost-multiples trailing a moving body.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

McLaren's procedure → a bulk invoice import in an accounting tool (desktop, 1280 × 800), off-white ground, one brand green, the system sans. No black, no sound, nothing scratched, nothing that splits.
1. *Limit.* One mark: a flat band, 40px high, in a strip at the right of the view (320 × 640). One operation: appear and decay. Nothing in the view slides in, scales up or bounces.
2. *Script.* Unit 40ms. Three levels: slot 160ms (4 units), server batch 1.28 s (8 slots), stage about 15 s (12 batches). The events, with rates: row accepted (hundreds a second), row rejected (one every few seconds), stage finished (five in an import).
3. *Duration as extent.* The running stage is one rule, 8px thick (1% of the height), across 1216 of the 1280px (95%), under its name; it stands while the stage runs. A rejection is the band: 80–320px wide, 0.3–1.25% of the view. An unreadable file, the one heavy event, gets a band of about 10% of the view, once.
4. *Strict and loose.* Strict: rejections. Every rejected row gets exactly one band; its height on the strip is the row's place in the file, in 16 steps, and its width is the number of faulty fields (80px each, up to 4). Loose: accepted rows get no mark of their own; about every second batch draws one thin tick at its place on the strip, and the exact count is kept in text.
5. *Arrive, decay, rest.* The band is at full tint in the frame the rejection is received: half at 80ms, a tenth at 320ms, gone by 880ms. That step of the strip then stays empty for at least 240ms. Rejections closer together than 340ms are gathered into the next slot as one band, so no more than 3 arrive in a second.
6. *Motion.* The rule stands still. When its stage ends it leaves by acceleration alone: steps of 4, 8, 16, 40, 100, 250 and 800px over 7 units (280ms), and the next stage's rule is there, whole, on the following frame. Nothing else travels.
7. *Density.* One rule: the table of rejected rows admits new lines only on the batch clock, every 1.28 s, as one group, so the list never jumps under the reader. It grows from 1 line to at most 20.
8. *Whole.* The import opens on one rule over an empty field. The fullest passage is validation, the fourth of five stages (80%). Before the result the strip is plain ground for one batch (1.28 s). It ends on one line of text, still: the number imported and the number to fix.
- *Colour.* The ground keeps over 90%. Bands and rule are one neutral, three value steps from the ground, through a whole stage; the brand green appears only on the closing line. Nothing is coloured per row.
- *Type.* One weight. Each rejected row is one text line that appears whole and stays; counts are tabular and step once per batch. Stage names stand beside the rule, never on it.
- *Reduced motion.* A band appears at full tint and stays until the next rejection replaces it; the rule is swapped in one step; ticks are not drawn. Places, widths, the batch clock, the cleared strip and the closing line are the same.
Result: a pale, mostly still import screen. No black, no flicker, no lines breeding, no trails. What survived is the single mark, the counted script, the strict line beside a loose one, the whole arrival with its front-loaded decay and empty place, acceleration kept for the exit, the one growth rule and the return to one.

## Space & composition
- One flat ground, one colour, no horizon or depth: red (Dots 98% of frame), blue (Synchromy 80–90%), black (Blinkity 95%+, Begone II 90%, Pas de deux 85–98%). Figures cover 0.2–3% of it for most of Dots, 1–7% in Blinkity.
- Figures are usually in the middle third but use the whole frame in turn: Dots' opening falls down one side at x ≈ 0.2–0.4, later sounds land anywhere; Boogie's bars cross the whole width.
- Symmetry about a vertical axis where two parts speak together (Begone I chevrons meeting in the middle; Synchromy columns mirrored about the centre; Pas de deux mirrored pair at 300–306 s).
- Edges matter: bars, strokes and lines enter and leave at the frame's edge, and run the full height (Begone II: lines 96% of the frame height); Lines' turning points at 0.16 and 0.86.
- Column layout: one to eleven equal columns of about 9% of the width each (Synchromy), each stripe stack starting from one side of its column and filling it when loud.
- Empty frame held as a place for the next event: 2–6 empty frames (Dots), 21 (Blinkity), 5 s (Begone II).

## Form, line & typography
- Vocabulary of few shapes per film: circle or blob, star, cross (Dots), disc and line figure (Boogie), vertical line and speck (Begone II), dashes (Begone I), scratched spiky balls, spirals, birds (Blinkity), straight lines (Lines), rectangles (Synchromy), filmed human outlines (Pas de deux).
- Edges: soft pen rim (Dots), misregistered two-colour edge (Boogie: white on one side, black on the other), torn paint (Begone), scratched with jitter that changes every frame (Blinkity). Lines ruled true by a six-foot steel rule yet with small nicks and slightly ragged ends kept.
- Line weights: 1.1–3.5% of frame width (Lines), 1.3–2.9% (Begone II), Synchromy blocks from a hairline to the full column width according to loudness.
- Lettering: multilingual title cards scroll on one blue field (Dots, Boogie: eight languages set in eight different styles, one line each); hand-painted script in three colours (Begone, Neighbours); scratched capitals that flicker (Blinkity); plain serif capitals set either side of the line (Lines: bilingual titles flanking the vertical line; END on the left, FIN on the right).
- Letters are treated as film events. Blinkity's credit frames show "MAURICE" as slashes and the cast as scratches; Dots' title is drawn in outline on the same red.
- Repetition with per-frame variation of the outline is the life of the mark (Blinkity: no two frames of the ball match).

## Rhythm, motion & time
Core timings (24 fps film, 23.976 fps transfer; 1 frame = 42 ms).

| Film | Pulse / unit | Figure life | Empty before next | Event vs sound (raw) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dots | 1.00 s solo, then 0.5, 0.25 s and faster | birth 1 frame, half area 2 frames (83 ms), 90% gone 7–17 (typ. 8), last speck 19–22 | 2–6 frames | birth 143 ms before sound ±≈20 (15 beats); 88% of births matched vs 41% chance |
| Boogie-Doodle | 0.325 s beat, 1.29 s bar (bar every 4 beats) | bar on screen 4–14 frames; disc shrinks in 5–6, regrows in ≈14 | none | bars 1.25–1.34 s apart (first 20 of 116 events) |
| Begone I | phrase by phrase; note by note in bursts | paint 1.3 s in a piece; dashes hold 4–6 frames | 0.1–0.25 s | 40% of flips on a note at lag 0–40 ms vs ≈20% elsewhere |
| Begone II | notes 0.22–0.23 s | mark 2 frames; line 1–8 s | 3–5 frames, up to 5.2 s | mark leads 64–110 ms |
| Begone III | 17-frame cycle, 0.71 s | stroke 3–4 frames, burst 1 + 6 frames decay, accent 3 | 5–8 near-white | no single lag found |
| Blinkity | free | 1 frame in 64% of runs | 1 frame 34%, 4–12 frames 27% | 60–90 ms; 47% of onsets, chance 26% |
| Neighbours | swap of sides 0.8 → 0.5 s | continuous | none | r ≤ 0.06 at 1 frame; 0.4–0.6 on a 0.5 s scale in the fight |
| Lines | 15.0–15.3 s | constant speed 0.10–0.15 frame/s | none | not a hit; counts with clock |
| Pas de deux | none regular; 0.75 s to collapse | trail 2–11 copies × 2–20 frames | none | ≈0 on frame scale |
| Synchromy | 167 ms slots (4 frames) | stripes 1 frame | 3 frames | offset 125–165 ms, then ±0 by calibration |

- **Onset shape.** The image peaks on its first frame in every note-driven film; there is no attack ramp. Decay is front-loaded: 50% in 2 frames, 90% in 8 (Dots).
- **Easing.** Constant (Lines, Boogie's bars), multiplicative acceleration before a break (Begone II: each step 1.5–3.3× the last over 6 frames), a one-frame turn at the edge (Lines, both versions), hold then spread (Pas de deux), no slow-in anywhere.
- **Density over time.** Dots 0.2% → 2.8% of frame, n 1 → 34; Lines count 1 → 33 → 2 (coverage 1.7% → 29%); Synchromy 1 → 11 columns, colour changes from one per 41 s to one per 1.3 s; Pas de deux luma 0.4% → 14.7%; Blinkity 1–2% → 4–7% late.
- **Tempo scale.** 42 ms (one frame, a stripe) : 167 ms (slot) : 1.29 s (bar) : 15 s (a new line) : 5–13 min (the form). Films pick two or three of these steps and nothing between.
- **Cuts.** Dots and Boogie have one ground and almost none (2 and 9 detected). Neighbours has 77 (median 3.3 s, shorter in the fight). Begone has 108 detected in parts I and III (median shot of the whole film 2.0 s, minimum 1 frame) and none in the 143 s around part II.
- **Stillness and black.** Held frames (change <0.12% at native rate): 41% Blinkity (75–135 s), 45% Begone II, 73% Synchromy in its first minute, 45% Pas de deux opening; 0% Lines and Boogie. The still frame is a musical rest, not a loading state.
- **Trail timing.** A 3–5 frame lag per copy over up to 11 copies (≈2 s of time visible in one picture, measured 742–745 s); a pause collapses the set in about 0.75 s (534.5–535.25 s).
- **Interleave.** Alternate-frame drawing gives two motions at 12 fps each (Dots 130.2–131.6 s).

## UI translations
1. **Feedback timing:** Show a press or a received event at full size in the same frame, then let it go: half the area in 80ms, 90% in about 330ms, the last trace by 0.9 s (*Dots*: 0.88, 0.49, 0.40, 0.33, 0.31% on five frames). No scale-up, no fade-in.
2. **Feedback timing:** Keep the place empty for 80–250ms after a mark has gone (*Dots*: 2–6 empty frames before each birth). Gather faster events into the next 160ms slot as one mark, and never show more than 3 arrivals a second.
3. **Feedback:** Let the neighbour of a struck element react: shrink in 210–250ms, recover in about 580ms (*Boogie-Doodle* disc: 5–6 frames down, about 14 up).
4. **State change:** Hold a pose 170–250ms and change it in one frame (*Begone Dull Care* dashes); where two states share an outline, redraw one into the other over 250–500ms without a cut (*Boogie-Doodle* heart to lobes).
5. **Transitions:** Give ease to exits only. A panel that is being replaced stands still, then leaves in 6–7 steps, each 1.5–3.3 times the last (about 290ms), and the new one is whole on the next frame (*Begone* II line: 0.246 to 0.523 of the width, breaking on the piano entry).
6. **Transitions:** Mark the boundary of a cycle with one divider that crosses the view at constant speed, on screen 170–580ms, once per cycle (*Boogie-Doodle*: one bar line every 1.29 s, four beats).
7. **Progress:** Move a progress mark at constant speed and turn or stop it within one frame (*Lines*: 0.10 of the width per second, turning at 0.16 and 0.86 with no slowing). Do not slow it near the end.
8. **Progress:** Let the count be the clock: admit one new unit per fixed interval, about a twentieth of the expected length (*Lines*: a line every 15.0–15.3 s), and finish by reducing to one.
9. **Loading:** A known wait is a sparse run of one-unit marks in fixed slots (*Synchromy*: one frame on in four, 73% of frames still in the first minute), slowed in an interface to 3 a second or fewer; under reduced motion a still column and a stated time.
10. **Data in motion:** Bind one slow series strictly (place from one attribute, size from another, one mark per value) and sample the busy ones at about half their events (*Dots* theme against *Blinkity Blank* at 47%).
11. **Data in motion:** Write a sustained state as a full-height line and an instant as a speck two units long (*Begone* II), so duration is read as extent.
12. **Data in motion:** Trail a moving value with up to 11 copies 125–210ms apart, about 2 s of its past, and fold them into the value within 0.75 s when it stops (*Pas de deux* 534–537 s). A value at rest has no trail.
13. **Density:** Start a live view with one unit, reach the fullest state at 80–90% of the sequence (*Lines*: 1 to 33 lines, 1.7% to 29% cover), clear it for a beat before the largest picture and end on one.
14. **Stillness:** Treat the unchanged view as a rest: when no events arrive nothing moves, for as long as 5 s at a time (*Begone* II: 27% of frames empty, 45% held). No idle loop fills the gap.
15. **Colour:** One flat ground at 80–98% (`#B70000`, `#0029ED` or `#050505`) and 2–3 hues per section; change colour only at a section boundary, with ground retints over 1.5–13 s (*Lines Horizontal*: `#004ABD` to `#E5BCA0` to `#B027B6`).
16. **Type:** Set the two labels of a pair on either side of the mark they describe (*Lines*: END left, FIN right of the last line); text appears whole and stays.
17. **Accessibility:** Do not carry the single-frame flashes (*Blinkity Blank*: 64% of runs one frame, flashes up to 93% of the frame), the alternate-frame interleave of *Dots* (12 a second) or *Synchromy*'s 6 notes a second: all exceed 3 flashes a second (WCAG 2.3.1). Under `prefers-reduced-motion` marks appear and leave in one step, travel becomes placement and trails are not drawn.
18. **Accessibility:** A decaying mark never carries a state alone: every event that matters later has a text line that stays.

## What this artist would reject
- Easing in: figures do not grow to size, they arrive at full size (Dots, Blinkity, Synchromy).
- An image that keeps running through every sound: much of the time it is black (Blinkity 36%, Begone II 27%, and the gaps before each Dots birth).
- A one-to-one decoration for every sound throughout: the strict mapping is kept for themes and one line (Dots opening) and loosened later (Blinkity 47%, Begone I 40%).
- Motion with a generic ease curve: constant or exploding, never soft at both ends.
- Independent colours per item: colour is set for a section or phrase and held.
- A finished, cleaned-up mark: rims, misregistration, scratch jitter and torn paint are kept.
- Illustrated story as the point of the motion: Neighbours' story is told by tempo and figure, the others by count and pulse.
- Free-standing elements with no source: every figure comes from a sound, a splitting line or a delayed copy.

## Signature prompt seed
Design this screen as a McLaren film frame. One flat ground, red (`#B70000`), blue (`#0029ED`) or black (`#050505`), keeps at least 80% of the view, and there is one kind of mark only: a blob, a ruled line or a stack of pale stripes (`#C1C1EB`). Count the events in 40ms units first. Each mark lands at full size on its event, loses half in 80ms and most of the rest in a third of a second, then leaves its place empty. A lasting state is one thin line the full height of the view; an instant is a speck. One series is mapped strictly (place and size), the others get a mark about half the time. Lines travel at constant speed and turn in one frame; only a thing about to be replaced accelerates. Add one line on a slow clock, build to the fullest state late, clear the view, end on one. Colour changes only between sections. Hand-drawn edges stay rough. At most 3 arrivals a second; with reduced motion marks appear and leave in one step and nothing travels.
