# Saul Bass — Film Titles & Montage

## Essence
Bass reduces each film to one emblem (a broken arm, a cut-up body, a grid, split bars, a cat, a flower, a light field) and makes the credits the parts that build, cross or break that emblem. In the 1950s works time is ruled by stepped motion: short, hard moves of 0.2-0.9 s, then dead-still holds of 1.6-4.3 s while a word is read, the moves set to a musical count. In the late works made with Elaine Bass the same ruling becomes a metronome of fades over a slowly changing image, one credit per image, 2.3-6 s per beat.

## Detailed observations

**The Man with the Golden Arm (clip01, 23.976 fps, 854×480, black ground).** The colour is pure: black `#030303` covers 89.3% of the frame and white `#FBFBFB` 7.1% at the title (21 s). Bars are 31-45 px thick, 6.5-9.4% of frame height, and up to 0.94 of frame width. They never glide. The frame-diff event list shows each bar moving in a burst of 0.21-0.63 s, followed by a hold of 0.42-0.54 s before the next burst. Examples: 15.38-15.76, hold 0.54, 16.30-16.89, hold 0.54, 17.43-17.93. From 51.34 to 57.26 s a bar arrives or leaves every 0.42 s (10 frames), five times running. Bars extend in from the frame edge: at 24.1-24.6 s one bar runs from x 0.53 to x 0.03 (about 1.0 frame width/s), and at 25.13-25.64 s a new bar grows from x 0.03-0.08 to 0.95 (about 1.7 width/s). Words do not move. They cut on in one frame (10.26, 19.26, 37.05, 41.34, 44.05, 57.26 s) and mostly cut off; a few fade out over 6 frames (7.67-7.92 s). Both the star names and the title are held exactly 4.17 s (100 frames). The title leaves at 23.43 s as all bars but one fade within 0.42 s, so the old frame is cleared before the new one is built. Text is small: 2.6-4.1% of frame height (13-20 px), set flush in gaps between bars, sometimes ragged left (cast list right of x 0.60 at 27-35 s). Each burst adds 0.6-2.6 percentage points of white, so the ink share climbs from 1.25% (5.5 s) to 13.6% (27.9 s) and falls back. Between 61.35 and 77.32 s four vertical bars converge, cross (X shapes, 71-75 s), fuse into one block (76.0 s) and become the drawn arm (77.3 s). That arm is held 5.8 s, the longest hold, then fades over 1.2 s (83.5-84.7). Sound: the original Bernstein track is present. Loudness per 5 s block rises from −41.6 dB (10-15 s) to −25.3 dB (75-80 s), a 16 dB crescendo, then drops to −55 dB at 85 s with the fade. Against onsets, the first bar (5.33 s) is +116 ms before a strong onset (5.446), the title cut (19.26 s) is +18 ms before a weak onset (19.278), and the names cut (10.26 s) is 163 ms before the strongest onset (10.423). The jazz gives about 6.7 onsets/s at 50-59 s, so a 30-80 ms match there (51.34 vs 51.370, 51.76 vs 51.678) is no better than chance. The clip's pulse candidates (0.511 s, 0.186 s) do not equal the 0.42 s bar step. Art of the Title quotes Bass designing "to that beat" given by Bernstein, but the frames show a 0.42-0.54 s step that this transfer's measured pulse does not confirm.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **A static word, a moving ground.** In every work the words, once landed, are still; the bars, fragments, grid, water, flowers or light move around them. Static names: Golden Arm (cut on, 0 px movement while held), Anatomy (pops), Goodfellas (static name after the whip), Cape Fear, Age of Innocence and Casino (fades over moving ground). Exceptions are entries and exits (NxNW slides, Psycho strips, Vertigo drop-and-shrink), never during the hold.
- **Stepped motion, on a count.** In the 1950s works shape movement is broken into short moves with dead holds: Golden Arm bursts of 0.21-0.63 s with 0.42-0.54 s holds; Anatomy pops every 0.25-0.5 s; Psycho splits every 0.83 s. In Anatomy, five picture events fall within 0-47 ms after strong onsets. Art of the Title quotes Bass on designing to Bernstein's count for Golden Arm.
- **A fixed beat per film.** Each work has one credit cadence with little variance: Goodfellas about 3.1 s, Cape Fear 2.33 s, Age of Innocence 4.0 s, Ocean's 11 a 2.0 s hold, NxNW 4.2-5.5 s, Spartacus 6.0-6.7 s, Casino 5.3-5.4 s tightening to 3.4 s. The ratio from slowest to fastest beat is 3:1 (Spartacus 6.0 s, Cape Fear 2.33 s).
- **One emblem, built or broken by parts.** Golden Arm bars fuse into the arm (76.0-77.3 s); the Anatomy body is assembled in 2.5 s by 7 cuts; the AW80D train is built car by car over 13 s; Edifice is one tower; the Spartacus busts crack. The emblem is completed once, usually just before or at the title (Anatomy 19.84 s), or saved for the end (Golden Arm arm held 5.8 s, the longest hold).
- **The title gets about double the beat.** Golden Arm 4.17 s vs names 4.17 s (equal), Anatomy 4.30 s vs 1.71 s, Ocean's 4.04 s vs 2.0 s, NxNW 5.34 s vs 3.6-3.9 s, Psycho full hold 4.09 s vs 1.75-2.13 s, Vertigo about 4.1 s. Cape Fear is the exception (2.67 s vs 1.8 s).
- **Positions sweep, then return to centre.** Cape Fear and Casino step left-centre-right (x 0.21/0.50/0.79 and 0.29/0.54/0.74); Spartacus alternates right/left; Age of Innocence alternates cast blocks left/right; Psycho and Goodfellas stay on dead centre; titles return to centre in all works.
- **Transitions get softer over the career.** In 1955-59 changes are cuts and slides; Spartacus 1960 and later works use dissolves and fades almost entirely. Detector misses were 6/6 (Spartacus), 4-5 (Cape Fear, Age of Innocence, WSS) against 0 misses on the hard cuts of the NxNW street section.
- **Text size stays small and constant.** Running credits are 2.6-7% of frame height in every work except where the word is the image (Ocean's bulbs 12.3%, NxNW stars about 16% along the slant, Vertigo 9.4-11.3%, Anatomy title 11.5-12.3% per line). Sizes rarely vary within a sequence: Anatomy names all 6.3%, Psycho 6.3-7.0%, Casino 4.1-4.8%.
- **Long-slow, then a burst, then long-slow.** Walk on the Wild Side: shots averaging about 9 s for 130 s, then about 20 cuts in 12.4 s (mean about 0.6 s), then a 2.2 s still close-up and a 14 s walk. NxNW: a 55 s graphic stage, then 2.3-3.6 s street shots. Edifice: climb runs of 5-20 s alternating with 5-10 s gags. Casino: motion 1-8% per block, rising to 10.7-12.5% in the final flame section.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Saul Bass.** *Reduce the content to one figure made of a few parts and give every ordinary line one hold, sized to the longest: add one part per step by a short hard move, then a dead hold in which that part's line stands still inside it; complete the figure once, under the key line held about twice as long; clear before any new build; allow one burst.*
Process:
1. Name the one figure and count its parts. Reduce what the run says to a single figure that can be put together from 4–11 parts, each part able to carry one line of text, and pick one axis of travel for the whole run (across, up, or one diagonal). List the lines in order, one per part, with their character counts. If the figure changes, every later step is redone.
2. Set one hold, from the longest line. Every ordinary line is up for the same time, 1.6–4.6 s, at most 3 : 1 between a fast run and a slow one. Size the hold so that the longest ordinary line gets at least 1.3 times its reading time at 16 characters a second, then give every shorter line the same hold: the hold is counted by the run, never by the line's length. A line that cannot fit takes a second whole hold or is split in two; it never gets a shorter or a private hold. Long cards (a crew list) may share one longer hold, 6.8–9.8 s where measured. The start-to-start interval is one of two kinds: fixed within a group, 2.3–6.7 s, with the line up for 65–100% of it and the rest ground alone or a fade into the next line; or stretched by the build between lines, 1.7–9.3 s around the fixed hold. Between groups it may open to 10 s or more.
3. Split every beat into a move and a hold. The move is short and hard: 0.2–0.8 s at constant speed (1–2 widths of the view a second), with no ease in and none out. Then nothing moves while the line is up: 1.6–4.6 s for a name, up to 10 s for a long card or the closing figure; between the parts of one build the still gap may be as short as 0.4 s. The line arrives only when its part has come to rest, in one step or by a fade of 0.13–0.5 s, and it does not move, scale or change while it is up. Moving and reading never share an instant.
4. Build by adding. Each beat adds one part to what is already there and takes none away; a part arrives whole, from the edge along the axis or by a cut. Where several parts make one build, step them 0.35–0.5 s apart (never more than 3 a second) with a still gap of at least 0.4 s after each. Let the marked share of the view climb by 0.6–4 points per part, from about 1% to 14–21% when the build is done.
5. Seat the line in its part. The line sits inside the part that has just arrived, or in the gap it leaves beside its neighbour, never over the bare ground at large. Parts are large (0.6–0.94 of the view's width) and lines small (2.6–7% of its height), all lines at one size. Seats either stay on the centre line or step in a fixed round (0.2, 0.5, 0.8 of the width, or 0.34 and 0.67 by turns).
6. Complete the figure once. The parts close into the whole figure at one moment only, and the key line lands in that moment, at the centre, at the size of the other lines or up to twice it, and holds about twice as long (1.5–2.5 times an ordinary hold). Put the completion early, 6–28% of the way in, where it names what follows, or keep it for the last hold and make that the longest hold of the run (5.8 s where measured, then a fade of 1.2 s). Never both, and never a second completion.
7. Clear before a new build. When a group of lines is done, take away everything but one part in about 0.4 s, hold the near-empty view, and build again from that part. Across the whole run the join is of one family: cuts and slides along the axis, or fades and dissolves of 0.4–1.2 s. A change of scene may ride on travel instead: the finished figure moves off at about 0.3 of the width a second and the next ground enters behind it.
8. Allow one change of pace. Either one burst, placed in the second half (about three quarters through where measured), taking a tenth of the run or less, its units about a fifteenth of the usual length (never under 0.35 s) and followed by about 2 s of complete stillness; or one tightening of the beat to about two thirds, made once and kept. Everything before and after stays on the beat.
9. Keep the picture's count its own. The run is timed and proved with no sound. Where sound exists and the picture is locked to it (measured in one work only; elsewhere the picture keeps its own count), a part lands on the sound's event or up to 50 ms after it, never before, or a swell of about 1.3 s peaks within 0.1 s of the landing. Sound marks the move; the hold is silent as far as the picture is concerned.
10. Write the still form: one frame per beat, each showing the figure so far with its line seated, the completion frame given twice the room, in order. If the frames do not add up to one figure, step 1 was not done.
Why it works:
- Reading needs a fixed target, and motion takes the eye whether or not it is wanted. Putting the move and the hold in separate halves of the beat gives each of them the whole of attention. Let the line move while it is up, or let the ground move as hard in the hold as in the move, and the reader loses either the word or the place.
- A hold that does not vary is learnt in two or three lines, so the viewer knows when the next line is due and how long there is to read it (prediction). A hold counted from the line's length removes the count: every change comes as a surprise and the run reads as a list. With the hold fixed, a short line simply has time to spare, which is read as weight, not as waiting.
- Parts that arrive one at a time toward a shape the viewer can guess are held together by closure: each addition is checked against the expected whole, so the viewer keeps watching to see it finish. Show the whole at once and there is nothing to wait for; add parts that make no whole and it is a slide show; complete it twice and the second completion carries nothing.
- A line inside a part is grouped with it by common region, and because both arrive in the same beat they are bound as one event. A line floating on the open ground belongs to nothing, and the figure and the words become two programmes competing for one view.
- Duration is emphasis. About twice the hold on the key line at the moment of closure mark it as the name of the whole without a size step, so the scale stays level. Enlarge it instead and the fixed size that made the small lines equal is spent.
- The near-empty view after a clearing resets what the eye has adapted to, so the next first part is seen as a start. Build on a full view and the new group is read as more of the old one.
- A burst is salient by oddness only against a long level stretch (adaptation to the previous state). Multiply it and it becomes the new level; remove it and a long run has no landmark; the stillness after it is what lets the last lines be read at all.
- A part that lands on or just after a sound is heard as its cause; one that lands before is seen as a mistake. Tying only the moves to sound leaves the holds, where the reading is done, untouched when the sound is off.
Colour relations: one ground on 75–98% of the view, a single value or one flat hue; the figure's parts are the largest marks at 7–21% (up to 40% in the one fullest moment) and stand at the far end of the value scale from the ground; text is the smallest share (1–3%) at the highest contrast against the part that carries it, not against the ground; at most one chromatic hue per run, or one per section with the hue changing only on a line change; where the ground is dark and neutral a chromatic accent keeps 3–14% of the view.
Space and line: few large parts and small text (a part up to 0.94 of the width, a line 2.6–7% of the height); one axis of travel for nearly the whole run; parts enter from the edge and are cut square to the axis, one thickness per kind (6–9% of the short side for a bar); the horizontal centre line is the resting place, and the key line always returns to it; no line of text stands where no part or gap holds it.
Type requirement: one size for every running line, with a second, smaller size only for the role above a name; the face must stay legible at 2.6–4% of the view's height inside a part of opposite value, so open counters and even colour matter more than character; text is always level and still, even where its part travelled on a diagonal; lettering that is cut or drawn as part of the figure is allowed for the key line only.
Motion and interaction: hold 1.6–4.6 s for every ordinary line, long cards up to 10 s; a fixed start interval, where used, 2.3–6.7 s with the line up for 65–100% of it; move 0.2–0.8 s, linear, 1–2 widths a second; build steps 0.35–0.5 s apart; line in by a cut or a fade of 0.13–0.5 s, out by a cut, a fade of 0.1–0.5 s or a slide off along the axis in 0.4–0.5 s; clearing about 0.4 s; key line 1.5–2.5 times the hold; a closing hold the longest of the run, with a fade of about 1.2 s; sound 0–50 ms ahead of the picture, never behind. One exception to linear: a part that enters oversize may slow into its seat over 0.75 s, most of the change in the first 0.3 s. In a stepped medium (a deck, a story scroll, an onboarding flow) one step is one advance: the press starts the move, the hold lasts until the next press, and the minimum hold from step 2 is enforced before the control is live. Nothing moves in the hold, nothing loops, and no step repeats faster than 3 a second. Reduced motion: every move becomes a cut on its step, so parts appear whole in their seats; fades become cuts; the burst becomes one frame held for its length; the stills of step 10 are the sequence, stepped by the viewer. The order, the one figure, the single completion, the double hold and the clearing all remain.
Fails when: the content has no single figure, or its parts do not add up to one (a catalogue, a feed, search results); lines differ so much in length that one beat is either too short for the longest or dead for the shortest (running prose, legal text, a line of 120 characters among names); the order is the viewer's and not the author's, as in free navigation; the state must be complete at every instant (dashboards, tables under comparison), since a half-built figure says nothing; there are more than about a dozen parts, when the build outlasts the guess; the viewer must act on a part while it moves.
Signature (do not carry together): black cut-paper shapes with ragged scissor edges on one flat ground · a hand-cut, uneven capital title broken over stepped baselines · bars and fragments jerking into place on the count of a jazz or string score.

- **Reading pressure:** star names get 1.3-7× the reading time at 16 characters/s. The tight cases are crew lines and long notices: Golden Arm's two-line editor card 0.96, Age of Innocence's "Title Sequence by Elaine & Saul Bass" 1.08 (whole visible time; its full-strength hold of 1.63 s is only 0.72), Golden Arm's screenplay 1.22, Wild Side's producer 1.1. Short names are held by beat, not by length: Anatomy gives 10 and 22 characters the same 1.71-1.79 s, Ocean's gives 13 and 16 characters the same 2.0 s.
- **Charted cadence:** what stays fixed in a run is the hold, not the start interval. Ocean's 11 holds every name 2.0 s (22.79-24.87, 32.13-34.13, 40.79-42.79, 45.79-47.79, 56.54-58.54 s) while starts fall 9.3, 8.7, 5.0, 5.4, 5.3 and 6.25 s apart, so a name is up 22-40% of its interval. Anatomy's star names start 2.5, 1.75, 3.2 and 2.08 s apart and the crew cards hold 6.75, 8.25 and 9.75 s. Golden Arm's still stretches between moves run 0.42-10.4 s: 0.42 s steps inside a build (55.46-57.12 s), 4.2-4.75 s name holds, an 8.1 s cast card and a 10.4 s close (arm, fade and black). Regular intervals are a late-work trait: Age of Innocence starts every 4.0 s (6, 10, 14, 18, 22, 26 s) with the title up about 6 s (34.5-40.5 s) against 2.7-3.5 s per name; Cape Fear steps 2.33-2.5 s inside a group, the title is up about 3 s, then 10 s of water carries no text (64.5-74.5 s); Goodfellas whips come 3.0-3.9 s apart, mostly 3.2-3.4 s, each loudness swell peaking at or just after the landing. Walk on the Wild Side's burst runs 139.8-152.2 s, 78-85% of the way through a 165 s run and 7.5% of its length, with motion back under 2% from about 153 s. Golden Arm's moves fall within 80 ms of an onset 33% of the time, chance at 6.7 onsets/s.
- **Entries and exits by era:** 1955-62 hard cuts (Golden Arm, Anatomy, Ocean's), slides (NxNW, the Anatomy exits at about 1.3-1.5 width/s, Psycho strips at about 1.3 width/s) and dissolves of 0.4-0.9 s (Spartacus); 1990-95 fades of 0.13-0.5 s (Cape Fear, Age of Innocence) or a whip of 0.63 s at about 2 widths/s followed by a cut (Goodfellas).
- **Easing:** Golden Arm and Anatomy show no easing at 6-frame steps (constant-speed stop-motion moves). Vertigo's KIM NOVAK decelerates into place (ease-out over 0.75 s); VERTIGO accelerates out of the frame (ease-in, about ×1.5 width per second).
- **Image against sound:** in Anatomy picture events fall 0-47 ms after the strongest onsets (5 of 5 tested). In Golden Arm the matches are within chance given 6.7 onsets/s. Goodfellas' whoosh peaks within 0.1 s of each landing. Psycho's 0.83 s splits drift against the 0.395 s string pulse. All soundtracks are the films' originals as heard in these uploads; none is known to be re-scored, but none was checked by ear.
- **Stillness and black:** Golden Arm clears the frame to one bar before each new build (23.43-24.1 s); Age of Innocence leaves 1.3-1.4 s between names; Goodfellas and Anatomy use cut-to-black cards for the last credit (Anatomy 97.8 s). Cape Fear leaves no empty water inside a group: at 0.5 s sampling the outgoing and incoming names are both faintly up at 53.0, 55.5, 77.0 and 79.5 s.

## UI translations
1. **Pacing:** Hold every line of a sequence for the same time whatever its length: 10 and 22 characters both get 1.71–1.79 s (*Anatomy of a Murder*), every name 2.0 s (*Ocean's 11*). Set the hold from the longest line, not from each line; let the gap between lines stretch with the build (*Ocean's 11*: starts 5.0–9.3 s apart around the fixed 2.0 s hold).
2. **Reading time:** Check every line against 16 characters a second. Names in these works get 1.3–7 times what they need; the cards that fall to 0.96–1.22 (*The Man with the Golden Arm* editor card, 121 characters in 7.26 s) are the ones to split.
3. **Move, then hold:** Animate in a burst of 0.2–0.6 s and then stop completely for the reading (*Golden Arm*: bursts of 0.21–0.63 s, names held 4.17 s with no movement at all). No drift, no parallax, no slow zoom under text that must be read.
4. **Text entry:** Cut the word on in one frame once its carrier has stopped (*Golden Arm*, *Anatomy*), or fade it in 0.13–0.5 s (*Cape Fear*, *The Age of Innocence*). Animate the carrier, not the word.
5. **Easing:** Linear and clipped for parts that slide: 1–1.7 widths a second with no visible ease (*Golden Arm*, *Anatomy*). Ease out only when something oversize settles into a seat, 0.75 s with most of the change in the first 0.3 s (*Vertigo*, KIM NOVAK).
6. **Progress:** Let each completed step add one part to one figure, so the unfinished figure is the progress: a body in 7 cuts over 2.5 s (*Anatomy*), a train car by car over 13 s (*Around the World in 80 Days*), a count of 1 to 11 (*Ocean's 11*).
7. **Stagger:** Step a build on an even grid, 0.42 s five times running (*Golden Arm*) or 0.5 s (*Anatomy*), with a still gap after each. Keep to 3 steps a second or fewer; *Anatomy*'s 0.25 s steps are at the limit and are not carried.
8. **Emphasis:** Mark the key line by time and not by size: 4.04 s against 2.0 s (*Ocean's 11*), 4.30 s against 1.71 s (*Anatomy*); in *Cape Fear* the title is the same size as the names and simply stays longer (2.67 s against 1.8 s).
9. **Labels:** Seat a label inside the shape it belongs to or in the gap beside it (*Anatomy*: names inside the black parts; *Golden Arm*: lines in the gaps between bars), at 2.6–7% of the view's height against parts up to 0.94 of its width.
10. **Seats:** Step successive lines through fixed seats, x 0.21, 0.50, 0.79 (*Cape Fear*) or left and right by turns at 0.34 and 0.67 (*Spartacus*), and bring the key line back to the centre.
11. **Transition:** Clear before rebuilding: everything but one part gone within 0.42 s, then the new group builds from that part (*Golden Arm*, 23.43 s). Do not cross-fade one full state into another.
12. **Scene change:** Carry a change of context on travel that is already under way: the finished train moves off at 0.3 of the width a second and the next ground slides in behind it, with no cut (*Around the World in 80 Days*).
13. **Join family:** Pick cuts and slides, or fades and dissolves of 0.4–1.2 s, and keep to one for the run (*Anatomy* against *Spartacus* and *The Age of Innocence*).
14. **Change of pace:** Spend one burst in a long level run: about 20 cuts in 12.4 s after 130 s of shots near 9 s, then a still 2.2 s (*Walk on the Wild Side*). In a product keep each unit at 0.35 s or more; the film's 0.07–0.10 s cuts are over the flash limit and are not carried.
15. **Acceleration:** Or tighten the beat once and hold the new one: starts 5.3–5.4 s apart, then 3.4 s (*Casino*).
16. **Long scroll:** Alternate travel and rest on one axis: runs of 5–20 s at 0.16–0.39 of the height a second, then 5–10 s still while one thing plays out (*Why Man Creates*, "Edifice"). In a story scroll, snap to the rests.
17. **Sound:** Land the picture on the sound's event or up to 50 ms after (*Anatomy*: 0–47 ms after the note, five of five), or let a 1.3 s swell peak within 0.1 s of the landing (*Goodfellas*). Prove the timing muted first: the holds carry the reading and need no sound.
18. **Closing:** End on the completed figure held longest (5.8 s, then a 1.2 s fade, *Golden Arm*) or on one last line alone on a cleared ground (*Anatomy*, 97.8 s).
19. **Accessibility:** Under `prefers-reduced-motion` replace every move with a cut on the beat, so parts appear whole in place (the *Anatomy* build already works this way), turn fades into cuts, and let the viewer step the stills; the order, the one completion and the double hold stay.
20. **Accessibility:** Never shorten a hold below the reading time to keep a beat; lengthen the beat. Give auto-advancing sequences a pause control, and keep text still for the whole of its hold.

## What this artist would reject
- Moving the word while it is being read: no measured credit moves during its hold.
- Continuous, eased motion everywhere: the 1950s works move in clipped steps of 0.2-0.9 s, and the late works fade at fixed lengths.
- Holds sized to text length: one beat governs a whole sequence (Anatomy 1.71 s, Ocean's 2.0 s, Cape Fear 2.33 s period), whatever the number of characters.
- Many hues at once on black: one chromatic colour per work or per image, accents of 3-14% area.
- Type laid over an image without reference to it: names sit inside shapes, along grid lines, on walls, under eyes.
- Decorative transitions: changes are cuts, slides along the work's own axis, or plain dissolves.

## Signature prompt seed
Design the sequence as a Bass title. On one flat ground (black `#030303` or grey `#494949`) that keeps three quarters or more of the frame, build a single emblem from a few cut-paper parts in the opposite value (`#050505` on grey, `#FBFBFB` on black), each part entering from the edge in a hard 0.4 s move and then standing dead still. Give every credit one hold, 2–4 s, and cut each small white name (about 6% of the height, plain caps, a smaller lowercase role above) on inside the part that has just landed; never move a word while it is up. Step the build 0.5 s apart, complete the emblem once and hold the hand-cut title there for 4.3 s, twice the names' hold. Clear to one part before each new group. One hue at most. Joins are cuts and slides along one axis. It must read with the sound off; with reduced motion, parts appear by cuts on the beat and the stills carry the order.
