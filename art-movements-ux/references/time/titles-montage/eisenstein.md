# Sergei Eisenstein — Film Titles & Montage

## Essence
Eisenstein builds a sequence by colliding separate pieces: two set-ups alternated, one action shown again from several angles, a word card dropped between shots like another shot. He sets their lengths by a plan of acceleration and release, not by how long the action takes. Intertitles are cut into the stream as short, single-idea beats whose type size follows emphasis rather than text length. The same images come back again and again, and a sequence is built up from these returns.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Two set-ups alternated to build a beat.** Potemkin waves (rocks/breakwater ABAB, 2.3-3.5 s), plate face/inscription (1.4-5.8 s), October machine gun (face/gun, 1-3 frames), Strike bull/crowd (0.3-2.1 s), Old and New face/machine (1.5-2.5 s). The faster the alternation, the closer the climax.
- **One action repeated from several angles (overlap).** Plate smash: one swing, 4 shots, 3 set-ups, 3.84 s. "And suddenly" head jolt: 6 cuts in 1.6 s. Bridge: hair, horse and girders each return 4-6 times in 130 s. Lions: 3 poses of 0.72-0.84 s with position and scale held.
- **Acceleration by shortening, then release.** Odessa cuts per 30 s go 11 → 20 → 10 → 26 (pram) → guns. Strike shots shrink to 0.3-0.8 s at the knife. Each burst is followed by a long hold or black (machine gun burst then a 9.76 s square; Strike end then 10.3 s black).
- **Long held passages built on light or music, not cutting.** Potemkin mist: 9 shots of 2.6-12.5 s, dissolves, luma ramp 45 → 110. Nevsky dawn: 12 shots, ASL 6.9 s. Bridge holds of 13-14 s. Ivan II song shot 26.75 s. Eisenstein calls the mist montage by "light variations" (Fourth Dimension).
- **Word cards treated as shots.** Holds are 1-2.7 s for 1-29 characters inside sequences (Kerensky, Old and New, "And suddenly"), against 4-17 s for credit and quote cards. Size follows weight, not length: ДЕНЬГИ is 0.58 of the frame for 0.72 s, "И" 0.28 for 1.56 s, while ПРИБАВИЛОСЬ is 0.075 for 4.0 s. In silent films, cards between shots hold 1.5-3.8× reading need; sound-era credits hold 1.0-1.2×.
- **Stepped and staircase lines.** Potemkin "ЛЮДИ / И ЧЕРВИ", Old and New "Обманство / или / деньги", October "и прочая ×3": each line is offset diagonally from the one before.
- **Joins carried by value, position or background.** Nevsky charge white-out to white, lions centred and scaled alike, Ivan I credits over continuous moving smoke, Ivan II colour handing over from gold to red.
- **Sound against the cut, not on it.** In Nevsky the cuts land 0.7-2.9 s from musical events, while swells repeat every ~1.25 s. Eisenstein's own analysis matches the line of the picture to the line of the music rather than cut to beat.
- **Grey restraint, then one strong break.** Silent works are neutral black, white and grey (C 0). Nevsky has no pure black or white (L 16-81). Colour appears only once, in Ivan II, as saturated red (C 40-68) against black.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Sergei Eisenstein.** *Find two unlike pieces whose meeting says what neither says alone: alternate them at fixed seats until known, shorten toward one event, show that event again from other sides, pay for the burst with a hold several times its length; cut words in as their own pieces, sized by weight, held by length; carry one thing across every join.*
Process:
1. Name the pair, the event and the release before timing anything. A passage is built on two pieces that differ in kind (a cause and what it costs, a claim and its source, a part and the whole it belongs to) and that make a point only when they meet. Give each of the two one seat and one framing that it keeps every time it returns. Name the one event the passage leads to and the still view or empty ground that follows it. One pair and one event a passage. If the pair changes, every later step is redone.
2. Teach the pair, then shorten it. Alternate A, B, A, B at 2.3–3.5 s a piece (1.4–5.8 s at the widest) for at least two rounds, joined by cuts, or step the pieces down in length (5.4, 2.8, 2.1 s) on the way in. Then let the pieces shorten as the event comes near, down to 0.35–0.9 s at the event itself and nowhere else. Over a long passage the pace rises in two descents and not in one slope: from about 6 s a piece to about 2.2 s over the first 90 s, held there for about two and a half minutes, then back to about 4.5 s and down again to about 1.4 s, held for about 90 s, then back to about 2 s. The works go down to 1–3 frames (0.04–0.3 s); a product does not.
3. Stretch the event by showing it again. The event is shown 3–4 times from three sides (near, wide, across), each piece 0.35–0.7 s, the later ones starting before the earlier one ended, so it lasts about three times its own length (one move of a little over a second takes 3.8 s). Or show it as three states of one thing at one seat and one scale, 0.7–0.85 s each, with only the state changing. The slow form stretches by return: four pictures come back 4–6 times each over about two minutes, with holds of up to 13–14 s.
4. Release. After the burst that ends a passage (the event and any pieces under about 1.7 s that end it), one piece holds 3.6–3.75 times the burst's length (2.7 s of burst, 9.8 s of hold): a wide still view, or the ground alone. Nothing new starts inside it, and no word.
5. Cut words in as pieces. A word card stands between two pictures and never over one; it arrives whole by a cut (one or two empty frames may come before it) and nothing in it moves. One idea a card, 1–29 characters inside a passage. Hold it 1.5–4 times what a reader at 16 characters a second needs (the shortest words get the most), and never under 0.7 s; in a growing series hold 0.11 s a character, so cards lengthen as their text does. A statement of 60–200 characters stands outside the passages and holds 1.4–1.8 times its need.
6. Size by weight, on a ladder, and keep the largest brief. Fix 4–6 sizes between about 0.07 and 0.58 of the view's short side, with a step of 1.25–2 between neighbours. A card's size comes from the weight of its idea and not from its length: 6 characters may stand at 0.58 and 11 at 0.075. The two top sizes are kept for one to three cards a passage, each 1–10 characters across 0.7–0.87 of the width, held 0.7–1.7 s (2–4 times its need). The size never changes while a card is up; the change of size from card to card is the movement.
7. Where the point is a piling-up, set a growing series against a picture that does not change. The same picture returns at the same length each time (13.6–15.7 s in the work), and between returns stands one card of a series whose cards grow (from 8 characters to 29, from 0.9 s to 2.7 s), each at a different place along one level band. Where the point is a question, set it once small, then its terms one to a card, the last three at rising sizes with the gaps between them closing (about 14 s, then 4 s and 2 s); withhold the answer (about 100 s in the work), and set its last word small and slow (11 characters for 4.0 s).
8. Set one slow passage against the fast one. 9–12 pieces of 2.6–12.5 s (about 6.9 s on average), in which one property of the whole view moves one way from piece to piece (mean value from under a fifth of the scale to over two fifths in 60 s) and the pieces shorten a little toward the end. Only here may the join be a soft one, a dissolve of about 0.5 s. Everywhere else it is a cut.
9. Carry one thing across every join. Choose it per join: seat and scale (the next piece puts its subject where the last one stood), direction of travel (one way across the view, about 0.67 of the width a second), value (the old piece ends on a flat field for about 1.3 s and the new one begins from the same field), or a ground that keeps moving behind cards that come and go through about 0.5 s of ground alone. One thing is carried and everything else may change.
10. Break one level, once. Hold one property of the whole view level for the run: its chroma, or the end of the value scale its ground sits at. Break it a single time, by a cut with no transition, at the point the run turns on (in one work, the moment the answer is in). After the break a change of the dominant in that property may itself serve as a join (dominants held about 2–5 s).
11. Open and land in words and emptiness. Open on the title alone, held from 1.4 times its need up to 10–12 s, then any statements, card after card by cuts. Land in this order: the last statement at about twice its need, two cards from the upper sizes of 1.2–1.7 s each, then the ground alone for 3.6 times those two together (about 10 s).
12. Keep sound off the joins. Where there is sound, a join falls 0.7–2.9 s from the nearest strong sound event (an onset or a change of texture) and never on one; the two lines run side by side. Every word is a card, so nothing depends on hearing.
13. Write the still form: the pair as two stills side by side at their seats, with the count of rounds; the event as a strip of its three or four views at one size; the release as an empty panel 3.6 times the width of that strip; each word on its own card at its ladder size; the series as one still of the picture beside its cards in order; the slow passage as a row in which the one property is seen to move; the break as two neighbouring panels. If the pair says nothing side by side, step 1 was not done.
Why it works:
- Grouping by alternation: two things that take turns at fixed seats are read as happening together, and the viewer supplies the link between them, so the point is made in the viewer and not on the screen. Show A whole and then B whole and it is two subjects in a row. Make the two alike and there is nothing to link. Add a third and the link is no longer one sentence.
- Recognition gets quicker with each return, so a known piece can shorten without loss and the shortening is read as nearness. A new piece at 0.4 s is simply missed. A single slope tires: after the first crest the return to base sets the baseline again (adaptation), and that is why the second crest is felt at all.
- An event of a second or less is over before it is taken in. Showing it again from another side gives it time without slowing it, and because each view differs it reads as weight and not as a stutter. Remove the repeats and the passage has led to something nobody saw; repeat every event and none is the event.
- Three states at one seat and scale are one object changing (apparent motion, persistence). Move the seat and they are three objects.
- After a burst the eye has adapted to change, so a long still piece is felt at once and is where the link is drawn. Start the next thing in it and the burst is one more stretch of noise.
- A word that has the view to itself does not split the eye between reading and watching. Its size is seen before it is read, so weight arrives first (salience by size), and the hold tied to length makes sure the reading is finished. A word of a few characters at the top size is read in one look, so it can be the briefest thing in the run and still the heaviest.
- A picture that returns unchanged is the scale reference against which the growth of the words is measured. Change the picture too and nothing is seen to grow.
- One thing held across a cut keeps the eye where it is, so the change is the only news; a cut that carries nothing makes the viewer find the subject again.
- One break needs the level run before it (salience by oddness). Two breaks are a palette.
- A join on a sound event claims that one caused the other. Kept apart, picture and sound are two lines the viewer follows together.
Colour relations: the process owns no hue. Word cards take the widest value step: a ground at one end of the scale on 77–92% of the view, letters toward the other end on 8–13%; a title that is held long may sit at a middle value. Picture pieces live in the middle values, and a slow passage may avoid both ends of the scale altogether. Chroma stays level, at or near nothing in the works, until the single break. After it, view by view, the darkest value keeps the largest share (three tenths to a half), a deep saturated field takes up to a third, the highest chroma takes the smallest share (under a tenth), and a second hue of lower chroma about a sixth. The answer to a question may be marked by one inversion of ground and figure (95% to 5%).
Space and line: a word card holds one centred block across 0.57–0.85 of the width; the top sizes span 0.7–0.87. A card in a series is one line at about a tenth of the short side, in the upper middle (0.38–0.51 of the height), at a new place along that band each time. A card of two or three lines may step each line along from the one above. A thing that recurs keeps its seat and scale at every return. In the slow passage the subject may be a thin line near one edge (0.85–0.95 of the height) with 80–90% of the view empty; in the fast one the subject fills 0.6–0.9 of the height.
Type requirement: the kit's face must hold across a ladder of about nine times, from 0.07 to 0.58 of the short side, and a word of up to 10 characters must span 0.87 of the width at the upper sizes, so a narrow cut or tight setting is needed there. Punctuation carries the rhythm (a question mark, a dash that hands on to the next card), so those marks must be as heavy as the letters. Copy is written to the card: one idea, 1–29 characters, character counts in the brief.
Motion and interaction: teaching pieces 2.3–3.5 s; shortened pieces down to 0.35 s; event pieces 0.35–0.7 s, three or four of them; three-state form 0.7–0.85 s a state; release 3.6–3.75 times the burst; word cards 1.5–4 times their need (0.11 s a character in a growing series) and never under 0.7 s; top-size cards 0.7–1.7 s; statements 1.4–1.8 times their need at 16 characters a second; slow pieces 2.6–12.5 s with 0.5 s dissolves; title up to 10–12 s; closing emptiness about 10 s. Joins are cuts; a fade of about 1 s is allowed in from nothing at the opening and out at the end of a part. Nothing eases, and nothing moves inside a word card. No piece is shorter than 0.35 s, and the two members of a pair are kept close in mean value through the short stretch, or lengthened to 0.5 s, so nothing flashes more than 3 times a second. In a stepped medium (a deck, a story scroll, onboarding) the pair is two layouts that take turns, shortening means each return shows less, the burst is one advance that plays by itself and can be paused, the event is three views of one fact in a row, the release is an empty view the viewer leaves when ready, and every word card is a step of its own. Reduced motion: every piece is one still entered by a cut on the viewer's own advance; the pair stands side by side once with its rounds given as a count; the event is its strip of views; the release is an empty step; cards keep their ladder sizes; the slow passage is a row of stills; the break is one step. Pair, event, release, ladder, series and break all remain.
Fails when: the material is one thread with no second thing to set against it; the two things are only neighbours, since alternation asserts a link and with unrelated data the link is a false claim; there is no single event (a steady state, a table under comparison, a dashboard); anything must be read or pressed during the shortened stretch; order and pace are the viewer's own, as in free navigation, where piece lengths cannot be kept; the run is under about half a minute, too short to teach a pair and pay for a release; the words are of one weight (a list of names, running prose), so the ladder has nothing to rank; the audience or setting cannot take pieces under a second, where only the reduced form is honest; the subject is one where a staged shock would be an offence.
Signature (do not carry together): bold condensed capitals, light on a black card, jumping in size from card to card, their lines stepped down a diagonal · faces in close-up cut against machines, weapons or animals in pieces of a few frames · steep diagonals of steps, girders and spears under a low horizon, in grey, with a single passage of saturated red.

## UI translations
1. **Comparing:** Alternate two views, each at its own fixed seat and framing, A, B, A, B at 2.3–3.5 s (*Battleship Potemkin*, waves: two set-ups, 8 shots, ASL 2.4 s), so the viewer draws the link.
2. **Acceleration:** Shorten toward the event and put the shortest there: 5.4, 2.8, 2.1 s, then 0.35–0.9 s at the knife (*Strike*). In a product stop at 0.35 s.
3. **Pacing:** Raise a long passage in two descents: median shot length per 30 s falls from about 6 s to 2.2 s and holds there about 2.5 minutes, returns to about 4.5 s, then falls to about 1.4 s for 90 s, the pram (*Battleship Potemkin*, Odessa steps, detector with gun-flash runs left out); the return between is what lets the second descent register.
4. **Emphasis by repetition:** Show the decisive action again from other sides: one swing, 4 times, 3 set-ups, 3.84 s, about three times its length (*Battleship Potemkin*, plate smash).
5. **State change:** Show three states of one thing at one seat and scale, 0.72–0.84 s each, and let only the state differ (*Battleship Potemkin*, lions); the eye reads one object changing.
6. **Stillness:** Pay for a burst that ends a passage with a hold: 2.68 s of alternation, then 9.76 s on one wide view (*October*); two word cards of 2.84 s together, then 10.3 s of empty ground (*Strike*).
7. **Kinetic type:** Move type by changing its size between cards, not inside one: 0.07, 0.29, 0.09, 0.58, 0.15, 0.17, 0.22 of the height in successive cards (*Old and New*). Nothing travels in the frame.
8. **Type scale in time:** Size by weight and not by length: 6 characters at 0.58 of the height for 0.72 s, 11 characters at 0.075 for 4.0 s (*Old and New*).
9. **Reading time:** Inside a sequence give a card about 0.11 s a character, 1.5–2.0 times its need at 16 characters a second: 0.88 s for 8 characters up to 2.72 s for 27–29 (*October*, rank titles).
10. **Interruption:** Announce a turn with one short card at the largest size, cut in after two empty frames and held 1.64 s for 7 characters (*Battleship Potemkin*, "And suddenly", cap height 0.175).
11. **Lists that build:** Return to the same picture at the same length (13.6–15.7 s) and put one more item between returns, each card a single line at a new place along one band (*October*, the stair and the ranks).
12. **Question and answer:** Set the question small, its terms alone and large, the last three growing as the gaps close (14, 4, 2 s), hold the answer back, then invert ground and figure once for the result (*Old and New*, 95% light after the last word).
13. **Slow passage:** Join 9 pieces of 2.6–12.5 s by 0.5 s dissolves while mean value climbs from 45 to 110 of 255 (*Battleship Potemkin*, mist), or hold 12 pieces at 4.6–9.8 s (*Alexander Nevsky*, dawn). Dissolve only where one property is the subject.
14. **Extending a moment:** Bring four pictures back 4–6 times each over 130 s, with holds of 13–14 s (*October*, bridge), when the moment must feel long and not fast.
15. **Transition:** Carry value across the join: end on a flat field for 1.3 s and start the next view from the same field (*Alexander Nevsky*, charge). Or keep the ground moving and let cards come and go through 0.5 s of ground alone (*Ivan the Terrible, Part One*).
16. **Direction:** Keep one direction of travel across a run of cuts, about 0.67 of the width a second (*Alexander Nevsky*).
17. **Colour in time:** Hold chroma at nothing and break it once by a hard cut; afterwards let a change of dominant act as a join, one dominant for 4.6 s, the next for 2.0 s (*Ivan the Terrible, Part Two*).
18. **Opening:** Hold the title alone 10.2–11.7 s (*Alexander Nevsky*, *Strike*) and give statements 1.4–1.8 times their need (*October*, *Battleship Potemkin*); the 1.0–1.2 of the *Nevsky* credits is too tight to copy.
19. **Landing:** End on a statement at 2.1 times its need, two large words of 1.17 s and 1.67 s, then 10.3 s of nothing (*Strike*).
20. **Sound:** Keep joins off strong sound events, 0.7–2.9 s away (*Alexander Nevsky*, dawn), and put every word on screen, so the sequence reads muted.
21. **Honesty:** Alternation claims that two things belong together (*Strike* cuts a crowd against an abattoir to say so). Use it only for a link the data supports, and never carry the works' violence or their slogans.
22. **Accessibility:** Do not carry the 1–3 frame alternation (*October*, 30 changes in 2.68 s), the 0.16 s jump cuts (*Battleship Potemkin*) or the 2–3 frame strobe (*Old and New*): all pass 3 flashes a second. Keep every piece at 0.35 s or longer and the pair close in value.
23. **Accessibility:** Under `prefers-reduced-motion` show the pair side by side, the event as a strip of its views, the release as an empty step and each word as its own card at its ladder size, all on the viewer's own advance.

## What this artist would reject
- Continuous takes that let an action run in its real time. He criticises "40 metres" of uninterrupted playing in Room's *Bay of Death* (Dialectic essay); his longest holds are 13-27 s and are always set against the cutting around them.
- Cards set at one uniform size and duration whatever their weight.
- Cutting on the beat of the music. In Nevsky the cut and the musical event are kept apart.
- Transitions used as decoration: hard cuts dominate, and dissolves appear only where light itself is the subject (the mist, Ivan's smoke).
- Colour spread evenly over a film: in the films examined, colour occurs once, as a single break.

## Signature prompt seed
Cut the sequence as an Eisenstein passage in neutral greys. Open on a near-black card (`#030303`) with the title in bold condensed capitals (`#DCDCDC`) across 0.85 of the width, held 10 s, and cut card to card with no fades. Alternate two unlike set-ups at 3 s, twice, then shorten them to 0.4 s as the event nears; cut in one two-word card at the largest size for 1.6 s after two black frames. Show the event four times from three angles in under 4 s, then hold one wide still view for 10 s. Set word cards between shots, never over them, at 0.11 s a character, their size jumping with their weight from 0.07 to 0.58 of the height, lines stepped down a diagonal. Elsewhere, join nine long shots by 0.5 s dissolves as the light rises. Break the grey once with a hard cut to deep red (`#6A1C1B`) under black. End on two large words and 10 s of black. No shot under 0.35 s, cuts kept off the music, readable muted; with reduced motion each shot is one still stepped by the viewer.

Also covers the lions (the second shot's duration is calculated exactly) and Kerensky climbing at the same pace between rising titles.
- Eisenstein, *The Film Sense* (1942), including "Word and Image" and "Form and Content: Practice" (Vertical Montage, the Nevsky XII shots and 17 measures, and twelve repeats of one measure in the battle): https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.96592. Montage "resembles a creation — rather than a sum of its parts".
- Eisenstein, "The Fourth Dimension in Cinema" (1929), *The Eisenstein Reader*: https://www.torch.ox.ac.uk/sitefiles/eisenstein-the-fourth-dimenson-in-cinema.pdf. Metric, rhythmic, tonal and overtonal montage; the Odessa "drumbeat" of feet and the pram as accelerator; the mist built on "combination of shots according to their basic optical light variations".
- Cinemetrics, Salt on ASL and projection speed: https://cinemetrics.uchicago.edu/article/9890e39c-ead0-49ff-a800-37d83723836a

- Secondary summaries of the montage methods: https://portfolio.newschool.edu/lune/2016/11/03/montage-methods-in-sergei-eisensteins-article ; https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2004/cteq/eisenstein/
