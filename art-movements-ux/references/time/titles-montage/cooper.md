# Kyle Cooper — Film Titles & Montage

## Essence
Cooper treats the credit roll as the film's first scene: the names are put inside the film's own objects (a killer's notebook, a specimen drawer, an MRI scan, a spider web, a suburban fence) and the words are damaged, multiplied, inverted or rebuilt by that material while they are being read. Each sequence keeps one register for the type and switches the image underneath it fast. A typical name is held 1.3-2.5 s, while the images change every 0.2-0.6 s, so the word is the steady thing and the world around it is not. Each sequence uses one method for how words enter and leave and applies it to every credit. Sequences open with long, near-still shots and then step up to fast change; Se7en finishes in bursts of 1-3 frame inserts, while Donnie Brasco closes on one 46 s held shot.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **The type holds still while the image changes underneath.** Moreau (title fixed over ~15 eye changes; Thewlis over 6), Mimic ("mira sorvino" 4.6 s over 6 changes), Arlington (title 3.8 s over ~12 flickers), Donnie Brasco (credit held while a face dissolves under it), Dawn and AHS (cards on a fixed ground between montage). Measured: credit holds of 1.3-4.8 s against image shots of 0.2-0.6 s, a ratio of about 4-10:1.
- **One entry-and-exit method per film, applied to every credit.** Spider-Man: assemble 0.6-0.8 s / scatter 0.4-0.6 s. Dawn: cut in / bleed out 1.4-1.8 s. Hulk: fade in / 1-2 frame blow-up out. AHS: cut in / 2-4 frame burn out. Donnie: ~1.25 s fade in / 0.5-0.75 s fade out. Sphere: small name, then letter-by-letter giant re-spelling at ~0.3 s per letter.
- **Fixed card periods.** AHS 1.30 s (four cards identical to the frame), Dawn 3.6-3.8 s name to name, Spider-Man ~5 s per name. Se7en is the exception, with irregular holds of 0.8-2.6 s broken by single-frame inserts.
- **Damage on 1-2 frames inside an otherwise readable hold.** Se7en (SE7EN blow-up 27.80-27.88; 1-frame inserts at 18.22, 49.2, 53.4; first frame mirror-flipped at 9.57), Hulk (blow-up exit in 1-2 frames), AHS (burn-out in 2-4 frames), Mimic and Dawn (1-2 frame flashes that the detector double-counts). The word is readable for most of its hold and broken for under 0.1 s.
- **Words appear in the world before they appear as type.** Arlington (street sign, then title), Mimic (catalogue numbers), Hulk (names placed inside the MRI data layout), Donnie (Kodak edge print as text), Se7en (notebook handwriting and the "-D35" scrawl next to the title).
- **Carry-over across cuts by shape and position, not by subject.** Moreau keeps the pupil at frame centre across species and sky. Mimic flips the same composition between negative and positive. Donnie keeps the eye line. Spider-Man has no cuts at all and moves the camera through one space.
- **Long, quiet openings and dense endings.** First shots: Moreau 28 s, Donnie 60 s, Sphere 79 s, Spider-Man 148 s. Motion, loudness and onset rates rise toward the end in Se7en (motion 3%→15%; -41→-27 dB), Moreau (0%→22%; -63→-38 dB) and Sphere (onsets 0→10.8/s). The densest passages (Se7en end, Dawn 37-45 s, Donnie 60-74 s) run at 0.04-0.3 s per shot.
- **Little colour, one accent.** Each film is near-monochrome (black, grey, teal, sepia) plus one accent: Se7en oxblood, Dawn type red, Hulk green, Spider-Man red, AHS fire in the last 15 s only. Moreau, Sphere and Arlington are exceptions that alternate two strong tint families.
- **Small type.** Name caps are 1.5-4% of frame height in 9 of 10 works. Only AHS cards (22% per line) and the momentary blow-ups (Se7en 29%, Sphere 35-50%, Moreau 50-80%, Hulk 20%) are large, and the blow-ups last 2-12 frames.
- **Image changes follow sung or noise events, not the beat.** Se7en cuts fall at chance against the 0.667 s beat. Dawn and AHS card changes land 70-430 ms after phrase or noise-block onsets.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Kyle Cooper.** *Make the word the one thing that holds still: count each short line's hold from its length, keep it at one seat while the layer beneath changes several times around one kept shape, give every line the same entry, exit and slot, show the key term in its source before setting it, and open on one unchanging state from which the rate of change steps up.*
Process:
1. Count the words before anything moves. List every line of text the run must show, in order, with its character count. A line is a name, a label or a phrase of 5–30 characters; longer text is split or kept out of the run. Give each line a hold from its count: never under 0.065 s per character (15 characters a second is the ceiling), and inside one band chosen once for the run: brisk, 0.08–0.12 s per character with a floor of 1.3 s, or easy, 0.25–0.4 s per character (3–5 s for a short name). The sum of the holds sets the length of the run. If the list changes, every later step is redone.
2. Decide once how text and picture share the frame. Over: the line sits on the changing layer. Between: the line has a flat card of its own (84–95% of the frame in one quiet value) and the picture runs in the gaps, from one insert to about 9 s of footage, 0.4–1.4 s a shot. One of the two is the rule for the run; the other is kept for the title line of step 7.
3. Seat the line and keep it small. A line does not move, grow or change during its hold. The seat is off-centre, in one third of the frame (upper left, or the lower third), the line 25–35% of the width and its letters 1.5–4% of the frame's height. One seat for every line is the default; where seats differ from line to line they stay inside these limits.
4. Fix one entry, one exit and one slot, and apply them to every line. Entry: a cut, or an arrival of 0.6–1.25 s that eases out, the last 0.2 s nearly still. Exit: a cut, or a departure of 0.4–1.8 s that may run under the next line's entry. The slot, start to start, is constant within about 10%, and the clean hold of step 1 takes 30–75% of it; where the picture runs between cards, the hold is the fixed thing (to the frame) and the gaps may vary. No line gets a transition of its own; holds of unequal length are a mode for one whole run, never for one line.
5. Change the layer, not the line. During one hold the layer beneath changes 3–10 times, by cuts 0.35–0.8 s apart (hold to change 4–15 : 1; the works go down to 0.2 s, a product does not). Every change keeps one shape at one position and a close value, or restates the same layout in the opposite value or another tint, at most once per hold. The mean brightness under the line barely moves: what changes is content, not light.
6. Meet the key term in its source. Before the run's key term is set as type, show it where the material itself carries it (a label, a row, a sign in the picture) for about 1.4 s, then set the line at that place. Other lines may borrow the material's own text layout as their seat (beside its columns or captions), never a new frame.
7. Give one line the exception. The line that names the whole arrives 18–23% of the way through, at a seat no other line uses, and holds 2–3 times a normal hold (4.8–9 s). It alone may leave the entry and exit pair, switch between over and between, and carry the run's one size event: a jump of 10–20 times in letter height lasting 2–12 frames, or about 4.5 s when the word is re-spelled letter by letter at the large size, after which it returns to its small size.
8. Allow one interruption per hold at most. It lasts 1–2 frames (under 0.1 s): the line displaced, doubled or replaced by a single foreign frame. It leaves an unbroken run at least as long as the reading time (0.065 s per character) on one side of it. Never two in a hold, and so fewer than one a second across the run.
9. Compose the rate of change over the whole run. For the first fifth or more, the layer is one state that does not change while lines already pass over it on their slots. From there the share of the frame that moves steps up (from 0–1% to about 5–15%) and stays above the opening level for most of the run; it may keep rising to the end, or close on one more held state.
10. Take cues from events, never from a grid. Where narration or a soundtrack runs, a line changes 0.07–0.43 s after the start of a phrase or a block of noise, and the layer's changes ignore the beat. With the sound off nothing is lost, because every hold was counted in step 1 from the text alone.
11. Write the still form: one frame per line, the line at its seat over the kept shape, numbered "n of N", with the title line at its own seat. If these frames in order do not tell the run, the list of step 1 was wrong.
Why it works:
- Figure and ground in time: in a frame where everything changes, the one thing that does not is the figure, so a line of 2–4% of the height wins the eye without size or contrast. Change the line with the layer and nothing is figure; hold both and the frame is a slide that is read once and left; enlarge the line and it competes with the picture it was meant to ride.
- Reading time is a count, not a feeling: about 0.06 s per character is what a short line costs, so a hold of 1.3–3 times that is safe at any tempo of the picture. Lines pressed to 17–20 characters a second are the ones that cannot be read, however calm the frame.
- Adaptation to the slot: an identical entry, exit and slot are learnt within two or three lines, after which the viewer knows when the next word comes and how long it stays, and attention goes to the word. A new transition per line makes the transition the content; a different slot per line makes every line a small alarm.
- Persistence of one object: changes that keep a shape at one place and a close value are read as one thing changing its state, so the eye is not pulled off the line to search the new picture. Remove the kept shape and each cut costs a fixation; the word then needs a longer hold or is lost.
- Recognition before reading: a term already met in the material is recognised when it is set, not deciphered, and the set line is read as belonging to the material. Set it cold and it is a caption laid on top.
- Salience by oddness: with every line equal in size, seat and slot, one line at another seat held twice as long is the title without a larger face. Multiply the exceptions and there is no rule left for them to break.
- A hold of several seconds is adapted to and stops being seen. One break of under 0.1 s re-arms it by its onset and costs no reading, since a clean run of the full reading time remains. Lengthen the break and the word is not read; repeat it and it becomes flicker.
- A rate that steps up is read against the still opening as its baseline, so the step is felt without any single fast passage. Open busy and there is nothing to rise from.
Colour relations: at the moments when a line is up, a quiet ground at one end of the value scale holds 35–95% of the frame. The field is near one hue family at low chroma, and there is one accent: the most saturated thing is small (the line itself at 1–3% of the frame, or one passage of the layer), and chroma above the middle of the scale appears nowhere else. Text on a card takes 1–11% of it. A second strong tint family is allowed only as the alternation of step 5, one family at a time, never two in one frame. The line keeps one colour through every change beneath it.
Space and line: lines of 5–30 characters; letters 1.5–4% of the frame's height; a line 25–35% of the width; seat off-centre in one third; title at a seat no other line uses; card ground 84–95%; 3–10 layer changes per hold, each keeping one shape at one position; one size event of 10–20 times for 2–12 frames, or about 4.5 s as a letter-by-letter re-spelling; black or empty ground as the beat between pictures in between mode; a rule or mark from the material may cross the line, never box it.
Type requirement: one register for every line of the run (one face, one size, one case), with role words in a second, smaller plain face. The face must hold at 1.5–4% of the frame's height over changing material: at 2% or under, capitals tracked by about one letter's width; at 2.5–4%, a bold condensed face may be set tight. Check the line against the lightest and the darkest state of the layer beneath it; if it fails on either, it goes to between mode on a card. Lines are counted in characters, so the count is part of the copy brief.
Motion and interaction: hold 0.08–0.12 s or 0.25–0.4 s per character, floor 1.3 s, never under 0.065 s per character; slot constant within about 10% (or a fixed hold with varying gaps between cards); entry a cut or 0.6–1.25 s ease-out; exit a cut or 0.4–1.8 s, allowed to overlap the next entry; layer changes are cuts 0.35–0.8 s apart, a word passes from card to picture by a 0.4 s dissolve; title hold 4.8–9 s; interruption 1–2 frames, once per hold; cue lag 0.07–0.43 s. No change of the whole frame from light to dark and back more than 3 times a second: the kept shape and close value of step 5 are what make fast content changes safe, and a change that fails them is slowed to one per hold. In a product the slot is a default: a tap or key advances to the next line at once, a pause freezes the line and the layer together, and nothing advances by itself before the reading floor. Reduced motion: one still per line at its seat over one state of the layer, entered and left by a cut on the slot or on the reader's own advance; no layer change inside a hold, no interruption, no size event; the source and its set line become two stills; the title keeps its own seat and its double hold; the opening keeps one layer state under the first fifth of the lines, and later lines each take a new state, so the rise survives as the rate at which the ground is replaced.
Fails when: the text is sentences or paragraphs, or any line exceeds about 30 characters; the layer must itself be read (a chart, a table, a map), since it changes 3–10 times per hold and is taken in as texture; there is no picture or data layer to change, which leaves a plain slide show; the words are controls the user must press while the layer changes; a message must stay until acknowledged; fewer than about five lines, so the slot is never learnt; several lines need the exception; the audience or the setting cannot take any layer change faster than one a second, where only the reduced form is honest.
Signature (do not carry together): scratched, jittering hand lettering misregistered on black · macro footage of specimens, notebooks and scans in a near-monochrome grade with one blood-red or acid-green accent · one-frame inserts and blow-ups cut in staccato under an industrial score.

## Space & composition
- Names sit off centre, small, often in the upper-left third (Se7en boxes x0.19-0.55, y0.23-0.38) or the lower third (Dawn y0.65-0.82). Title cards break from the name position (Se7en title at x0.63-0.75, right).
- Black ground is 35-95% of frame area in credit moments: Se7en dark frames 34.7%, Spider-Man 73%, Dawn cards 95% black.
- Scale jumps are extreme and brief: Se7en 3%→29% cap height for 2 frames; Moreau ~5%→80% letters cropped by the frame; Sphere 2.5%→50%; Hulk 1.5%→20% in 1-2 frames.

- Layering: type over imagery in Moreau, Mimic, Arlington, Hulk and Sphere; type on its own black or grey card in Se7en, Dawn and AHS; type inside a 3D space in Spider-Man.

## UI translations
1. **Captions over footage:** Keep the caption at one seat and let the picture change beneath it: "mira sorvino" holds 4.6 s over about six changes (*Mimic*), the *Arlington Road* title 3.8 s over about twelve. In a product keep the changes 0.35 s or more apart.
2. **Reading time:** Count characters before timing anything. At 15–17 characters a second a name of 8–16 characters needs 0.5–1.1 s; hold it 1.3–3 times that. "CREATED BY RYAN MURPHY & BRAD FALCHUK" at 20 characters a second (*American Horror Story*) is the case to avoid.
3. **Stagger of equal items:** Give every item the same slot, not a curve: 3.2–4.0 s from name to name (*Dawn of the Dead*), cards of 1.30 s to the frame with gaps of 2.8–8.8 s (*American Horror Story*), 4.4–5.4 s per name with 3.6 s locked (*Spider-Man*).
4. **One transition per sequence:** Choose one entry and exit pair and use it for every line: assemble in 0.6–0.8 s and leave in 0.4–0.6 s (*Spider-Man*); cut in and dissolve away over 1.4–1.8 s (*Dawn of the Dead*); fade in 1.25 s and out 0.5–0.75 s (*Donnie Brasco*).
5. **Continuity across a cut:** Keep one shape at one place through a run of changes (the pupil at frame centre through about fifteen eyes, *The Island of Dr. Moreau*), or restate one layout in the opposite value (*Mimic*, negative and positive).
6. **Label from the content:** Show a term where the material carries it, then set it: the photographed street sign for 1.4 s, then the typeset title over it (*Arlington Road*); names set beside a scan's own data columns (*The Incredible Hulk*).
7. **Title or key figure:** Put the one line that names the whole at a seat nothing else uses, about a fifth of the way in, and hold it twice as long: 26.94 s into 121 s, at the right, for 4.78 s, against names at the left for 1.5–2.6 s (*Se7en*).
8. **Small type on a busy frame:** Letters at 1.5–4% of the frame's height are enough when the line is the only still thing; at 1.5% track capitals by about one letter's width (*Donnie Brasco*, *The Incredible Hulk*).
9. **Emphasis as an event:** Spend size for a moment, not as a state: 3% to 29% of the height for two frames (*Se7en*), 1.5% to 20% in one or two frames as the exit (*The Incredible Hulk*). Once per sequence in a product.
10. **Opening:** Start on one state that does not change while the first lines pass over it: 28 s of sky (*The Island of Dr. Moreau*), 60 s of one close-up (*Donnie Brasco*).
11. **Pacing a deck or story scroll:** Let the amount of change step up from the still opening and stay above it for most of the run: moving share 3% to 15% per block (*Se7en*), 0% to 22% (*The Island of Dr. Moreau*); a last held shot may close it (46 s in *Donnie Brasco*).
12. **Cards between pictures:** Where a line cannot hold over the picture, give it a flat card (84% ground, 10.5% type, *American Horror Story*; 95% ground, 1–3% type, *Dawn of the Dead*) and run the picture in the gaps.
13. **Narration and sound:** Change a line just after a phrase begins (70–250 ms, *Dawn of the Dead*; 100–430 ms after a noise block, *American Horror Story*) and leave the picture's cuts off the beat (*Se7en*: at chance against a 0.667 s beat). The timing must still hold with the sound off.
14. **Reduced motion:** One still per line at its seat, numbered, advanced by a cut; the title keeps its own seat and its double hold.

## What this artist would reject
- Credits that fade in and out identically over a static background with nothing in the image reacting to them (in nine of ten works the image changes under a held word, or the word is damaged by the image's material).
- Type at a constant generous size: names are 1.5-4% of frame height, and big type appears for 2-12 frames only (AHS is the exception).
- Cutting on the beat grid: Se7en cuts fall at chance against the beat, and Dawn and AHS changes follow sung or noise events.
- A different entry animation per credit: each film uses one method for every name.
- Full-colour footage: each film is near-monochrome plus one accent hue.
- Clean, unbroken holds: readable holds are broken by 1-2 frame inserts, flips, ghosting or burns.

## Signature prompt seed
Design the sequence as a Cooper title. List the lines and count their characters; hold each 0.08–0.12 s per character and never under 1.3 s. Set every line small (3% of the frame's height) at one seat in the upper left, in one face, pale `#FDFEF1` on a near-black ground `#060506` that keeps a third or more of the frame, with one accent (`#820B05`) as the smallest area. Hold the line still while the picture beneath changes 3–10 times around one kept shape, cuts 0.35 s or more apart. Give every line the same entry, exit and slot. Show the key term in the material for 1.4 s before setting it. Bring the title a fifth of the way in, at the right, for twice a normal hold, with one two-frame jump in size. One break of under 0.1 s per hold at most. Open on one unchanging state and let change step up from it. It must read with the sound off; no flashing above 3 per second; with reduced motion, one numbered still per line.
