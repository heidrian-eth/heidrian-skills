# Pablo Ferro — Film Titles & Montage

## Essence
Ferro runs two clocks. One is a slow, steady pulse of hand-drawn words: thin and very tall, set over moving pictures, each held about 3 s and replaced every 2–5 s. The other is a percussive cut of single words and single images, each 2–8 frames long, that alternate like syllables against a beat. Both treat the frame as a field to be divided: by letters that fill 70–80% of its height, by tiles that multiply one shot up to 54 times, and by panels that grow out of black until they become the whole screen.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Two clocks: a slow card pulse and a syllable cut.** Title sequences hold each credit for 2.9–4 s on a regular period (Stop Making Sense 2.07 s name changes, L.A. Confidential ≈3.5 s, Men in Black 5.0 s, Beetlejuice 5.3 s, Strangelove 5–6 s between card changes). Trailers and commercials cut single words and single images at 2–9 frames (Strangelove trailer 6–9 frames per word, countdown digits 2 frames every 6; Clockwork 3–8 frames; Dulux 0.3–0.6 s).
- **The word as a shot.** In the Strangelove trailer, the Clockwork trailer and the Dulux spot a sentence is spelled one word per full-frame card, alternating with pictures, and a word is sometimes repeated around one image (Clockwork SATIRIC–face–SATIRIC). Two typefaces alternate in the Strangelove trailer and the Dulux spot.
- **Size by role, not by hierarchy of reading ease.** Short words are drawn to fill the height (Strangelove trailer "I", "TO", "OR:" ≈85–90%; SELLERS 68%; MIB block 79%). Long words are set smaller at the same width. Credits in the 1968 panel works and L.A. Confidential are tiny (3–4% of the height).
- **Thin, tall, hand-drawn letters over a running image** (Strangelove 1964 at ≈5 : 1 with a 1.2% stroke; Stop Making Sense 1984; Men in Black 1997 at ≈3.6 : 1, beaded). Over 33 years the letter keeps its single stroke, its tall proportion and its near-zero leading; the image shows between the strokes.
- **Overlap instead of gaps (1964) and gaps instead of overlap (1988–97).** Strangelove cards cross-dissolve for 1.5–2.5 s, so no frame is empty; Beetlejuice and MIB leave 1.5–2 s of empty picture between names.
- **The continuous shot under changing words.** Beetlejuice (36 s and more without a cut), Stop Making Sense (62 s), MIB (star field, then one CG flight) and the Strangelove side view (22.4 s carrying three cards) keep the picture going while only the words change. In the countdown even the inserts do not stop the plane shot.
- **An anchor that stays while its partner changes:** WITH held 18 s over nine names (Stop Making Sense); L.A. Confidential names held across 1–2 picture cuts; Thomas Crown mosaic outlines held while single cells change.
- **Black as the main area, images as panels.** Thomas Crown lit area ≤43% (often 5–25%); polo multiples on 51% black; Bullitt 75% near-black; Clockwork insets on black. Panels enter by sliding (≈2 W/s fast entries with a dead stop; 0.1–0.4 W/s drifts), pop in a few at a time (≈9 tiles/s), multiply one shot (22 and ≈54 copies), or grow from 17% of the height to full frame over ≈16 s, accelerating.
- **The word as a window.** Bullitt names slide off and leave letter mattes through which the next shot appears (48.5–58 s).
- **Graphic carry-over across cuts** (Dulux stroke over 4 pieces; the B-52 through the countdown; one ball shot repeated through grid, inset and full frame).
- **Flicker above the safety limit in the trailers:** Strangelove countdown 4 flashes/s; Clockwork insets ≈3 bright flashes/s with a full-field white flash. The title sequences never flash.
- **Sound.** In the trailer the words and their sound onsets change together, within about a frame (countdown and question words) and a voice speaks each word as it appears. In the Strangelove titles legato music and dissolves run independently. The quiet Stop Making Sense open is held in near silence.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Pablo Ferro.** *Keep one carrier running unbroken and change one thing on it at a time: put words that must be read on a fixed period with a counted reading budget, hold whatever successive cards share, give a glance-sentence one word per beat at one seat between pictures of matching value, divide the held frame instead of cutting it, and land on the longest wordless hold.*
Process:
1. Choose the carrier and never stop it. One thing runs through the whole passage: a continuous picture, a frame that is never replaced as a whole, or a word that stays. It spans at least three changes of the other layer (three cards on one 22s shot; one word over eight or more names in 18s; 36–62s of picture with no cut). Whatever covers the carrier replaces its time and does not pause it: when it shows again it is where it would have been. If the carrier changes, every later step is redone.
2. Sort the words onto two clocks, and keep the clocks in separate passages. Anything that must be read and kept (a name, a figure, a fact, an unfamiliar word) goes on the slow clock. A short sentence of familiar single words that only has to be caught goes on the fast clock. A word is never moved from one clock to the other to save time.
3. Slow clock: fix one period for the passage and count the reading. Period 2–5.5s, the same for every card of a run; a title card or a block of names gets one longer slot of 7–8s. Each card is fully up for about three fifths of it, with fades of 0.25–0.5s, and the join is chosen once: either the carrier alone for the last 1.5–2s of each period, or the outgoing and incoming cards overlapped for 1.5–2.5s with no empty frame and, in exchange, two or three wordless rests of about 4s. Only the time a card is fully up counts as reading time: characters ÷ that time is 3–6 for a key item and at most 12–16 for a supporting block. A card over 16 is split across periods; a card at 30 or more is texture, and is used only where nobody needs to read it.
4. Hold what successive cards share. Where cards have a common part (a role, a heading, a unit), set it once, leave it up for the whole run (it may dim, it never leaves) and change only the partner under it; the period may then drop to about 2s, the partner fully up for half of it. Where the picture has to cut, the word that is up stays still across the cut, for one or two cuts.
5. Change one thing per interval. One word, one cell or one panel changes while everything else holds; inside a held group, members change in turn 0.25–0.9s apart and the group's outline does not move. Two changes are never timed together unless they are one event.
6. Fast clock: one word per card, at one seat. Every word sits at the same centre and is drawn to the same width (65–75% of the view), so its height falls as its letters rise (about 85% at one to three letters, about 22% at eleven). Cards alternate with single pictures: word, picture, word; a word may come back once on the far side of its picture. A word holds 0.3–0.6s and a picture 0.2–0.5s, with characters ÷ hold at 15 or less (Ferro's trailer words run 0.13–0.5s; the shortest are not read). The word card and the pictures beside it stay within about a tenth of the value range of each other, so the alternation is a change of content and not of light, and nothing swings light to dark more than 3 times a second. One graphic element continues through the picture pieces (a stroke, a moving object) so that they read as one action. A sentence of three or four words takes 1.5–3s. A passage ends on a single card that may invert the values, once.
7. Divide the frame instead of cutting it. When several pictures are needed, the frame holds and they enter as panels on a ground that keeps more than half of it (lit share 5–25% for most of the run and never over 43%), separated by gutters of about 1.5% of the width. A panel enters fast and stops dead (two view-widths a second, no easing), drifts slowly if at all (0.1 a second) and leaves at about 0.4. To weight one action, multiply it (the same picture in 20–54 synchronised copies, popped in about nine a second) and do not cut to a second action; hold each layout 1–3s and part two multiples with one full-frame single of about 2s. Words sit in the ground beside the panels, never on them.
8. Cross through the word just read. Where one view must give way to another, the word moves off in a straight line at constant speed (0.15–0.3 of the height a second) after a still hold of about 1s, and the next view first appears inside the footprint it leaves, spreading from there over about 2s.
9. Open on the carrier and land on a wordless hold. The carrier runs alone for 4–10s before the first word. Density rises to its one full state a fifth to a quarter of the way in and thins again (lit share about 5 → 27 → 42 → 20 → 3%). The landing takes about a tenth of the whole: either the longest wordless rest between cards (about 2.5 times the other rests) and then the last card, or one panel growing from a sixth of the height to the whole frame, each stage quicker than the last, and then a cut to the ground.
10. Write the still form. Every slow card is its own still, in order, with the shared part printed once above its partners; the fast sentence is set whole on one frame with its pictures in a row beside it; the divided frame is shown at its fullest layout; the landing is the last card alone. If the order of the stills does not carry the sequence, step 2 was sorted wrongly.
Why it works:
- Persistence of one object: a carrier that never stops stays the same thing, so each word is an event on a known ground and is found by oddness. Cut the carrier with every word and both layers restart together: the viewer has to find the subject again in the same moment as reading.
- Prediction: on a fixed period the viewer learns when the next word is due and spends the gap on the picture, so the two layers are attended in turn and neither is lost. Make the period uneven and the eye waits at the word's seat. Remove the gap and reading competes with the picture all the time, which is why the overlapped join needs its long rests.
- Reading time is a count, not a feeling. The cards that could be read whole stood at 3–16 characters a second of full view; the ones at 32 and 52 could not, and read as a mass. Equalise all cards to one length and the long ones fail unnoticed.
- One seat and one width: no eye movement is needed between cards, so a single glance takes a word. Put two words on a card, or move the seat, and the eye has to travel and the next card is missed.
- Neighbours in time are grouped (proximity): a word between two pictures names both, and a word repeated around a picture frames it. Remove the alternation and the words are a list and the pictures a slideshow.
- Adaptation to the held part: what is read once and stays becomes the baseline, so only the difference is read. Remove the anchor and each card costs twice the reading; change several things at once and no change can be attributed.
- Figure and ground: panels on a ground that keeps the majority stay separate figures in one stable field, and several can be compared at once. A cut shows them one after another and leaves the comparing to memory. Count carries weight: one action multiplied is heavier without lasting longer.
- A fast alternation between frames of near-equal value is a change of meaning and not of brightness (a cut detector missed all 14 such cuts in 2.75s). Widen the value step and the same rhythm becomes a flash.
- The longest stillness resets expectation, so the last card needs no emphasis of its own. A part that grows into the whole hands the frame to the content without a break.
Colour relations: one ground value on half or more of the view (51–96%); words at the far end of the value range from their ground and small in area (1–8%); saturated colour a few per cent at most (under 1% to 5%), kept for one word or one mark; where panels are tinted, each takes one flat hue and no two neighbours share it; in the fast clock word card and picture share a value level; one inversion of word and ground, at the end only.
Space and line: one centred seat, a single word or a vertical stack; a word lies over the picture only when its strokes are thin enough for the picture to show between them (stroke about 1.2% of the height, total cover 2–8%), otherwise it sits in the ground beside the panels; word width constant and height variable; panels unequal or a grid of identical tiles, gutters about 1.5% of the width; the frame never fully lit.
Type requirement: the kit's family needs a narrow cut and a wide range of sizes, so that a one-letter word and an eleven-letter word reach the same width (roughly 22–90% of the height). Two contrasting cuts may alternate from card to card in the fast clock to mark the word boundary. Read-and-keep words may be small (3–4% of the height) if they are held at 3–6 characters a second in clear ground. Text that fails the count is split, never shrunk.
Motion and interaction: slow period 2–5.5s, one slot of 7–8s for a title or name block; fade in 0.25–0.5s, out 0.5–1s; overlapped dissolve 1.3–2.9s; gap 1.5–2s; rests 3–4s and one of about 10s; fast pieces 0.2–0.6s by cut; cell changes 0.25–0.9s apart; panel entry 0.25–0.5s, linear with a dead stop; exits and slides linear; only the final growth accelerates. The slow clock can be handed to the viewer: one press advances one card while the carrier runs on, and any passage that advances by itself has a pause that holds the card. Sound lands with its word, within about a frame (40ms) either way; every word is on screen in full, so nothing depends on the sound, and the slow clock runs free of any music. No full-field flash, no piece under 0.2s, no more than 3 light-to-dark swings a second. Reduced motion: fades become cuts, the carrier becomes one still per card, slow cards advance on a press, the fast sentence is one still frame with all its words, panels appear in place in their final layout, and the landing is the last card held.
Fails when: there is no carrier (unrelated screens with nothing that continues); the text is prose, or items that must be compared or copied; the viewer must act on a word while it is up; fast-clock words are long, unfamiliar or in a second language; a card cannot be split under 16 characters a second; the list is long enough that the period times the count outlasts the visit; everything on the view changes at once (live boards); the pictures are too bright or too varied to hold a value level with the word cards.
Signature (do not carry together): very tall, thin hand-drawn capitals filling the height of a running picture · one white word per black card cut against film frames on the beat · a black frame split into tinted panels and grids of one repeated shot.

## UI translations
1. **Captions over video:** Run every caption on one period and let the picture run on: up about 3s, picture alone 1.5–2s, fades 0.25–0.5s (*Men in Black*: 5.0s period; *Beetlejuice*: 5.3s over one uncut aerial shot).
2. **Reading time:** Count characters against the time a card is fully up: 3–6 a second for a name or figure (*Dr. Strangelove* SELLERS 5.6, *Bullitt* 4.4, *Beetlejuice* 3.1), no more than 12–16 for a block. The *Strangelove* crew cards at 32 and 52 a second cannot be read whole: split such a card.
3. **Deck or onboarding steps:** Set the part every step shares once and hold it; change only the partner under it, about every 2s, fully up for half of that (*Stop Making Sense*: WITH held 18s while names change every 2.07s).
4. **Slides over a cut:** When the picture behind a caption has to change, keep the caption still across the change (*L.A. Confidential*: names held over one or two cuts on a 3.5s period).
5. **Kinetic type:** Spell a short sentence one word per card, every word at one centre and one width (65–75% of the view), with one picture between words (*Dulux* spot: PROVE / FOR / YOUR / SELF, words 0.3–0.6s, pictures 0.2–0.5s).
6. **Emphasis by repetition:** Show a word, one picture, and the word again (*A Clockwork Orange* trailer: SATIRIC, face, SATIRIC) to tie a word to an image without a longer hold.
7. **Countdown or interruption:** Let an overlay replace the running content and not pause it: when it clears, the content is where it would have been (*Strangelove* trailer: the plane's position advances behind the digits).
8. **Continuity across pieces:** Carry one graphic through a run of short pieces so they read as one action (*Dulux*: a painted stripe continued over four pieces and closed as an L).
9. **Dashboard intro or data story:** Hold the frame and bring views in as panels on a ground that keeps most of it (lit share never over 43%, *The Thomas Crown Affair* titles); change one cell at a time, 0.25–0.9s apart, with the block's outline still.
10. **Emphasis by count:** To weight one item, show it many times at once (22 and about 54 copies of one shot, *Thomas Crown* polo montage) for 1–3s, then once at full size.
11. **Panel entry:** Fast and linear with a dead stop (about two view-widths a second, *Thomas Crown* cast mosaic); drifts at 0.1 and exits at 0.4 of the width a second. No ease on arrival.
12. **Transition:** Open the next view inside the footprint of the word just read: hold it about 1s, slide it off at 0.15–0.3 of the height a second, and let the new view spread from its place over 2s (*Bullitt*).
13. **Pacing by density:** Start sparse, reach one full state a fifth to a quarter of the way in, and thin out again (*Thomas Crown*: lit area 5 → 27 → 42 → 20 → 3%).
14. **Opening:** Give the picture 4–10s alone before the first word (*Men in Black*: 4s of star field; *Strangelove*: about 10s of planes).
15. **Landing:** Put the longest wordless hold just before the last card (*Strangelove*: about 10s against rests of 4s), or grow one panel from a sixth of the height to the full frame, quicker at each stage, and cut (*Thomas Crown* coda, about 16s).
16. **Sound:** Land a cue on its word, within about a frame either way (*Strangelove* trailer countdown and question words); run slow cards free of the music (*Strangelove* titles). Every word is on screen in full, so the sequence works muted.
17. **Accessibility:** Do not carry the trailers' flicker: digits flashing 4 times a second (*Strangelove*) and bright insets about 3 times a second with a full-field flash (*Clockwork Orange*) are at or over the limit of 3 flashes a second (WCAG 2.3.1). Keep word cards and pictures at one value level and pieces at 0.2s or longer.
18. **Accessibility:** Words at 21–32 characters a second (*Clockwork* EXCITING: 6 frames) are not read. In a product hold fast words 0.3–0.6s, at 15 characters a second or less, and give every self-advancing passage a pause that holds the card.
19. **Accessibility:** Under `prefers-reduced-motion` each slow card is a still advanced by a press, the fast sentence is one frame with all its words, panels appear in place, and fades are cuts.

## What this artist would reject
- Uniform type size: the same credit level is drawn at 4% or 85% of the height depending on the word and the medium.
- Cutting a running shot to insert text: the plane keeps moving through the countdown, and the aerials run uncut under the names.
- An evenly lit, full frame: black takes 51–96% of most colour works.
- Mechanical, typeset regularity in the titles: the signature letters wobble, outline and bead.
- Moving everything at once: one cell, one name, one panel changes while the rest holds (Thomas Crown cells, WITH, L.A. Confidential names across cuts).
- Equal pacing across media: cards hold 3–5 s in titles and 2–9 frames in trailers.

## Signature prompt seed
Design the sequence as a Ferro title. Run one continuous picture under everything and never cut it for a word. On a near-black ground (`#030303`) set one name at a time in white single-stroke capitals drawn by hand, about 4 : 1 tall, stroke near 1.2% of the height, the block filling 70–80% of the height at the centre. Each name fades in over 0.3s, stays 3s and fades out, with 1.5–2s of empty picture before the next: a 5s period. Hold a small role word above while the names change under it. For several pictures, keep the frame and slide in flat-tinted panels (`#6E016B`, `#7796A4`) with thin black gutters, lit area under 43%, small white credits in the black beside them, one red word (`#C30606`) at 2%. End on 10s without words, then one last card. No flicker, no full-field flash; with reduced motion show each card as a still in order.
