# John Cage — Process Music

Evidence notes. Nobody listened for this file. Everything below was read from waveform and spectrogram sheets, from measurements, from scores and images, and from published accounts. Character words (timbre, mood, instrument) carry a source. "agy listening" marks an earlier machine description of a neutral-named file; three usable ones exist (Sonata II Ferreira, Indeterminacy, Fontana Mix). A fourth (Music of Changes, Sultan) names an instrument the work does not call for and is not relied on.

## Essence
Cage fixes a container of time first (a bar count, a square-root proportion, a clock bracket, a tape length) and then lets something other than taste fill it: a table of prepared strings, coin tosses on 8 × 8 charts, stones traced on paper, transparent sheets dropped on each other. The container is exact and the contents are free to be loud, soft, dense, or nothing at all. In the measured recordings the frame holds to within a second or two over tens of seconds, while no event inside predicts the next.

## Detailed observations

**First Construction (in Metal).** The published plan is 16 units of 16 bars in 4/4, grouped 4-3-2-3-4, each 16-bar unit divided into bars by the same 4-3-2-3-4, then a 9-bar coda. The plan can be read off the opening minute. Attacks begin at 1.4 s. A broadband roar fills 1.4–9.7 s and cuts off there: 8.3 s, four bars at 2.1 s. Detail continues to 15.6 s (3 bars), then four isolated soft hits at 15.6, 16.65, 17.7 and 18.75 s, 1.05 s apart (two bars marked in halves), then a full change of texture at 20.05 s. The next 3 + 4 bars run to 33.8 s, where a hard dry texture enters. So the first unit is 32.4 s: 9 bars in 18.65 s (2.07 s a bar) and 7 bars in 13.75 s (1.96 s a bar). The second unit joins at 33.8, 41.4, 47.0, 51.05 and 56.65 s. The lengths are 7.6, 5.6, 4.05 and 5.6 s, and at 1.9 s a bar that is 4.0, 2.95, 2.1 and 2.95 bars, i.e. 4-3-2-3 with the last 4 beginning. Every join is a hard change of sound group across the whole spectrum, not a fade. The texture at 33.8–41.2 s is bright and gapped (energy above 2 kHz, decays of 0.1–0.2 s, silences between strokes). The texture at 41.4–47.0 s is a low continuous band (near 100 Hz) with a hiss above it. The bright one returns at 47.0–50.5 s. Pulse: quaver 0.22–0.26 s (strongest periods 0.221, 0.453, 0.673, 0.906 s, a clean 1:2:3:4 series), so a crotchet is about 0.45 s. Tempo drifts from 0.26 s (0–15 s) to 0.23 s (from 60 s) and gives other periods later (0.32–0.35 s at 315–345 s and 480 s, but at strengths of only 0.10–0.27, so a tempo change there is uncertain). Density per 5 s block runs from 0 to 5.6 onsets/s. A quiet stretch at 183–230 s sits at −51 to −56 dB, 20–25 dB below the rest, with single strokes about every 2.15 s (191.1, 193.3, 195.6, 197.8, 200.2, 202.4, 204.7, 206.6, 209.1, 211.2, 213.3). The returning full texture at 235 s is at −23 dB by 240 s: −49, −38, −32, −23 in four 5 s blocks, a rise of 26 dB in 15 s. From 480 s everything decays: three low strokes at 488.0, 489.7 and 491.6 s, then a tail to 505 s. The steady broadband band at 512–531 s looks like audience noise, not music (unconfirmed). Energy is split into a low peak (30–110 Hz, 27%) and a high one (above 1.76 kHz, 54%) with almost nothing at 220–880 Hz (10%). The strike sheet at 100–104 s shows an even run, `x x x x x x . x x x x x x x x x x` at 0.23 s, with a low gong-range swell entering at 101.4 s under it, two layers.

**Sonatas and Interludes.** Wikipedia's table (after Pritchett) gives each sonata a unit of bars and a proportion: Sonata V is 9 bars × (2, 2, 2½, 2½), so A = 18 bars, B = 22½. The whole set is ordered symmetrically: I–IV, interlude, V–VIII, two interludes, IX–XII, interlude, XIII–XVI. *Sonata V (Berman).* A continuous quaver stream of 0.163 s (strengths 0.65, 0.61, 0.56 at 1, 2, 3 quavers; every 10 s block gives 0.15–0.16 s). A starts at 0.86 s and starts again at 23.65 s, after the only gap (0.35 s of near-silence at 23.2–23.6 s), so A = 22.8 s. At 18 bars that is 1.27 s a bar, exactly 8 quavers of 0.159 s. B starts at 46.8 s with a short figure and a rest, four times: 46.8, 49.4, 52.1, 55.4 s. The rests after the figures grow (about 0.3, 0.8, 0.9, 1.3 s). A loud run follows at 58.8–69.5 s, two ringing strokes at 70.4 and 71.35 s, a decay, and silence to 77.1 s. Then B repeats: 77.1, 79.8, 82.6, 85.85, 89.3, 101.2, 102.4 s, each 30.2–30.8 s after its first version (correlation peak 30.58 s). The predicted B is 22.5 × 1.27 = 28.6 s; the player's version is about 1.8 s longer, all of it in the rests. My correlation tool alone suggested A = 27–28 s. The sheet (the clear restart at 23.65 s) shows 22.8 s, and the sheet is followed. Loudness: A −37 to −44 dB, B's figures −48 to −57, B's run −35, i.e. a fall of 10–15 dB and then a jump of about 18 dB, at the same place in both statements. Energy sits at 110–220 Hz (44%) and 440–880 Hz (34%), with the octave between at 17%: the low repeated figure and a higher one read as two lines. Ferreira's Sonata V: pulse 0.151 s, B repeats after 25.56 s, A after about 20.9 s by correlation only (A/B 0.82; the table predicts 0.80; Berman's is 0.75). *Sonata II (Berman).* A repeats after 22.84 s (correlation 0.35, a clear peak) and B after 49.78 s (0.40). The table predicts B/A = 2⅜ : 1½ = 1.58; the measured ratio is 2.18. The sheet shows why B is long: it holds single ringing strokes (119.4, 121.2, 129.15 s) that decay over 2–8 s, short rests of 0.5–1.5 s, and a closing even tremolo of about 6 s (89.35–95.3 s, and again from 139.5 s) at 0.17 s a stroke. Whether the extra length is slower playing or unequal bars in the table I could not resolve. Pulse 0.244 s in A and 0.16–0.17 s in the tremolos. Density jumps from 1.4–3.6 onsets/s to 10.2 and 10.8 onsets/s in single 5 s blocks at 90 and 140 s. Those blocks are 50 s apart, the same figure in both statements of B. Ferreira's Sonata II shows 0.255 s and the same shape (8.2 onsets/s block late in B). agy listening describes it as a single prepared piano of dry thuds and ringing bell-like strokes, 1–2 layers, A A B B.

**In a Landscape.** Structure 15 × 15 bars, 5-7-3 (Wise Music note), both pedals held. Drury: onset period 0.406 s (then 0.813, 1.219, 1.625: 1:2:3:4), and 0.38–0.44 s in every 20 s block of the whole 583 s. Löffler: 0.37–0.39 s, and 538 s long. The ratio of lengths 1.084 equals the ratio of pulses 1.08: one player only runs the same counts faster. The opening bar is six pulses with the weight on two of them. Accents at 5.05 | 6.17, 6.56 | 8.52, 8.91 | 10.9, 11.3 | 13.35, 13.8 | 15.9, 16.3 | 18.3, 18.7 | 20.9, 21.5 s: pairs of accents 0.4 s apart, every 2.45 s (`X X . . . .` on a 6-pulse cell), with the quiet notes between continuing at the pulse. 15 bars of 2.5 s make 38 s. One 15-bar unit can be seen whole. From 230.5 s to 268.5 s (38.0 s) the low register drops out, the energy sits above 600 Hz at −45 dB, and a single strong note returns at 230.55, 233.3, 235.8, 238.5, 241.2, 243.75, 246.35, 248.9, 251.35, 253.85, 256.4, 258.95, 261.5, 263.95 and 266.6 s: 15 accents, mean spacing 2.57 s, so fifteen bars. At 268.5 s the low layer returns and the level climbs from −45 to −31 dB by 275 s. Counting from 5.05 s in 38 s units, 268.5 s is the end of unit 7, which is neither a 5 nor a 12 boundary of the 5-7-3 plan (about 195 s and 461 s). The best long-range similarity is at 190.1 s, near the 5-unit point, but it is weak (0.09). Loudness range 18.6 dB (10th to 95th percentile), the narrowest of the set. The 5th-percentile level is −46 dB, only 22 dB under the 95th, so there is no true silence until the last decay: −47 at 565 s, −64 at 575 s, −83 at 580 s. Register 220–880 Hz holds 82% of the energy, nothing below 110 Hz; a steady line at 55 Hz on the sheet is mains hum. The long horizontal lines on the sheet (2–6 s) fit the published instruction to hold both pedals: rests are filled by ringing.

**Music of Changes, Book I.** Every chart is 8 × 8 = 64 cells; sounds sit only in the odd-numbered cells and the even ones are silence. Phrase lengths follow 3, 5, 6¾, 6¾, 5, 3⅛ (sum 29⅝) for the whole work. Density is 1–8 layers; a quarter note is always 2.5 cm of paper; dynamics run pppp–ffff (Wikipedia, Getty). Book I is one of four; Tudor plays it in 235 s of sound. Reading the sheet: first attack 2.9 s, then 5.3 s (loudest so far), 6.65, 8.65, 10.65, 15.7, 19.05, 20.1, 22.2, 22.75, then a full chord at 25.3 s, the 5 s block going from −57 to −37 dB. Single notes such as the one at 19.05 s (a partial line at about 350 Hz, 1.5 s) sit beside chords that fill 100–4000 Hz. No pulse: the best candidate (1.057 s) has strength 0.19 and is not a harmonic series. In 45–60 s there are only five attacks (50.2, 53.6, 55.0, 56.0, 56.7). In 108–124 s there are about 24, with 3–4 overlapping decays. Density per 5 s block runs 0–3.6 onsets/s in no order (2.0, 0.6, 0.2, 0.2, 1.6, 2.8, 3.2, 1.2, 0.0 …). Silent stretches of ≥ 1 s below floor + 10 dB: at 45.9, 48.8, 82.1, 149.2, 151.2, 157.5, 218.8, 228.5 s (1.0–3.5 s), 15 s in all, 6% of the book. Loudness range 48.8 dB, the widest of the set, which fits the pppp–ffff chart. The agy text on the Sultan file calls this a prepared piano in soft dynamics; as far as I know the work is for an unprepared piano, played also on the strings, and the Getty and Wikipedia texts name no preparation, so that text is set aside. Where they agree: long gaps at irregular places (Sultan: 23 gaps ≥ 1 s in 240 s, 58 s total, none longer than 5.4 s).

**4'33".** Three published sets of lengths, all totalling 4:33: 30" / 2'23" / 1'40" (the Woodstock and Kremen scores) and 33" / 2'40" / 1'20" (printed in the 1960 tacet score as the premiere's). The sources cannot explain the difference. The Kremen score is nearly blank pages with vertical lines. It is calibrated "1 page = 7 inches = 56 seconds" (1 inch = 8 s); the first movement is the 3¾-inch gap between two lines. In the Tudor film the music-free part runs from about 24 s to 318 s. The floor is −67 to −71 dB (5 s blocks), 25–30 dB under the surrounding speech (−41 to −54). In 294 s the onset counter finds about ten events. The spectrum is a fixed hum (lines near 55, 110, 165–190 Hz) plus a soft hiss. The only incidents are short broadband double clicks about 0.2 s apart, above 880 Hz, at 36.3, 55.0, 149.5, 153.8, 184.5 and 192.5 s, and clusters at 20.5–24.3, 226–240 and 317.8–322 s. Reading the clusters as movement ends gives a last movement of about 238–318 s, 78–80 s, close to 1'20" and far from 1'40". A second movement of 68.4–226.2 s would be 158 s, close to 2'40". The first is ambiguous: 24.3 to 55.0 s is 30.7 s, 24.3 to the faint tick at 57.7 s is 33.4 s. So the film leans to the 33" / 2'40" / 1'20" set, but the assignment is mine and the clicks (lid, stopwatch or page turn) are not identified. My own sheets cover 130–190 and 300–348 s; the rest is from the click list.

**Fontana Mix.** Score image: a 5:1 rectangle of grid, about 100 × 20 cells by proportion, lies tilted about 20° across sheets of curved lines, with a straight line crossing it at about 8° the other way. About 60 round black points are scattered over and beyond it, roughly half outside the grid. The curves come in three weights and in solid and dotted forms, loop freely and cross. The rectangular sheets show as overlapping grey tones, each turned a few degrees differently. Sources disagree on the parts list: 10 sheets + 12 transparencies (indeterminacy.ac.uk), 10 + 11 (Peters), 20 in all with one straight-line sheet (sin80). Agreed: six kinds of curve per sheet, ten point sheets with 7, 12, 13, 17, 18, 19, 22, 26, 29 and 30 points, one straight line, one graph. Measured length along the graph is time; height where a curve meets the line gives a value (source class, volume, tape length). Six source classes: city, country, electronic, manual, wind-produced, small amplified sounds (sin80). Recording: 655 onsets in 240 s (2.7/s), median gap 0.174 s; no steady pulse (candidates 0.511, 0.766 and 1.045 s at 0.41–0.24, which come from fast-cut passages; agy listening also reports none). Density is 0.2–5.0 onsets/s per 5 s block but flat at a longer scale: 20 s blocks stay within −23 to −33 dB for the whole 694 s, the flattest long form of the set. The two channels are independent: envelope correlation is 0.42 (median of 20 s blocks), against 0.92–0.99 in every other file. On the sheet at 60–80 s the left has one 0.15 s burst at 63.4 s, a continuous block at 65.4–68.3 s cut dead at its edge (a vertical wall in the spectrogram at 68.29 s), silence to 70.2 s, then a chain of bursts to 75.2 s. The right in the same time carries low thumps, a burst at 73.45 s, another at 78.5 s. Several sounds end as straight vertical edges, full level to floor with no decay. Gaps ≥ 1 s: 11 in 240 s, 17 s in all, none over 2.7 s. agy listening: spliced voices, choir and organ fragments, sine tones, squeaks, 1–2 layers at a time.

**Indeterminacy.** Cage's rule: each story takes one minute, so a short story is read slowly and a long one fast (Gramophone). Tudor, in another room, plays from his second realization of the Solo for Piano: ninety minutes, one page a minute, sparse attacks re-spread by chance (Getty). The voice track keeps the clock: agy listening places the end-of-block silences at 0:52–1:00, 1:54–2:00, 2:52–3:00, 3:56–4:00 of the window that starts at 120 s, and the sheet shows the last words of the 300 s story at 354.7 s with a last burst at 356.1–357.2 s (voice or noise, unclear) and silence to 360. Speech density per minute is uneven. Counted speech/noise bursts per minute, 0–12 min: 41, 70, 72, 84, 91, 50, 77, 40, 77, 77, 79, 59. Active share 0.35–0.88. In the 300–360 s story the phrases are separated by rests of 1.0–3.0 s (e.g. 318.9–320.2, 324.9–326.0, 350.4–352.3). In the 480–540 s minute the same voice runs almost without rests: after the opening, the longest pauses are about 0.5–1.0 s, and syllable strokes are packed at several a second. That is the rule made visible: one frame, two speeds. The second layer arrives without regard to words: a 0.25 s crash at 300.4 s before the first word, a high squeal in 310.8–313.9 s inside a sentence, a tone burst at 335.6–338.5 s, a crash at 342.35 s on a word ending, a hiss at 343–345 s. Over 120–360 s, silences ≥ 1 s: 16, total 24 s (10%). Energy is high (880–3520 Hz, 56%) because noise events are bright; the voice occupies 110–880 Hz.

**Variations II.** Eleven transparent pieces, five with one point and six with one line. They are dropped; from each point a perpendicular is measured to each line, giving frequency, duration, timbre, amplitude, time of occurrence and structure (Pritchett), so 5 × 6 = 30 numbers per throw. Tudor replaced scales by two values, simple/complex, made fifty sets of readings, and set the time of each event on a 60 s stopwatch field. His first notes give a twenty-minute total; the recording lasts 21:19. Measured: 141 onsets in the first 240 s (0.6/s), active time 53%. First attack at 2.0 s over 1–7 kHz and a low tone, then a 6.3 s swell of 100–220 Hz ending dead at 8.35 s, one at 18.55 s, and a third swell growing to 30 s (−33 dB) that dies to −72 dB by 42 s: almost nothing above the floor remains from 42 to 50 s. Then short brush-like broadband strokes at 50.0–56.8 s and a dense block from 57.0 s. Gaps ≥ 1 s in the first 240 s: 16, total 105 s; the longest are 25.7 s (208.8), 14.4 s (171.8) and 13.4 s (126.9). 20 s blocks swing between −24 and −63 dB across the piece: loud plateaus at 260–340 s, 460–500 s, 540–560 s, 880–960 s, 1140–1180 s and dead patches at 160, 200–240, 340–360, 380–420, 500, 820 s. Energy 30–220 Hz is 60%, with a second hump at 1.76–3.52 kHz (13%). Tudor held the damper pedal down throughout and mixed three kinds of pick-up, so every strike feeds a long ring (Pritchett).

**Ryoanji.** Fifteen stones (the garden's count) are drawn round on paper made of two rectangular systems, two sheets per solo. Where lines overlap, the parts go to separate tape systems, up to four. The percussion is one unchanging compound sound in unison, five strokes set by chance in metres of 12–15 beats. Cage wanted rhythm that the mind could not analyze (PAJ 93). The accompaniment starts almost a minute early and ends late; silences between solos are of undetermined length. The preface says two pages make one garden of about two minutes and that the score is a "still" photograph of mobile parts. *Score page (double bass, PAJ 62, image 1501 × 1193):* two systems, each a box 1391 × 347 px (4:1), split by one middle line; eight pitch names up the left edge, a to e, a perfect fifth in semitone steps of about 50 px. Time is horizontal, no beat marks, no bar lines. In the first system the first 26% is empty; in the second a 730 px gap (52% of the width, about 16 s at 30 s a system) is empty. Four line types: solid, dotted, dashed, dash-dot. Every curve has a pitch name with an arrow at its ends and a Roman numeral naming its sound system. Ink covers 2.9% (voice page 3.3%). The pencil drawing (2R)/9 has under 0.3% dark pixels, gathered in the right half of the sheet. *Recording (600–890 s):* strokes with a steep edge at 602.7, 612.1, 616.8, 620.0, 625.7, 637.75, 640.9, 650.0, 659.1, 670.7, 682.6, 694.7, 703.8, 712.8, 723.7, 726.3, 732.2, 744.1, 756.3, 759.5, 768.2, 777.3, 786.3, 798.3 s and on: 35 in 283 s. Fitted to a 3.00 s beat (error 0.07 and 0.04 of a beat), the gaps in beats are 4 1 3 3 4 4 4 3 3 4, then 1 2 4 4 1 3 3 3 4 1 3 3 2 4 4 2 4 2 2 2. As strokes: `x...xx..x..x...x...x...x..x..x...x` (625.7–723.7 s) and `xx.x...x...xx..x..x..x...xx..x..x.x...x...x.x...x.x.x.x`. No gap exceeds 4 beats and the mean is 2.9 beats: 5 per 14.5, inside the 12–15 rule. Each stroke is identical in shape: energy at 110–330 Hz and at 880–1200 Hz (the published wood-and-metal pair), gone in 1–1.5 s. Over it the solos draw gliding lines: 600–604 s a line rises from 350 to 450 Hz; one creeps up from about 320 Hz at 600 s to about 440 Hz at 605 s and then sinks slowly to 614 s; another climbs from about 950 to 1600 Hz over 681–690 s; a second, at 880–1000 Hz, enters under it at 690.9 s. Two lines sound close together from 622.6 to 628 s. (Pitches read off a log axis, good to a tone or so.) Energy 880–1760 Hz is 53% (solo), 220–440 Hz 26%. Silences ≥ 1 s in the window: 19, total 56 s (19%), longest 10.9 s: from about 856 s the solos stop and only strokes remain, 4–6 beats apart, as the published note says (silences between solos while the accompaniment continues). Whole file by minute: −25 to −42 dB.

**Two.** Ten brackets per part, nine flexible, the eighth fixed (Pritchett). The first piano bracket printed is start 0'00"–0'45", end 0'30"–1'15". The right hand has four chords (f, then mf at the end), the left hand three (f, mf, unmarked), and each hand keeps its own order but the two hands may overlap freely. No sound repeats inside a bracket. Measured (0–60 s): piano attacks at 1.61 (one tone, about 420 Hz), 6.9, 11.3 (soft), 23.1, 30.75, 36.55 s, then nothing until 57.8 s. That is within the bracket (0–75 s) but its end cannot be named exactly. A quiet line near 300 Hz (read as the flute) holds at about 14–30 s and starts again at 52.1 s, 5.7 s before the next chord: it overlaps but does not align. 25 stronger attacks in 290 s, gaps 3.8–38.9 s, median near 7.5 s. Silences ≥ 1 s: 37, total 93 s (32%). Tones ring 2–6 s and decay to the floor (−63 to −70 dB). No pulse (best strength 0.012). Near the end: 18 s of only room noise (507–526 s), a soft line at 526.6 s, chords at 533.1, 538.55, 543.95, 548.3, 558.0 s.

**Solo for Piano (score pages).** 84 graphs on 63 sheets of 11 × 17 in (Getty). The instruction pages are columns of hand-lettered capitals, 4 to a page, one large initial (A, B … CE) per paragraph, paragraphs of 2–10 lines. Graph pages hold two or three unlike drawings (a harp-shaped curve with ledger lines, a chain of arrows, boxed staves) at different scales, with 60% of page 5 empty. One printed graph (J) is arrow-ended polygons pointing both ways; another (AY) is small rectangles on a grid. Tudor's readings sit on ruled manuscript cards.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Duration before sound.** In the early works length is decided before any pitch: First Construction's 16 × 16 bars; Sonatas' bars × ratio (V: 9 × 2, 2, 2½, 2½); In a Landscape's 15 × 15; Music of Changes' 29⅝ units. Later it is clock time: 4'33", the 60 s story, the 60 s stopwatch field of Variations II, the 45/30/75 s windows of Two.
- **The same ratio at two scales.** First Construction's phrases (7.6, 5.6, 4.05, 5.6 s = 4-3-2-3 bars) are the 16 units in small; In a Landscape is 15 × 15 grouped 5-7-3; Music of Changes keeps 3, 5, 6¾, 6¾, 5, 3⅛ throughout.
- **Hard joints.** A change of section is a change of sound group, cut at a boundary: 9.7, 20.05, 33.8, 41.4 s (First Construction); 46.8 s (Sonata V); 230.5 and 268.5 s (In a Landscape); 68.29 s tape wall (Fontana Mix).
- **Chance sits at the choice, not at the frame.** Coin tosses pick a cell (64 per chart), a measurement picks a value (30 per Variations II throw), stone outlines pick the line; the proportion, bracket or page length does not move.
- **Silence is a cell, a window, or a rest, and its share is a setting.** Half of the Music of Changes sound cells are silent. Measured silent share (gaps ≥ 1 s): 4% Fontana Mix, 6% Music of Changes, 10% Indeterminacy, 19% Ryoanji, 32% Two, 44% Variations II. Sonata V: rests of 0.3–3.6 s inside each B. 4'33": 100% of the score.
- **Sound and silence have the same duration rule.** In Music of Changes silences are drawn from the sound charts like sounds; the 57½" string piece lets one hexagram number decide sound (odd) or silence (even) and its duration (Pritchett).
- **The player or the place finishes the piece.** Two leaves the alignment of hands and of the flute open; Variations II lets Tudor reduce six scales to two values; Indeterminacy fixes only the minute; 4'33" leaves all of it to the room.
- **Two independent layers, unrelated by beat.** Fontana Mix channels (0.42 correlation); Indeterminacy voice and noises; Two's flute against piano; Ryoanji's stroke against glissando; First Construction's 0.23 s run against the swell at 101 s. Each layer keeps its own clock and they meet only at edges.
- **One pulse, one count, held.** In a Landscape 0.41 s for 583 s (0.38–0.44 per block); Sonata V 0.16 s for 112 s; First Construction 0.23 s for most of 480 s. The pulse belongs to the pre-1950 works; the later works have none (Music of Changes 0.19, Two 0.01, Ryoanji's only pulse is 3 s).
- **A small set of sounds reused in many orders.** First Construction: 16 motives (Wikipedia) and stroke types; Ryoanji one compound stroke; Sonatas one prepared keyboard of 45 notes; Solo for Piano 84 graphs cut and re-sequenced. Novelty comes from order and length.
- **Loudness by plan or by lot, mostly in steps.** Sonata V jumps about 18 dB at 58.8 and 89.3 s; Music of Changes spans 48.8 dB (10th to 95th percentile) with neighbours 20 dB apart; In a Landscape stays in 18.6 dB; Two varies chord by chord. Real swells exist: First Construction's return at 225–240 s (26 dB in 15 s) and the 4–8 s swells of Variations II.
- **Where accounts and measurements differ.** Sonata II's ratio (1.58 published, 2.18 measured); Sonata V's B is 1.8 s longer than its count; 4'33" has two movement sets; the Fontana parts list has three versions; First Construction's published bars are one tempo but the recording drifts 0.22–0.35 s.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**John Cage.** *Fix the frame before the content, divided by one short series used at two scales; fill its cells by a seeded draw from a closed chart in which empty is an entry with a set share; never edit a result, only the chart; run a second layer on an unrelated interval; give windows, not points; join by cuts.*
Process:
1. Fix the frame before any content. A frame is a whole count of equal units (15 × 15 or 16 × 16), or one fixed window of space or time. It never grows or shrinks to suit what goes in it: content is fitted to the frame, set loose when short and tight when long, so that it occupies 35–88% of its frame, and the last 7–13% of every frame stays empty as its close. If the frame changes, every later step is redone.
2. Divide the frame by one short series of 3–5 whole numbers that add up to the count (4-3-2-3-4 for 16; 5-7-3 for 15), and use the same series twice: to group the units into sections, and to divide each unit into its parts. Two scales, one series, no third.
3. Write the chart before filling anything: a closed table of at most 8 × 8 entries per property (kind, span, level). The stock of kinds is small (1–16) and nothing joins it later. Empty is an entry in the chart, and its share is a setting chosen now and stated: 5–10% for a full view, 20–30% for a sparse one, up to 45%. Spans come from the same table for empty and filled cells alike. A scale that cannot be judged finely is cut to two values.
4. Fill by draw, in cell order, from a seed (a person, a date, a place), so the same seed always gives the same view. Each cell takes kind, span and level from the chart. Bound the draw with counts, not with taste: about 5 marks in every 12–15 units, no gap over 4 units, mean gap near 3.
5. Never edit a result. If a view is unacceptable, change the chart or a bound and draw again. Do not sort, grade or centre the outcome afterwards: levels fall in steps, neighbours may differ by the whole range, and there is no build toward a peak.
6. Add at most one second layer, on an interval of its own that is not a multiple of the first; the two share only the frame's outer edges. Make the second layer one unchanging mark or one continuous kind of line, 2–4% of the first layer's rate, and let it begin before the first layer and end after it. Neither layer is snapped to the other.
7. Give a window where a point is not needed. A start and an end are each a range covering about 60% of the span and overlapping by about 20% (start anywhere in 0–45, end anywhere in 30–75), and whoever receives the view (the reader, the device, the data) settles the exact value. About one item in ten may be pinned exactly.
8. Join by a cut at a frame boundary. A new section is a change of the whole group of kinds at once, edge to edge, with no blend; inside a section the group does not change.
9. State one conversion and keep to it ("1 unit = …"), printed on the view, so every length can be read as a quantity. Each kind of mark carries one property and no other: line pattern names the layer, distance from a datum gives the value, a label at each end of a line fixes it. Where nothing was drawn, nothing is put.
Empty mode (in place of steps 3–5): keep the frame, its divisions and the conversion, and put nothing in. What the receiver brings is the content, and the frame's edges are the only marks.
Why it works:
- Scale reference: when the frame holds still, how full it is becomes a quantity the eye can compare from cell to cell. Let the frame grow with its content and every cell looks equally full; the amount has gone into the layout and cannot be read off it.
- Figure and ground: an empty cell with the same edges and the same span rule as a full one is read as a member of the set. Take away the frame or give blanks their own rule, and the same blank reads as a fault or as something still loading.
- Grouping by texture: an unsorted draw from a small closed stock makes one even texture without a figure, so the eye stops ranking and reads any cell on its own terms. The bound on gaps is what keeps it even: without it chance makes clumps, and a clump is read as a figure nobody intended. Sort or grade the outcome and it becomes a ramp that promises a climax.
- Adaptation: with no regular recurrence there is no expected next state, so no item is odd and none is salient by accident. Put the same draw on a steady beat and every departure from the beat turns into an accent.
- Grouping by common change: two layers that never move or step together separate without tint or depth. Align their intervals and they fuse into one pattern whose coincidences read as meaning.
- One series at two scales lets a part stand for the whole: someone who has read one unit can place themselves in the frame without a map. Hard joins keep that structure legible, since the frame is the only regular thing on the view.
Colour relations: two values and nothing else: a ground of 87–97% and marks of 0.3–3.3% (up to 7–8% where the view is running text). Kinds are told apart by line pattern and position, never by hue. One saturated tint at most, under about 2.5% of the view, and it belongs to the receiver's own marks (annotations, selections), never to the drawn content. Level is one of a few stepped values taken from the chart; colour does not mark what matters, because nothing is ranked.
Space and line: frame 15 × 15 or 16 × 16 units; series of 3–5 terms at two scales; chart at most 8 × 8; empty share 5–45% by setting; content 35–88% of its frame; closing margin 7–13% of the frame; about 5 marks per 12–15 units, no gap over 4, mean near 3; blank runs of 26–52% of a row are normal, and a view may be 60% empty with its block set low (first line near 57% of the height); one line weight per kind and up to four kinds by pattern (solid, dotted, dashed, dash-dot); the measuring grid is ruled and regular, and what lies on it is free.
Type requirement: text is fitted to a fixed frame, so the face must stay legible across a range of setting widths, from loose to tight, at one size. Figures of equal width, with a clean sign for a range, since quantities are written as windows ("0–45 ↔ 30–75") and as one conversion line. Blocks of 2–10 lines, each keyed by a letter or number larger than the text, in up to four columns. One weight; no emphasis style, since nothing is ranked.
Motion and interaction: nothing has to move, because the draw is in the layout. A seed holds for a stated period (a session, a day), so the view never rearranges under the pointer; at the period's end the new draw lands as one cut of 100ms or less, at a frame boundary. An update without urgency may land anywhere inside its window and need not be on a beat. If the second layer is shown in time, it is one identical mark lasting 1–1.5s, at gaps of 1–4 beats of 3s, never regular and never faster. Controls, navigation and anything a user must find again are outside the draw and keep fixed places. Reduced motion: the second layer is drawn as still marks at its positions and every change is instant; nothing is lost, because no step depended on movement.
Fails when: one item must win the first glance, or the items have a rank or an order that matters (search results, chronology, steps); there are fewer than about 12 cells, so a draw reads as a mistake and an empty share cannot be seen; content cannot be fitted to a fixed frame (legal text, forms, long tables); a blank would be taken for a failure and cannot be explained on the view; an update is urgent, since a window delays it; the same thing has to be in the same place for every person.
Signature (do not carry together): nearly blank cream pages with a few ruled lines and traced curves · transparent sheets of points and loops dropped over a tilted grid · a titled, timed stretch of nothing presented as the work.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

"On the shelf today" for a neighbourhood tool-lending library, desktop 1280 × 800, deep teal ground, pale marks, the brand's yellow, a slab serif. No sound, no score, no cream paper.
1. *Frame.* A wall of 16 columns of 72px (1152px between 64px margins) and 640px high under a 96px header, with a 64px strip below. The wall is the same size whether 12 tools are in or 200.
2. *Series.* 4-3-2-3-4 twice: the columns form five bays of 288, 216, 144, 216 and 288px, and each column's 16 units of 40px form five cells of 160, 120, 80, 120 and 160px. 80 cells.
3. *Chart.* One table of 64 entries. Kinds: tool cut-out, name only, a borrower's note, a count, a category mark, and empty. 19 of the 64 entries are empty (30%, stated in the header as "about a third of the wall is left open"), so about 24 of the 80 cells. Span is 1 or 2 cells for empty and filled alike. Level has two values, pale and paler.
4. *Draw.* Seeded by the date, in cell order. Bound: no more than 4 open cells in a row along any line.
5. *No editing.* The staff cannot pin a favourite into a cell. If a day's wall shows too many notes, the chart gets fewer note entries and the day is drawn again. Nothing is sorted by popularity.
6. *Second layer.* In the strip, one identical 8 × 8px mark per tool due back, placed by date on a 14-day scale of 82px a day. It shares only its two ends with the 72px columns, and it runs past both edges of the wall into the margins.
7. *Windows.* A loan is written as two ranges, "collect Mon–Wed ↔ return Wed–Fri": each is three of the five days and they share one. The borrower settles the days.
8. *Cut.* The five bays alternate two ground values with a hard edge. Each bay draws from one category group only.
9. *Conversion.* Printed once under the strip: "full width = 14 days". Dashed outline = out on loan, solid = in.
- *Type.* Notes are fitted to their cell: a short note is set loose and a long one tight, at one size, and each stops short of the cell's last tenth.
- *Motion.* None in a session. At midnight the next draw replaces the wall in one step. Search and the bay labels sit in the header and never move.
- *Reduced motion.* Identical.

Result: a teal wall of tools with open cells, different each day and the same all day. What survived is the frame fixed before the content, one series at two scales, a stated share of empty cells drawn by the same rule as the full ones, a second layer on an interval of its own, windows in place of points, and cuts. Nobody would caption it with a composer's name, or take it for a picture of music.

## UI translations
1. **Layout:** Decide the frame before the content: a fixed count of equal units (First Construction 16 × 16, In a Landscape 15 × 15) or a fixed window. The frame does not resize to what it holds.
2. **Layout:** Divide the whole and each of its units by the same short series (4-3-2-3-4, or 5-7-3). Do not introduce a second series for the detail.
3. **Density:** Fit content to its frame instead of the frame to its content: loose when short, tight when long, between about 35% and 88% full (Indeterminacy's active share per minute), with the last 7–13% of the frame left open.
4. **Emptiness:** Make empty a value with a share that is set and stated, 5–45% of the cells (measured gap shares run 4% to 44% across the works), and give empty cells the same sizes as full ones.
5. **Empty state:** Keep the frame, its divisions and its scale line, and put nothing in (4'33": three timed parts, a blank page with ruled lines). Do not fill the space with illustration.
6. **Generative variation:** Fill repeated cells by a seeded draw from a closed chart of at most 8 × 8 entries (Music of Changes) and bound it with counts: about 5 marks per 12–15 units, no gap over 4 (Ryoanji). Fix the seed for a stated period.
7. **Generative variation:** When a draw looks wrong, change the chart or a bound and draw again; never move one cell by hand, and never sort the outcome into a ramp.
8. **Layers:** Run a second layer on an interval unrelated to the first and let the two meet only at the frame's outer edges (Fontana Mix channels correlate at 0.42 where every other file is above 0.9). Tell the layers apart by line pattern, not by hue.
9. **State change:** Give non-urgent events a window for their start and a window for their end, each about 60% of the span and overlapping by 20% (Two: 0'00"–0'45" and 0'30"–1'15"), and write them as ranges.
10. **Sections:** Change section by a cut that replaces the whole group of kinds at a frame boundary (First Construction's joins at 9.7, 20.05 and 33.8 s). No crossfade.
11. **Labels:** Print one conversion on the view and keep to it ("1 page = 7 inches = 56 seconds"). Each kind of mark carries one property; a line is labelled at both ends.
12. **Colour:** Two values, with marks at 0.3–3.3% of the view (the Ryoanji drawing and score pages). At most one saturated tint under 2.5%, kept for the user's own marks.
13. **Accessibility:** Controls and navigation stay out of the draw and keep fixed places. An empty cell is announced as empty by intent, not as missing. Under reduced motion the second layer is still and every change is instant; nothing loops unprompted.

## What this composer would reject
- A layer that depends on another for its timing: in the measured works voices, channels and parts keep their own clocks.
- A single built climax: Music of Changes, Fontana Mix, Ryoanji and Two show no direction in their block levels; joints are mostly cuts.
- Silence treated as a leftover: it is a cell, a bracket or a frame.
- Repetition whose only purpose is to make a hook: repeats are whole sections of equal length, counted.
- A performer's or designer's taste choosing each event.
- Decoration of the score: marks carry a parameter (line kind = part, distance = value), and the page is left blank where nothing is needed.
- Fixing every parameter when a range is enough (Two's brackets, Variations II's "simple/complex").

## Signature prompt seed
Design the screen as a frame fixed before its content: a whole count of equal units divided by one short series (such as 4-3-2-3-4) that is used again inside each unit. Fill the cells by a seeded draw from a small closed chart in which "empty" is an entry with a stated share (5–45%), sized like any other; bound the draw by counts (no gap over 4 units) and never adjust a cell by hand. Fit content to its cell, loose or tight, and leave the cell's last tenth open. Two values only, marks at a few percent of the view, kinds told apart by line pattern. Add one second layer on an unrelated interval that meets the first only at the outer edges. Write starts and ends as overlapping ranges, print one scale line, and join sections by hard cuts. Nothing ranks, builds or animates unprompted; controls keep fixed places outside the draw.
