# Steve Reich — Process Music

## Essence
Reich takes one short pattern (8 attacks in 12 beats, a 0.80 s loop of speech, an 8-beat bar) and lets one rule act on it slowly and in the open: two copies slide apart, beats replace rests, a chord is stretched over a section. Pulse, pitch set and loudness stay almost fixed, so the only thing that moves is the relation between identical layers. The piece ends when the rule has run its course, usually back at unison.

Nobody listened to these recordings for this file. Everything below comes from onset and autocorrelation measurement, spectrogram sheets, score images and published texts. Claims about instruments or character name their source. No usable "agy listening" file exists (all were quota errors or interrupted).

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Two identical layers, one clock.** It's Gonna Rain, Come Out, Piano Phase, Clapping Music, Drumming, Electric Counterpoint: the same material sounds twice or more and only the delay differs. Schwarz quotes Reich's reason: the process is clear only when the voices match in timbre.
- **Change too small to see per repeat, plain over a minute.** It's Gonna Rain about 1.6 ms per 0.80 s repeat (2 ms per second); Come Out about 2.2 ms per second; Piano Phase one sixteenth (0.128 s) per 42 s; Drumming one hit per 12-14 s.
- **Plateau, then transition, then plateau.** Piano Phase: 13 holds of 20-58 s against slides of 2-14 s (about 80:20). Drumming: a full pattern alone for 37 s before it moves. Clapping Music and Electric Counterpoint: holds with zero-length transitions.
- **Continuous or stepped, chosen per piece.** Tape and piano slide; Clapping Music steps (one bar of shift, no blur); Different Trains and the late keys of Electric Counterpoint cut.
- **The cycle closes.** Drumming Part One thins back to few hits at 640 s. It's Gonna Rain Part I returns to 0 ms at 440 s, Clapping Music to its first bar after 12 shifts, Piano Phase section 1 to unison at 510 s, 18 Musicians to chord I at 229 s. The system defines the end; Pendulum Music ends when all tones are continuous, then power off.
- **Constant outer frame.** Loudness range within 2-4 dB for minutes (Piano Phase, It's Gonna Rain, Six Pianos), same pitch set for 290 s (Six Pianos, novelty under 0.001), same 5 to 11 pitch classes; spectral centre flat (Piano Phase 875-905 Hz).
- **Fill the gaps, not the volume.** Pendulum: floor rises 34 dB, peak 5. Come Out: range falls 13.6 → 5.1 dB. Clapping: 8 to 12 slots. Drumming: 1 to 8 of 12.
- **Speed as a by-product.** Drumming's bar shortens from 1.28 to 1.05 s while it fills, and the same in the second recording, with no tempo marking for it.
- **Lengths in proportion.** Piano Phase 12 : 8 : 4 pattern notes take 49 : 33 : 17% of the time (a performer's choice of repeats, yet almost exactly proportional); Electric Counterpoint key alternation in 8, 4 and 3 cycles; 18 Musicians two breaths per chord across eleven chords.
- **Two kinds of time layered.** 18 Musicians: a fixed pulse (0.138 s, constant) against breath swells (9.1 s). Pendulum: a fixed pass (1.64 s) against a growing sound time. Different Trains: motor figure against speech interval (3.9 s).
- **New voice enters under full texture.** Electric Counterpoint bass at 43 s, 18 Musicians low band at 25 s, Six Pianos beats at 17.6 s: entry is one register or one channel at a time, and the rest does not stop.
- **The generative pattern seen:** pick one short cell; run one rule on it at a pace slower than noticing; hold each result long enough to be learned (12 bars, 20-58 s, 12 s); keep pitch, pulse and level fixed; let the overlap create the new lines; stop when the rule returns to its start.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Steve Reich.** *Set one short cell on a grid of equal slots, lay an identical copy beside it, and let one stated rule change one slot per step (shift, fill or spread), holding each state long enough to learn; lock emphasis and value set, raise density by filling gaps, and end where the rule returns to its start.*
Process:
1. Fix the cell and its grid. One short pattern on 8 or 12 equal slots, about two thirds of them filled (8 of 12), the filled slots in groups of unequal length, each closed by one empty slot. Every module is the same width whether it is empty or full. Nothing outside the cell is ever added; if the cell changes, every later step is redone.
2. Lock the frame. The value set (5–11 members), the slot size and the emphasis are fixed for the whole view or session: no size, weight or contrast step is added while the rule runs. Whatever differs later can then only be the rule.
3. Duplicate the layer. A second copy, identical in every property, sits so close to the first that the two read as one object (the gap inside the pair about 1/7 of the gap between pairs). One copy never changes: state it once and abbreviate it afterwards to a repeat mark. Only the other copy is written out.
4. Choose ONE rule in ONE mode and print it as one sentence on the view. Shift: the moving copy is displaced one slot per step, so a cell of n slots gives n steps and the state after the last equals the first. Fill: one empty slot is filled per step, from 1 filled up to the cell's full count, in an order that is not left to right. Spread: the period stays fixed while the share of it that is "on" grows from about 20% to 100%. Stepped or continuous is decided here, once.
5. Set the pace by the hold. One step changes one slot and nothing else. Each state is held for at least 12 cell-lengths (11–35 measured), and a finished build about three times as long before anything else moves. Holds take about 80% of the time and transitions 20%, or the transition is a cut. The still form is the default: every state drawn as its own equal module, in order, three to a row.
6. Raise density by filling, never by emphasis. When more is happening, empty slots close (8 → 10–12 of 12 once two copies overlap; an on-share of 21% → 93%) while the strongest mark gains at most about a seventh of what the emptiest part gains. Keep the gaps wherever items must be read one by one; close them where the items should read as one tone.
7. Add at most one second clock and one reserved band. The slow clock runs about 66 times the fast unit, two cycles per section, and moves one secondary attribute about three times as far as the whole moves. One band stays empty for the first 10–17% of the sequence; one new layer enters there alone, while everything else carries on, and that entry is the only structural marker. Sections take time or space in proportion to the length of their cell (12 : 8 : 4 slots → about 49 : 33 : 17%).
8. End from the rule. The sequence stops at the state it started from (or when the fill or spread is complete), with a cut and no fade, and does not start again by itself. In the still form the last row is shorter than the others (about 0.35 of a row) and that is the end mark.
Why it works:
- Grouping by likeness: two layers that match in every property fuse into one object, so the only thing left to read is the offset between them, and the lines that neither copy contains by itself appear in the overlap. Give the copies different colours and they become figure and ground; the offset then reads as two separate things, not as one thing changing.
- Adaptation to the previous state: a hold many cells long makes the state the baseline, so a change of one slot is found by oddness with no added emphasis. Shorten the hold and the steps blur into continuous movement; change two slots at once and the rule can no longer be told from noise; raise the emphasis at the change and the eye reads an alarm, not a step.
- Fusion of fine detail into tone: separate items need the empty slot between them to stay items. As the gaps fill, the range between strongest and weakest collapses (to about a third of what it was) and the row is read as one texture. Density can therefore carry "more" while the frame stays quiet. Raise the level instead and the frame is no longer constant, so nothing is left to measure the rule against.
- Closure: a rule that returns to its start lets the viewer predict the end and confirms that the rule was understood. In a shift the states mirror around the middle step, so the second half is recognised, not learned. A looped or faded ending removes that proof.
- Two clocks far apart (about 66 : 1) are read as different kinds of thing and do not compete; brought near each other they beat against one another and both are lost. The entry into the band kept empty is found by oddness of place, so it needs no emphasis of its own.
Colour relations: one mark value on one ground, with the ground at about 84% of the view and the marks at 6–15%; the two process layers share one colour, with no second hue between them; texture, where there is any, stays in near-neutrals whose chroma is a small fraction of any accent's; the darkest value is the smallest share (about 1.5%) and belongs to labels. The process has no accent of its own: a case colour can only mark the single entry in the reserved band. A change of colour is a cross-fade on the same slots, after the rule has finished.
Space and line: 8 or 12 equal slots per module, about two thirds filled; modules equal in width, three to a row; gap inside a pair to gap between rows 1 : 7; the fixed layer stated once, then a repeat mark; the rule as one line above the field; a shorter last row (0.35) as the end; one band empty for the first 10–17%; section extents proportional to cell length; no outline, no hero, no centre.
Type requirement: text is set by slot, so figures and labels are of equal width and a module keeps its width at any fill. One size and one weight inside the field. Modules are numbered. The rule is one plain sentence with its count ("each state 12 times"). Longer instructions are prose in as many paragraphs as there are phases, separated by a gap of about two lines.
Motion and interaction: the still form carries the whole process, so nothing has to move. Where time is used, steps are bound to the user's own events (a save, a new day, a page), one slot per event, as a cut or as a slide lasting at most a fifth of the shortest hold (200–400ms for an event step). A spread indicator keeps one period of 1.6s or longer and widens its on-share from 20% to 100% as work completes, with no change of speed and almost none of brightness. A slow swell of about 9s may move one secondary attribute by a small amount, two per section. Urgency comes from halving the interval between two alternating states (8, 4, then 3 cells) while the unit stays the same. No mark passes from light to dark more than 3 times a second. Reduced motion: every step is a cut, the swell is off, and the spread indicator becomes a still row with n of 12 slots filled.
Fails when: the content has no repeating cell (unique items, long prose, a single figure); a change must be noticed at once (errors, alarms), since a rule slower than noticing hides it; the layers must be told apart by category, so they cannot be identical; fewer than about 8 slots fit; the visit is shorter than a few holds and the states cannot be laid out side by side; the brief needs a hero or a climax.
Signature (do not carry together): two identical tracks drifting out of step between left and right · a twelve-slot row of hits and rests rotated one place at a time · a fast even pulse under slow swells of about two breaths.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Crew rota for a ferry operator, desktop 1200 × 800, deep navy ground, pale sand marks, one brand coral, a humanist serif (the works are sound, in ink on paper where they are written down at all).
1. *Cell and grid.* One week is 12 shift slots (6 days × 2). A crew works 8 of them in groups of 4, 1 and 3, each closed by a slot off. A slot is 30px wide, a mark 26 × 20px; a module is 360px of slots in a 368px column whether it is full or empty.
2. *Frame.* Two values only for the field (ground and mark), one type size, one mark size. Nothing is bolded, enlarged or tinted when a week changes.
3. *Duplicate.* Crew A and crew B are two lines of identical marks 6px apart; pairs are 42px apart (1 : 7). Crew A's line is drawn in week 1 and shown as a repeat mark from week 2; crew B's line is drawn in every week.
4. *Rule.* Shift, stepped. One line above the field: "Crew B starts one shift earlier each week; after 12 weeks both crews are back in step."
5. *Pace.* One slot per week. All 13 weeks are on the screen as 13 numbered modules, three to a row with 24px gutters: four rows of three and a last row of one.
6. *Density.* Beside each module, the count of slots covered by either crew: 8, 11, 11, 12, 11, 10, 10, 10, 11, 12, 11, 11, 8. The uncovered slots are simply empty; they are not outlined or coloured, and the eye finds them because the rest has closed up.
7. *Second clock and reserved band.* No slow clock is needed. A strip along the bottom, one slot per week, is empty for weeks 1–2 (15%); from week 3 it holds the relief crew, in the brand coral, 0.6% of the screen. Nothing else changes when it enters.
8. *End.* Week 13 equals week 1 and sits alone on the fifth row (about a third of its width). The plan stops there; a new cycle is a new screen.
- *Motion.* On Monday the "this week" label moves to the next module as a cut. Stepping through the weeks with the arrow keys moves one module per press.
- *Type.* Tabular figures and day initials in slots of equal width; week numbers 1–13 at the left of each module.
- *Reduced motion.* Identical: the layout is the process.

Result: a dark, serifed rota in which two crews' weeks slide past each other and meet again. The marks cover about 11% of the screen. Nothing pulses, loops or sounds, and there are no notes, waveforms or rings. What survived is the identical pair, one slot per step, every state held and shown, density from covered slots, and an end that is the beginning.

## Space & composition
Texture is layers of the same or near-identical material, usually with no figure over ground. Register is narrow and mid: Piano Phase lives from E4 to D5 plus a few higher partials; the bongos at two pitch regions; Clapping without pitch; tape within about 100-3500 Hz. The low band is withheld and then admitted once (18 Musicians from 25 s, Electric Counterpoint from 43 s and out at about 212 s), so bass entries work as structural markers. Stereo is used as a layer separator: the two loops sit in left and right in It's Gonna Rain and Come Out; the guitar layers in Electric Counterpoint arrive left first, right second; in 18 Musicians the right channel alone shows the pulses plainly. Density per second: Clapping 4.2 rising to 5.6, Drumming 0.8 to 7.4, Piano Phase 6.0-8.2 and flat, Different Trains 1-5, Pendulum about 0.2 to 5. The frame: start abruptly at full texture (Six Pianos, Music for 18 fades in over about 20 s, Drumming from one hit), stop without ritardando: Pendulum's cut, Clapping on unison, Six Pianos. Silence is rare and placed at joints: the 1.5-2 s after each statement in Come Out, the 13 s between It's Gonna Rain's parts, the black between hits in the first 20 s of Drumming.

## Color
Timbre is one colour per piece, multiplied. Two pianos, two pairs of hands, one voice, one bongo type: identity of tone lets the overlap read as one object (Schwarz, quoting Reich). When colour changes it changes by fading one instrument in over another playing the same pitch and rhythm (Drumming, Reich's note, "gradual changing of timbre"). Roles in Music for 18 (composer's note): pianos and mallets carry the constant pulse; voices and winds carry the breath; a vibraphone cues the change of section; nothing is sung as text. Different Trains: speech and train recordings are source colour, strings copy. No source colour here is measured beyond the spectrum: centroid by piece, Piano Phase ≈ 890 Hz, tape ≈ 1000-1500 Hz, Clapping ≈ 3000 Hz, Pendulum falls from about 2000 Hz (set-up noise) to about 450 Hz as the tone takes over.

## Form, line & typography
- **Equal modules.** Clapping Music: 13 bars, three to a row, all the same width; the last row is shorter and that is visible as the end. Drumming: eight equal staves in one column.
- **One line fixed, one line moving.** Upper line never changes in Clapping (13 of 13 bars); the moving line shifts one slot. Six Pianos: fixed parts shown once and then as repeat signs, moving parts written out.
- **Rule as one sentence.** "Repeat each bar 12 times" for Clapping; numbers of repeats and a note on how to move in Drumming ("at least 6 or 8 repeats"), ranges of repeats in Piano Phase (Horlacher), no notes at all in Pendulum Music.
- **Repeat marks as structure.** Double-bar repeat signs sit at both ends of every bar (Clapping), slash repeat signs for static parts (Six Pianos), dotted lines between bars for the phase slide in Piano Phase (reported in a search excerpt only; not seen).
- **Prose as form.** Pendulum Music sheet: big thin capitals, small capitals, handwritten text; four paragraphs, four stages; signature and date at lower right.
- **Diagram forms seen:** a drum of two rims side by side, each cell numbered 1-2-3 within its clap group, the front rim turned by one cell (Commons visualisation of Clapping Music; a contributor's diagram, not Reich's); a ring of chained loops crossed by four diameters with numbered arrows, titled final structure and order (Commons, Violin Phase; a contributor's diagram, apparently of the dance, not Reich's).

- Transition styles measured: zero length (Clapping, 18 Musicians chords, Electric Counterpoint keys, Different Trains tempi); short blur of 2-14 s (Piano Phase); slow drift of hundreds of seconds (tape works, Pendulum).
- Holding: the length of one hold is much longer than the cell. Cells per hold: Clapping 12, Piano Phase ≈ 23 (score gives ranges), Drumming 11-13 then about 35 for the finished pattern, It's Gonna Rain continuous.
- Acceleration by structure: Electric Counterpoint's alternation interval shrinks 15 → 7.5 → 5.6 s while the pulse stays at 0.156 s; Pendulum's overlap rises while the pass does not. Pulse is never sped up as a gesture. Drumming's bar shortening, 18%, comes from players.
- Disagreements: printed Clapping 160-184 against 188 measured on the Nonesuch recording, and the first and last state about 6 bars in place of 12; Drumming's score asks at least 6 or 8 repeats, measured 11-13, so they agree; Reich's "15 to 20 seconds" per chord agrees with 17-20 s. Published "five-minute" section lengths in 18 Musicians were not measured (only minute loudness). It's Gonna Rain Part I stalls at 392-428 s before closing: the drift is not linear, so "gradual" is a machine drift, not a drawn line.
- Reich's 1968 statements of method (Synthtopia copy of the essay; wording checked only there): "I am interested in perceptible processes"; a process should happen "extremely gradually" and once set up it "runs by itself". The comparisons are the pulled-back swing, the hourglass and feet buried by waves; the watch's minute hand stands for the pace. He also accepts all results without changes, and names by-products (sub-melodies, harmonics, slight irregularities) as the content that keeps it open. Schwarz notes that Reich later disavowed much of the essay for his own newer work.

## UI translations
1. **Layout:** Draw a sequence as equal modules, three to a row, each holding two lines of the same marks; the last row holds a single module (0.35 of the row), and that short row is the end.
2. **Layout:** Set the two lines of a pair 1 : 7 as close as the rows (28px against 197px on the Clapping Music page), so a pair reads as one object and a row as one step.
3. **Layout:** Keep every module the same width whether it holds one mark or twelve; an empty slot is drawn as a slot.
4. **State:** Write the unchanging part once and replace it with a repeat mark in every later module; write out only the part that moves, so the eye finds it without colour.
5. **Rule line:** Print the rule as one sentence with its count ("repeat each state 12 times") above the field. If a process cannot be said in one line, it is not shown.
6. **Density:** Show load by filling slots (8 of 12 up to 12 of 12), not by size, weight or a louder colour; the strongest mark stays within a narrow band while the gaps close.
7. **Progress:** Build a pattern one slot per step in a scattered order (slots 8, 10, 7, 0, 1, 4, 2, 6 of 12), hold the finished pattern about three times as long as a step, and only then let anything else change.
8. **Loading:** One indicator with a fixed period of 1.6s or longer whose on-share widens from 20% to 100% as work completes; speed and brightness do not change. Under reduced motion, a still row with n of 12 slots filled.
9. **Motion:** Hold each state at least 12 cell-lengths; transitions are a cut or take at most a fifth of the hold. One step moves one slot.
10. **Motion:** Layer two clocks about 66 : 1 apart: the fixed unit, and a 9s swell on one secondary attribute, two per section, switched off under reduced motion.
11. **Entry:** Keep one band (the lowest register of the view) empty for the first 10–17% of a sequence and let one new layer enter there alone while the rest carries on.
12. **Sections:** Give each section time or space in proportion to the length of its cell: 12, 8 and 4 slots take about 49, 33 and 17%.
13. **Onboarding:** State the cell alone, unaltered, three times, each followed by an empty interval about a third of its length, before any rule starts.
14. **Urgency:** Alternate two states at an interval that shrinks from 8 cells to 4 to 3 while the unit stays fixed; never speed the unit up.
15. **Color:** One ink on one paper (`#F7F8F9` at about 84%, or cream `#FFFEE2`), no second hue between the two layers; texture greys keep a chroma of 9 or less (`#949C9E`, `#CDD3D4`), and near-black (`#161616`) is label only, about 1.5%.
16. **Type:** Put longer instructions in prose, one paragraph per phase, with gaps of about two lines; set the title in tall thin capitals about 8% of the page height, with a date and signature at the lower right.
17. **Ending:** Stop on the starting state with a cut. No fade, no automatic loop.
18. **Accessibility:** The measured pulses run at 6–8 per second; never render them as light and dark changes (limit 3 per second). Show a pulse as spacing, not as flashing.

## What this composer would reject
- Hidden structure: the 1968 essay rejects devices that cannot be heard, against serial method and against chance that leaves no trace.
- A single dramatic event: none of these pieces uses a crescendo as an argument; loudness stays in a narrow band and density does the rising.
- Ornament added on top of the process: material outside the cell does not appear (Clapping 8 hits, Piano Phase 5 pitches, 11 chords).
- Timbral contrast between process voices while phasing (Schwarz, quoting Reich on clarity); contrast arrives only once phasing is over.
- A process that runs faster than it can be followed: holds are longer than needed (12 bars, 20-58 s).
- An ending that does not come from the rule: stops at unison, last chord, or power off.

## Signature prompt seed
Design this screen as a Reich process: one cell of 12 equal slots, 8 filled in groups of 3, 2, 1 and 2, drawn twice as two identical lines of one ink on pale paper (`#F7F8F9`, about 84% of the view), the pair 1 : 7 as close as the rows. The upper line never changes and becomes a repeat mark after the first module; the lower line shifts one slot per module. Lay out 13 equal modules three to a row, the last alone on a short row, back in unison. Print the rule as one sentence above the field. Show density by filled slots, never by size or weight. No second hue, no hero, no fade at the end. Hold each state; steps are cuts; any indicator keeps a fixed period and widens its on-share; still under reduced motion.
