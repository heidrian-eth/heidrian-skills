# Ettore Sottsass — Memphis / Postmodern design

## Essence
Sottsass treated an object as a character, not a neutral container for function. He built it from cheap, loud, mass-printed surfaces and stacked primary shapes into figures that look back at you: a bookcase that resembles a robot or a many-armed goddess, a typewriter in a short red skirt. Usefulness stays, pointedly not the whole point. His line against functionalism ran roughly: *"It's not enough. Design should also be sensual and exciting."*

## Key works examined
- **Valentine portable typewriter (with Perry King, 1969)**: red injection-moulded ABS, orange spool caps, and a plastic bucket case that hangs from the machine's own handle. It teaches that an ordinary tool ("like a pencil") can still have a personality. *(visually checked: Cooper Hewitt 18621871, AIC 233227. One glossy red #F63838 (AIC, white background; the Cooper Hewitt photo reads #E96069 under grey light) covers ≈90% of the object, with two orange spool caps #FFBA6F and black keys as the only other colours. The case does hang from the machine's own handle.)*
- **Bacterio laminate (Abet, 1978)**: a field of black squiggles on a pale ground, abstracted from the surface of a temple in Madurai. Pattern as a material you cut panels from, not added decoration. *(seen only on Carlton's plinth (Cooper Hewitt, AIC): short black dashes and hooks, evenly spread and not aligned to any grid, covering ≈15% of a neutral light-grey ground #D4D1D0, not cream. The Abet sample itself was not obtained.)*
- **Carlton room divider (Memphis, 1981)**: 196 cm tall, laminated MDF on an equilateral-triangle system, with slanted shelves and two red drawers above a Bacterio base. It teaches that a figure can stand on a small solid core (seen in Carlton only, not a general Sottsass rule) and that one component can do three jobs (divider, bookcase, drawers). *(visually checked: Cooper Hewitt 18620781, AIC 103026, V&A design drawing O539978 dated 1.5.81. The silhouette is ≈1:1 (872 × 898 px). The green shelf sits at ≈51% of the height. The only closed volume is the two-drawer red box (≈4% of the silhouette); the Bacterio plinth is ≈8% of the height and ≈53% of the width, and the green shelf is ≈1.9× the plinth's width. Arms run at ≈30° (blue) and ≈55–60° (yellow, black legs, the pink triangle), every joint is braced by a small black triangle, and about twelve colours are mirrored left to right.)*
- **Tahiti lamp (1981)**: a bird with yellow neck, pink head, red beak on a black triangle and Bacterio plinth. Every part gets its own colour; joints stay visible. *(not obtained: no image at V&A, AIC, CMA or Cooper Hewitt; the Met has no open image; Commons RATE_LIMITED. Unverified.)*
- **Casablanca sideboard (Memphis, 1981; added)** *(visually checked: V&A O58652)*: a totem with arms angled up at ≈30–45°. Every surface is printed laminate (red with black speckle on the frame and arms, grey speckle on the doors, yellow speckle on the drawer) over a plain black base, so the pattern itself becomes the colour.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Ettore Sottsass (Memphis).** *Build the thing as a standing figure: separately treated parts joined in the open, mirrored about one axis, and balanced on a base narrower than its reach.*
Process:
1. Split the content into parts by job and give each part one whole-surface treatment (a flat colour or one printed texture). A treatment changes only at a joint, never inside a part. Count: 1–12.
2. Draw one vertical axis. Put everything closed (what stores, holds or acts) on it as a trunk, 4–35% of the silhouette. Nothing closed leaves the axis.
3. Branch open members from the trunk in mirrored pairs, their outlines cut at two angles from one family (between 30° and 60°). Void stays at half or more of the bounding box (≈55%); the widest member, near mid-height, spans the full width.
4. Assign the treatments from step 1: twins across the axis match; every tier up the axis changes; no two touching parts share one. Past six treatments, split them about evenly into pale and saturated, and let one pair of the same hue at far-apart values touch (the one "wrong" meeting).
5. Stand the figure on a base 8–12% of the height and 40–55% of the full span, in the surface mode the body does not use: textured (≈15% cover) under a plain body, plain and darkest under a textured one.
6. Mark every joint with a small brace in the darkest value and leave it visible; no part merges into the next.
7. Mode for a single tool: one treatment covers ≥85%, and two small accents (≤3% each) go only on the parts the hand moves.
Why it works:
- Mirror symmetry about a vertical axis, spread wider than its footing, is what the eye reads as a body keeping its balance. Widen the base to the full span and it is a block (a container); break the mirror and it is a heap.
- One treatment per part, meeting hard at joints, segments the object by job before any label is read, and matched twins group across the axis by likeness, so twelve treatments read as pairs on a spine and not as noise. A gradient, or a colour that crosses a joint, fuses the parts and the "assembled" reading is gone.
- The only closed mass in an open frame is the odd one, and it sits where weight is expected, so the main thing can stay small. A base in the other surface mode reads as ground, not as one more part.
Colour relations: colour is the identity of a part, never its rank; touching parts always differ in hue or in mode; pale to saturated ≈1:1 by count; the darkest value only at joints, legs and base (≤10%); the smallest closed part carries the most saturated treatment; texture counts as a treatment and never lies under reading text; no gradients, no shadows; the ground behind the figure is neutral and empty.
Space and line: one vertical mirror axis, with all variety running up it; angles only on the outlines of members (text and controls inside them stay level); void ≥ half; crossbar ≈1.9× the base; no drawn outlines, since an edge is two treatments meeting.
Type requirement: letters built like the parts: a display face of one uniform stroke weight, set level on a single-treatment block; a fixed-pitch label voice (one character, one cell, ≥12px), each label on the part it names.
Motion and interaction: a state change is an assembly move, not a lift. A part travels along its own member and stops dead at its joint (120–180ms; instant under reduced motion); nothing tilts, fades or casts a shadow, and textures never move. Focus enlarges the part's joint brace and adds a ring; selection swaps the part's treatment AND changes its brace shape and label, so the selected part is the one asymmetry in the mirror. Hit areas ≥44px even on a thin member.
Fails when: the content is n equal items (a feed, a table, a long form), so nothing can be trunk or arm and per-part colour becomes a rainbow list; more than about twelve parts are in view; reading text has to sit on texture; the format is one narrow column with no room for mirrored pairs (run the trunk alone: tiers changing up the axis, over the base).
Signature (do not carry together): black squiggle laminate · pastels beside primaries in flat blocks · a totem of slanted shelves.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Sottsass's procedure → a dark-mode deploy console in one green-grey hue plus a single amber (desktop, 1440 × 900).
1. *Parts.* Nine: release trunk, status crossbar, four arms (build, tests | staging, production), environment head, log base, joints. Treatments are five value steps of the green-grey, one dot texture and the amber.
2. *Axis and trunk.* Axis at x = 720. The trunk is one closed block, 280 × 200px (4.3% of the screen), its foot at 86% of the height. It holds the build hash and the only Deploy button (56px high). Nothing else on the screen is a box.
3. *Members.* Crossbar full width at 50% of the height, 44px thick. Upper arms 360 × 56px with ends cut at 60°; lower arms 300 × 56px with ends cut at 30°, tied to the trunk by 12px struts at 60°. Void = 58% of the figure's box. All labels level.
4. *Treatments.* Upper pair flat at L 34, lower pair flat at L 24, each matching its twin. Up the axis: amber trunk (the only warm, the most saturated, the smallest closed part), crossbar L 45, head L 62. The wrong meeting: a pale amber tag directly on the amber trunk.
5. *Base.* 81px high (9%), 720px wide (50%), dot texture at 15% cover on L 16; the log tail sits on a flat inset strip, not on the dots.
6. *Joints.* Eight 14px braces at L 6, one at every meet.
- *Type.* The environment name in a single-stroke display face on the head; 13px fixed-pitch labels, each on its own part.
- *Interaction.* Selecting Staging: the bar slides 24px along its own length into its joint and stops (150ms); its brace becomes a full-height bar and a "selected" tag appears; its twin keeps the old treatment, so selection is the one asymmetry. Focus: brace 14 → 28px plus a 2px ring. No lift, no tilt.
- *Phone.* Trunk only: four tiers stacked on the axis, each a different value, over a base band.
Result: a nocturnal monochrome pipeline. No pastel, no squiggle, no shelves. What survived is the mirrored open figure, one closed core, treatment per part and visible joints.

## Space & composition
- **Figure on a small core** (Carlton only; not seen across the other works). Carlton is not a storage block with a figure on top: it is open throughout. A full-width shelf at ≈half height carries the figure (arms, triangle, a framed "head"). Below it, splayed legs frame one small closed box (the red drawers, ≈4% of the silhouette) on a patterned plinth (≈8% of the height). On screen: one compact solid block at the centre bottom on a patterned base band, with open, symmetrical arms branching above.
- **Triangulated, not orthogonal.** The diagonals run at 60° and 30°, the angles of an equilateral triangle, not 45°. Slant dividers, shelf lines and section bottoms at 30° from horizontal.
- **Solids and voids alternate.** Coloured members take ≈44% of Carlton's bounding box and the void ≈56%, so open space sits between thin coloured bars rather than between tiles.
- **Symmetry, then sabotage.** Carlton is mirror-symmetric in form and in colour (each left arm matches its right twin). The sabotage is vertical: every tier down the centre axis changes hue (grey frame, olive post, pale yellow, pink, green, light blue, pale pink, red). Small black triangles brace each joint like visible hardware.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

The logic is one hue per component, never a gradient, and colours meet edge to edge with no neutral gap between them. Cheap printed colour is valued above "noble" materials. Pastels (pale yellow, pale pink, powder blue) sit next to saturated red, cobalt, green and orange, held together by black joints and a black-on-white micro-pattern neutral. Pastels and saturates come in roughly equal numbers (Carlton: 4 pale, 5 saturated, plus black and brown).

| Sun yellow, for structural arms and highlights (Carlton's yellow is pale; no saturated yellow seen) | `#F7E6A0` |
| Memphis blue, for secondary panels (Carlton lower arms) | `#014E95` |

Rule: no two adjacent panels share a hue (confirmed on Carlton). Mirror colours across the vertical axis, and change hue at every tier along it. Pair one "wrong" colour combination (pink against red, as Carlton's pale-pink shelf on the red drawers) per screen. The lighting changes these colours a lot (Carlton's drawers read #C42F42 at AIC and #82000E at Cooper Hewitt), so take hexes from white-balanced shots.

## Form, line & typography
The shape language is primary solids (circle, square, triangle, cylinder) joined by visible seams, plus a hand-drawn squiggle as the one organic gesture. Edges are hard, and flat colour is outlined only where pattern needs to be held in. Type should feel geometric, chunky and toy-like.
- **Rubik Mono One**: blocky display caps for headings that behave like objects.
- **Syne** (Google Fonts): its wide weights have an eccentric, postmodern swing, which suits subheads and body text.
- **Space Mono**: a nod to the Valentine, for labels, metadata and form inputs.

## Rhythm, motion & time
Motion is **playful and mechanical, never liquid**: components pop in with a hard overshoot (`cubic-bezier(.34,1.8,.64,1)`, 180–240 ms), like a plastic part clicking into its socket. Slanted elements can wobble ±3° on hover like a figure balancing on a shelf. Patterns stay still while solid shapes move. Stagger at irregular intervals (40, 90, 60 ms). No fades or soft blurs: things appear, flip and tilt.

## UI translations
1. **Layout (Carlton-specific):** put the working content in one compact centred block near the bottom, on a full-width patterned base band (≈8% of the height). Above it, a full-width bar at about half height carries symmetrical 30°/60° "arm" elements (header blocks, CTA panels), and the bar overhangs the base by ≈1.9×.
2. **Layout:** slant section dividers at 30° (`clip-path: polygon(0 0,100% 0,100% 85%,0 100%)`), alternating direction.
3. **Texture:** use a tiled SVG pattern of short black dashes and hooks (≈15% coverage, black on `#D4D1D0`) as a "plinth" under cards, footers and empty states. Never use it behind body text.
4. **Color:** give every card a different solid hue from the palette, mixing pastels and saturates about 1:1, with no white cards. Mirror hues across the vertical axis. Pick the one primary action and make it the only red.
5. **Color:** use exactly one clash per view, such as a pink badge on a red surface or a mint chip on yellow.
6. **Type:** put headings in Rubik Mono One at 3–4× body size, black on a solid colour block, tightly set (`line-height: .9`).
7. **Type:** put form labels and metadata in Space Mono at 12–13px, uppercase with letter-spacing `.08em`, like a typewriter's key legends.
8. **Form:** compose icons and avatars from 2–3 primitives, for example a circle head on a triangle body, each piece in its own hue, braced by small black triangles at the joints instead of outlines.
9. **Motion:** on mount, drop components in with an overshoot spring. On hover, tilt cards by 2–3° around their bottom corner.
10. **Interaction:** give the main tool control a "spool cap", a small round knob in a contrasting orange or pink that physically rotates on press, so the control feels like a toy part.
11. **Interaction:** containers hang from their content's own visible grip tab, like the Valentine's bucket case.

## What this artist would reject
- Grey-on-white "tasteful" neutrality, with a single blue accent standing in for personality.
- Soft shadows and frosted glass faking preciousness or depth; laminate is flat and proud.
- Smooth gradients and colour ramps. He would use hard colour cuts instead.
- Self-effacing tool UI, meaning the idea that a good interface is the one you don't notice.
- Strict orthogonal 12-column grids with every card the same size and colour.

## Signature prompt seed
Design this app screen as a Memphis object with a personality. Use a neutral light-grey base band tiled with black dash texture. Put one compact solid content block on it, with cards in different flat pastels and saturates (no white cards, mirrored left to right). Above it, a full-width green bar at half height carries symmetrical arms slanted at 30° and 60°. Make one red primary action and one deliberate pink-on-red clash. Set headings in chunky Rubik Mono One and labels in Space Mono. Use hard edges, small black triangle joints, no shadows and no gradients. Components click in with springy overshoot and tilt on hover. Keep it useful, but make it sensual, ironic and slightly rude.
