# Mimar Sinan — Islamic Geometry

## Essence
Sinan (chief imperial architect 1538–1588) starts from one circle and works downward: the dome is the whole, and everything else is a set of smaller shapes of the same kind that carry that circle to a square floor. He keeps the circle and changes the polygon under it (square, then hexagon, then octagon), and each polygon's corners can be read inside, outside and in the window count. Each change of shape (circle to polygon to square) happens at one named level, marked by a band or a zone of muqarnas, so a heavy structure reads as a calm, bright, single room.

## Key works examined
- **Şehzade Mosque, Istanbul, 1543–48 (stone, lead, marble; dome about 19 m; Şehzadebaşı).** The square with four half-domes: one shape handed down by halves. Teaches two-way symmetry and height equal to two dome diameters. *(visually checked: Gurlitt 1912 plan and section, public domain, Wikimedia Commons, proportions in px against the scale bar; Commons photos of pier, vault zone and exterior (CC BY-SA 4.0, CC0), colour measured)*
- **Süleymaniye Mosque, Istanbul, 1550–57 (dome about 26.5 m; height about 53 m).** Two half-domes on one axis, ABABA small domes on the other; teaches a silhouette built from one profile at 5–6 sizes. *(visually checked: Gurlitt 1905–06 / 1912 side elevation with drum plan, measured against scale bar; aerial photo CC0; interior photo CC BY-SA 4.0, colour measured)*
- **Rüstem Pasha Mosque, Istanbul, c. 1561–63 (octagon of eight supports inside a wide rectangle; Iznik tile).** Teaches a cut line: pattern below the cornice, plain above, pattern again only in the pendentives. *(visually checked: Commons ceiling photo 2024 and tile close-up, CC BY-SA 4.0; colour measured)*
- **Mihrimah Sultan Mosque, Edirnekapı, c. 1562–65 (square baldachin, dome about 20 m wide, 37 m high, ratio 1 : 1.85).** Teaches the wall as a window grid that fits a semicircle by dropping two per row. *(visually checked: Commons interior photo and fisheye (CC BY-SA 4.0), pitch, counts and glass share measured)*
- **Mağlova aqueduct, near Istanbul, 1554–62, rebuilt 1563–64 (stone; 258 m long, about 35–36 m high, largest span 18.4 m).** Teaches the same system with no ornament: two tiers, wide and narrow alternation, piers that widen downward. *(visually checked: two Commons photos, one historic black-and-white and one modern; bay ratio by px)*
- **Sokollu Mehmed Pasha Mosque, Kadırga, 1571–72 (hexagon in a rectangle, dome 13 m).** Teaches the hexagon whose counts all divide by six, with one wall tiled. *(visually checked: Commons photo of the dome from below; the picture has boosted contrast, so counts and layout used, colour approximate)*
- **Sokollu Mehmed Pasha Mosque, Azapkapı, 1577–78 (octagon with eight arches and a mihrab recess).** A later octagon, plainer surfaces. *(visually checked: one Commons fisheye filed under "Sokollu Mehmet mosque", identified as Azapkapı from its marble, untiled recess; identification mine, moderate confidence)*
- **Selimiye Mosque, Edirne, 1568–75 (dome 31.2–31.5 m on eight 12-sided piers; four minarets 70.9 m).** The end of the series: octagon readable from floor to skyline; everything on a √2 ladder from one pier diameter (Diker). *(visually checked: two dome photos and hall photo CC BY-SA, exterior CC BY 2.0, courtyard CC BY-SA 2.0, muezzin platform CC BY-SA 4.0, fountain; Diker 2026 plan and section drawings viewed locally; window counts by polar unwrap and FFT, colour measured)*
- **Privy Chamber of Murad III, Topkapı, 1578.** Square room 21 × 28 arşın (3:4) grown from a round pierced stone. *(text and drawings in Diker 2026 only; no photograph examined)*
- **Haseki Hürrem baths, 1555–56.** Four equal squares of 22 arşın side by side, about 1:5 overall. *(not obtained: read in Diker 2026, not viewed)*

## Detailed observations

**Şehzade, plan.** Gurlitt's plan (9.74 px per metre): the courtyard outer square is 434 × 435 px and the mosque body about 433 px, so building = two equal squares side by side, one open, one covered. The inner hall is 363 × 363 px (about 37.3 m). The dome circle is 183 px (18.8 m): 0.504 of the hall. So the plan is a square with a circle of half its width, the four half-domes fill the four sides, and four corner domes (about 56 px, 0.30 of the main) fill the corners. The four free piers are octagons about 4.6 m wide (0.25 of the dome), with their corners cut toward the centre. The courtyard is a 5 × 5 field of bays with the inner 3 × 3 left open: 16 identical domes of about 7.2 m (0.38 of the main). Outside, tombs stand as separate octagons and one hexagon: the same polygons as free objects.

**Şehzade, section.** (25.7 px per metre in the enlarged crop.) Inner height about 37.1 m against a dome width of 18.8 m: 1.97, close to 2:1. Horizontal bands run right round the room at about 8.7 m, 15.0 m, 19.5 m and 26.7 m (0.24, 0.41, 0.53, 0.72 of the height); the dome starts near 0.83. The first three storeys are 8.7, 6.3 and 4.5 m: each about 0.71–0.72 of the one below (close to 1:√2; my measure from a printed drawing, may be accident). Arches step down 18.1 m (main), 8.7 m (middle of a half-dome), about 5.8 m (wall arches): about 1 : 0.48 : 0.32. Windows are in five tiers: wide rectangles with iron grilles at floor level, two per bay; tall-short triplets in the second tier; singles in the small half-domes; a close row at the foot of each half-dome; a close ring in the drum. Openings get smaller and closer together as they go up. Muqarnas bands sit at exactly two places: below the second cornice and under the drum.

**Şehzade, pier and vaults (photos).** The pier is an undecorated stone mass (`#908474`, `#9A8F7F`, L 56–60, C 10) that turns from square-ish at the base to octagon above: the dropped inner corner is closed by a small muqarnas niche with a stepped outline, about a quarter of the visible face width. Two gilt-brown cornices and a fluted white cap end the pier, then red and white arch stones rise from it. The half-dome surfaces are nearly blank (L 44–51, C 3–4 in shade), each window wears a small painted crown, and there is a single inscription disc at each half-dome's crown. Muqarnas in the squinch is drawn in thin lines and low relief, the same white as the vault. A square Kufic panel with a central interlaced swirl sits in the mid-band on both sides of the axis. Most painted baroque scrolls are later restoration.

**Süleymaniye, elevation.** (14.9 px per metre.) From ground to dome crown: 790 px = 53.0 m. Dome at springing 378 px (25.4 m), at the eaves 407 px (27.3 m): width is about half the height. Akyürek and Kahraman give dome diameter over height to dome as 51%. Domes by diameter: 378 (main), 164 and 156 (side, two and one), 106 and 101 (pier turrets and the small side domes), 79 (courtyard), 63 (buttress turrets): steps of 2.4, 1.5, 1.3, 1.25. Five side domes run wide-narrow-wide-narrow-wide (164-101-156-101-164) while the arcade under them runs narrow-wide-narrow (three arches) so the wide dome sits over the wide arch. The skyline climbs in separate steps: side wall, side dome, pier turret, drum buttress, dome. Line from wall edge to dome foot is about 54° at the bottom, 47° mid, about 40° at the top. Stepped (staircase) extrados above the big arch shows where the load turns from arch to buttress. The tympanum is a semicircle with a pyramid of windows and two small roundels. The elevation has almost no ornament; hierarchy is by size and by depth of shadow.

**Süleymaniye, skyline and interior (photos).** From the air: 1 main dome, half-domes, side domes, pier turrets and a ring of courtyard domes, all lead grey with fine ribs. They are all the same cap on the same drum at decreasing size. Minarets stand at the four corners of the court: the pair near the dome taller, the outer pair shorter, so the height rises toward the dome. Inside: four large grey-white piers (`#6C6C68`, `#818584`, C 2) with red and white arch stones only in the big arches; two glass side lunettes with several stepped rows each; a ring of small windows around the drum. The stained glass appears only on the prayer wall, in the lower part; everything else is clear glass. Colour is concentrated in one place, at the target.

**Rüstem Pasha, ceiling photo.** From the bottom: tile on piers and walls, a straight dark-red cornice, then a white zone with no tile at all; then a red and white arch, then a tiled triangle between arches, then the painted ring. Two small arches stand below each corner half-dome and an inverted muqarnas triangle hangs between them, so one half-dome is resolved by two arches and a point. The corner half-dome is blank apart from one black and red sun at its crown. Two stone muqarnas brackets, each about 0.1 of the photo width, carry the cut corners (the change from straight wall to curve). Glass is a stone lattice of round holes in a hex stack. Tile areas (measured over a tile-only crop): `#31475D`, `#12224A`, `#5B6E81`, `#8C9EAD`; arch red `#552F2D` (dark in this exposure).

**Rüstem Pasha, tile panel photo.** One tile: white ground (`#94988E` here, shaded and slightly dim, 34% of photo) with a lobed red rim around a wheel. Rim about 665 px wide, inner red disc about 231 px (0.35), blue rosette about 180 px (0.27), dot in the centre. About 16 leaf sprays turn around it, all leaning one way. Corners hold quarter rosettes that complete across four tiles. A deep blue border strip (`#1A2E4F`, L 19 C 22) and a turquoise line separate the panel from the next one; red (`#56281E`) is a small share, about 10% including rim and flowers. Wikipedia notes that patterns are cut arbitrarily at edges: pattern fills, architecture crops.

**Mihrimah Edirnekapı, tympanum.** Columns every 78 px, rows every 84 px, a constant lattice that the arch simply crops. The bottom row has 7 windows, the middle 5, the top 3 = 15; each row gives up its two end places, and in rows 2 and 3 a small roundel takes the place given up: 4 roundels in all, 19 openings. Window width is about 0.69 of the pitch, so mullion about 0.31. Glass (very bright pixels) covers about 36% of the semicircle in this exposure, with overexposed haloes included. Below the tympanum: a long gallery, then a band with a tall-low-tall triplet under each small arch, then two big stained windows per bay, then lettered panels over the ground windows: size of opening is graded by tier and the stained colour sits low and around the middle. Dome ring: 9 stained windows visible on the front arc, each with a small spike of painted ornament between (full count not taken). The stone (`#C1AA8E`, L 72 C 18) is the base tone; ornament is limited to the pendentive discs, a narrow red border along the arch edges and the glass.

**Mağlova.** A pure two-tier arcade: wide arch over wide arch, and, on each pier, a small arch over a small arch through the pier's own thickness. Wide span about 3 times the narrow (132 vs 45 px in the middle bay). A moulding line runs unbroken between tiers for the full length and is also a walking path. The lower piers are wedges, wide at the ground and narrow at the moulding, ending in a triangular cut with a sloping top: the load path is the profile. Piers become slimmer up the height and the top edge is one straight line for the channel. No tile, no carving: wide and narrow, two tiers, one horizontal. Ktb.gov.tr gives 8 large arches below and 8 smaller above.

**Kadırga Sokollu, dome.** Six tiled triangles between six arches, so a hexagon by count. The drum ring holds 18 windows: 3 per side, and the pier axis falls on a solid strip between two windows, not on a window. The painted lozenge rings and rim ornaments are also about 18. The dome field (`#C9B182` and `#AD9155`, processed picture) is empty between sparse ornaments: the bare ground is the larger part, by eye. Tiles are reserved for the six triangles, the rim and the mihrab wall (TDV: the whole wall between the piers); other surfaces plain. TDV adds that the two side half-domes are turned 60° to the walls, so no buttress arch is needed.

**Azapkapı Sokollu (fisheye, identity moderate).** The dome ring is carried on a ring of small arches with plain half-domes (apparently eight; not all visible), with one large arch opening on a marble mihrab recess with red pendentive triangles, coloured glass in the recess only. Grey veined marble and white plaster dominate; strong colour lives in the recess windows.

**Selimiye, dome from below.** Concentric rings on one centre: outer black cornice (radius 353 px), the inner cream ring, a lilac ring with pendants, a star-and-medallion field, and a centre lamp disc (84 px). Radii 353 : 248 : 207 : 84. 353/248 = 1.42 (√2), 248/207 = 1.20, 207/84 = 2.46. The paint is mostly 19th century or later restoration, so the painted rings cannot be used as proof of Sinan's ratio; the structure (windows, arches, piers) can. The unwrapped window ring has 40 bays (strongest frequency 40) with every fifth bay grey and dim: 8 dim bays and 32 bright ones, which FFT shows as a second peak at 8. The eight dim bays sit exactly on the eight pier axes, where the eight exterior turrets stand behind them, and the eight calligraphy medallions stand on the same axes: octagon by omission. Between two piers: 4 bright windows, confirmed outside (4 windows between two turrets). Around the ring, eight equal arches: four on the main axes end in straight, glazed walls, four on the diagonals open into small half-domes with a lettered disc at the crown. The bands of the tympanum: 6 windows over 8 = 14, in two rows, the upper row's ends cut by the curve; the glass is round panes in a hex stack. Between the arches and the cornice is a triangle of flat muqarnas cells drawn in red line, one per corner (8), closing the octagon to the circle. Arch stones are zigzag red and cream (about 30 per arch, count by eye, `#5E3119` about 23% of the arch crop, `#988C76` ground).

**Selimiye, section and plan (Diker drawings).** Plan: an octagon of 8 twelve-sided piers (3.67 m, 5 arşın) tangent to a circle of about 41.5 m; a rectangle 60 × 44 m hall plus an equal courtyard. Section: piers are 5 times their own width high, with the profile change at 3; inside height from the dome crown to the top of the central pool about 41.5 m (the same circle, C8, that is tangent outside the eight piers); the minaret shafts have the same diameter as the piers. Deviations from the ideal module are marked on the drawing mostly between 0 and 3.3%, with one at 8.4% (central pool). Dome area over floor area is 40% (Hagia Sophia 14%); each pier is about 10.8 m² (Hagia Sophia about 54.5).

**Selimiye, exterior (photo).** The octagon of turrets stands on the skyline as a clear coronet: 8 turrets (6 visible), between them 4 drum windows, under each axial side one wide lunette with 6 windows over 7–8, then a row of three lunettes below, then galleries and small arched openings. The same arch-and-window unit repeats at three sizes. Turret and buttress form one line from drum to ground in a sloping cap. The four minarets mark the four corners of the hall: each shaft is fluted and has three balconies at close to equal steps, and their diameter equals a pier. Sky is blank; the building is pale stone in sun (`#CCC2B6`, L 79 C 6), half-tone stone (`#99928B`), near-black lead on drum and turrets (`#31343B`, L 22) under a light lead dome, and gold only on the finials. The dark drum makes the dome seem to float on a shadow line.

**Selimiye, hall and muezzin platform.** The platform is a low timber pavilion on 12 short arches exactly on the dome's centre, a room inside a room; its fringe painting is the best-preserved original colour: dark green-black ground with cream peonies (`#312B28`, `#504439`), red bands (`#815750` worn, originally brighter) and cusped arch edges. It is the one dense, small-scale, low-lit thing in a bright hall. Directly beneath it is a small pool. Hall colours: warm stone (`#786640`, `#AC9758` lit), dark (`#372C1B`), red and white arches above, white glass (L 84, 5.6%).

## Process and relations (observed)
- **One circle first, then a polygon, then a square** (Şehzade square, Kadırga hexagon, Rüstem Pasha and Selimiye octagon, Mağlova no circle). The dome circle is about half the room (0.50 in Şehzade by px; 31.3 of 58.7 m = 0.53 in Selimiye). The polygon's corners are the only freestanding supports, and the room is the square that holds it.
- **Same cap, repeated at smaller sizes** (Süleymaniye 378-164-106-79-63 px; Şehzade 18.8-7.2-5.7 m; Selimiye courtyard domes). A dome on a drum is one unit; no other roof shape appears. Step between sizes is 1.25–2.4 and the biggest is far larger than the next.
- **Height is twice the dome** (Şehzade 1.97, Süleymaniye 2.0 by px and 51% published). Selimiye drops to about 1.36 to 1.42 (41.5–43 over 31): the widest dome is the lowest in proportion.
- **√2 ladder from one small measure** (Diker for Selimiye, Murad III chamber, Haseki baths). One pier diameter (5 arşın) times √2 in eight steps ends at 16 times it (80 arşın = the outer boundary): C1 3.67, C2 5.19, C3 7.34, C4 10.4, C5 14.7, C6 20.8, C7 29.4, C8 41.5, C9 58.7 m. Same step found in Şehzade's first storeys (0.71–0.72). A second study (Erdoğan 2024) warns that the ratios do not follow one tidy series across Ottoman mosques; the ladder is an analytic model, though with small deviations.
- **Change shape at one level, and mark that level** (Şehzade four cornices; Süleymaniye, Selimiye). Erdoğan reads Selimiye from the top: circle until the drum's outer piers, many-sided at that line, octagon at the arch keystones, square at the arch foot. Each change happens at a horizontal line and at no other place. A band of inscription, cornice or muqarnas always sits on the line.
- **Muqarnas only at corners, where a flat meets a curve** (Şehzade pier niche, Rüstem Pasha corner brackets and hanging triangle, Selimiye spandrels). It is a local device for a transition, the same colour as the ground, and never a pattern covering a field.
- **Half-domes and squinches shrink the problem** (Süleymaniye two, Şehzade four, Selimiye four small, Rüstem four). A large span is divided into two smaller ones below it, each time by the same curve: one half-dome, two arches, a point.
- **Count follows the polygon** (Kadırga 18 = 6 × 3; Selimiye 40 = 8 × 5 with 8 blind; Mihrimah rows 7-5-3). The rhythm of windows tells which polygon carries the roof. In Selimiye one bay in five is dim, in Kadırga the pier axis lands between windows.
- **The window wall is a constant lattice cropped by the arch** (Mihrimah 78 px pitch; Selimiye 6 over 8). Row loses two windows per step; end places left over are filled with small roundels or left solid. The lattice never bends.
- **Openings grade from low and wide to high and small and close** (Şehzade five tiers, Süleymaniye drum, Selimiye hall). Floor: rectangles with grilles; middle: groups; top: ring. More light comes from the high small ones, so the room glows from the top and the sides.
- **Strong colour is kept for one purpose and place** (Süleymaniye stained glass on the prayer wall; Rüstem below the cornice; Kadırga mihrab wall and six triangles; red-white stones on load-bearing arches only). Mass surfaces are white or stone at C 2–18. Rüstem Pasha is the exception (tiled walls throughout); Sinan normally kept decoration subordinate (Wikipedia: the patron may have asked for it).
- **Structure drawn on the outside** (Süleymaniye stepped arch and turrets; Selimiye eight turrets; Mağlova wedge piers). Each interior support has an exterior knob and a slope that leads to it; nothing on the outside is there to fill a skyline. Buttresses are pulled inside walls and hidden by galleries, so bulk is absorbed (Akyürek and Kahraman).
- **Same building retried, never repeated** (Şehzade four half-domes → Süleymaniye two → Mihrimah none → Rüstem octagon → Kadırga hexagon → Selimiye octagon). Each changes one thing (the polygon or the number of half-domes); all keep the dome, the drum ring, the lattice wall. Sinan's later accounts call Şehzade, Süleymaniye and Selimiye apprentice, journeyman and master work (secondary sources; the wording was not read in the translation).
- **More supports, each thinner** (Akyürek and Kahraman). On four piers the total pier area is high (Süleymaniye 33% of dome area, above Hagia Sophia's 27%, kept deliberately cautious). On eight, each Selimiye pier is about 10.8 m² against Hagia Sophia's 54.5 m². Extra corners shorten the arches, thin the piers and free wall for glass.
- **The generative rule:** set one circle half as wide as the room and place it at about twice its own width above the floor (in the octagon, about 1.4); carry it on 4, 6 or 8 supports; let each level change the shape once and mark that level; below, repeat the same cap at 0.4–0.5 of the size, then at 0.3; fill walls between supports with a constant lattice of openings, dropping two per row and rounding the ends; make the count of every ring divide by the number of supports; use muqarnas only in corners; keep strong colour in one target zone and on the load-bearing arches.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

*Standing of the evidence: proportions, counts, pitches and area shares were measured on two printed survey drawings (Gurlitt) and on photographs. The √2 ladder is Diker's reconstruction for one building and Erdoğan's study disputes a single series. The order of decisions (whole first, then supports, then levels) is read off the buildings and the secondary literature; Sinan's own accounts and Necipoğlu's study were not read, so it is a reconstruction of his method, not a record of it. Painted surfaces are largely later restoration and were not used for ratios.*

**Mimar Sinan.** *Fix one whole half the view wide and carry it on four, six or eight thin supports; step it down to the frame one marked level at a time, in smaller copies of itself; make every count a multiple of the supports, fill between them with a constant lattice that the boundary crops, and keep strong colour for the carriers and one target.*
Process:
1. Fix the whole first: one undivided figure about half the view's width (0.50–0.53), centred, with the ground under it left clear. Stand it at twice its own width above the base line (about 1.4 when it rests on eight supports and is at its widest). Nothing else of its kind may exceed 0.42 of it (a step of 2.4 or more).
2. Choose the support count n: 4, 6 or 8. The n supports are the only freestanding elements; push them out toward the frame, and let the frame be the rectangle that holds them. More supports means thinner ones and more open field between them: about 0.25 of the whole's width each at four, about 0.12 at eight.
3. Hand the whole down to the frame in levels. Each level changes one thing only (the number of sides or divisions, or the edge shape) and changes it on one horizontal line that runs the full width as a continuous band; no change happens anywhere else. Measured band heights in one work: 0.24, 0.41, 0.53, 0.72 and 0.83 of the height, with the whole above the last.
4. Below the whole, repeat its own shape smaller, never a second shape: a first size at 0.4–0.5, a next at about 0.3, then steps of 1.25–1.5 down to about 1/6, five or six sizes in all. A span too wide for one copy is resolved as one copy over two half-size copies and a point between them. In a row of copies, alternate wide and narrow (wide to narrow up to 3 : 1), a wide one over a wide interval.
5. Take every repeat count from n: a row or ring holds n × 3 to n × 5 units. Show the supports by omission, not by a mark: either every (count / n)th unit is muted and sits on a support axis, or the axis falls on the solid gap between two units.
6. Fill the field between two supports with one lattice of constant pitch (opening about 0.69 of the pitch, bar 0.31) and let the boundary crop it: where the field narrows, each row gives up its two end places (7, 5, 3), and a vacated place takes one small filler or stays solid. The lattice never bends, rescales or changes pitch to follow the boundary.
7. Grade the openings by level: few and wide at the base (two per interval), grouped in threes in the middle, small, many and close at the top, directly under the whole. The brightest value lives in these small units, 5–14% of the view.
8. Put a faceted transition piece only where two geometries meet at a corner (a straight edge passing into a turned or cut one): small, about 0.1 of the view's width or a quarter of the face it sits on, stepped in outline, in the ground's own colour. It never covers a field.
9. Keep the mass low in chroma and spend contrast in two places: an alternating two-value pair (about 30 L apart, the darker about a quarter of the member) on the members that carry one level to the next, and one target zone that holds the deepest colour and the only patterned surface, cut off by a straight line with plain ground beyond it. One point accent of about 1%.
10. Draw the structure on the outline: every support shows as one marker on the outer edge, with a stepped line leading to it (about 54° near the base, 40° near the top), and nothing is added to the outline for its own sake. For the next view, keep the whole, the ring and the lattice and change one parameter only (n, or the number of half-size copies).
Why it works:
- Grouping by likeness and scale reference: one shape at five or six sizes reads as one family, and a top step of 2.4 or more leaves a single winner. Equalise two of the large copies and the view has two centres; introduce a second shape and the small parts stop measuring the large one.
- Figure and ground at the levels: a change that happens on one marked line is read as a decision, so the eye counts stages and a heavy assembly reads as a calm stack. Spread the change over the height, or leave the line unmarked, and the same parts read as a lump with accidents in it.
- Salience by oddness: in an even ring the muted unit or the wider gap is found without any ink, and it falls where a support stands, so the rhythm states the structure. A count that is not a multiple of n makes the oddness drift, and the ring becomes decoration.
- Grouping by texture: a lattice of one pitch fuses into a tone, so the cropping boundary becomes the figure. Bend the lattice to the boundary and each opening turns into a separate object competing with the edge it was meant to show.
- Colour as load and target: when the mass is near neutral, the alternating pair says "this carries" and the one deep zone says "go here". Spread pattern over every surface and both messages are lost (the one fully patterned interior in the series is credited to its patron).
Colour relations: ground and mass share one low-chroma family (chroma 2–20) in three values, lit, unlit and shaded, and hold most of the view; light is the brightest value (L 84–92) in small units, 5–14%; the carrying pair is one dark and one pale member of the ground's family about 30 L apart, the dark about a quarter of the member and 7–8% of the view; the deepest, most saturated colour is confined to the target zone, where it runs dark 19%, two mid tones about 25% and 22%, pale ground 34% and a warm second hue 10%; one point accent of about 1%; the darkest band sits directly under the whole, so the whole lifts off a shadow line. Depth of colour rises toward the target.
Space and line: whole 0.50–0.53 of the width; height 2 × the whole (1.4 on eight supports); n = 4, 6 or 8; supports 0.25 down to 0.12 of the whole; copies at ≤0.42, about 0.3, then steps of 1.25–1.5; three zones by height, closed 0–0.4, open 0.4–0.75, ring and whole 0.75–1; lattice opening 0.69 of the pitch; rows 7, 5, 3; wide to narrow up to 3 : 1; an approach area of the same outer size as the main one, ringed by copies at about 0.38 with its middle open. Straight horizontals are few and continuous. Sizes may be drawn from one ladder: a small measure (the support's width) times √2, eight steps to sixteen times itself; this is one scholar's model of one work, fitted within 0–3.3% with one miss at 8.4%, and a second study finds no single series across the buildings, so use it as a convenient scale and not as a law.
Type requirement: every text run sits in its own closed frame, one face and one size per frame, light on dark, at a fixed station (the centre of a transition piece, the crown of a copy, the head of an opening, the top of a support); a level band may carry one line of text along its length. Sizes step with the levels. Text never crosses a band and never fills a field as texture. Sacred text is never decoration; frames hold the interface's own labels.
Motion and interaction: nothing moves toward the viewer; the only motion is a change of level. Entering goes from the small repeated copies through one narrow threshold to the whole; opening a copy raises it one size step in place (250–400ms, ease-out, no overshoot) while the bands stay fixed. In a live ring the lit units change and the muted ones never do, so they stay as markers. Selecting a support lights its marker on the outline and its interval of the lattice. Reduced motion: cut to the level.
Fails when: there are many peers and no single whole; the content has no stable count, or one that is no multiple of 4, 6 or 8 (padding the ring with dummies breaks step 5); the view is mostly long running text; the content is one-sided and cannot take a two-way symmetry; the format is so narrow that the smallest copy (about 1/6 of the whole) must be a control under 44px; muted units carry a state by dimness alone, without a label or a second cue.
Signature (do not carry together): lead-grey hemispherical caps stepping up to one large dome between pencil minarets · red-and-white striped arch stones · blue-and-white floral tile under a cornice with honeycomb corner vaulting and lettered roundels (sacred text is never decoration).

## Space & composition
- **Square hall plus equal open square** (Şehzade 434 × 435 px vs 433; Selimiye 60 × 44 m vs the same): open and covered halves have equal outer size, a pair.
- **Circle : room about 1:2** (0.50 Şehzade, 0.53 Selimiye on the outer circle). **Height : circle about 2:1** (Şehzade, Süleymaniye) or 1.4:1 (Selimiye).
- **Central void.** The central square is empty (pool and platform at Selimiye, nothing at Şehzade); supports are pushed back near the walls; dome area 40% of floor in Selimiye, 13% in Süleymaniye.
- **Three zones by height**: solid with few openings (0–0.4), arches and windows (0.4–0.75), ring and dome (0.75–1.0) for Şehzade.
- **Silhouette**: stepped caps, 40° near the top and 54° near the base (Süleymaniye).
- **Corner minarets**: verticals outside the mass, inner pair taller (Süleymaniye), identical four (Selimiye); they stop the silhouette.

## Form, line & typography
Form: circle, half-circle, quarter-sphere, polygon, pointed arch, cube; every shape is made by cutting or repeating a circle. Straight horizontals are rare and strong (cornice, gallery, iron tie rods). Edges are one line between two surfaces, often a thin black and white or red border. Muqarnas is faceted but low relief, drawn with thin line. Script: discs at crown points and corners (round, in a dark field with cream or gold letters), long friezes on red or blue ground, square Kufic with a central swirl in a mid-band. Letters are confined to round or rectangular frames and read as seals. Glass: round panes packed in a hexagonal lattice, framed by thin black beads, in round-topped windows. What the lettering shows: every inscription has its own closed frame, one script and one size per frame, light letters on a dark field, and a fixed station (pendentive centre, dome crown, window head, pier top).

## Rhythm, motion & time
- Vertical: five tiers of openings from slow and big to fast and small (Şehzade).
- Horizontal: wide-narrow-wide (Süleymaniye 164-101-156-101-164; Mağlova wide arch, narrow arch at 3:1). Arches in threes (tall in the middle) under each bay.
- Ring: 40 beats with every fifth muted (Selimiye), 18 = 6 × 3 (Kadırga), 7-5-3 (Mihrimah tympanum).
- Sequence: small repeated domes of the court (about 0.38 of the main at Şehzade), then a deep portal with a muqarnas hood, then the single large room (courtyard photo and plans). Upward the eye meets arch, half-dome, window ring, dome. The eight dim windows at Selimiye stay dim because turrets stand behind them, so they act as fixed markers in the lit ring.
- Stillness: no element moves toward the visitor; scale change is the motion.

## UI translations
*For strict studies and homage only; for design, use "Process (derived)". Sacred text and devotional images are never used as decoration, in a study or anywhere else.*

1. **Whole at half.** Centre one summary figure at half the view's width and keep the area beneath it clear; no second figure above 0.42 of it.
2. **Count the supports.** Carry the view on 4, 6 or 8 slim posts placed toward the edges and make every tab row, ring and grid count a multiple of that number.
3. **One change per band.** Stack the view in levels divided by full-width bands; let the layout change only at a band (one becomes two, two become four, panels become rows) and name the band.
4. **One shape, five sizes.** Repeat the summary's own shape for every lower total at about 0.4, 0.3 and then steps of 1.25–1.5; resolve a wide span as one over two.
5. **Ring by omission.** Put a ring of small equal indicators under the summary and mute every fifth, or widen every third gap, on the post axes; draw no tick.
6. **Cropped lattice.** Fill a non-rectangular region with cells of one pitch (cell 0.69 of the pitch) and drop two cells per row as it narrows, 7, 5, 3; an empty end place takes a small dot or nothing.
7. **Small and many at the top.** Wide, few controls at the base, groups of three in the middle, a close row of small indicators at the top; the lightest value belongs to these small units (5–14%).
8. **Stepped corner piece.** Where a straight edge passes into a cut or rounded one, place one small stepped piece in the ground's colour, a quarter of the face wide, and nowhere else.
9. **Stone ground, two-value carriers.** Hold the mass in warm low-chroma neutrals (`#C1AA8E` lit, `#908474` unlit, `#6E6863` shaded) and stripe only the connectors between levels in a dark and pale pair 30 L apart (`#5E3119` on `#988C76`).
10. **Pattern below a line.** Allow one patterned, deep-coloured zone (`#12224A` at about a fifth of that zone) at the destination, cut off by a straight band with plain ground above.
11. **Dark band under the whole.** Set the darkest neutral (`#31343B`) as a narrow band directly beneath the summary so it lifts off the stack.
12. **Framed labels at stations.** Set each label in its own closed dark frame, one size per frame, at a post top, a panel crown or a band; interface labels only.
13. **Knobs on the outline.** Show one marker per support on the view's outer edge or in its thumbnail, joined to it by a stepped line.
14. **√2 scale.** Take sizes from one module times √2 (1, 1.41, 2, 2.83, 4 … 16); Diker's model for the Selimiye, used here as a scale and not as proof of the maker's method.

## What this artist would reject
- A dome, cornice or frieze that stands on a wall without a visible polygon carrying it.
- Two competing big shapes of similar size; the central cap is at least 2.4 times the next.
- Pattern applied over a structural edge or across a transition; pattern wallpaper outside a frame.
- Different curve types for small and large parts; mixed arch shapes on one level.
- Windows that follow the curve by distorting; unequal pitch; a count that does not fit the polygon.
- Muqarnas used as ornament away from a corner or springing.
- Bulk hidden by added decoration when a hidden buttress or gallery can carry it.
- Decoration competing with structure (tile everywhere is the patron's, as at Rüstem Pasha).

## Signature prompt seed
Start from one undivided figure half as wide as the view, centred, with clear ground beneath it, and let nothing else of its kind pass 0.42 of it. Carry it on four, six or eight slim supports pushed toward the frame. Bring it down to the frame in levels, each changing one thing on one full-width marked band, using only smaller copies of the first figure, one over two. Make every row and ring count a multiple of the supports and show each support by a muted unit or a wider gap, never by a mark. Fill the fields between supports with a lattice of one pitch that the boundary crops two cells at a time. Put few wide openings low and many small ones high. Keep the mass near neutral; give an alternating dark and pale pair to the connectors between levels and the deepest colour to one target zone below a straight line. Set every label in its own frame at a fixed station.
