# The Topkapı Scroll masters — Islamic Geometry

## Essence
The scroll is a workshop's catalogue of rules, not of finished pictures. Each frame holds the smallest piece that can regrow the whole (usually a quarter), drawn over a scored, uninked web of circles and radii. A second, dotted layer of polygons may sit between the web and the ink; the ink crosses each polygon's edge and the polygon is meant to vanish on the wall. Colour, stipple and stroke weight are codes (which layer, which tier, which tile), so a page that looks like ornament reads as instructions.

## Key works examined
Scroll drawings are numbered as in Necipoğlu's catalogue (1995/96). All are ink on heavy cream rag paper, late 15th or 16th century (Necipoğlu's range), western or central Iran, Topkapı Palace Museum Library, MS H. 1956; roll 33–34 cm high × 29.5 m; no date, signature or explanatory writing. Dimensions are panel height × width from the catalogue. Every one of the 60 colour plates (cat. 1–114) was seen in contact sheets; the ones below were studied at full plate size (150 ppi).

- **Cat. 28, three-layer quarter repeat unit (25.5 × 49.5 cm, right edge torn off).** Teaches three scales or roles on one surface: dotted polygons, black strapwork, orange large pattern. *(visually checked: Getty PDF p. 315, also Commons Panel29.JPG; line angles, ink share, segment lengths measured)*
- **Cat. 53 and 54, two-layer quarter units (24.5 × 18 cm each).** One skeleton drawn twice: fully dotted and thick, then nearly bare and thin. Teaches how much of the scaffold can be dropped. *(visually checked: Getty PDF p. 331; angles, stroke and frame widths measured; cat. 53 reflected ×4 on screen)*
- **Cat. 34 (25.8 × 25.8 cm) and 31 (25.7 × 35.5 cm), orange over black.** Large pattern drawn through the centres of the small one. *(visually checked: Getty PDF pp. 317, 319; angles measured on 34; scored-line overlay of cat. 31 seen on p. 265)*
- **Cat. 49, relief mosaic quarter unit (24.5 × 46.5 cm).** Heavy blank polygons floating on a hair-thin net. *(visually checked: Getty PDF p. 327 and overlay p. 273; area shares and angles measured; reflected ×4)*
- **Cat. 38, the fully coloured panel (25.7 × 40–40.5 cm).** Colour by tile shape, large shapes left blank, script in a hexagon. *(visually checked: Getty PDF p. 321; palette and angles measured)*

## Detailed observations
**Cat. 28.** A long rectangle bounded by one fine black line; about 4 cm of empty paper above and below. Three line kinds, none thick. (1) Red dotted polygons: decagons, pentagons, rhombuses, bow-ties, elongated hexagons, the module edge about 3.1 cm (90 px at 29 px/cm). (2) Solid black lines: they cross in an X at the middle of every dotted edge and run straight into the neighbouring polygon until they meet another line. (3) Solid orange lines, ruled over black dots: 8–10 long segments, 6 to 21 cm, crossing the whole panel. The measured line directions are 19°, 53°, 91°, 127°, 161°: five directions, 36° apart, nothing else, and the orange layer obeys the same five. Orange lines turn and cross at centres of black ten-pointed stars; each large line threads small centres. Lines are about 1 px median (≈0.4 mm); 94% of the panel is bare paper. No star is filled, outlined or emphasised: star centres are blank holes where lines happen to surround a point. The eye follows orange first because it is the only continuous long line, then falls into the black net, then notices the dots. Two opposite corners hold quarter stars (one is torn away). Accidents show: a line stops short, some are retraced darker, the stains repeat at about 21 cm intervals because the roll was wet while wound.

**Cat. 53/54.** Portrait panels, 1.36:1. Cat. 53's straps are ≈2–2.5 mm wide, measured 6 px of 715 px; its dotted line is under 1 mm. Directions 17°, 53°, 89°, 125°, 161°. At each dotted edge two straps cross at the midpoint, at about 54° to the edge by Wikipedia's girih entry. The one exception is the diagonal dotted edge through the centre: it is cut in three by two pairs of lines, which makes the small rhombus in the middle. That one different edge is the only asymmetrical-looking event in the panel. Cat. 54 keeps the same centre rhombus, reduces the dotted layer to a single Z-shaped path of 5 segments, and draws hair lines only: more open ground, fewer pieces, same skeleton. The quarter stars sit at upper left and lower right, so the panel has two-fold rotation, not mirror symmetry; when I reflected it four ways a closed field appeared with large ten-sided rings, pieces that no single quarter shows.

**Cat. 31/34.** In 34 the small black net has cells of roughly 1–2.5 cm and the orange shapes span 10–25 cm; one orange shape covers very roughly 15–25 black cells (by eye). Both layers use five-fold angles (1, 37, 69, 91, 109, 141 including the frame). The overlay for 31 shows the hidden part: five whole wheels of 20 radii each around star centres, plus part-wheels at corners and edges, so the final pattern is the set of places where spokes from neighbouring wheels meet. The final drawing shows none of these wheels.

**Cat. 49.** Two planes with a hard difference. Plane 1: a net of hair lines (decagons, hexagons and bow-ties tiling, per the catalogue) over the whole field. Plane 2: about 20 blank polygons with a thick outline (≈2.5–3 mm) that interrupt the net completely. Measured: net 60% of the field, blank interiors ≈31%, thick outline 9–10%. The eight whole large polygons are almost equal, each 2.6–3.3% of the area; half pieces at the edges 1.2–1.5%; three-pointed star bits ≈0.2%, so sizes fall into three clear steps of about 14:7:1. Outlines use the same five directions as the net (1, 35, 71, 107, 143) and their corners land on small star centres (catalogue). The catalogue adds that the raised polygons, joined, would tile without gaps: they are the same tiling pulled apart to leave a channel for the net. Two sides (bottom and right) have no outline, where the copy continues. Reflected ×4: a ten-pointed star in the middle, quarter stars at the four corners.

**Cat. 38.** The only panel coloured all over, and it is a small-tile net with large blank plates over it, like cat. 49. Colour is assigned by shape: light blue to twelve-pointed stars, orange to five-pointed stars, grey to eight-pointed, yellow and white with tiny rosettes and black bow-tie slivers to the rest (the catalogue says the same). Measured share: black 19%, red/orange 12%, blue 11%, yellow 6%, paper and white ≈30% (the blank plates alone about 15–20% by eye), the rest mixes of line and edge. Rings of yellow tiles around grey stars read as circles at a distance. The kufic hexagon at centre is red on paper, three rhombuses making a cube illusion, and sits where the plates leave a place for it. Directions 1, 31, 59, 91, 121, 149: a 30° family, not 36°. The left and top border lines are missing on purpose (the copy continues there). There is a mistake in one letter of the third rhombus (catalogue).

**Muqarnas plans, cat. 5, 11, 24, 25.** Each is a quarter plan of a ceiling. One corner is the centre of the hood; the opposite corner is where the tiers meet the walls. Tiers spread as an arc of star-shaped cells around the corner; stars of several orders (five to about ten points, by eye) share one plan. Three codes: stipple (small black dots on white) marks one set of units; solid red and solid black, the darts and kites between units; the rest blank paper. Red and black parts are kept apart by a thin white edge (cat. 5), so each reads as a separate tile. Measured: cat. 5 dark 2%, red 3%, paper ≈91%; cat. 11 dark 6%, red 4–5%; cat. 25 dark under 1%, red 3%. Cat. 5 leaves one large uninked quadrant (about 32% of the panel, hood side) and cat. 11 about 19%; cat. 25 fills its hood with nine stippled ribs fanning from one corner point, with eight blank round-ended flutes between them; each rib ends in one black dart and one red five-pointed star, so the count nine is carried from hood to tier. The outer edge of each tier steps in and out with no outline of its own, so the plan looks broken but reads as depth.

**Squared-grid and script panels.** Grid of 0.3–0.5 cm squares inked in fine black, and every second operation is filling a whole cell. In cat. 43 (49 cells across, 24.5 cm) the filled cells are 33% of the panel and the empty grid shows through the rest; the script "Allah" is set diagonally, on-grid, stroke about three cells, in the middle of the octagon; the word spans about 44% of the panel's width (320 of 726 px). In cat. 51 the panel is split into strips and boxes of unequal size by bars of the same width as the script; 39% dark, 2.8% orange/red (box e is orange, box i on the next plate red, box c two-toned to show two brick options). Stroke 1 cell, gap 1 cell in the square kufic; the panel reads as texture first, text second. Text direction is one thing the code breaks: cat. 51 has nine pieces in three sizes and rotated copies of one phrase. Cat. 74 is the loudest: gold (15%) and dark olive panels (≈18%, blended with the grid lines at this resolution) holding black script, each with a red outline (≈6%), interlocking in T, L and Z shapes; the ground is nearly all letters. Cat. 75 is black letters on paper with a stepped red line one cell wide threading between.

**Cat. 4a, 61, 62, 114.** Cat. 4a shows only dots: a tiling of polygons with no lines, i.e. the construction stage by itself. Cat. 61 starts from one small dotted square whose four sides are prolonged like a pinwheel until each hits the frame; those hits fix the star centres, and the thick straps follow. Cat. 62 puts hair lines over a dotted skeleton of pentagons, an irregular star-like polygon and part-decagons, symmetric left to right. Cat. 114 is the one all-curve panel (six-petal rosettes, small stars in the gaps): no straight line except the frame; it is the last in the roll (the frame closes on the right) and carries a pinwheel in one rosette.

**Gunbad-i Kabud.** On a brown brick wall (hues from dark `#6A5638` to pale `#B9A68F`, one hue family, C 15–20) an upper net of round-fronted bricks projects from the plaster, strap about 60 px wide; between, a lower net of flat terracotta ribbons about 30 px wide, half the width. The eye takes the big net first because it casts a shadow; from the ground (full tower view) only the big net remains. The big net has only straight runs (the oblique view spoils angle measurement) and does not stop at the panel's edge: it crosses from a flat face to a rounded corner shaft without restarting, so the pattern wraps the tower. Large regions are damaged, and the lower net still reads. (My observation; a claim that the two nets are strictly nested is in the literature, not verified here.)

**Darb-i Imam north gate.** Measured on a vertical photo (963 × 1500 px; colour shifted to blue by the slide). A central ten-pointed star, 58% of the width, outlined by a pale beaded strip, filled with blue-and-white arabesque; around it six-sided raised plaques about 130 px and five-pointed stars about 150 px across; cut fragments of ten-pointed stars at the visible right-hand corners; background of small black pentagons on white, cell ≈20 px. Five directions 19, 53, 91, 129, 163. Cobalt/dark ground `#152242` (16% of the picture), mid blues `#334165`, `#546386` (36%), pale `#CFD4E3` (11%), almost no warm colour except a tan thread in the arabesque. The plate borders are the only thick lines, like cat. 49. The reflected quarter of cat. 49 gives the same scheme: ten-star centre, quarter stars in the corners, polygons and five-point stars between. I compared by eye; nobody's catalogue entry ties this panel to 49, and Commons' "1453" is not verified.

**Friday Mosque panel.** A near-square panel (about 1:1.03) reflected on both axes: a many-pointed star (about twelve) at centre, four half stars on the sides, quarter stars at corners, four octagons with kufic at one-quarter points. Pale strapwork with a thin dark edge, about 2.5–3% of the panel width, runs unbroken through the pieces, and the straps are the only thing with no ornament in them. Colours: cobalt `#2A2860` (12%), turquoise `#558395` (12%), ochre `#896D39` (10%), dark `#34312E` (11%), straw strap `#A4957D` (23%). The fill varies by piece: floral scrolls, not geometry.

**Persan 169.** One panel per page, about half of the page's width, letters at points, text running in the margin and turning 90° down the right edge of the panel. The scaffold is dashed, the final pattern solid: the same two-kind line grammar as the scroll, but with names instead of colours. Fol. 192r adds a small circle-and-arc figure under the text (apparently an auxiliary compass construction): the page carries its own tool.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Draw the least that can grow the whole.** 71 of 150 catalogue subjects in the scroll's list say "quarter repeat unit", 7 "half", 12 "repeat unit" or "linear repeat" (automatic count of the catalogue text, whose headings split lettered drawings; treat as ±3). In my ×4 reflections of cat. 49 and 53, star centres that are only fragments on paper became whole; borders along the edges that continue are left off (cat. 38 top and left; cat. 49 bottom and right).
- **Two sets of lines, one kept, one discarded.** Scored, uninked circles with 4–24 radii underlie 136 of 158 technique notes (text count); a small part of the roll also has a visible red- or black-dotted tiling, explicitly meant to be erased (catalogue on cat. 28, 59). Cat. 54 strips the dotted layer from 12+ segments to 5 and the pattern is still the same pattern.
- **Lines cross at the edges' middles.** In cat. 28, 50, 52, 53, 58, 61 two lines cross at the midpoint of each polygon edge, then continue until they meet another. One edge is allowed to break this: "trisected" by two pairs, giving a rhombus (cat. 53, 54, 48, 56).
- **Few directions per panel.** Measured: cat. 28, 34, 49, 53 and the Darb-i Imam gate use five directions 36° apart (±2–3°); cat. 38 uses six at 30°; cat. 43 uses two; the Friday Mosque panel is dominated by four at 45°. The set follows from the star family. Exception: panels that mix unlike stars (cat. 30: 16 and 13; cat. 42: 9 and 11; cat. 44: 5, 10 and 12) bend the polygons between, and use more. Muqarnas plans (cat. 5, 11) use many more directions: they are radial, not translational.
- **Panel shape follows the angles.** Of 96 main panels, 52 are square within about 6%, 27 narrower, 17 wider. Most other sizes cluster at 0.71–0.735 (5 panels) and 1.335–1.39 (6 panels), that is cot 54° = 0.727 and tan 54° = 1.376: the rectangle whose diagonal is at 36° or 54°. Necipoğlu states the 36° diagonal rule.
- **Scale jump, then step-back.** Dual-level works use one large figure per very roughly 15–25 small cells (cat. 34), widths of 6–21 cm against 3.1 cm modules (cat. 28), raised blank plates against a hairline net (cat. 49; net 60%, plates 31%, outline 10%), or a wide strap against half-width ribbon (Gunbad-i Kabud, 2:1). Large-figure corners fall on small-figure centres (cat. 28, 34, 49).
- **Weight is a layer code, not emphasis.** In cat. 49 one 2.5–3 mm stroke says "raised"; in cat. 53 the thick strap is "final", thin lines in 54 "alternative"; the 0.4 cm frame (cat. 37, 40–114) shows which roll a piece came from. Only a few panels have thick straps (50, 53, 57, 61).
- **Colour is a key to shape or tier.** Cat. 38 gives each tile shape one colour. Muqarnas: black, red, stipple and blank are four states of a unit, the coloured ones under ~10% of the area, the rest blank paper.
- **Blank carries weight.** Cat. 5 leaves a third of its panel as bare quadrant; cat. 49's blank plates are the raised part; star centres are always blank. Nothing is shaded; depth is never drawn.
- **Grid-cell writing.** Letters, knots and checker share one cell module (0.3–0.5 cm; stroke 1 cell, gap 1 cell in square kufic), with rotations of the same phrase (cat. 41, 51, 72, 75).
- **Error is corrected in place, not redrawn.** Whitened wrong cells (cat. 1, 51), missing lines, dots in the wrong place; the catalogue says such slips were harmless as long as trained artisans could read the scheme. Cromwell finds built two-level work pragmatic at the corners too.
- **The generative rule (as seen):** (1) choose one star family and its angle set; (2) make a rectangle whose diagonal matches; (3) score circles at corner and mid-edge centres, divide into equal radii; (4) let radii meet to make a polygon tiling in contact; (5) cross two lines at each polygon's edge midpoint and extend till they meet; (6) optionally repeat at a larger scale through small centres; (7) erase the scaffold; (8) code layers by colour, weight or stipple; (9) hand over only the piece that can be reflected.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

*Standing of the evidence: directions, panel proportions, area shares and line weights were measured on the plates. The scored circles and the tiling "meant to be erased" are the catalogue's report (Necipoğlu), seen here only in two overlays. The scroll carries no writing, so the order of steps 3–4 is a reconstruction of the makers' method, not a record of it.*

**The Topkapı Scroll masters.** *Fix a closed set of two to six directions and a format cut to them; over a hidden scaffold, run every line through the middles of cell edges until it meets another; thread one larger figure through the small centres; erase the scaffold, code layers by weight and colour, and ship the smallest piece that regrows the field.*
Process:
1. Close the direction set before anything else: 2 to 6 directions, equally spaced (measured sets: two; four 45° apart; five 36° apart; six 30° apart). Every edge, rule and connector in the view takes one of them within 2–3°. Prefer the set the content already has.
2. Cut the format to the set: all panels share one height, and each width is chosen so the panel's diagonal lies on a direction (a square for the 45° set; 0.73 or 1.38 for the 36° set). Close each panel with one frame line of a single weight, not with empty space; leave about 13% of the band's height empty above and below, and a gap of about 7% of the height between panels.
3. Lay a scaffold that will not ship: anchors at the panel's corners and mid-edges (not its centre), each divided into equal directions from the set; where their rays meet, fix a layout of cells in contact, no gutters. Draw the scaffold in its own line kind (dotted) while working.
4. Run every final line by one local rule: it enters and leaves a cell through the midpoint of an edge, where two lines may cross; it keeps one direction; it stops only at another line or at the frame. No line ends in open ground or changes weight along its run. One edge per unit may break the rule (cut in three, not in two), and it is the unit's only irregular event.
5. Leave the nodes blank. A place where lines converge gets no fill, outline or badge; the convergence marks it.
6. Add at most one larger scale, in the same direction set, locked to the first: its corners and turns fall on centres of the small layout, one large figure per roughly 15–25 small cells. Draw it either as the only long continuous line (segments 2–7 times the small module) or as blank plates with one heavy outline that interrupt the fine net completely (net about 60% of the field, plates 30%, outline 10%; plates in three size steps near 14 : 7 : 1; the channel between plates one constant width).
7. Erase the scaffold and check that the result holds without it. Then assign codes, each with one meaning for the whole product: line weight says which layer (a ladder of 1 : 6 : 10, the heaviest for the frame or a raised plate); hue says which kind or which tier; a dotted texture is one more state; blank is a state too. Nothing is shaded.
8. Put text on the scaffold's cell: letter stroke, the gap between strokes and every divider bar are one module wide; mixed contents share the same bars.
9. Hand over the least piece that regrows the whole (most often a quarter): focal places sit as quarters at its corners and halves on its edges, the middle is a place between them, and any edge where the field continues carries no border. Reflect it four ways before shipping: figures must close that the piece alone does not show. Correct a slip in place and leave the correction visible.
Why it works:
- Grouping by likeness: with a closed direction set any two lines are parallel or meet at one of a few angles, so the field fuses into one texture and a single stray angle would be the loudest thing in the view. Free the angles and every crossing asks for attention.
- Figure and ground: a node left blank and surrounded by arriving lines is found without spending any ink on it, and the field keeps no centre, so it reads as a window on something that continues. Fill one node and it becomes a stamp with a field behind it.
- Fusion of fine detail into tone: from far the small layer merges into an even grey and only the large figure reads; near, the small one opens. Because large corners sit on small centres the two read as one system at two distances; unlock them and they read as an overlay fighting its ground. Equalise the scales and neither is found first.
- Salience kept for meaning: when weight and hue are spent only as codes on a ground that is 80–94% bare, each difference is a fact about the content. A frameless edge is read as "more of the same follows", a closed frame as "this ends here".
Colour relations: ground nearly unsaturated and the largest share (80–94% of a line view); one dark neutral as the first ink (5–9% as line, a third or more where cells are filled); one mid-value saturated hue second, as a state or tier code (3–5%), or as the long line of the larger scale (under 1% of the area, read first because it is the only continuous one); coded fills together under 10%. Where the whole surface takes colour, one hue per kind (4–5 kinds, 6–19% each), blank plates keep 15–20%, and the lines or plates stay pale and plain. No colour for shading or mood.
Space and line: directions 2–6; panel proportion from the direction set; equal panel height; frame line, not whitespace, closes a panel; anchors at corners and mid-edges; line weights 1 : 6 : 10; thin line about 1/600 of the panel height, heavy outline about 1/90; large to small 15–25 cells or strap to ribbon 2 : 1; a blank quadrant of 19–32% is allowed where tiers fan from one corner toward the opposite one, the count at the corner carried into each tier.
Type requirement: monoline letters whose stroke and inner gaps are whole modules of the cell, even in colour so a block of text reads as one tone before it reads as words; dividers the width of the stroke; a featured word up to about 45% of its panel's width. Text stays upright and in reading order (the sources rotate and mirror theirs, which an interface cannot), and no word is used as texture.
Motion and interaction: the build order is the only animation: scaffold dots appear, lines extend from edge midpoints until they meet (300–500ms, linear, no overshoot), the scaffold fades and stays off. Zooming out drops the fine layer and keeps the large one; zooming in restores it. Selecting a line lights its whole run to both ends. New content arrives across a frameless edge as the next reflection of the unit. The scaffold may be shown on request as an inspection overlay, never as ornament. Reduced motion: the final state at once.
Fails when: one item must own the view (the field has no centre by construction); the content is long running text or cells of unrelated sizes that share no directions; the direction set is not the orthogonal one and the view is mostly text boxes; the fine layer falls under about 1px or 3:1 contrast on the device; mirrored units would reverse a reading order or a left-to-right sequence; a code is carried by hue alone without a label or a second cue.
Signature (do not carry together): interlaced star-and-polygon strapwork on five- or eight-fold angles · a red-dotted scaffold under black and orange ink on cream paper · words built of filled grid squares (sacred text is never decoration).

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Build-pipeline monitor, desktop 1440 × 900, near-black ground, off-white ink, one signal orange, monospaced and terse (the works are ruled ink on cream paper).
1. *Directions.* Four, 45° apart: 0, 45, 90, 135. Every rule, connector and edge takes one. No curve, no other angle.
2. *Format.* The 45° set gives squares. One band of four stage panels, each 320 × 320px, 22px apart (7% of the height), 42px of empty ground above and below the band (13%). Each panel is closed by a 1px frame line.
3. *Scaffold.* Anchors at each panel's four corners and four mid-edges, each with eight rays 45° apart; their meetings fix a 4 × 4 layout of 80px job cells in contact, no gutters. The scaffold exists as a dotted inspection overlay only.
4. *Local rule.* A dependency leaves its job cell through the midpoint of an edge and runs straight at 0, 45, 90 or 135° until it meets another connector or the panel frame, where the next stage's panel picks it up. None ends in open ground. One edge per panel is cut in three: the fan-out of the matrix build, the panel's only irregular event.
5. *Blank nodes.* Job cells have no box and no dot. A job is the blank place where its connectors arrive, with its name set there on bare ground.
6. *Larger scale.* One line per band: the critical path, turning only at cell centres, 3–6 cells per segment, the only continuous line across all four panels. One job per band may be pulled out as a blank plate (the failing job's summary) with a 6px outline whose corners sit on cell centres and which interrupts the connectors completely.
7. *Erase and code.* Overlay off. Weights: 1px connector, 6px plate outline, 10px frame of the pinned stage. States of a cell: blank = queued, dotted = running, solid off-white mark = passed, solid orange mark = failed, each with its word. Ground about 90%, off-white ink 7%, orange under 3% including the critical path (under 0.5%, and it also carries the label "critical").
8. *Text.* Monospace with a 10px advance, an eighth of the cell; rules between log strips are the letter's stroke width; stage names are 14 characters or fewer, under 45% of the panel.
9. *Least piece.* The unit is one stage panel. A job shared by two stages sits as a half on their common edge and becomes whole when both are on screen. Where more stages lie off-screen the outer frame line is left off; the true first and last panels are closed.
- *Motion.* On load the dotted scaffold shows for 200ms, connectors extend from the edge midpoints until they meet (400ms, linear), the scaffold fades. Below 50% zoom only the critical path and plates remain. Reduced motion draws the final state.

Result: a dark route diagram for builds, closer to a circuit board than to any ornament; no star, no tile, no interlace. What survived is the closed direction set, the format cut to it, a scaffold that is erased, lines that pass through edge middles and stop only on each other, a second scale locked to the centres of the first, weight and hue as codes, and frameless edges where the field goes on.

## Space & composition
- **Roll of framed lots.** All panels are the same height (24.5 cm in the thick-frame part, 25.5–25.7 in the thin-frame part), variable width, set side by side with gaps of 1.8 cm (cat. 53/54) and about 4–4.5 cm of empty paper above and below the frame (13% of roll height each). A frame line, not whitespace, closes each panel.
- **Corners and mid-edges carry the weight.** Stars are quarter at corners, half on sides; the middle of the panel is usually a place between stars, not a star (cat. 53, 42, 34), but the copied whole centres on a star (cat. 49 ×4; Friday Mosque panel).
- **No centre, no margin inside.** The line net runs edge to edge and is cut by the frame; the pattern is a window on an endless plane (Necipoğlu's reading too).
- **Two-plane layouts** (cat. 38, 49): plates occupy 20–40% of the panel, the net the rest; plates have three size steps, about 14:7:1 in cat. 49; borders between them carry the same width everywhere.
- **Mixed contents share the same bars.** In cat. 51 strips and boxes of different sizes are divided by bars of the script's width.

## Form, line & typography
- **Line.** Straight, ruled, uniform, ending only at another line or the frame. Three visual kinds: solid black, dotted, solid colour; plus the scored kind no one sees. Thin ≈0.4 mm, strap ≈2–2.5 mm, outline 2.5–3 mm, frame 4 mm: a 1:6:10 ladder. Curves exist only in cat. 114 and the round-ended fans of shells.
- **Shape.** Decagon, pentagon, bow-tie, elongated hexagon, rhombus for tens; octagon and square for eights; pieces are equal-sided (Wikipedia's girih tile set; cat. entries list the same five shapes).
- **Square kufic.** Letters made from grid cells, sometimes rotated in four directions about a centre (cat. 1, 41, 74); diagonal kufic at 45° (cat. 43); bannai strip in 45°, 90°, 135°. No modulated strokes, no baseline hierarchy; the legible phrase is only one of the rotations.
- **No running text.** The only words on the roll are in panels as pattern; labels and reading aids are absent. The page numbers (pencil) are later additions.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- **Beat of the net.** In cat. 28 and 31 the eye moves along one long line, turns at a star centre, and falls into the next, never to a focal point. At a distance the net is texture; nearer, an overlapping set of stars; nearest, crossings.
- **Repetition is a rule of copy, not a tile stamp.** Reflecting the quarter (cat. 49, 53) makes star centres and pieces that the quarter does not contain.
- **Reading through layers.** Cat. 28: dots, then black, then orange, three speeds of reading. The wall keeps only two or one.
- **Time in the roll.** The same panel idea appears as dotted tiling (4a), as dotted plus final (50–54), as final only (42), as colour (38): a sequence of states, though the roll is not in order (parts pasted together haphazardly).
- **Shift of scale.** Gunbad-i Kabud: net from afar, ribbon at arm's length. Muqarnas plans: a hollow ceiling unfolds from corner to wall by tiers.

## UI translations
*For strict studies and homage only; for design, use "Process (derived)". Sacred text and devotional images are never used as decoration, in a study or anywhere else.*

1. **Quarter unit.** Draw one quarter of a panel and produce the rest by reflecting it on both centre lines; quarter figures at the corners, halves on the edges, the middle a place between them.
2. **Five directions.** Rule every line at one of five directions 36° apart (or six at 30°), and nothing else, in a panel of 0.73 or 1.38 proportion.
3. **Dotted scaffold in a study view.** Show the construction tiling as a fine dotted line under the final lines in a "how it is built" view only; the shipped view has none.
4. **Midpoint crossings.** Cross two lines at the middle of every scaffold edge and extend each until it meets another; allow one edge cut in three.
5. **Three line kinds.** Solid dark for the final net, dotted for the scaffold, one solid colour for the large figure; weights 1 : 6 : 10 for hairline, strap and plate outline.
6. **Large through small.** Thread a long coloured line through the centres of the dark net, one large figure per 15–25 small cells.
7. **Plates over a net.** Float blank, heavy-outlined plates over a hairline net: net 60%, plates 30%, outline 10%, plate sizes 14 : 7 : 1, with one constant channel between them.
8. **Colour by shape.** In a fully coloured panel give each shape class one flat hue and keep the large plates blank; coloured fills in a line panel stay under 10% on a bare ground.
9. **Four flat states.** Blank, dotted, solid dark and solid colour as the states of a unit, each kept apart from its neighbour by a thin edge of ground.
10. **Roll of framed lots.** Panels of one height and varying width side by side, a frame line around each, 13% empty ground above and below, the frame left open on sides where the pattern continues.
11. **Cell-built labels.** Build short interface labels on the squared grid, stroke one cell and gap one cell, with bars of the same width dividing strips and boxes; upright, in reading order, and secular.
12. **Fan from a corner.** For a quarter view of a radial structure, spread tiers as arcs from one corner to the opposite one, carry the corner's count into every tier, and leave the unbuilt quadrant blank.

## What this artist would reject
- Star motifs placed as stamps; a star that is not the meeting of lines already running.
- A panel with its scaffold left as decoration (the dots are to be erased) or its colour used for mood only.
- Arbitrary frame proportions: a rectangle whose diagonal has nothing to do with the angles inside.
- Lines that stop in empty space, curve without rule, or change weight along their run.
- Shadow, perspective or gradient for depth, where one flat code (stipple, red, black) is enough.
- A central logo-like focus in a field meant to continue.

## Signature prompt seed
Choose a small closed set of directions and let every line in the view take one of them; give each panel one shared height and a width whose diagonal lies on a direction, and close it with a single frame line. Lay a hidden scaffold from the corners and mid-edges, then run each line through the middle of a cell edge until it meets another line or the frame. Leave the places where lines converge blank. Thread one larger figure through the centres of the small layout, as the only long line or as blank heavy-outlined plates. Remove the scaffold. Keep the ground nearly bare and use weight, one dark ink, one saturated hue and a dotted texture only as codes for layer and state. Set text on the same cell module. Build one unit, reflect it to make the field, and leave the frame open wherever the field continues.
