# Qavam al-Din Shirazi — Islamic Geometry

## Essence
Qavam al-Din (died 1438, chief builder to Shah Rukh and Gawhar Shad) took the old three-part stack of square room, corner transition and round dome and replaced it with one thing: wide arches that cross each other. The crossings draw a smaller square in the air, the next crossing turns it into an octagon or an eight-pointed star, and the number of sides doubles (4, 8, 16, 32) until it is a circle. The ribs are kept plain, banded and legible, and all the fine work is poured into the cells between them, so the same wall reads at 100 metres, at 10 metres and at arm's length.

## Key works examined
- **Gawhar Shad mausoleum, Herat: the inner vault (complex 1417-38, mausoleum about 1432), painted plaster over brick.** Four crossing arches make a square; four small arches make an octagon; 16 shells make a circle. The clearest statement of his process. *(visually checked: Wikimedia Commons photo by M. ## Detailed observations

**Herat, the inner vault.** Seen from below, four broad arches run across the room in two parallel pairs and cross near the corners, so their inner edges cut a near-square. In the frame (1280 x 853) the square measures about 505 x 460 px (distorted by the tilted camera). It is about 0.55-0.6 of the apparent full bay. The ribs are flat strips, about 0.13 of the square's side, with a fixed cross-section: thin row of red-brown dots, red line, pale strip of blue writing, red line, dots. Writing sits only on these four members. Inside the square, four short arches cut the corners and leave an octagon about 0.87 of the square's side. Inside the octagon the plaster breaks into small muqarnas, then a ring of red-brown fluted shells. Polar unwrap: 14 shells survive with a gap (later plain patch) worth two more, so 16; inside them 16 pale six-sided medallions with a red flower, and then a diamond ring. The medallion ring is about 0.55 of the square's side. The centre itself, about 0.29 of the side, is now bare brown plaster, a loss. Outside the square every cell between ribs holds a fan: on each of the four sides the order is white, blue, white, in widths about 0.53 : 1 : 0.5, and a further tall blue fan stands in each corner kite. So blue marks the eight axes and white fills between. Each cell has the same recipe (small stepped niches at the bottom, ribbed shell on top) at a different size. Colour today is nearly all earth: pale ivory strips `#BCA490` (L69 C15), ground `#977762` (L53 C19), red-brown about 3% (`#6D3F2C`, L32 C28). Blue is still clear to the eye but, flecked and faded, only 2-3% of a blue fan's pixels pass as blue (`#423953`, L27 C18). Lightness by ring is flat (L40, 47, 44, 55, 47 from centre out); the brightest ring is the rib square. The eye finds the bright square first, then the octagon, then the spinning ring. Deliberately absent: any shadowed gap or thick cornice between wall and dome, and any figure on the ribs besides writing.

**Herat, the exterior.** Three stacked masses with three different plans: a box, a cylinder, a bulb. Box width 833 px, height 473 px; drum plus collar width 519 px; dome greatest width 542 px. Dome to box width is 0.65. The dome overhangs its drum slightly (542 against 519, +4%) on a small stalactite collar. Total height to box width is about 1.29. The front is three arches: panel widths 175, 206, 176 px (0.85 : 1 : 0.85) with piers of 53 px, about a quarter of a panel. The central one is wider and broken into two openings; the side ones are blank. Dome ribs: about 98 px pitch at centre on a 1430 px dome crop, so about 44-48 around. Each rib is about 55% of its pitch and the groove the rest. Plain turquoise in the groove, lozenges of cobalt and white on the rib, all starting at a level band. Tile today survives only in that band and on the drum and the arch corners. In the band blue is 24% and turquoise 17%; on the box about 4% in all. The 1928 colour photo confirms the order: bare brick below, tile above. From a distance you read three shapes; nearer, the bands at their joints; nearer still the lozenges.

**Khargird, the hall seen straight up.** Dark heavy ribs outside make an eight-pointed star. Inside it a bed of muqarnas rises to a 16-sided sill. On the sill stands a low drum with 16 bays: 8 windows alternate with 8 blind niches of the same shape (windows counted open in the polar strip, with a clear 1-0-1-0 rhythm). Above, a muqarnas collar of 32 cells (spacing 56 px in an 1800 px unwrap), then a thin zigzag moulding and a plain spiral-looking brick cap. Apparent widths, with perspective shrinking the high parts: cap 460 px, collar 600, sill 920, star points about 1240; that is 0.50 : 0.65 : 1 : 1.35. Counts run 8, 16, 32, circle. Lightness by ring: cap L43, collar L57, drum L67 (up to L98 at the windows), outside the sill L24. So the brightest ring is the transition, not the dome and not the wall: a gap of 43 L between drum and the star ribs around it. The cap hangs as a dark disc inside a ring of light. Half of the muqarnas ring is new plaster, half old brown; the geometry carries across both. Absent: colour, painting on the cap, and any ornament at the centre point (a bare lamp).

**Khargird, the hall elevation.** The wall has one tall pointed arch; inside it a smaller arch; inside that a blank niche; below it a plain door. Out of the back of the big arch two thin ribs lift and bend in to cross the ribs of the neighbouring walls; their crossings form the star's points. The cells between the ribs are small and each has its own surface: a half shell over a muqarnas tier, a flat panel with an incised ten-sided star net, a net of raised dots with hexagon cells, a ribbed fan over five tiers of small arches. In the part visible no two touching cells share a fill, and the left half mirrors the right closely. Below the springing the wall goes quiet: flat panels, painted dado, two slim corner colonnettes. Lightness from the top: L76 in the lit lantern, then L43, then L36, 36, 35 all the way down. About 4/5 of the height sits within one value. Detail grows with height: none in the lowest 30%, tiers of niches in the middle, the finest plaster under the lantern.

**Khargird, the entrance front.** Total 918 px wide, portal 213 px (23%), corner towers about 95 px each. Portal height 305 against wing height 198 (1.54 : 1). Each wing has three arches, narrow, wide, narrow (about 44 : 82 : 54 px, roughly 0.6 : 1 : 0.6), the wide middle one set in a deep patterned frame, the narrow ones recessed and dark. Strips of cobalt and turquoise brick frame each bay and run round the wing as one continuous border. The portal in contrast has almost no tile on its face (about 5% blue-grey) and holds its tile inside the arch. Blue on the wings measures only about 3% (the photo is in strong low sun) while ground brick runs `#BA8D6D` (L63) to `#94643C` (L46). Two low domes rise symmetrically over the front halls, half hidden.

**Khargird, the court.** Every side has the same wall: iwan in the middle, two tiers of arched rooms, two bays each side plus a bevelled corner bay. Bay pitch 129-137 px (within 6% of each other); iwan opening 215 px, 1.6 bays; iwan frame 297 px, 2.2 bays. Iwan frame height 412 against wing 367 (1.12 : 1), far calmer than the portal's 1.54. Upper storey 166 px, lower 201 px including a 62 px plain plinth, so the two arches themselves are nearly equal. Each corner is cut at an angle, so the corner is a bay of the same kind and not a fold; the arcade turns without stopping. In each arch three patterns sit at three depths: the reveal (large lozenges), the back wall (small lozenges), a small turquoise window grille. Tall thin turquoise lines, one brick wide, trace every arch and every pier. Plain plaster fills the spandrels, and the tile mosaic once on the piers survives in scraps. Floor: herringbone brick.

**Khargird, the wall panels.** The brick course is the pixel. Turquoise headers three abreast draw one unbroken diagonal net; cobalt headers inside each lozenge draw a cross of four small rings. The panel is exactly two lozenges wide, so its edges cut lozenges in halves, never in odd slices. Continuous line is always turquoise, separate figure always cobalt. In the panel, colour is about 8% turquoise and less than 1% passing as blue in this photo, with blue-black bricks counted by eye near 10%; the rest is bare brick `#96949B` (a night photo, greyed). In the narrow border strips the ratio turns over: cobalt 28%, turquoise 9%. The strip alternates a small turquoise diamond with a big cobalt X, pitch about 1.6 times its width, and does not run off the bottom: it ends in a little outlined blind arch, the building's own arch at 1/20 size. The glaze catches light where the brick does not; in raking light parts of the cobalt turn white, so the pattern changes as you walk.

**Khargird, the star tile.** A 40 cm 12-pointed star; outline 12, inner interlace two six-pointed stars, one white and one turquoise; leaf palmettes grow from the white star's points; gold flowers in the lobes. Cobalt field `#333664` about 24% of the crop (L26 C29), turquoise edge 11% (`#6098A9`, L60 C20), white line about 12% (`#D8D5C8`, L84), red-brown under 2% (`#7C443A`), gold less. The tile keeps the building's grammar (turquoise bounds, cobalt fills, white draws). It is the only one of his scales where curved plant forms take over from straight line.

**Mashhad, the old photograph.** One iwan beside a two-storey arcade. The frame is built as a vertical stack: a square with a turning-knot star, an arched cartouche, a chequer panel, an arched star panel, a square of Kufic, another arched panel; six panels, no two alike, all the same width, each in a brick guilloche border. The same stack, in the same order, appears on the modern outer portal, and I read the modern surface as a careful repeat. Above, one band of cursive writing fills the width between the two knot squares. Every zone of the frame is patterned, but all at one value in the faded print, so the iwan is one pale sheet with one very dark arch.

**Mashhad, the main iwan.** Frame 381 px wide between minarets, 462 px high; courtyard arcades 210 px, so the iwan stands 2.2 times as tall. Minarets rise from the pavement, 65-80 px wide and 672 px tall (about 1 : 9), and the pattern on them shrinks toward the base. Nested arches, outer to inner, 920 : 680 : 580 : 475 px in the crop, that is 1 : 0.74 : 0.63 : 0.52, each carrying a different surface (tile star net, cursive writing, small Kufic, dark mihrab). Writing appears at three sizes in the frame: the great band about 5% of the width, the gold-on-violet upper band, small Kufic medallions; and as Kufic 'allah' around the minaret bases. In this crop blue is about 18%, turquoise 8%, white and stone near 30%, the void of the arch near 28%. Turquoise appears as 1-2% wide lines that separate every zone. Dome shows only as a small turquoise segment behind the straight top edge.

**Mashhad, the dome seen from below.** The dome is a clean circle held between four great arches; the area between is a net of thin dark ribs: straight-looking lines that cross at shallow angles, in four corner groups, each corner about five lines. Where they cross they leave a small inset diamond. The ribs are drawn in one thickness, about 1% of the dome's width, on white. Dome surface: 8 medallions, 8 bracket-like wedges and 16 small dashes, all multiples of 8, on a mostly empty warm field. The sides, in white, hold arches at three sizes in a row, with writing bars between (dark blue ground, white script).

**Mashhad, the outer portal.** A wall-size net of dark double lines makes crosses, hexagons and lozenges. Inside the cells stands Kufic in turquoise brick, stepped so that every stroke runs along the 45-degree grid. Four writing sizes are visible in one frame: brick Kufic (about 1/3 of the arch's width per word group), a cursive band, a small square of gold Kufic, and a cursive plaque over the door. From far it is a lattice; from mid distance a texture of meanders; close up it is readable words. Three ornament densities sit side by side: brick net (low), tile star panels (mid), leafy spandrel (high).

**Gazurgah, the courtyard.** Long blank-brick walls with deep arches; tile is kept to a strip around each arch and to the arch's inside. Each arch back wall shows the same brick Kufic at building scale: bands in turquoise and dark, at 45 degrees, filling the whole arch with words or zigzag. The reveal's pattern is different from the back's and the strip's is different again. Tile strip outside: a net of six- and eight-pointed stars in white, blue and violet, dense; spandrels in deep blue floral mosaic, now in scraps. The main iwan is about 30 m (Iranica) and stands before the tomb as a screen: there is no dome behind it. Court and rooms: only the west half has rooms behind the facade. Iranica describes two halls of five bays divided by transverse arches with three-part transverse vaults and plaster muqarnas.

**Gazurgah, the blue dado.** On stone slabs about a metre high, one thin line of blue glaze, about 9% of the area (`#1B1844`, L14 C29), draws three horizontal bands of ten-sided star figures with pentagon and kite cells between. Big cells hold blank stone (one later inscription). The line has constant width, about 1/25 of the pitch between big cells (10 px against about 250); joints between stone pieces are hidden under the line. It repeats the interior's grammar (line and cell) with no colour fill at all. Stone `#A07F5F` (L55 C24) and `#B49677` (L65 C19) together about 60%.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Cross, then shrink (Herat vault, Khargird hall, Mashhad vault).** Arches are laid across the bay and their crossings define the next, smaller figure. Herat: arm square about 0.55-0.6 of the bay, octagon 0.87 of the square, circle 0.55 of the square. Khargird: star, sill, collar, cap at 1.35 : 1 : 0.65 : 0.50. The transition is the drawing, not a layer between two drawings.
- **Doubling ladder (Herat 4, 8, 16, 16; Khargird 8, 16, 32, circle; Mashhad 8 and 16).** Every level's count is twice the last or equal to it, never an unrelated number; the circle arrives only when the step has got too small to see.
- **Ribs constant, cells varied (Herat, Khargird, Mashhad).** Members hold one width and one section (about 0.13 of the square's side in Herat, about 1% of the dome in Mashhad) and carry only writing or nothing. Cells carry all variety: shell, fan, muqarnas, star net, dots. Neighbours in a run differ; mirror pairs match.
- **Same cell recipe at many sizes (Herat fans, Khargird niches).** Small niches below, ribbed shell above; repeated from wall-arch size to thumbnail. Herat: blue-white fans run white, blue, white at 0.53 : 1 : 0.5 on all four sides.
- **Narrow, wide, narrow (Herat front 0.85 : 1 : 0.85; Khargird wings about 0.6 : 1 : 0.6; Herat fans 0.53 : 1 : 0.5).** The middle is wider and more open, the flanks blank or recessed.
- **Light put in the joint (Khargird drum L67 and up to L98, between L43 cap and L24 star ribs; Herat's brightest ring is the rib square L55).** The eye goes to the seam between geometry and geometry.
- **Plain body, rich joint (Herat exterior about 4% tile on the box against 41% in the dome band; Gazurgah blank walls with tile only round the arches; Khargird portal face about 5%).** Colour marks where one shape turns into another.
- **Two roles for two blues (Khargird panels, star tile, Mashhad iwan, Gazurgah).** Turquoise makes continuous boundaries and nets; cobalt fills bounded figures. The ratio flips with size: large panels about 8% turquoise and nearly all ground; narrow strips 28% cobalt, 9% turquoise; the 40 cm tile 24% cobalt, 11% turquoise. The share of colour climbs as the element gets smaller and closer.
- **Four reading distances, each with its own content.** Far: arch, drum, dome. Mid: lozenge or Kufic lattice in brick (visible from the court). Near: star mosaic and bands. Hand: leaf, gold and white line (star tile). No distance is left empty and none repeats another.
- **Writing is a structural member (Herat ribs, Mashhad frame bands, Mashhad portal Kufic, Gazurgah arches).** It runs along ribs, frame bands and lattice and is sized by them; at least three sizes per frame, the largest built from brick ends on a 45-degree grid.
- **Nested arches with a ratio about 3/4 to 1/2 (Mashhad 1 : 0.74 : 0.63 : 0.52).** Each nested arch changes its surface as well as its size; depth is read from material, not shadow only.
- **Corners are bays (Khargird bevelled corners).** A bay of the same kind cuts each corner, so rhythm continues; the pitch varies at most about 6%.
- **Pattern ends on purpose (Khargird strips end in a small arch; panel width is exactly two lozenges; lattice cut in half at edges).** Modules are cut at their axes or closed with a figure.
- **Symmetric plan, unequal emphasis (Khargird: portal 1.54 times wing, court iwan 1.12; Mashhad main iwan 2.2 times the arcade).** Everything is mirrored; hierarchy comes from height and depth, not from moving things off the axis.
- **The surface changes with light (Khargird glazed headers flash white, matte brick does not).** One pattern, two states, set by the angle.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence. No drawing or text by Qavam al-Din was seen: the order of the steps is read back from the finished buildings, and "spanning members first" is a reconstruction, not his documented method. Khargird was finished by Ghiyath al-Din Shirazi; Mashhad's dome chamber was rebuilt in the 1960s and its tile is largely renewed, so only geometry is taken from it. Figures are readings of photographs, several of them oblique.*

**Qavam al-Din Shirazi.** *Join two unlike frames with the join itself: lay a few wide plain members across the whole field so that their crossings draw the next, smaller figure; double the count at each level until the steps are too small to see; keep members one width and carrying only words, put all variety in the cells between, and spend light and colour at the seams.*
Process:
1. Name the two frames the view must join: the outer one the format gives (few parts, often four) and the inner one the content needs (many parts, or continuous). Make the passage between them the most worked band of the view and the widest of the three zones on any axis (surround, passage, centre; two thirds of the radius in one measured view), and give it no separate connector piece.
2. Draw that passage with spanning members: lay 4 wide members across the whole field in two parallel pairs so that they cross near the corners. Their inner edges bound the next figure at about 0.55–0.6 of the field. Each further figure is cut from the one before by members between its corners, at 0.5–0.9 of it (measured chains: 1 : 0.87 : 0.55 and 1.35 : 1 : 0.65 : 0.50).
3. Count by doubling: every level has twice the parts of the last or the same number (4, 8, 16, 32), never an unrelated one, so each division of a level lands on a division of the next. Go continuous only when one more doubling would make steps too small to tell apart. Slots of a ring may alternate two states in one shape (8 open, 8 closed).
4. Give the members one width and one cross-section per system: about 0.13 of the inner figure's side for the bearing set, about 1% of the field for a fine net, a net line about 1/25 of its own pitch. A member carries a line of words or nothing: no picture, no fill pattern, no second width.
5. Put all variety in the cells between members. One recipe (a plain stepped base, a finer top) is repeated at every size; two cells that touch never share a fill; cells mirrored across the axis match. Detail rises toward the focus: none in the outer 30%, the finest next to the centre, and the centre point itself stays bare.
6. Set every row as narrow, wide, narrow (flanks 0.5–0.85 of the middle, separators a quarter of a part) at view, panel and cell scale, with the middle open and the flanks closed. Hold one pitch within 6% and turn each corner with a cell of the same kind. Mirror everything on the axis and rank only by height and depth (the main part 1.1–2.2 times its neighbours), never by moving it off the axis.
7. Frame an opening 3–5 times, each frame about 3/4 of the last (1 : 0.74 : 0.63 : 0.52), and change the surface at every step so depth is read from the change, not from shadow.
8. Spend value and colour at the seams. The bodies stay plain (5% colour or less; a large panel about 90% bare ground) and the band where one figure turns into another takes it (about 40%). The band furthest in value from the ground is the transition, 10–40 L from both the centre and the surround. Two colour roles only: one hue for continuous boundaries and nets (lines 1–2% of the width), one darker hue for bounded fills. Colour's share climbs as the part gets smaller: about 10% on a large panel, about 35% on a narrow strip or a hand-size piece.
9. End every pattern on purpose: a panel is a whole number of modules wide (2), a module is cut only on its own axis (halves, never odd slices), and a strip closes with a small outline of the view's main frame (about 1/20 size) and does not run off the edge.
10. Check four reading distances: whole shapes, a coarse net, bands and fine nets, the hand-size detail. Each must show something the others do not, and none may be empty.
Why it works:
- Grouping by continuation: when the members that span the field also draw the smaller figure, the eye is led from frame to centre along one line and the join needs no explaining. Hide the join, or hand it to a third element, and two shapes simply collide.
- Grouping by alignment, then fusion of fine detail into tone: with doubled counts every second division of a ring meets one of the ring outside it, so the levels read as one thing dividing. Put 5 over 8 over 12 and the misfits read as errors; at the last doubling the steps fuse into a curve, which is why the continuous form can arrive without a break.
- Grouping by likeness: members of one width fuse into a single net however many there are, and everything inside the net is then read as content. Reverse the roles (patterned members, plain cells) and the structure breaks into pieces while the content has nothing to say.
- Salience by value and scarcity: on bodies left nine tenths bare, the light and colour at a seam are the odd thing, so attention lands on the place that explains how the parts relate. Colour spread evenly marks nothing.
- Scale reference: raising colour's share as parts shrink keeps a small part visible and a large one quiet, so each distance has its own event and the whole keeps one tone.
Colour relations: ground one low-chroma middle value and the largest share (60% and more, about 90% of a large panel); a *line* hue, middle value, only as continuous boundaries and nets (8–17%); a *fill* hue, darker and the most saturated, only inside bounded cells (under 1% on large panels, 24–28% on strips and small pieces); one light neutral that draws inside the fills (about 12% at the smallest scale); one further accent under 3%; the richest material only at hand size. The line hue is always lighter than the fill hue.
Space and line: figures inscribed in figures at 0.5–0.9 per step; counts 4, 8, 16, 32; transition band wider than the surround and the centre (2/3 of the radius in one view); bearing member 0.13 of the inner side, net line 1/25 of its pitch, one weight per system; rows 0.5–0.85 : 1 : 0.5–0.85 with separators 1/4 of a part; nested frames about 3/4 each; pitch within 6%; corners as cells; strips at a pitch 1.6 times their width; mirror symmetry with unequal height.
Type requirement: text lives on members and frames, in single-line bands sized by the member that carries them (about 5% of the frame's width for the main band, about half the member's width on a bearing member). At least three sizes per view, each tied to one level of the structure and none between. Letters with strong, evenly spaced uprights, so a band reads as an even texture before it reads as words; the largest size may be built on the module, stroke one module wide. Upright, in reading order; no text inside a cell's fill.
Motion and interaction: build in the order of the steps: members first, then each level by doubling, the count rising as the parts shrink, until the last ring is still (400–600ms per level, no overshoot). Zoom is semantic: each of the four distances swaps in its own content, it does not scale the previous one. A live state is a lighter value travelling round the slots of the transition ring. Line and fill elements have a second value state when the pointer or the scroll passes; the ground never changes. Reduced motion: the final state at once.
Fails when: the view has nothing to join (one long text, a plain list, a single hero with no surround); the data fixes counts that do not double (7 days, 12 months, 5 plans); the content has a direction that mirroring would reverse (a conversation, a left-to-right timeline); the screen is too small to hold four levels, so the finest falls under about 1px or 3:1 contrast; cells must hold running text; state is carried by the two hues alone, without a label or a shape.
Signature (do not carry together): crossing arched ribs that climb to a dome · two blues on bare brick, the lighter as line and the darker as fill · pointed frames nested in frames with bands of script (sacred text is never decoration).

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Run view of a build-and-deploy service, desktop 1440 × 900, dark graphite with a lime and a violet given by the brand, one monospaced face, terse and technical (the works are warm, slow and built by hand).
1. *Two frames.* Outer: the page and the run's 4 stages. Inner: the live log, a continuous stream. The passage from 4 stages to one stream (the bars, then jobs, shards and test groups) is the widest zone on both axes: 256px across against 234px of surround and 230px of half-log, and 186px down against 121px and 144px.
2. *Spanning members.* Two horizontal and two vertical bars run edge to edge and cross near the corners. Their inner edges bound a centre pane of 835 × 522px (0.58 of the page each way). A second frame inside it at 0.87 (726 × 454px), a third at 0.55 (459 × 287px) for the log.
3. *Doubling.* 4 stages on the four sides, 8 jobs (2 per side) on the second frame, 16 shards, then 32 test groups as ticks (8 per side); the log is continuous. A stage with 3 jobs shows a fourth slot of the same shape, closed.
4. *Members.* All four bars are 68px (0.13 of 522) with one section: hairline, a 32px band of text, hairline. They carry the stage name and duration and nothing else. The frame lines inside the pane are 1px, one weight.
5. *Cells.* Each side region holds a stage summary: a plain stepped base (the last five durations as steps) under a finer top (the current step list). Touching cells differ in fill type (steps, list, dot matrix, bare); left mirrors right. The outer 30% of the page has no detail finer than a label, and the log's centre holds no badge.
6. *Rows.* Each side region is split 218 : 411 : 206px (0.53 : 1 : 0.5): the middle holds the current run, the flanks the previous and the queued run, dimmed. The four corner regions are cells of the same kind and hold the hand-off between neighbouring stages. The deploy side is 1.5 times the depth of the other three; nothing leaves the axis.
7. *Nested frames.* The log is reached through four frames at 1 : 0.74 : 0.63 : 0.52, each a different surface: bars with text, job slots, shard matrix, bare log.
8. *Seams.* Page ground L 18; bars L 30; the job and shard ring L 55, the lightest band, 33 L above the log (L 22). Lime only as 1–2px boundaries of every zone and the tick net; violet only as the fill of a shard slot that needs attention, with a glyph and a word. Side regions carry under 5% colour, the shard ring about 35%.
9. *Ends.* 8 ticks per side, whole; the history strip in each flank is 2 modules wide and closes with a 42 × 26px outline of the centre pane (1/20).
10. *Distances.* Four zoom levels: stages; jobs; shards; one test's lines. Each swaps in content the level above does not show.
- *Motion.* On load the bars draw first, then 8, 16 and 32 appear in turn, 500ms each. The running shard is a lighter value moving round its ring.

Result: a dark hash-shaped pipeline board in a monospaced face, with no arch, vault or glazed surface, that nobody would caption with a builder's name. What survived is the join drawn by the spanning members, counts that double, plain members around varied cells, and light and colour kept for the seams.

## Space & composition
- Bay, crossing, star, ring: space is made as a chain of inscribed figures, each rotated 45 degrees or doubled. Square to octagon to 16 to 32 to circle.
- The transition is the widest and the most worked band, not a seam. Khargird's star and drum together take about 2/3 of the apparent radius.
- Three to five nested frames per opening, widths falling about 1 : 0.74 : 0.63 : 0.52.
- Bays in the a-B-a rhythm (0.6 : 1 : 0.6 to 0.85 : 1 : 0.85), repeated at facade, wall and cell scale; piers about a quarter of a panel.
- Stacked masses in different plan shapes (box, cylinder, bulb), dome 0.65 of the box width, with an overhang of about 4%.
- Rows, columns and corners kept to one pitch, bevelled corner bays.
- Empty ground is deliberate and big: about 90% plain brick in the Khargird panels, blank slabs in the Gazurgah dado, an undecorated lamp point at Khargird.
- Hidden grid: star nets and Kufic on a square lattice turned 45 degrees; brick course is the smallest unit.

## Form, line & typography
- Lines have one weight per system: structural ribs wide and plain, net lines thin and equal, arch outline one header of turquoise.
- All arches are four-centred or pointed, same profile from iwan to a 1/20 arch in a strip.
- Three families of fill, kept apart: straight-line star nets and lozenges in brick and on the dado; stepped niches; curved leaf scrolls in mosaic spandrels, tile and the hand scale only.
- Writing comes in two voices. Square Kufic built of brick ends at 45 degrees fills wall-size fields and reads as texture first; thick-thin cursive (thuluth) runs in long single-line bands with tall rhythmic uprights, white on blue, and sits on ribs and frames. Small Kufic squares act as punctuation.
- Bands are proportioned to the member that carries them, 5% of frame width at Mashhad, 0.13 of the square side at Herat.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- Approach is a sequence of reveals: mass, then lattice, then script, then leaf. The same wall gives new information at each distance.
- Looking up turns the room into a slow spiral of doublings; the count increases while each element shrinks (4 ribs, 8 arcs, 16 shells, 32 cells) until it is still.
- Light is a ring in the joint and moves around the 16 bays through the day (8 windows open, 8 blind).
- Glazed brick flashes, then goes dull as you pass; the pattern answers the walker.
- Rhythm in plan: even pitch with a wider and deeper beat at each axis (bay pitch 1, iwan opening 1.6).
- Time is in the repairs: half the Khargird ring is new plaster and the order still reads, because the geometry is independent of finish.

## UI translations
*For strict studies and homage only; for design, use "Process (derived)". Sacred text and devotional images are never used as decoration, in a study or anywhere else.*

1. **Hash of four bars.** Cross two pairs of wide bars over the whole view; the square they enclose, about 0.55–0.6 of the field, is the main pane.
2. **Square, octagon, sixteen.** Cut the pane's corners to an eight-sided figure at 0.87 of its side, then a ring of 16 units at 0.55, with a bare centre.
3. **Eight, sixteen, thirty-two.** For a view seen head-on, rings of 8, 16 and 32 units at 1.35 : 1 : 0.65 : 0.50, the 16-ring alternating open and closed slots.
4. **Light in the middle ring.** Make the transition ring the lightest band, about 20–40 L above the centre and the surround.
5. **Banded bar.** Give every bar the same section: dots, line, a pale strip of text, line, dots, the bar about 0.13 of the pane's side.
6. **No two neighbours alike.** Fill each cell between bars with a different texture (stepped tiers, fan, dot net, line net), mirrored left to right.
7. **Narrow, wide, narrow.** Three-part rows at 0.6 : 1 : 0.6 or 0.85 : 1 : 0.85, the middle open and framed, the flanks recessed and darker, piers a quarter of a panel.
8. **Four nested frames.** Frame the main opening four times at 1 : 0.74 : 0.63 : 0.52, each with its own surface, the innermost dark.
9. **Line hue and fill hue.** A lighter hue for every continuous boundary, one unit wide; a darker hue only inside bounded figures; a light neutral for drawing within them.
10. **Plain body, coloured band.** Keep large surfaces about 4–5% colour and put about 40% in the band where two shapes meet.
11. **Share by size.** About 8–10% colour on a large panel, 35–40% in a narrow strip, with the fill hue leading in the strip.
12. **Strip with an end.** Alternate a small and a large figure at a pitch 1.6 times the strip's width and close the strip with a small outline of the main frame, 1/20 size.
13. **Whole modules.** Make a panel exactly two modules wide and cut the net at its axes on every edge.
14. **Corner bay.** Bevel each corner into a bay of the same kind so the row turns without a break, pitch within 6%.
15. **Three sizes of secular text.** One band about 5% of the frame's width, one smaller band, one small square label, each on a frame or bar; the largest may be built on a 45° module grid.
16. **One line over blank slabs.** For a quiet surface, a single line of constant width (1/25 of the pitch, about 9% of the area) draws the net and the cells stay empty.

## What this artist would reject
- A dome on a box with a hidden or heavy corner fix in between; the join must be the event.
- Decorated ribs and plain panels (the roles reversed), or ribs of many widths.
- The same fill in every cell, or random fill without mirror.
- Counts that do not double: 5 over 8 over 12.
- Full-strength tile on every surface; colour where nothing meets.
- Ornament cropped at an odd place, or a strip that just runs off the edge.
- One scale of detail only, either all far or all near.
- A corner that breaks the rhythm.

## Signature prompt seed
Join the page's few outer parts to its continuous centre with the join itself: cross four wide, plain bars over the whole view and let their inner edges enclose the main pane, then step inward through rings of 8, 16 and 32 units until the centre is one unbroken surface. Keep every bar one width, carrying a single line of text and nothing else, and give each cell between them its own fill, mirrored left to right. Set rows narrow, wide, narrow, turn corners with a cell of the same kind, and frame the main opening four times, each frame three quarters of the last. Leave large surfaces nearly bare and put the lightest value and most of the colour in the transition ring; use one hue for continuous lines, a darker one for bounded fills. End every strip with a small closing figure. No sacred text, anywhere.
