# Ibn al-Bawwab — Islamic Geometry

## Essence
Ali ibn Hilal, called Ibn al-Bawwab (d. Baghdad 1022), made one pen width, one line interval and one column do all the structural work of a book. The reading text carries no frame, no ruling and no colour: order comes from the constancy of the letter module (alif about 8 dots) and of the 15-row page. Everything that is not reading (counting verses, naming chapters, enumerating letters) moves to its own zone and its own material, and is aligned to the line it refers to; geometry proper, frames and colour are kept for the opening and closing pages.

## Key works examined
All of the first eleven belong to one book: **the Qur'an of Ibn al-Bawwab, Chester Beatty Is 1431, Baghdad, AH 391 / 1000–1001, ink, colours and gold on paper.** The library gives 282 folios and 183 × 145 × 58 mm for the volume; the 2016 Edinburgh plates volume gives leaves of 17.5 × 13.5 cm. The leaves have been trimmed (marginal ornaments are cut), so outer margins were larger. The library calls the script naskh or rayhan.

## Detailed observations

**Text page, f.33r.** A portrait leaf of about 1465 × 1947 viewer px, ratio 1.33. The text block is about 1000 × 1500 in the same units: 68% of page width, 77% of page height, so about 53% of the page, and close to 2:3. Margins as shares of page: head 11%, foot 12%, gutter 10%, fore-edge 21.5% (so fore-edge about 2.1 × gutter, even after trimming). There is no frame, rule or bounding line. Fifteen lines; after correcting the photo's 2° tilt the mean interval is 104.3 px with a standard deviation of 3.5 px (3.4%). No ruling is visible at this resolution. The right edge, where lines begin, is straight; the left edge ends within a band of roughly 6–10% of the measure, with final bowls of nun and ya swinging past it. The ink is warm dark brown, `#422A1F` in solid strokes (L 20, C 15), on paper now `#AB8052` (L 57, C 33): a 37 L step with the same warm hue. Consonants and vowels are in the same brown; only tiny circles in red and blue-grey, and verse marks of three small blue-grey dots, differ. One gold medallion sits in the outer margin. Gold, counted geometrically, is about 1.5% of the page (medallion 1.2–1.5%, in-line roundel and drop about 0.25%). Blue is below 0.02%: seven triangles the size of a letter dot.

**The letter.** On f.200r, fifty free-standing uprights (alif and similar) have a median height of 163 px and an interquartile range of 154–169; single dots have a median side of 20.4 px. That is 8.0 dots per alif. On f.33r: 148 px against 19.6 px, 7.6 dots. Stem width is about 11.5 px (f.200r), so height to stem is about 13.7:1; the pen's typical stroke is 10–16 px, thickening in horizontals and bowls. Height varies by 12% (coefficient of variation, including some misread uprights). Alif is about 0.66 of the line interval (163 of 247 px at native size), and about 6 mm on the page if the leaf is 175 mm. Stems are upright to within 1–4° of the line normal, with a very small barb at the head and no foot. Counters of waw, fa and mim stay open; teeth are low; descending bowls are broad shallow arcs, at times two to four dots deep, that reach under the following word and into the next line's air. Vowel strokes run at a steady slope of about 25–30° and are thick enough to make a second, lighter row above and below each line. So each interval is filled: baseline script, then marks, then descenders, touching but seldom colliding. Ink covers 15–20% of the text block (thresholded; show-through inflates it slightly).

**Text page, f.200r.** Same scheme: text 986 × 1507 on a 1450 × 1890 leaf (68% × 80%, 54% of the area), margins head 10%, foot 10%, gutter 9%, fore-edge 24%. Interval 101.8 ± 4.9 px. Line 3 has no room for its last letter: the remaining ba is written alone in the outer margin as a long horizontal of about a fifth of the measure, and no line is squeezed to hide it. A gold-on-black roundel after a verse end carries the letter-numeral for ten; its ring is about 200 px, 1.2 alif or 0.8 line interval. Level with it, in the centre of the fore-edge margin, a medallion spells "ten" in gold Kufic letters. A red circle touches the end of the line nearby.

**The marginal medallion (f.33r, f.200r).** Measured at native size on f.33r: central disc 300 px, gold ring to 529 px, thin ring at 585 px, rays to about 695 px. In line intervals (253 px): 1.2, 2.1, 2.3, 2.75. The outer ring carries 14 small triangles, alternately seven blue and seven brown, at steady intervals of 49–53° for each colour (7 × 51.4° = 360°). f.200r repeats the seven-and-seven, with intervals of 45–60°, so the division is set by eye. Its white-dotted red-brown disc holds the number in Kufic. Every ray ends in a hair of brown ink: gold, then a drawn line, then hair, then bare paper. The edge fades outward in four steps. f.10r's medallions, at the start of the book, are richer (petals, scales, a blue disc) than those from f.33r on: ornament decays as reading gets under way.

**More text pages.** ff.10r, 60v, 100r, 138v, 153r all carry 15 lines; mean intervals 104.0, 100.6, 102.4, 100.4, 100.0 px, SD 3.1–5.2%. f.60v is the steadiest (3.1%). The figure is about the same on first and last pages, so the interval is a habit of hand. f.138v has a tall framed box (four short gold lines with a triangular tab) and three medallions stacked in the fore-edge column, so the margin can be half full while the text block does not change. Catchwords at the foot of versos are small, oblique and grey-brown.

**Opening of text, f.9v.** A column about 1090 px wide. It is divided into: a small gold-lettered box (570 wide, 52% of the column) sitting on top of a full-width heading panel; four lines of text; a second heading panel; five lines. The panels are 280 and 300 px high, close to 3 line intervals of 98 px, and their width equals the text measure. The first panel has letters reserved in paper colour on a near-black ground (`#3D2F22`, L 21) with white-dotted scrolls; the second has outlined letters on a gold-flecked brown ground. Frame from outside in: thin blue line, gold guilloche of slanted strokes, dotted band, field. The basmala beneath each panel stretches one stroke (the sin to the mim) over about 64% of the line width, so that a four-word phrase fills the measure. Margin ornaments are level with what they name: palmette centres match panel mid-heights to within about 1% of page height; the medallion next to the fifth verse is level with its in-line drop. Palmettes and medallions are cut by the fore-edge. The two outer-margin sizes (palmette 380 px high, medallion 300 px) are about 3.9 and 3 line intervals.

**Chapter heads inside the book, ff.278v–279r and f.250r.** No panels. The heading is in gold letters outlined with a fine dark line, roughly twice the height of the text, on bare paper. Line positions show it occupies two rows of the 15: on f.278v the last line of one chapter and the basmala of the next are 3.0 intervals apart (245 px at 81 px); (last − first) / interval = 14.1, so the page still totals 15 rows. f.250r: 14.0. Every basmala again has its long stroke (about 61% of the line on f.278v). Each heading throws out one palmette into the margin (gold, red-brown, blue) joined to it by a thin stem, and under it the place of revelation in small gold script. Blue on this page is 0.3% of area. The left column of f.279r leaves the last line of a chapter short, at 34–65% of the measure, with nothing set to fill it.

**Counting cartouches, f.6v.** Seven round-ended cartouches stacked in one frame, each about 860 × 170 (5:1), row pitch 183 px. Grounds read paper, blue, paper, black, paper, blue, paper: a palindrome, ABACABA, with the only black in the exact middle. Letters on colour are reserved in paper with gold outline; letters on paper are gold with outline. Between the cartouches run dotted bands; the triangular leftovers take small palmettes. Text gives the totals: 114 chapters, 6,236 verses, then words (the last line breaks off and goes on to f.7r). So the first decorated thing in the book is an inventory. Frame 1005 × 1400, ratio 1.39. Blue is 4.5% of the panel (2.7% at full strength), the highest of any page here. A gold and blue palmette stands out of the middle of the fore-edge side, level with the black cartouche (centre of the frame).

**Octagon pages, ff.7v–8r.** Frame 1048 × 1472 (1.40) on f.8r. Inside is a field of about 907 × 1335 that is 2 × 3 squares of about 450 (centres at 455 px across and 445 px down). Each square holds a large octagon built of two interlaced straps; the nodes between four large octagons hold small octagons of about 0.55 the size, whole at the two inner nodes, halved at the sides, quartered at the corners: the pattern is cut by its frame without adjustment. The straps run at about 22.5° to the axes. Spaces left between octagons become two shapes, a vertical and a horizontal cartouche, with a four-petalled flower. Blue appears only in the small node octagons (1.8% of the panel, 1.1% strong). Row grounds: black hatched, red-brown, black hatched (A B A). Each large octagon holds one or two words in gold script; read right to left and down, then over to f.8r, the twelve form a single sentence citing the Kufan count and the chain of its transmission. The page looks like a tiling and is read like a line.

**Interlaced frontispiece, f.8v (9r its pair).** Frame 1070 × 1500 (1.40); on the whole leaf it is about 50% of the area, with margins of gutter 10%, head 13–14%, foot 13–14%, fore-edge 21–23%. The centre is one strap, double-outlined, with a band of zigzag hatch. Two circles of diameter about 750 (0.82 of the 915 inner width), centres about 505 apart (0.67 D), overlap on the vertical axis; a third wider lobed figure about the middle, and arcs coming from the four corners, pass over and under them. The overlay fits the outer arcs within about 2%; inner crossings are adjusted by hand to bring the bands together. Four small knots tie the strap to its border at the middle of each side. The leftovers are of three kinds: blue triangles with a gold flower (four, at N, S, E, W), star-and-hexagon fields in green and gold (four), star fields with white and blue (the outer corners), and a central palmette scroll on gold. Blue is 1.8% (0.7% strong), green about 1–2%. The border is a wide gold braid. The fore-edge palmette is 28% of the frame's height and width, its centre at the frame's mid-height, and it fills about 90% of the margin. No word appears. f.9r: 1044 × 1490 (1.43); the same scheme, with small differences in the scrolls.

**Finispiece, ff.284v–285r.** Frame 620 × 920 (1.49), braid border. Two large vertical lobed figures meet two horizontal ones; four knots on each axis of the frame and one in the centre; corners are star diaper. Above and below the crossing a large aubergine-brown palmette (`#38271B`) points in from the ends; a pair of blue curved triangles with flowers faces across the centre (blue 2.0%, 1.2% strong); four rose triangles carry an interlocking fret pattern (`#8E624E`). Darker overall than the frontispiece: dark pixels are 23% of the panel against 12%. The spread is two copies side by side facing the gutter, with the palmettes pointing outward, so that the pair is symmetrical about the fold though each page is not centred on its leaf.

**Letter-count tables, ff.285v–286r.** Two columns in one gold frame with a blue line outside (1040 × 1550, ratio 1.49–1.50). The label column is 147 px wide and the value column 740 px: 1 : 5.0 (f.286r 1 : 5.6). Fourteen rows, pitch 102 px, cell 88, gutter 14 filled with two lines of dots. The label is the plural name of a letter ("alifs", "bas"...) in blue Kufic on solid gold; the value is the count, spelled out, in gold round script outlined in brown. Rows alternate in treatment without altering any size: odd rows have a faint diagonal hatch and blue dots in groups of three; even rows have a faint scroll ground and brown dots. The two pages hold 2 × 14 = 28 rows, one letter each (f.285v runs from alif to sad, f.286r from dad on). A round medallion in the fore-edge margin, 25% of the frame's height, is level with the centre. Every stretch letter in a short count is elongated to fill its cell; a long count has none.

**Colophon, f.284r.** A frame with gold bands above and below (top about 1/11 of the height) and two text panels, six lines and four lines, set apart by a braid strip. Line interval about 98 px, the same as text pages, in the same brown. Lines narrow towards the end, the last centred, closed by a drop-shaped device. Grounds of tiny gold scroll and dots run behind the letters, so here alone ornament shares the text's ground. The surrounding marginal notes are by a later hand (the date appears to read 1144 AH) and run obliquely across the margin; they show how clean the original margin was.

**After and before.** Yaqut (TIEM 507): 11 lines, frame, full gold headings top and bottom, rosette at every verse; ornament has moved into the text area. Yaqut (ms. 4277): heading panel and 12 lines, pitch 95.3 px, SD 2.5%, tighter than Ibn al-Bawwab's 3–5%, thicker-to-thinner contrast, every verse end a rosette, and the basmala's stroke again long. Uljaytu Qur'an: five lines, gold and black alternating line by line, interval roughly 290 px on a 1680 px page, strokes with sharp tips and long shallow tails. The earlier five-line folio: slanted heads, flat baseline, sharp triangular joints, a tall interval with much empty paper. What is particular to Ibn al-Bawwab against both is many lines, even interval, rounded and upright forms, and unframed text.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **One interval, every page** (ff.10r, 33r, 60v, 100r, 138v, 153r, 200r, 250r, 278v). 15 rows; mean interval varies only 100–104 px at 1800 px width, SD 3.1–4.9% in text pages. Headings, colophon and tables keep near the same interval (98–105 px): the row is the module of the whole book.
- **Headings cost whole rows** (f.278v, f.250r; f.9v). A chapter break occupies 2 rows as bare gold letters, or 3 as a panel (280–300 px), and its basmala 1; the last row stays on the same 15-row grid ((last − first)/interval = 14.0–14.1).
- **A letter measured by itself** (f.200r, f.33r). Alif = 8.0 and 7.6 dots; stem : height 1 : 14; alif = 0.66 interval. This agrees with the 1 : 8 alif that later writers credit to the Ikhwan al-Safa, and not with the five or seven of other scripts (PMC7255603). It is measured on two pages only.
- **Text block about 2 : 3 in a page of 4 : 3** (f.33r 1.50, f.200r 1.53; leaf 1.30–1.33). Text takes 53–54% of the leaf, decorated frames about 50%. Early frames 1.39–1.43 (ff.6v, 8r, 8v, 9r); later ones 1.49–1.50 (ff.284v, 285v, 286r). Margins: gutter 9–10%, head and foot 10–14%, fore-edge 21–24% (trimmed).
- **The margin is a working column** (ff.10r, 33r, 138v, 153r, 200r, 279r). Its medallions sit on its centre line and level with the line they name: medallion centre to in-line marker differs by about 0.1–0.3 interval on f.200r, f.33r, f.153r. Its only content is finding aids: ten, five, chapter name, section name.
- **A three-step scale of verse marks** (f.33r, f.200r, f.153r, f.278v). Every verse: three small dots (sometimes raised within the line of words, with no space made). Every fifth: a drop the height of an alif or a bit more. Every tenth: a roundel 0.8–1.2 alif, plus a margin medallion 3.5 alif across. Small marks add nothing to the line length; large marks displace words.
- **Ornament decays inward, peaks at the ends** (f.6v–9r, f.9v, f.10r, f.33r, f.200r, f.284v–286r). Full geometry first and last; richest margin medallions on the first text pages; a plain run in between. Illumination is placed at thresholds.
- **Numbers as frontispiece** (ff.6v–8r, 285v–286r). Two spreads at the front and one at the back state how much is in the book: chapters, verses, letter counts. Illumination does a job of auditing.
- **Blue is 0.02% to 4.5%, and always inside** (f.33r <0.02%; f.278v 0.3%; f.8v 1.8%; f.284v 2.0%; f.285v 3.1%; f.6v 4.5%). Never a ground for a whole page; it fills closed cells bounded by a line or gold.
- **Palindromic order** (f.6v ABACABA; f.7v–8r ABA rows; f.284v mirrored pair). Repeating rows are distinguished by a symmetric change of ground, and the centre row is the only one of its kind.
- **Alternate by treatment, not by size** (f.285v–286r odd and even rows; f.33r medallion seven blue and seven brown triangles; f.9v two panels with reversed figure and ground).
- **Elastic parts absorb the remainder** (basmala stroke 61–64% of the measure on f.9v, f.278v, f.250r; table cells; f.200r's overflowing ba; small raised words at line ends on f.33r and f.10r). Letters keep their size; the long stroke, the tail in the margin and the raised word do the justifying.
- **The pattern meets its frame by cutting** (f.7v–8r half and quarter octagons; f.8v corner arcs). No unit is squeezed to fit; cuts fall on symmetry lines.
- **Two scripts, two roles** (f.285v–286r Kufic label and round value; f.33r, f.200r Kufic word in the medallion, round text in the body). A facsimile listing says Kufic is not used; the labels here are blue Kufic.
- **Practice rules from the poem** (Zakariya's translation). Choose a straight reed, cut the nib between straight and very slanted, rehearse on a slate that can be wiped, copy good models; the trim of the nib is held to be the heart of the matter and left partly unsaid.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence. The letter proportion is measured on two pages and agrees with a rule that later writers report; no ruling is visible, so the even interval is recorded as a result and not as a known procedure.*

**Ibn al-Bawwab.** *Take one unit from the stroke and set the letter, the row and a fixed row count from it; keep the reading column bare and send every count and name to one outer column, level with its line; let strokes and the margin take the slack, and keep pattern, frames and colour for the first and last views.*
Process:
1. Fix the unit before any layout. Take the smallest whole mark of the reading face (its dot) as the unit: the tall upright is 7.6–8 units high and its stem about 1/14 of that height. Set the row interval so the upright is 0.66 of it (about 12 units). Every later size is stated in units or rows.
2. Fix a row count per view and keep it in every kind of view (15 in the source; choose the count the screen holds, then hold it). Give the text column about 68% of the sheet's width and 77–80% of its height (53–54% of the area), with unequal slack: 9–10% on the inner side, 10–14% above and below, and 21–24% on the outer side, at least twice the inner.
3. Leave the reading column bare: no frame, rule, tint, indent or gap between items. Every mark that belongs to reading takes the one reading value, at least 37 L from the ground and on the ground's side of warm or cool. Marks cover 15–20% of the column, and that density is the same on every view.
4. Charge everything else in whole rows on the same interval. A section heading takes 2 rows as bare letters about twice the text height, or 3 rows as a panel exactly as wide as the measure; an opening formula takes 1; table rows keep the interval (cell to gutter about 6 : 1, label column to value column 1 : 5.0–5.6). Check that the last row still lands on the grid: (last − first) / interval is a whole number.
5. Make the outer slack a working column that holds finding aids only (a count, a section name). Each aid sits on the column's centre line and level with the line it names, within 0.1–0.3 of an interval. The column may be half full while the text column does not change.
6. Count the sequence on a three-step scale tied to the unit. Every item: a mark of about one unit that takes no space. Every fifth: a mark about one upright high. Every tenth: a mark 0.8–1.2 uprights high that displaces words, repeated in the working column at 3.5 uprights across and written a second way in a second, compact face. That second face carries short labels only, the reading face carries values and text.
7. Put the slack in what has no fixed size, never in the letters. A short phrase reaches the measure by one extended horizontal stroke (61–64% of the line). A line that overruns sends its remainder into the working column on the same row (about a fifth of the measure). The line ends stay inside a ragged band 6–10% of the measure, and a section's last line stays short (34–65%) with nothing added to fill it.
8. Tell repeated rows apart by treatment at constant size. Where grounds alternate, order them as a palindrome with an odd count (A B A, or A B A C A B A) so that the centre row is the only one of its kind.
9. Keep pattern, frames and strong colour for the first and last views, and let both of those start with an inventory of what the whole contains (how many sections, items, parts). A framed view takes about 50% of the sheet at 1.39–1.50 : 1 with a border band of 6–7% of its width and one tab on its outer side at mid-height (25–28% of its height). A pattern field has square cells of half the column width, with secondary units at the nodes at 0.55 of the main one, and it meets its frame by being cut at halves and quarters on its lines of symmetry; no unit is resized. The richest finding aids fall on the first reading view and thin out after it.
10. Fence the one colour that opposes the ground's temperature: under 0.02% of a reading view, about 0.3% of a view with a heading, 1.8–4.5% of a first or last panel, always inside a closed cell bounded by a line or by the second material, never as a ground.
Why it works:
- Equal intervals and equal mark density fuse the column into one even tone, and the eye groups it by texture. A frame would add an edge that competes with the text; with none, the only odd things on the view are the finding aids, and they win by oddness alone. Colour inside the text would break the tone at every use.
- An aid level with its line is tied to it by position along one axis, so the eye travels sideways at the same height and needs no leader. A note placed wherever it fits has to be searched for.
- The count scale ranks size against frequency: the commonest mark is the smallest and costs the line nothing, the rarest stops the eye and sends it to the margin. Equalise the three and either every item interrupts or nothing is a landmark (the later Yaqut pages put a rosette at every verse).
- The letter is the scale reference of the whole view. Stretch or squeeze it to fill a line and both the texture and the unit are lost; a stroke, a tail in the margin and a short last line have no size to betray.
- The eye adapts to the previous state. After a dense patterned view the bare column reads as calm, and the count given at the start tells the reader how much is ahead. Pattern on every view removes the threshold.
- A single opposed colour reads by simultaneous contrast at very small areas; closed in a cell it stays a point, where a field of it would tint its neighbours and take the ground's role.
Colour relations: ground 80–90% of a reading view; reading value 8–11% of the view (15–20% of the column), 37 L from the ground on the same side of warm or cool, so contrast is by value alone; a second material at about 1.5% of a reading view, only 3–9 L from the ground, which therefore separates by a fine outline in the reading value and is kept to marks and to letters at least twice the text height; one opposed colour, the darkest saturated one, fenced at 0.02–4.5% by view type; a near-dark ground only locally (2–26% of a panel) to reverse figure and ground for a heading; the lightest value only as pinpoints on colour.
Space and line: upright 8 units, stem 1/14, upright 0.66 of the interval; fixed row count; text column 68% × 77–80%; outer slack ≥ 2 × inner; headings 2 or 3 rows; aids level within 0.1–0.3 interval; count marks at 1 unit, 1 upright, 0.8–1.2 uprights and 3.5 uprights; elastic stroke 61–64% of the measure; frames about 50% of the sheet with a doubled inner line and a single outer one; pattern cell half the column, cut by the frame.
Type requirement: one upright reading face of near-even stroke (stem about 1/14 of the upright height) with open counters and low joins, set unjustified at one size for a whole document, the upright at 0.66 of the line interval so that marks above and tails below fill the interval without colliding; headings are the same face at about twice the height; a second, compact, angular face for labels of one or two words, never for running text. No size is chosen that is not a whole number of units.
Motion and interaction: scrolling and paging move by whole rows; the working column travels with the text, so an aid never floats free of its line; the tenth-marks are the jump targets (hit area ≥ 44px, focus ring on the mark), and jumping lands the named line on a row. Content that arrives late or overruns is fitted where it falls (a tail into the working column), and the lines already set do not reflow. A document opens by degrees (inventory, pattern, text) at 300–500ms a stage and can be skipped; reduced motion opens on the text with the inventory one row above it.
Fails when: the sheet is too narrow to give up 21–24% of its width and keep a readable measure (most phones in portrait); the content has no countable sequence, so the working column has nothing to hold; modules need heights that are not whole rows (charts, media, maps); meaning must be flagged inside the text itself (inline errors, diff colours); a view is too short to have a first and a last; there are so many annotations that the outer column would pass half full.
Signature (do not carry together): a rounded pen script with long stretched connecting strokes · rayed gold roundels and leaf finials standing in the margin, with fenced blue · full-page interlaced strap and octagon fields at the front and back. None of these belongs in a kit, and sacred text is never used as decoration.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Policy and changelog reader for a payments API, desktop pane 960 × 800, near-black with one acid accent, a grotesque sans and a monospace; terse and technical (the source is warm, slow and written by hand).
1. *Unit.* The sans is set with an upright height of 12px: unit 1.5px, stem about 1px, row interval 18px (12 / 0.66).
2. *Rows.* Text column 652px (68%), inner slack 96px (10%), outer column 212px (22%), 85px above and below: 630px, 35 rows. Every view in the product has 35 rows.
3. *Bare column.* Clauses run on with no rules, zebra, indents or space between them; links and inline code keep the one reading value, 60 L above the ground and on its cool side.
4. *Whole rows.* A section heading takes 2 rows (36px) in letters 24px high; the document title is a 3-row panel (54px) across the full 652px with figure and ground reversed; the "effective from" line takes 1. Last baseline minus first is 34 × 18 = 612px.
5. *Working column.* Section names and clause counts only, each on the column's centre line (106px in) and within 3px (0.17 of a row) of the baseline it names. No comments, help or promotion.
6. *Count scale.* Every clause ends in a 2px mark that takes no space; every fifth in a mark 12px high; every tenth in a 14px numeral that pushes the words along, with a 42px marker in the outer column giving the same number spelled out in monospace capitals. The marker is the jump target, with a 44px hit area.
7. *Slack.* The one-row "effective from" line reaches the measure by a single 1px stroke of 404px (62%). An endpoint path too long for its line continues into the outer column on the same row, up to 130px. The last line of each section stays as short as it falls.
8. *Alternation.* The endpoint table has a 108px monospace label column and a 544px value column (1 : 5), 18px rows, odd rows with a faint hatch and even rows plain, all the same size. The three summary rows read plain, reversed, plain; the reversed one holds the count of breaking changes.
9. *Thresholds.* The first view is the inventory: 14 sections, 212 clauses, 9 changed. The second is a field of 326px square cells (half the column), cut at the frame's edge, each cell holding one or two words of the title in reading order. The last view is the per-section table of counts. Between them, 35 bare rows a view.
10. *Fenced colour.* Acid appears in closed cells only: 3% of the first and last views, 0.3% beside a heading, and a 2px point inside each tenth-marker on reading views. A changed clause is flagged by a marker in the working column, never by colouring its text.

Result: a dark, sharp technical reader that does not read as a manuscript. What survived is the unit that sets letter and row, the fixed row count, the bare column, the working column level with its lines, the three-step count, slack taken by strokes and margin, and pattern and colour only at the two ends.

## Space & composition
- **Page.** Portrait leaf 1.30–1.33 : 1. Text 68% of width, 77–80% of height, 53–54% of area. Margins as shares: gutter 9–10, head 10–11, foot 10–12, fore-edge 21–24 (trimmed). The text sits toward the gutter; fore-edge is at least twice the gutter.
- **Frames.** About 50% of the leaf; border band 6–7% of frame width; only outer edge a thin blue line; inner line always doubled.
- **Vertical rhythm.** 15 rows; heading 2 (3 as panel); basmala 1; last line short when the chapter ends (34–65% of measure) with no filler.
- **Outward pointing.** Every illuminated frame has one fore-edge ornament at mid-height (palmette 28%, roundel 25% of frame height), like a handle.
- **Tile.** Square cell of 0.5 column width (f.7v–8r): 2 × 3, node octagons 0.55 of the main.
- **Deliberately absent:** a frame or rule round running text, indents, space between verses, ornament in the gutter, figures, and colour in the script.

## Form, line & typography
- **Letter.** Round naskh (library: naskh or rayhan), upright stems, stem about 1/14 of alif height, alif about 8 dots. Slight barb on the head of alif and lam; bowls broad and shallow; teeth low; counters open. Monoline feeling with moderate swelling in horizontals.
- **Marks.** Vowels thick and evenly sloped; same ink as the text.
- **Kashida and tails.** Stretch strokes are straight with a slight dip and can reach 64% of a line.
- **Heading letters.** Gold, about twice text height, with a fine dark outline; f.9v adds reserved letters on dark ground.
- **Labels.** Kufic with flat baseline and angled heads: short, dark, as a solid token.
- **Line drawing.** Straps double-ruled, braid borders, dotted bands; hairlines end the rays of medallions.
- **Proportion theory.** Later literature (PMC7255603) derives letters from alif and a circle of the same diameter; that text stresses Ibn Muqla did not use the dot as unit, that writers give 5 to 10 dots and practice often departs. Measured here: 8 in one page, 7.6 in another.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- **Steady pulse.** 15 equal beats; the eye drops one interval at a time. Because ink density is the same on every page, the pages differ only by their margins.
- **Grouping by marks.** 1, 5, 10: small dots keep continuity, drops pause, roundels stop and send the eye to the margin.
- **Long stroke as a breath.** The basmala's stroke delays each chapter's first verse; it repeats at the same height in the line.
- **Opening and closing.** Inventory, sentence in tiles, wordless geometry, text, wordless geometry, inventory: the book is entered and left by degrees (checked on ff.6v–9r and 284r–286r).
- **Corrected in motion.** The overflow ba (f.200r), small raised words, and medallions drawn to fit the space show fitting at the pen, line by line. One exhibition text says he corrected as he wrote, with very few errors.
- **Slow and repeated learning.** The poem asks for a slate first, then paper, then years of copying.

## UI translations
1. **One unit, stated.** Take the reading face's dot as the unit and publish letter height (8 units), stem (1/14 of the height) and row interval (upright = 0.66 of it) from it.
2. **A fixed row count.** Every view of a document has the same number of rows; headings, tables and closing blocks are charged 1, 2 or 3 whole rows and never get a grid of their own.
3. **Unframed reading column.** Running text has no card, rule, tint or indent; its order comes from the constant interval and the constant density of marks (15–20% of the column).
4. **Unequal slack.** Text column about 68% of the sheet's width; the outer slack (21–24%) at least twice the inner (9–10%).
5. **The margin works.** The outer column holds finding aids only (counts, section names), each on the column's centre line and level with the line it names within 0.1–0.3 of a row.
6. **Count in three sizes.** Every item a unit-sized mark that takes no space, every fifth a letter-high mark, every tenth a mark that displaces words and is repeated in the margin at 3.5 letter heights, written a second way.
7. **Letters never stretch.** A short line reaches the measure by one extended stroke (61–64% of the line); an overrun continues in the margin on the same row; a section's last line stays short with no filler.
8. **Two faces, two roles.** A compact angular face for labels of one or two words in solid cells, the round reading face for values; label to value column 1 : 5.0–5.6.
9. **Alternate by treatment.** Odd and even rows differ by ground texture and mark colour at identical size; stacked bands order their grounds as a palindrome (A B A C A B A) with the single odd ground in the middle.
10. **Inventory first and last.** Open and close a long document with a view that states what it contains (sections, items, parts), set as a table on the same row interval.
11. **Pattern only at thresholds.** Frames, pattern fields and strong colour belong to the opening and closing views; the richest margin aids fall on the first reading view and thin out afterwards.
12. **Cut the pattern, don't fit it.** A repeating field uses a square cell of half the column width and is cut by its frame at halves and quarters; a short text may ride the cells one or two words at a time in reading order.
13. **Fence the opposed colour.** The one colour that opposes the ground's temperature (in the source, blue `#3E4B6A` on tan) stays under 0.02% of a reading view and 1.8–4.5% of a threshold panel, always in a closed cell.
14. **Outline what is close to the ground.** A material only 3–9 L from the ground (in the source, gold) is used for marks and large letters and always carries a fine outline in the reading value.
15. **A handle on the frame.** A framed panel throws one tab into the outer margin at mid-height (25–28% of the frame's height) as its handle and label.
16. **Move by rows.** Scroll and jump in whole rows; margin aids travel with their lines; reduced motion opens on the text with the inventory one row above.

## What this artist would reject
- A frame or rule round running text to make it look ordered.
- Scaling or squeezing letters to fill a line; the strokes or the margin are the place for slack.
- Ornament within the text column at every verse (Yaqut's later opening pages do this; he did not).
- Colour in the script to mark importance.
- Marginal notes floating where they fit, instead of level with the line they refer to.
- A new grid for headings and tables.
- Pattern units resized to finish neatly at a border.
- A page that begins with the text and has no threshold.

## Signature prompt seed
Take the reading face's dot as the unit: letters eight units high, the upright two thirds of one row interval, and the same number of rows on every view. Leave the reading column bare, with no frame, rule or colour, about 68% of the sheet's width, and give the outer side twice the inner slack. Use that outer column for finding aids only, each level with the line it names. Count the sequence in three sizes of mark (every item, every fifth, every tenth), the largest repeated in the margin. Charge headings and tables whole rows. Never stretch letters: extend one stroke, send the overrun into the margin, leave last lines short. Open and close with an inventory, and keep frames, pattern and the single fenced opposed colour for those two ends.
