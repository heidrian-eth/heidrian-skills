# Charles and Ray Eames — Cranbrook / organic modernism

## Essence
The Eameses take one material or one standard part and push it through a process (heat and pressure on veneer, a catalogue steel section, a power of ten) until it fits a body or a life. The result is a small set of strict parts: shells, rods, frames, panels, steps. They are joined in plain view, then loosened by colour, collected objects and hand-made pattern. The structure stays legible and nothing is ornament except what the owner adds.

## Key works examined

## Detailed observations

**Leg splint.** A single tapered trough about 107 cm long and 19.5 cm wide at most, an aspect of about 5.5 : 1. In the V&A top view its width runs from ≈0.17 of the length at the thigh end down to ≈0.12 at the ankle. The narrowing is not linear: it swells slightly again around the knee (≈0.14) before the ankle pinch, following a calf. The cross-section is a shallow U whose rim turns up 10.5 cm. At the foot end the trough closes into a rounded heel cup, finished with one small round hole and a notch cut into the end edge. The openings are all rounded slots, never sharp. Near the thigh end are two small near-square holes close to the corners and short slots on each edge. Two long parallel slots run down the middle third. In the lower third (from about 60% of the length) two long openings take out about a third of the surface, so the fill there drops to ≈0.65 against ≈0.95 above. The V&A record says the holes relieve stress in the moulding and pass the bandages, so every void has a job. Small V-shaped splits and a lap line cross the surface where the five veneers were cut to different shapes. The stamped part number ("S2-1790") is the only marking. Colour is the veneer alone: honey birch `#C48D41` (L 59, C 47) under studio light, a redder `#BB7448` / `#8F4A35` (L 55 / 39, C 43 / 36) on the second example, with no finish or paint. Seen side-on on a wall, the deep rim throws a long soft shadow and the slots print bright lozenges onto that shadow. There is no hardware, no straight line, and no flat area anywhere.

**LCW.** Five moulded parts: a seat, a back, a front U-leg, a rear U-leg (shorter than the front, so the seat tilts back) and a curved spine that rises from the rear frame to carry the back. The seat height is 39 of 67 cm (0.58), and the chair is wider than it is tall in plan terms (56 × 61 cm footprint), so it reads low and broad. The back is a rounded trapezoid, wider at the top, with corner radii of roughly a quarter of its height. It floats: a visible gap separates it from the seat, and it touches the chair only at the spine through rubber shock mounts glued to its back. The seat is dished front to back, its front edge rolled down. The legs are flat plywood straps that bend once at the knee into the seat rail and taper to the floor. Every edge shows the laminations as a thin line. Colour is one wood throughout: the back panel measures `#6C3F21` / `#7F512F` (L 31–39, C 30) in light and shade, `#8D6547` on highlights. Nothing is upholstered, nothing is covered, and no bolt or screw shows from the front.

**DCW.** Seen frontally in the V&A store, the back is ≈0.61 of the seat's width and its proportion is about 1.6 : 1 (wide). The spine below it is ≈0.29 of the back's width. Two small bolt heads sit at the centre of the back, ≈0.1 of its width apart: the only hardware seen, set where the shock mounts are. The seat's front edge bows downward at the middle and rises at the corners, like the LCW but higher. The four splayed legs stand outside the seat's footprint at the floor. The finish is pale birch, lighter than the LCW sample.

**Eames House.** The east facade is a black steel grid of 7 ft 4 in bays, each holding two standard steel sash 3 ft 4 in wide, 20 ft deep and about 17 ft high (Eames Foundation and National Register text). The house runs 8 bays, the courtyard 4, the studio 5 (sources differ by one bay). Inside the grid each cell is filled independently, and the photos show at least six kinds of infill. Clear glass in a 6–7-pane vertical stack covers the lower storey. Wired or translucent glass and pale Cemesto-like panels in cream `#BBBB9F` and pale grey-green `#B0BFB7` fill much of the upper storey. A whole bay of upper storey is ultramarine `#3A63B4` (L 43, C 49), measured `#104074` in shade on a second photo. A short horizontal strip of two gold panels, `#F4ED85` (probably lighter than true in a filtered photo), sits at floor-line height, about a sixth of a storey tall. One small red-orange panel, `#E43D20` (L 51, C 80), is set inside the glass at eye height: one bay wide, about a fifth of a storey tall. One full lower bay is opaque black, crossed by an exposed X of steel tension rods with turnbuckles. On the cropped facade the black frame and black panels take ≈33% of the area, glass and its reflections ≈40%, pale panels ≈10%, blue ≈8% and red ≈1.4%. Colour is never on the frame, only on single cells, and the strong colours never touch each other: each is isolated by black mullions and pale or glass neighbours. From the meadow the long side sits behind a row of eucalyptus trunks. The house is placed behind the trees, not in the open, so the grid is always seen through and broken by bark (light grey trunks, `#BCBFB7`-range, in front of black). The two-storey living room inside has vertical timber boarding (`#634C36`, L 33), a ribbed steel deck ceiling, floor-to-ceiling translucent curtains on the glass wall, and a built-in steel-and-plank bookcase about 2 storeys high with a library ladder. The room is crowded with books, folk toys, lamps and textiles. Warm yellow lamp light `#F1C95B` (≈3%) and a single orange cushion `#65180A`-range are the only saturated colours inside. The architecture is plain and repetitive; the variety comes from the objects.

**ESU 421-C.** Two equal bays across, four tiers up, on short legs (leg clearance ≈7% of the height). The tiers are nearly equal in the photo (top 225 px, open 230, drawers 210, bottom 275 with perspective), about 1 : 1 : 0.9 : 1.2. Of the 8 front cells, 4 are sliding plywood doors (top and bottom tiers), 3 are open shelves and 1 holds three drawers. The doors are pressed with a regular grid of round dimples, 6 across × 3 down per door, 72 in all; the dimple diameter is ≈0.7 of the pitch. The uprights are thin zinc-plated angles punched with holes at a regular pitch along their whole length. They stay visible at every corner and continue below as the legs. In one open cell an X of thin steel rods braces the back, and the next cell has a perforated metal back with a fine hex hole pattern. The side panels are separate colours: white, a grey-blue upper side and a black lower side. Measured over the front: wood doors `#A97528` / `#C19741` (L 53–65, C 50) ≈43%, white panels `#F7F3F0` ≈24%, grey and perforated metal `#B9B3A3` / `#868374` ≈23%, walnut-dark drawer fronts `#865525` ≈8%, black ≈2%. The frame, the cross-brace and the screw heads are all exposed. There are no handles on the sliding doors, only on the drawers (three plain chrome pulls, centred).

**"Cross Patch."** An all-over field of small squares, each about 1.1 in wide, set edge to edge with thin cream gaps. Each square takes one of about six states: solid peach, solid grey or solid plum, any of them crossed by a white X or marked with a centre dot, or an outline only in grey, peach or plum with an X or a dot inside. The squares are drawn by hand: edges wobble, corners are not true, rows drift by a few pixels and no two squares match. The grid holds while the marks loosen. Autocorrelation shows a near-repeat every 7 cells across (r 0.68) and every 4 rows down (r 0.60), and the full design repeats at about half the width (the left and right halves match). The order is therefore loose and recurring, not random. Measured: cream ground `#E9DFD8` (L 90) 24%, pale grey `#C7C5C1` 23%, mid grey `#A7A4A0` 21%, peach `#E7C7AE` (L 82, C 17) 16%, grey-mauve `#897979` 8%, plum `#644B50` (L 34, C 12) 7%. The darkest value is the smallest share. Chroma never passes 17: the colour is carried by warm/cool shifts among near-neutrals. The selvedge carries "CROSS PATCH by ray eames" in her handwriting, plus "SCHIFFER PRINTS / VAT DYE HAND PRINT" in small hand-lettered capitals.

**"Sea Things."** One ink, yellow-green, on cream. Starfish, urchins, seaweed sprigs, shells, spirals, tiny stars and dots are scattered with no up or down; motifs point every way. The largest motif (a starfish about 100 px on the cropped panel) is about 20 times the smallest dot (about 5 px), and between them sit many middle sizes. The density is even overall but clumped locally, with drifts of dots filling the gaps between large motifs. The ink covers ≈20% of the clearer sample (`#69834B`, the most saturated cluster, L 52, C 35, measured under a colour cast) and ≈7% of the paler print. The ground is a warm cream `#EBE3CA` (L 90, C 13). There is no grid, no border and no single focal motif. The marks are line drawings with filled small shapes, so the drawing hand is visible.

**Hang-It-All.** A horizontal rectangle of white wire carries four vertical wires, each bent into a zig-zag that rises above the rectangle and drops below it. Branches carry 14 balls in all: 4 large at the top tips, 4 small on side branches, 2 small at the centre, 4 large at the bottom tips. The small balls are ≈0.73 of the large diameter. The layout is symmetrical in structure but not in colour: no two neighbours share a hue. The photographed reissue (exposure ≈30 L low) shows ochre-gold, red, pink, dark green, navy, chartreuse, plum, magenta, blue, yellow and near-black, measured `#9C7211`, `#850E0C`, `#07513B`, `#1C2A4F`, `#A3A53F` among others. The balls take ≈7–8% of the view and the white wire is almost invisible against the wall, so the colours appear to float as dots. Mia's 1953 example lists black, pink, gold, green, red, natural wood and yellow. Each hook ends in a ball, so there is no sharp tip.

**Lounge Chair 670.** Three separate moulded rosewood shells (headrest, back, seat), each a deep tray holding a loose black leather cushion. The shells are linked only by two slim aluminium spines with visible gaps between them, so the chair reads as stacked plates. Armrests are separate cushioned bars. A five-star black base with a polished hub sits under it. The dark rosewood outside (`#473523` / `#351F10`, L 14–24) and black leather (`#252724`, L 15) together take ≈50% of the cropped view against the pale ground. The shell edges show a thin bright line of laminated plies, the lightest thing on the chair.

**The Colouring Toy (graphics).** The lid is a photograph from above of a table covered with the toy in use: hands colouring a cut-out, crayons scattered, finished pieces in bright crayon colours next to plain black-line ones. A black bar laid across it at a slight angle carries the title in widely spaced white slab-serif capitals. On the side of the box the word "COLOURING" is set in large black slab-serif capitals, and the counters of the O's are filled in with colour. The measured palette is mostly the greys of the photo (`#8A8580`, `#B7B3B0`, `#616260`, ≈67%) with crayon red-brown `#77382F` (C 34), plum `#78454E` and ochre `#A6923D` (C 44) at under 5% each. The graphic shows the process (hands, tools, half-finished work) rather than a finished product.

**Powers of Ten.** Every frame is a square image centred in a black widescreen field. In the picnic frame the square fills the full height, 420 of 575 px (73% of the width). The left black band carries the scale in words ("1 meter", "100 ångstroms"), set vertically and reading bottom to top. The right band carries the same scale as a power ("10⁰ meters", "10⁻⁸ meters"), white sans-serif, top right. In the 10⁻⁸ frame a small square outline at the centre, ≈1/10 of the frame's side, marks what the next step will fill. The picnic blanket is turned ≈15–20° off the frame's axis, so the square does not rhyme with its subject. Pace from the Library of Congress and Wikipedia: outward one power of ten every 10 s, from 1 m to about 10²⁴ m; back at one power every 2 s; then inward to about 10⁻¹⁶ m in a carbon atom in the sleeping man's hand. The 42 production panels match that range (10²⁵ to 10⁻¹⁶). The camera never cuts: one centre point holds for the whole film. The 1968 sketch version added two clocks for relativistic time; the 1977 version dropped them.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Material pushed to the body** (splint, LCW, DCW, Lounge 670). Forms are taken from the leg, the seat and the back: the splint swells and pinches along a calf (width 0.17 → 0.14 → 0.12 of length); the LCW seat sits at 0.58 of the height and tilts back on shorter rear legs. Every surface is a compound curve and there are no flat planes in the wood.
- **Separate parts, soft visible joints** (LCW five parts, DCW, Lounge 670 three shells, ESU). Back and seat never merge; a gap is left and a small connector (rubber mount, aluminium spine, steel angle) bridges it. On the DCW the only hardware seen is two bolt heads ≈0.1 of the back's width apart. On the 670 the gaps between shells are as legible as the shells.
- **Catalogue frame, cell-by-cell infill** (Eames House, ESU). A grid of standard steel (7 ft 4 in bays; equal ESU tiers ≈1 : 1 : 0.9 : 1.2) is filled one cell at a time from a small set of panel types: clear, translucent, opaque pale, opaque black, one strong colour, perforated, braced. The ESU front has 8 cells and 4 infill types. The frame is always black or bare metal and never coloured.
- **Strong colour as isolated single cells, small and few** (Eames House blue ≈8%, red ≈1.4%; ESU black ≈2%). Saturated panels never touch each other. They sit among black, glass and pale neutrals, and the strongest chroma (red, C 80) takes the smallest area.
- **Structure shown, bracing as drawing** (Eames House X of rods on a black bay; ESU X of thin rods in one open cell). Diagonal bracing is placed in one cell and left exposed, the only diagonal in an orthogonal grid.
- **Holes and dimples as regular texture with a job** (splint slots relieve stress and pass bandages; ESU 6 × 3 dimples per door, diameter 0.7 of pitch; perforated metal backs). Voids and bumps repeat at an even pitch and are always rounded.
- **Hand-drawn pattern over a loose order** (Cross Patch: ≈1.1 in squares, 6 states, near-repeat 7 × 4; Sea Things: one ink, 20 : 1 motif size range, no orientation). Ray's textiles keep a system (grid or even scatter) and let the hand show in every mark.
- **Low chroma fields, saturated dots** (Cross Patch C ≤ 17; Sea Things C ≈ 35 at ≈20% ink; Hang-It-All balls ≈7–8% of the view on near-invisible white wire; Colouring Toy crayon colours under 5% each on grey). Colour arrives as many small separate points, not as large planes.
- **Collected objects complete the plain frame** (Eames House living room: books, toys, lamps, textiles on a plain board wall and steel deck; Colouring Toy lid as a tabletop of work in progress). The built shell is repetitive and quiet; the richness is added by use.
- **One fixed centre, steps of ×10** (Powers of Ten: square frame, centre held for 9 minutes, a 1/10 square marking the next step, labels in words on the left and as a power on the right, 10 s per power out, 2 s back).
- **The generative pattern seen:** choose one material or one standard part; form it to the body or the task; make the parts separate and join them with small visible connectors; set them in a plain repeating frame filled cell by cell from a few infill types; place one or two strong colours as isolated small cells; and let pattern, objects and the hand add the variety.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Charles and Ray Eames.** *Set out one plain, uncoloured frame of equal cells and a short catalogue of infill types named by job; fill each cell as its own decision, keep the parts inside separate with visible joints, place strong colour only as small cells that never touch, and leave the variety to what the user puts in.*
Process:
1. Choose one stock unit and repeat it. One bay size serves the whole view, set out as equal bays in one or two directions (from 2 × 4 up to a run of 8), with tiers within 1 : 0.9–1.2 of each other. Every part is sized in whole bays. If the bay changes, every later step is redone.
2. Draw the frame once: thin lines in ONE neutral value, visible at every corner and carried on past the last cell as the support (a clearance of about 7% of the height). The frame never takes a hue and never changes weight.
3. Write the infill catalogue before filling anything: 4–6 types named by job and ranked by how much they let through (open, veiled, closed light, closed in the frame's value, textured, strong hue). Then fill cell by cell, each cell a separate decision that need not match its neighbour. No type takes more than about 45% of the cells, the open types take about 40%, and the frame plus the cells closed in its value stay near a third of the view.
4. Make the parts inside a cell separately. Size each from what it holds, not from its neighbour (a secondary part comes out near 0.6 of the primary's width). Leave a visible gap between any two parts and bridge it with one small visible connector, about 0.1 of the part's width. Only the parts that are touched leave the frame's straight lines: their corners round at about a quarter of their height, and no opening or end is sharp.
5. Place strong colour last, as whole cells: one cell in a strong hue (up to about 8% of the view) and one much smaller cell in the strongest chroma (1–2%, about a fifth of a tier tall). They never touch: frame lines and at least one neutral cell lie between them. The total stays at or under a tenth. Alternative mode: spend the same budget (7–8%) as a dozen or so small points in two sizes (the small 0.7 of the large) on a structure close to the ground's value, with no two neighbouring points alike in hue.
6. Show the working in exactly one cell: the element that holds the system together (a dependency, a link, a rule) is drawn there as the only diagonal in the view, thin and in the frame's value.
7. Add texture only where it does a job. On closed cells that are gripped, moved or vented, repeat one rounded mark at an even pitch (mark about 0.7 of the pitch, about 6 × 3 per cell). For a patterned field, take one unit in about 6 states on a loose repeat (every 7 across and 4 down), each unit slightly irregular, chroma at about a fifth of the strongest colour's or less, and the darkest state the smallest share (about 7%).
8. Stop early. Frame and infill stay plain and repetitive; the variety comes from the content that lands in the open cells, and the user chooses which infill each cell takes. Nothing is added to the frame to make it look finished.
Why it works:
- A frame in one value that never takes hue is read as constant, so it groups by likeness everything set inside it. Each cell can then differ from its neighbour and the view still holds as one thing. Colour the frame, or vary its weight, and the differences between cells read as noise because nothing is left to measure them against.
- Two strong colours that touch fuse into one patch, and simultaneous contrast shifts both. Kept apart by neutral cells and frame lines, each is found by oddness and read as its own fact. The most saturated one can be the smallest because saturation and area trade off: enlarge it and the open cells lose; multiply it and it becomes a pattern.
- A gap makes each part a figure with its own outline, and the small connector states how the two relate. Merge the parts and the eye can no longer tell which one does what; hide the connector and they float with no relation at all.
- One diagonal among right angles is the odd line, so the place where the system is explained is found without size or colour. A second diagonal starts a pattern and the explanation is lost.
- Small low-chroma units, each slightly irregular, fuse into one tone at a distance and separate into marks up close, so a closed surface gains interest without competing with the strong cells.
Colour relations: the frame is one neutral value at an end of the scale, and with the cells closed in that value takes about a third; open and see-through cells take about 40%; pale closed cells 10–25%; one mid-value family may carry up to about 43% where closed cells dominate; one strong hue as a single whole cell (about 8%); the strongest chroma as the smallest cell (1–2%); strong colours never adjacent and at most a tenth in total. Points mode: 7–8% in many small dots. Pattern fields: chroma about a fifth of the strongest or less, darkest state smallest.
Space and line: equal bays, tiers within 1 : 0.9–1.2; 4–6 infill types over 8 or more cells; one frame line weight; one diagonal, in one cell; secondary part about 0.6 of the primary's width, connector about 0.1 of the part's width, a visible gap at every joint; touched parts rounded at a quarter of their height; repeated mark 0.7 of its pitch; the whole raised about 7% on its own frame lines.
Type requirement: text is an infill like any other. It fills whole cells and never crosses a frame line. Labels belong to the frame: one plain face in one small size and one weight, set in the bands beside or between the cells, where a label may run vertically along an edge. One display line per view sits on its own bar, in widely spaced capitals with sturdy even strokes that hold at small sizes over a textured cell. A quantity that changes by steps is stated twice, in words and as a figure, on opposite sides.
Motion and interaction: a cell changes infill in place (open, veiled, closed) and the frame never moves; the user can re-assign infill cell by cell. Scale mode (one work): hold one centre point for the whole journey and change scale by a constant ratio, with an outline about 1/10 of the view's side marking what the next step will fill; going out is slow and the return about 5 times faster, with no cut between steps. Connectors are the handles: a part is moved or detached by its joint. Reduced motion: steps replace the continuous zoom, and the outline and both labels stay.
Fails when: the content is one continuous thing that cannot be cut into bays (long prose, a full-bleed canvas or editor); many categories each need a colour and must sit side by side, so strong cells would touch; fewer than about 6 cells fit, and the frame becomes a border; there is no user content yet, since the plain frame depends on what is put in it; the brand needs a large saturated field.
Signature (do not carry together): moulded plywood shells floating on thin legs or spines · a black steel grid filled with glass and a few primary-coloured panels · many-coloured balls on white wire.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Server fleet console, desktop 1440 × 900, dark slate with one brand magenta, monospace, cold and technical (the works are warm, domestic and hand-made).
1. *Stock unit.* One bay 176px wide; 8 bays = 1408px plus 16px margins. Four tiers of 196 / 196 / 180 / 212px (within 1 : 0.92–1.08), between two 58px label bands. 32 cells, each one cluster.
2. *Frame.* 2px lines in one pale neutral, the same at every crossing, running on below the bottom tier as two 58px supports (6.4% of the height). No line is ever tinted, not even around an alert.
3. *Catalogue.* Six types by how much they let through: open (live chart on the ground), veiled (the same chart at 40% contrast, no axes), closed light (a three-line text summary), closed in the frame's value (decommissioned), textured (log tail), strong hue. Filled one by one: 13 open (41%), 8 veiled, 4 closed light, 4 closed in the frame's value, 2 textured, 1 strong.
4. *Separate parts.* In each cell the name, the chart and the action control are three parts with 8px gaps. The control is 106px wide (0.6 of the chart) and hangs from the chart by one visible 18px tick (0.1 of the bay). The control is 44px tall with 11px corners; nothing else on the screen is rounded.
5. *Strong colour.* One whole cell (tier 1, bay 2) in a deep muted teal marks the on-call engineer's own cluster: 2.7% of the screen. The brand magenta, the highest chroma, is one strip 176 × 78px inside tier 3, bay 6: the open incident, 1.06% of the screen, with a text label and an icon. Three neutral cells lie between them.
6. *Working shown.* One cell, the failover pair, shows the link between two regions as two thin crossed lines in the frame's value. They are the only diagonals on the screen.
7. *Texture with a job.* The closed cells can be dragged to reorder: each carries a 6 × 3 field of round dots, 5px on a 7px pitch, as its grip. The log-tail cells are a field of small marks in 6 states, low chroma, never magenta.
8. *Stop early.* No illustration, gradient or hero. The operator pins which clusters are open, veiled or closed, and that choice is the screen's character.
- *Motion.* Fleet to cluster to host is one zoom about a fixed centre. An outline the size of one cell (0.12 of the width) marks the next step. Going in takes 500ms a step and coming back 100ms. The left band names the level in words and the right band as "2 of 3".
- *Type.* One monospace at 12px for every label, in the bands. One display line, the fleet name, in spaced capitals on a bar in the top band.

Result: a dark, monospaced console with no wood, no primary panels and no furniture curves. What survived is the uncoloured frame, a catalogue of infills chosen cell by cell, separate parts with visible joints, two strong colours that never meet, and one cell that shows how the system holds.

## Space & composition
- Grids of equal bays (Eames House 7 ft 4 in × 20 ft × 17 ft; ESU 2 bays × 4 near-equal tiers) where each cell is an independent decision.
- Composition by infill: on the house facade glass ≈40%, black ≈33%, pale panels ≈10%, colour ≈10% in two cells.
- Floating parts with gaps: the LCW back is separated from the seat by visible space, and the 670's three shells are spaced apart.
- Low, broad stance: LCW seat at 0.58 of the height; ESU raised ≈7% on legs that are continuations of the uprights.
- Sited behind screens: the house sits behind a line of eucalyptus, seen through trunks; the interior is seen through translucent curtains.
- A single fixed centre in time-based work (Powers of Ten), with the side bands kept for labels and the square kept for the image.

## Form, line & typography
- Compound curves in wood: rounded trapezoids (LCW back), dished trays (seats, 670 shells), the splint's trough. Corner radii about a quarter of the panel height.
- Straight lines in steel: thin uprights punched at a regular pitch, open-web joists, rods in an X.
- All openings rounded (splint slots, ESU dimples, Hang-It-All ball ends): no sharp tip anywhere a hand goes.
- Laminations shown as a thin bright line on every plywood edge.
- Lettering: Ray's handwriting on the Cross Patch selvedge; hand-lettered small capitals ("SCHIFFER PRINTS"); widely spaced slab-serif capitals on the Colouring Toy, with coloured-in counters on the box side; plain white sans-serif scale labels in Powers of Ten, one set vertical.

## Rhythm, motion & time
- Even pitch, irregular hand: Cross Patch squares at ≈1.1 in with a near-repeat every 7 × 4 cells; ESU dimples 6 × 3; facade bays at 7 ft 4 in.
- Two speeds in Powers of Ten: 10 s per power outward, 2 s per power back, one continuous move with no cut; a 1/10 square previews the next step.
- Scatter without orientation (Sea Things): motifs in every direction, a 20 : 1 size range, even overall density.
- Use over time: the house interior changes with its objects; the ESU was configured by the buyer; the Colouring Toy shows work half done.

## UI translations
1. **Frame:** Draw a grid of equal bays in thin black (`#0F1212`) lines and never colour it; let the uprights run past the bottom row as short legs (about 7% of the height).
2. **Infill catalogue:** Fill each cell independently from a short list: clear, translucent, pale panel (`#BBBB9F`), black, perforated, and one strong colour. Neighbouring cells need not match.
3. **Shares:** On a main view keep clear cells near 40%, black frame and black cells near 33%, pale panels near 10%, one ultramarine cell (`#3A63B4`) near 8% and one red-orange cell (`#E43D20`) near 1.4%, one bay wide and a fifth of a row tall.
4. **Isolation:** Strong colours never share an edge; put black lines and at least one clear or pale cell between them.
5. **Floating parts:** Separate a card's header from its body by a visible gap and join them with one small connector about 0.1 of the header's width, like the back on its shock mounts.
6. **Shell and cushion:** Hold loose content in a dished tray with corner radii about a quarter of its height; show the tray's edge as a thin bright line, the lightest thing on the component.
7. **Dimpled doors:** Give closed sliding panels a 6 × 3 field of round dimples (diameter 0.7 of the pitch) as the grip, with no handle; put pulls only on drawers, centred.
8. **X-brace:** In one cell only, show the relation that holds the layout (sync, dependency) as an X of thin rods. No other diagonal appears.
9. **Coloured ends:** For a small set of states use up to 14 dots in two sizes (1 : 0.73) on near-invisible hairlines, no two neighbours alike, 7–8% of the view; each dot also has a label.
10. **Cross Patch field:** For a background or empty state, tile small hand-drawn squares in six states (solid, outline, crossed, dotted) in cream, greys, peach (`#E7C7AE`) and plum (`#644B50`); chroma stays under 17 and the darkest state is the smallest share (7%).
11. **Sea Things scatter:** One ink (`#69834B`) on cream (`#EBE3CA`), motifs in every direction across a 20 : 1 size range, 7–20% coverage, no grid and no focal motif.
12. **Powers of Ten stage:** Centre a square stage in a black field; label the scale in words in the left band, set vertically, and as a figure at the top right; mark the next step with an outline a tenth of the side; step by ×10 about one fixed centre, slowly out and five times faster back.
13. **Type:** Set the title in widely spaced slab-serif capitals on a black bar laid across the view at a slight angle (for example Zilla Slab or Roboto Slab), and labels in a plain white sans-serif.
14. **Finished by use:** Leave shelves open. The interface shows the user's own objects and work in progress, never a staged finished state.

## What this artist would reject
- Hidden structure: they expose shock mounts, bolts, cross-bracing, punched uprights and ply edges.
- Ornament applied to the structure: colour goes in infill cells, never on the frame.
- Large saturated fields: no strong colour exceeds about a tenth of a view in the works measured.
- Monolithic one-piece forms that merge seat and back: parts stay separate with visible gaps.
- Flat, featureless surfaces where the body touches: seats and backs are dished.
- Sharp ends and square holes.
- Perfect mechanical repetition in pattern: Ray's grids and scatters are drawn by hand.

## Signature prompt seed
Design this screen as an Eames frame: a thin black grid (`#0F1212`) of equal bays, never coloured, standing on its own uprights. Fill each cell on its own from a short catalogue: clear, translucent, pale panel (`#BBBB9F`), black, dimpled birch (`#A97528`), with one ultramarine cell (`#3A63B4`) and one small red-orange cell (`#E43D20`) that never touch. Keep the parts separate: headers float off their bodies on small visible connectors, trays are dished with soft corners and a bright ply edge. Brace one cell with an exposed X. Set the title in spaced slab-serif capitals on a black bar. Let the user's own objects fill the open shelves.
