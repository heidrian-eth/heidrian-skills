# Karl Blossfeldt — Growth & Form

## Essence
Blossfeldt made a plant's growth rule visible by removing everything else. He trimmed the specimen, lit it evenly against a plain ground, enlarged it 3–30× and centred it on the frame's axis, so one repeated module (a node, a leaf, a bud, a coil) becomes architecture. He was a sculptor and a design teacher, and the photographs were models of form for his students. The method is subtraction, then magnification, then repetition.

## Key works examined
- **Urformen der Kunst, Plate 1: Equisetum hiemale, young shoot ×25 (1928; English ed. *Art Forms in Nature*, 1929).** A ribbed column stepped at its nodes. It teaches how one module repeats with a growing interval. *(visually checked: Internet Archive scan of the Wellcome Library copy, item b31360336)*
- **Plate 6: Equisetum hiemale ×8, and E. arvense fruiting cone ×12.** Two tall panels side by side, one a branching node and one a cone tiled in hexagons. It teaches the pairing of a linear module and a packed module. *(visually checked: same scan)*
- **Plate 30: Saxifraga aizoon leaf rosette ×8.** A spiral rosette seen from above, in which every leaf carries a pale beaded rim. It teaches phyllotaxis, with the edge as the separator. *(visually checked: same scan)*
- **Plate 54: Adiantum pedatum, young rolled fronds ×8 and ×12.** Croziers on parallel stems entering from the right edge. It teaches the unrolling spiral and the cropped stem. *(visually checked: same scan)*
- **Plate 113: Asclepias speciosa umbel ×3.** A sphere of identical star flowers, pale on black. It teaches radial packing of identical units, and inverting the ground for a pale subject. *(visually checked: same scan)*
- **Wundergarten der Natur (1932).** The second volume. *(not obtained: not looked for; the first volume was enough for the rules)*

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Subtraction is part of the method.** The captions say so: "with the needles removed" (Pl. 21), "with the sepals removed" (Pl. 12), "bracteoles, with the flowers removed" (Pl. 92). He pruned the specimen until only the repeating structure was left.
- **One subject, on the axis, filling the frame** (Pl. 1, 6, 30, 113). The subject spans ≈70–90% of the print's short side and its centroid sits at 0.49–0.50 of the width. Tall subjects run off the bottom edge (Pl. 1 is cropped at the base) or enter from one side (Pl. 54's stems come in from the right), so the frame cuts the growth axis instead of floating the object.
- **Interval grows, module stays** (Pl. 1). From the apex down, the segment heights measure ≈45 : 145 : 280 px, with a fourth segment still longer and cut off. That is roughly ×2–3 per node, while the rib count stays constant and the width saturates (≈290 → 340 → 360 → 365 px). Older internodes have lengthened more, so the profile is a telescope, not a uniform stack.
- **Spiral packing with growing units** (Pl. 30). The leaves grow ≈3.5–4× in length from centre to rim and spiral round the rosette, so no two leaves stack radially. The divergence angle cannot be measured from one photograph; the arrangement is consistent with the Fibonacci angle (≈137.5°) but that is botany, not a measurement here.
- **Edge as separator** (Pl. 30). The dark leaves overlap dark leaves. What separates them is a pale beaded rim (lime glands) on every leaf margin: a value step at the edge, not a change of fill.
- **Ground value chosen against the subject.** Dark subjects sit on light grey (Pl. 1: ground ≈82% of the value range, subject median ≈35%; Pl. 6 median ≈70%). The pale umbel sits on near-black (Pl. 113: median ≈11%, highlights ≈82%). (Values measured on a 0–255 scale: 209, 89, 178, 28, 208.) The ground is always plain and untextured, and it always takes the opposite end of the value scale.
- **The generative rule:** strip the subject down to its repeating module; centre it on the frame axis at 70–90% of the short side; let the frame crop the growth axis; repeat the module with a constant shape and a geometrically growing interval or size (×1.5–3 per step) along a line or a spiral; separate overlapping units by a value step at the edge; put the ground at the opposite end of the value scale. This works with any subject, palette or medium.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Karl Blossfeldt.** *Subtract until only the repeating module is left, enlarge it to fill the frame on its axis, and let the interval between repeats, never the module, carry time.*
Process:
1. Prune. Remove every part that does not recur in every instance until one module remains, and say in the caption what was removed.
2. One subject per frame, its centroid on the frame axis (0.49–0.51 of the width), spanning 70–90% of the short side. State the enlargement factor.
3. Set a plain, untextured ground at the opposite end of the value scale from the subject (medians at least half the value range apart). The subject takes the other end and the middle; no second hue and no cast shadow.
4. Repeat the module with constant shape and constant internal count. Only the interval changes, geometrically, ×1.5–3 per step counted from the growing end; cross-size settles within three steps (about +25%, then constant).
5. Let the frame cut the growth axis: the old end runs off the edge, and the growing end (the head, the current item) stays whole inside.
6. For a radial set, place units on a spiral, each turned a fixed angle from the last (near 137.5°), with size growing 3.5–4× from core to rim, so no unit sits directly behind another.
7. Where units of like value touch or overlap, give every unit a rim one value step toward the ground's end of the scale. No gap, no shadow, no change of fill.
Why it works:
- A single thing at 70–90% on an empty ground of the opposite value leaves the eye one variation to read, which is the repetition itself, so structure becomes the figure. Shrunk to a third of the frame, the same thing reads as an object in a space and the counting stops.
- The eye judges intervals by ratio, so a geometric series reads as even growth and position reads as age. Equal spacing claims every item is the same age; changing the module along with the interval confounds two variables and neither is read.
- An edge cut across the growth axis is completed by the viewer as "this continues", while the whole head gives the place to start. Crop the head and there is no entry point; float the whole column and the sequence has ended.
Colour relations: value only. Ground one flat value at one end; subject 3–5 values of one family at the other end and the middle; rims at the extreme opposite to the units they separate; the ground inverts when the subject's value does, never mid on mid.
Space and line: one subject, axial, 70–90% of the short side; interval ratio ×1.5–3; no drawn line, since an edge is two values meeting; comparison is 2–3 equal-height panels side by side with a narrow gutter (≈2%), one module each; light even and directionless.
Type requirement: letters that read as one repeated module (even stroke, near-equal widths, upright), set as a single column on the axis; captions small, in the margin, giving name, part and factor; never on the subject.
Motion and interaction: modules arrive in order at delays that grow by ×1.3–1.6 (60, 90, 140, 210ms); zoom is the main interaction, in labelled doublings, the subject staying centred. Reduced motion: all modules at once and zoom by cut.
Fails when: the items are heterogeneous (nothing recurs, so pruning leaves nothing); several things must be compared at a glance beyond three panels; the sequence has no direction of growth; a user must reach an old item fast and the widening intervals have pushed it far off screen (cap the interval, or offer a jump).
Signature (do not carry together): magnified plant parts in monochrome photogravure · a pale grey studio ground · a plate number with a Latin name and "×25".

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Transaction history in a consumer banking app, phone 390 × 844, saturated violet brand on white, colourful and transactional (the plates are monochrome, single and still).
1. *Prune.* A row keeps what recurs in every transaction: payee and amount. Removed and stated in the screen's sub-line ("categories, balances and avatars hidden"): category icon, running balance, avatar, chevron.
2. *One subject.* The month is one column 328px wide (84% of the short side), centred on x = 195 (0.50). No side rail, no floating button over it.
3. *Ground.* Rows are deep violet tiles (L 28); the ground is plain white (L 98), a step of 70 L. No gradient, no card shadow.
4. *Interval.* Rows are a constant 56px with constant content. The gap before each older day-group grows ×1.5: 8, 12, 18, 27, 40px, capped at 48. Today is tight; last week is airy.
5. *Crop.* The list runs off the bottom edge with no footer or "end" marker. Today's header and total stay whole at the top and never scroll under chrome.
6. *Spiral.* Not used: the set is linear. (The year overview could use it: 12 months on a 137.5° spiral, the current month at the core at 44px and the oldest at the rim at 150px.)
7. *Rim.* Same-day tiles touch. Each has a 1.5px rim at L 46, one step toward the ground, so a block of five reads as five without gaps.
- *Comparison.* "This month / last month" is two equal-height panels with an 8px gutter (2%), one column each.
- *Zoom.* Pinch steps day → week → month → year, each labelled ("×7", "×30"), the column staying centred.

Result: a violet-and-white ledger with no plant, no grey and no photograph. What survived is the pruned module, the single axial subject, the geometric interval and the edge-step separator.

## Space & composition
- **Single subject, axial.** One object per frame, centred left–right. The vertical position may sit low when the subject grows upward and exits the top or bottom.
- **The frame crops growth, not form.** Stems and shafts run out of the frame; heads, buds and rosettes stay whole. The cut always falls on the axis of growth.
- **Paired panels.** Plates 6 and 54 combine 2–3 tall panels of equal height, separated by a narrow paper gutter and each holding a different module, a comparison by adjacency.
- **Magnification as hierarchy.** The caption gives the enlargement factor (×3 to ×50). Scale is stated, not implied.
- **Even light, little shadow.** Light is diffuse; volume comes from the subject's own value variation, not from cast shadows on the ground.

## Form, line & typography
Form is a column, a coil or a rosette, built from one module at a constant shape with a changing interval. There is no drawn line: edges are where two values meet, and the rims of units are the lightest lines on the page. Type requirement: a monumental, upright, evenly spaced face whose letters read as one repeated module (a constant stroke, a regular rhythm, near-identical widths), set as a single column on the axis. The caption is small, Latin-style (name, part, magnification) and placed in the margin, never on the image. For example, an upright grotesque with uniform strokes, such as Work Sans or IBM Plex Sans.

## Rhythm, motion & time
Time is the growing interval. A sequence reveals itself module by module, with each delay ×1.3–1.6 longer than the last (for example 60, 90, 140, 210 ms) and each module the same shape. Zoom is the core interaction: magnification steps (×2, ×4, ×8) are labelled, and the subject stays centred while it grows. Reduced motion: show all modules at once, and zoom by cut, not by animation.

## UI translations
1. **Strip to the module.** Before designing a list or card, remove every element that does not repeat. Keep one repeated module and show it alone, centred.
2. **Telescoping list.** Space rows with constant row content but intervals that grow by ×1.5 per row from the top (8, 12, 18, 27 px…), capped at 48 px. Recent items sit tight and older ones spread, like internodes.
3. **Spiral overview.** For a collection under ~60 items, place thumbnails along a 137.5° phyllotaxis spiral (rᵢ = c·√i, θᵢ = i·137.5°), with sizes growing from the centre (newest) to the rim (oldest) by up to 3.5×. Provide a plain list view of the same items for screen readers and keyboard users.
4. **Separate with a light rim.** When dark tiles touch dark tiles, separate them with a 1–2 px rim one value step lighter than both, not with a gap or a shadow.
5. **Invert the ground for pale content.** Light media go on a near-black field (L≈10–20) and dark media on a light field (L≈80–85). Never put mid on mid.
6. **Crop along the growth axis.** Timelines, feeds and progress bars run off the screen edge in their growth direction; heads (a title or the current item) stay whole.
7. **State the magnification.** Every zoom level shows its factor ("×4") in a small corner caption, and zoom steps are powers of 2.
8. **Paired panels for comparison.** To compare two variants, use two equal-height tall panels with a narrow gutter (≈2% of the width), each holding one centred module.
9. **One hue, value steps only.** Build the screen in one hue family. States use value plus an icon or label, not new hues.
10. **Even light.** No drop shadows. Depth comes from value within the object and from overlap.

## What this artist would reject
- Several heroes competing in one view.
- Decorative backgrounds, textures or gradients behind the subject.
- Retouched, idealised forms that hide the real repetition.
- Uniform spacing that pretends all items are the same age.
- Drop shadows and dramatic lighting.

## Signature prompt seed
Strip the screen to its one repeating module and show it enlarged, centred on the vertical axis and filling most of the short side. Let lists and timelines run off the edge along their direction of growth, keeping each head whole. Space repeated items at intervals that grow by about half each step, and keep every item identical in shape. Separate touching dark units with a thin light rim. Use a plain ground at the opposite end of the value scale from the content, in one hue family with no shadows. Label every zoom with its magnification.
