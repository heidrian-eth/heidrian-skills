# Ernst Haeckel — Growth & Form

## Essence
Haeckel drew organisms as solutions to a symmetry problem. He sorted each creature by its symmetry type (his "promorphology"), then laid out a plate as a second symmetry around the first. One large specimen sits on the plate's vertical axis, smaller relatives sit in mirrored slots around it, and the plate fills edge to edge. Each body is a repeated unit turned around a centre a fixed number of times. The beauty is that integer, multiplied.

## Key works examined
- **Kunstformen der Natur, Tafel 1, Phaeodaria (Circogonia), 1899.** A field of radiolarian skeletons in one warm ink family. It teaches you to build a body by repeating one spine or arm around a centre: here 3-, 5- and 6-fold units, and a sphere bristling with radial spines. *(visually checked: Internet Archive scan of the 1899 Leipzig edition, item KunstformenderN00Haec)*
- **Tafel 4, Diatomea (Triceratium), 1899.** 22 diatom valves: triangles, a five-pointed star, ovals, discs and a central disc. It teaches strict n-fold geometry with subdivision that densifies outward. *(visually checked: Internet Archive, same scan)*
- **Tafel 8, Discomedusae (Desmonema), 1899.** Four jellyfish, one with tentacles trailing across the whole plate. It teaches you to break symmetry with one flowing element while the bodies stay radial. *(visually checked: Internet Archive, same scan)*
- **Tafel 18, Discomedusae (Linantha), 1899.** Medusae in plan and elevation, printed in one blue-grey. It teaches the 4 → 8 → 16 ladder of the tetramerous body. *(visually checked: Internet Archive, same scan)*
- **Tafel 71, Stephoidea (Tympanidium), 1899–1904.** Ring-shaped radiolarian skeletons, white on a black ground. It teaches the inverted plate: the ground goes dark and colour is held back for the living cell inside the skeleton. *(visually checked: Internet Archive, same scan)*
- **Generelle Morphologie (1866), the promorphology chapters.** His classification of organisms by axes and symmetry order. It is the rulebook behind the plates. *(not obtained: text only, not looked at)*

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Plate symmetry, not shape symmetry** (Tafeln 1, 4, 18, 71). The largest specimen sits on the vertical axis, near the centre or just above it. The others sit in left/right pairs of equal size and height. Mirror overlap of the silhouette mask is only 0.42–0.67 (Pillow, frame interior), so the pairs mirror each other in **slot, size and value**, not in outline. Different species stand in symmetric positions.
- **One hero, then satellites at about a third of its size.** The central figure spans 47–48% of the frame width on Tafeln 4 and 18, and about 65% with spines on Tafel 1 (body ≈31%). The satellites are ≈1/3–1/4 of the hero's diameter. On the axial plates (Tafeln 1, 4, 18) the central cell of a 3×3 grid over the frame is the densest: 0.77–0.97 silhouette cover, against 0.25–0.73 elsewhere. Not every plate has an axial hero: Tafel 8 has none (four jellyfish, densest top-left to bottom-centre).
- **Fill, no void.** The silhouettes cover 50–73% of the frame interior and ink covers 25–52%. Gaps between specimens are roughly constant, so the plate reads as packed tiles rather than objects on a ground.
- **Integer rotational orders, stepped by doubling** (Tafel 18, fig. 1: a 4-armed cross mouth, an 8-sided ring, 16 marginal lobes, and 8 sense organs between lobe pairs). Tafel 4, fig. 13: 10 radial ribs end in 10 marginal nodes, and the sectors alternate dark/light, so a 10-fold geometry carries a 5-fold value rhythm. Its centre is concentric rings, and the radial cells multiply ring by ring so each cell stays near-square.
- **Symmetry broken once** (Tafel 8). The bodies stay radial, but one specimen's tentacles sweep diagonally across the plate, behind the others, as the only long free line. The plate's silhouette is still densest top-left to bottom-centre.
- **The generative rule:** choose an integer n; make one unit (an arm, rib or lobe); copy it n times around a centre; at each larger radius double n, or subdivide cells to keep them square. Then arrange several such bodies on a page with mirror slots around one axis, the largest central. Break the symmetry with at most one free-flowing element. This works in any medium: icons, layouts, motion.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Ernst Haeckel.** *Order by counting: build every body from one unit turned a whole number of times, double the count at each ring outward, and seat the bodies in mirrored slots round one axis.*
Process:
1. Fix one vertical axis and one gutter `g` (2–4% of the frame width). Every slot off the axis gets a mirror partner equal in size, height and value weight; the partner's content and outline may differ.
2. Seat the hero on the axis, centred or slightly high, at 45–50% of the frame width. Satellites are 1/4–1/3 of its diameter. No size exists between the tiers.
3. Pack. Bodies cover 50–73% of the frame and stop `g` from each other and from the frame; the central ninth is the densest (≥75% covered). Nothing floats in empty ground.
4. Build each body from ONE unit rotated `n` times about a centre, `n` an integer from 3 to 10. `n` is the body's kind: same kind, same `n`, whatever the unit looks like.
5. Grow outward by doubling: each ring holds twice the count of the ring inside it (4 → 8 → 16), new units interleaved between their two parents, or subdivide so that cells stay near-square (count ≈ 2πr ÷ cell width). When a unit would fall below 44px, cut the count, never the size.
6. Alternate two values of one hue family round a ring, so 2`n` sectors read as `n` pairs. Spend chroma last and inversely to area: the most saturated tone covers ≤6% (≤4% on an achromatic ground) and sits inside a body, on the part that is live, always with a shape or label.
7. Break the symmetry once at most: one long free line passing behind the bodies.
Why it works:
- Mirror symmetry about a vertical axis is detected before attention, so paired slots are read as one figure and the eye inspects half the page. That is what lets a field packed to 50–73% stay calm. Unequal partners (in size or height, not in content) turn the same density into clutter.
- A count is a category code that needs no colour, and doubling per ring holds the arc length of a unit constant as the circumference doubles. With equal wedges instead, cells thin to slivers at the core and bloat at the rim, and size starts to signal a rank that does not exist.
- One free element among strict repeats is salient by oddness. Two free elements are a second system, and the symmetry stops reading.
Colour relations: one hue family in many values does the drawing (a view is effectively monochrome); ground 40–45%, palest tint of the body hue ≈25%, near-neutrals ≈15–20%, mid chroma ≈10%, peak chroma ≤6% and enclosed; ground and figure may swap ends of the value scale with nothing else changing.
Space and line: axis first; hero 45–50% wide; tiers at 1 : 1/3; constant gutter 2–4%; cover 50–73%; hairline outline with value made by mark density, the finest detail at the margin of each body; a thin frame, with the caption apparatus outside it and centred.
Type requirement: two quiet voices on the axis (a name line and a smaller running line), strokes no heavier than the hairline drawing; labels are short keys (numbers), not prose; no text inside a patterned body.
Motion and interaction: replication, not travel. A body appears as one unit copied round its centre (40–60ms per copy); a level opens by doubling, the new units entering between the old; only the free line may drift. Reduced motion: the finished body at once.
Fails when: the items have no natural hero or cannot be paired (an odd, ranked or chronological list); the format is a narrow single column (no room for slots beside the axis); counts above ~16 per ring at touch size; long text has to live inside the bodies.
Signature (do not carry together): engraved marine organisms with stippled shading · a ruled plate with a Latin caption · curling tentacles and spines.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Service-health console for on-call engineers, dark, utility, desktop 1440 × 900 (the plates are pale, ornamental and still).
1. *Axis and gutter.* Vertical axis at 720px; `g` = 36px (2.5%).
2. *Hero.* The production region as one radial body 680px across (47%), centre 4% above the middle. Six satellites 210px across (0.31 of the hero) in three mirrored pairs: staging / canary, queue / cache, the two databases. Partners match in size and height, not in content.
3. *Pack.* Bodies cover about 58% of the frame; no card floats; the central ninth is 85% covered.
4. *Kind by count.* Stateless services are 4-fold bodies, queues 6-fold, datastores 3-fold. An engineer reads the kind from the count before reading the label.
5. *Doubling.* Hero rings: 4 zones → 8 clusters → 16 node groups, each group a 56px target. A 32-group ring would give 28px cells, so the fourth level opens as a list instead.
6. *Value and chroma.* One slate family; alternate sectors differ by 6 L. Chroma covers 3.5% of the screen: only the node groups with a live incident, each also carrying a glyph and a count.
7. *Free line.* The one traced request, drawn as a single path behind the bodies from the edge to the failing group.
- *Motion.* Drilling into a cluster doubles its ring, with new groups entering between parents at 50ms each. Reduced motion shows the ring complete.
- *Type.* Region name and timestamp centred under the field; ring cells carry numeric keys with a legend outside the frame.

Result: a dark radial console that nobody would caption with the artist's name. What survived is the axis with mirrored slots, the integer as category, doubling, and chroma only on what is live.

## Space & composition
- **Axis first, specimens second.** The vertical axis is fixed before anything else, and every slot has a mirror partner. The content of the partner may differ; its size and height may not.
- **Hero at about half the width.** On most plates (not Tafel 8, which has no central hero) one element spans ~45–50% of the frame width, centred or slightly high. Everything else is a fraction of it (≈1/3), never an in-between size.
- **Packed field.** Specimens touch the frame margin and nearly touch each other. The gutter is constant and narrow, about 2–4% of the frame width.
- **A frame line and a caption band.** A thin ruled frame holds everything; the title sits below in a centred band (Latin name, then German name), and the plate number sits above in the corner. The apparatus is quiet and symmetrical.
- **Ground can invert.** On Tafel 71 the ground is black and the skeletons white, with no other change to the layout logic.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

Colour runs on three relations. (1) One hue family per plate, in many values: Tafel 1 is all warm ochre and Tafel 18 all blue-grey, so a plate is effectively monochrome. (2) Where several hues appear, saturation is inverse to area. On Tafel 8 the paper ground is ≈43%, a pale tint of the body hue ≈24%, near-neutral blue-greys ≈17%, the coral of the long tentacles (C≈32) ≈10%, and the most saturated hue, orange gonads (C≈43), ≈6%, packed inside the bodies. (3) On dark-ground plates, ~96% of the area is achromatic (four greys from L16 to L65), and the only colour (≈4%, pink nuclei and yellow cells) sits inside the central skeletons, marking the living part. Value contrast does the drawing; colour marks function.

## Form, line & typography
The line is engraved: a hairline outline plus stippled or hatched fills, and density gives the value. Edges are never thick strokes. Marginal detail (spines, cilia, beads) is finer than the outline and breaks the silhouette into a soft halo. Type requirement: a calm, symmetrical apparatus of two voices. One is a roman serif or Latin small caps for the name, centred under the field. The other is a smaller italic for running heads. Labels are figure numbers, not prose. The face should have fine, even strokes that do not out-weigh hairline drawing (for example, an old-style serif such as EB Garamond). Never place text inside the patterned field.

## Rhythm, motion & time
The motion is rotation and replication. A body appears as one unit that is copied around its centre in sequence (n copies at ≈40–60 ms offsets). When it grows a level, its count doubles, and the new units interleave between the old ones. Nothing translates across the screen except the single "free" element, which may drift slowly. Reduced motion: show the finished body at once, with no rotation.

## UI translations
1. **Mirror slots around one axis.** Lay out a gallery or dashboard as a hero card (45–50% of the width) on the vertical axis, with satellites in left/right pairs of equal size and height. The pairs may hold different content.
2. **Size by thirds.** Make satellites ≈1/3 of the hero's width and the smallest tier ≈1/3 of that. Allow no size between tiers.
3. **n-fold icon system.** Build every icon in a set from one unit rotated n times (n = 3, 4, 5, 6, 8). Encode category by n, so the order of symmetry is the category.
4. **Doubling hierarchy for radial menus.** The inner ring has 4 actions, the next ring 8 and the outer ring 16; each new item sits between two parents. Keep targets at least 44 px at every ring, and reduce the count rather than the size.
5. **Square-keeping subdivision.** In radial or circular charts, increase the number of radial divisions at each larger radius so the cells stay near-square (≈2πr / cell width), rather than drawing equal wedges that get thinner toward the centre.
6. **Alternate value, not hue, between sectors.** In a 10-sector ring, alternate two values of one hue so the eye reads 5 pairs. State is shown by a mark or label, never by colour alone.
7. **Saturation inverse to area.** Give the screen one hue family; let the most saturated tone cover ≤6% of it and sit inside the most important object.
8. **Colour only for the living part.** On a dark, achromatic interface, colour only the elements that are live or active (≤4% of the area), always paired with a shape or text change.
9. **One free line.** Allow exactly one element (a connector, a timeline or a trailing path) to cross the symmetric layout diagonally, behind the cards.
10. **Packed gutter.** Use a constant narrow gutter (2–4% of the width) and let cards nearly fill the frame. Avoid floating cards in empty space.
11. **Captions below, centred.** Place a centred caption band below the field and a small running head above. Never print text over patterned specimen fills.

## What this artist would reject
- Asymmetric layouts with arbitrary sizes and no axis.
- Icons drawn freehand with no rotational logic.
- Equal saturation everywhere, or colour used as decoration on inert parts.
- Large empty grounds with one small object floating in them.
- Thick uniform outlines that flatten fine marginal detail.
