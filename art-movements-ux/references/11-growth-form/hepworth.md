# Barbara Hepworth — Growth & Form (biomorphism)

## Essence
Hepworth carved a closed organic mass and then opened it. The hole, or the hollow, is the subject: it lets light, colour and the viewer's eye inside the form, and makes inside and outside one continuous surface. Her rule is that one whole, smooth, natural mass is pierced once or twice, off-centre, and the inner surface is treated differently (paler, painted, strung), so depth is legible without any line.

## Key works examined
- **Pelagos, 1946 (elm and strings on oak base, 430 × 460 × 385 mm, Tate T00699).** A disc of elm that spirals in on itself like a wave or a shell. The interior is painted pale blue-white and crossed by taut strings. It teaches inside versus outside as two materials on one surface. *(visually checked: Tate collection image)*
- **Oval Sculpture (No. 2), 1943, cast 1958 (plaster, Tate T00953).** An egg pierced by two linked openings that tunnel through. It teaches several holes as one continuous passage. *(visually checked: Tate collection image)*
- **Sphere with Inner Form, 1963 (bronze, Tate T03146).** A hollow sphere opened by one large oval window, with a smaller pierced form held inside. It teaches nesting: a hollow inside a hollow. *(visually checked: Tate collection image, garden setting)*
- **Single Form (September), 1961 (walnut, Tate T03143).** A flat, tilted, rounded slab with one small shallow circular hollow near the upper right. It teaches how much a single small concavity weighs against a large plane. *(visually checked: Tate collection image)*
- **Single Form (Eikon), 1937–8, cast 1963 (bronze, Tate T00697).** A tall tapering blade on a block. It teaches a vertical whole with no piercing, the baseline the holes depart from. *(visually checked: Tate collection image)*
- **Pierced Form, 1931–2 (pink alabaster; destroyed).** Her first piercing. *(not obtained: destroyed in the Second World War; known from photographs)*
- **Single Form, 1961–4 (United Nations, New York).** *(not obtained: not searched; open images not found in the time available)*

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Whole first, then the hole** (all pierced works). The outer silhouette is one convex, smooth, closed curve (a disc, egg, sphere or rounded slab) with no protrusions. The opening is cut into it afterwards and never breaks the outline's convexity.
- **The hole is off-centre** (Pelagos, Single Form (September)). In Pelagos the opening's centroid sits ≈0.1 R right of and slightly above the disc's centre, and the shell is thickest on the left (≈0.15 of the diameter) and thins to a lip at the top right where the form curls in. In Single Form (September) one hollow ≈0.14 of the slab's width sits at ≈(0.71, 0.25) of its bounding box, near a third-line, high and to the outside.
- **Solid to void near half and half** (Pelagos). The elm covers ≈44% of the disc's bounding circle above the base line, the painted interior and the through-view the rest (Pillow; approximate, because the base cuts the bottom). In Sphere with Inner Form the window spans ≈2/3 of the sphere's diameter and the inner form's own hole ≈1/6 (by eye), a nested ladder of ≈1 : 0.68 : 0.17.
- **Inside 30–40 L away from outside, opposite in temperature** (lighter and cooler holds for Pelagos only). Exterior wood: L≈32–47, C≈20–30, warm. Interior paint: L≈73, C≈5, cool near-neutral. The step is about +30–40 L and a drop in chroma. In Oval Sculpture (one material, white plaster) the hollows are read only by shadow (L≈66–90 across the surface): light alone carries the depth.
- **Tension across the opening** (Pelagos). Strings, thin, taut and few (≈5–6), span the mouth from lip to lip. They are the only straight lines on the work.
- **Base as plinth, rectilinear** (all). Each organic form stands on a plain rectangular base, a clean orthogonal ground under the curved mass.
- **The generative rule:** make one closed, convex, smooth whole; cut one (at most two) openings off-centre, toward an upper outer third, sized from ≈1/7 of the width (a hollow) to ≈2/3 (a window); continue the outer surface unbroken into the inside; give the inside a quieter treatment 30–40 L away from the outside and opposite in temperature (in Pelagos, lighter and cooler); if the opening is wide, span it with a few straight tensioned lines; stand it on a plain rectilinear base. This works in any material, palette or medium.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Barbara Hepworth.** *Make one closed convex whole, then open it once, off-centre; the opening, finished as the inside of the same surface, becomes the subject.*
Process:
1. One mass per view: a closed, convex, smooth outline with no tab, notch or protrusion, the dominant shape on a plain ground. Seat it on one strict rectilinear base, which holds the only corners.
2. Cut ONE opening into the mass (two at most, and then joined as one passage). Choose its size by its job: about 1/7 of the mass's width for a hollow that only marks a place, up to 2/3 for a window that holds the content. The mass keeps at least 40% of its own area.
3. Displace the opening from the centre of the mass: a wide one by about 1/10 of the radius, a small one out to 0.6–0.75 across and 0.25–0.45 down. The surrounding wall then varies continuously between 0.05 and 0.15 of the diameter. The thinnest lip is the way in, and the control goes there.
4. Finish the inside as the same surface in a second state: value stepped 30–40 L away from the mass and chroma cut to a quarter or less, with no line at the lip. In a one-value system, model the hollow by shading on one side only (10–20 L).
5. Whatever enters the view from outside the mass's own palette (one accent, the live content) goes inside the opening, nowhere else.
6. If the opening is wide, span it lip to lip with 3–6 thin straight lines that show a relation between the two sides. They are the only straight lines on the mass.
7. Nest at most twice: each inner opening is about 1/4 of the one that holds it (2/3 → 1/6).
Why it works:
- The edge of a hole belongs to the shape around it, so what shows through is framed by the object and not placed on it. A badge on a card is a second object competing with the first; a hole is the same object pointing at its own content.
- A centred hole makes a ring: symmetrical, static, read as a symbol. Off-centre, the wall has a thick side and a thin side, which gives the mass a direction and the eye an entry at the lip.
- A convex outline has no feature of its own, so the only event on the mass is the opening. One protrusion adds a second focus on the outline and the opening loses.
Colour relations: outside the mass is one family, 2 values (lit and shadowed), the larger share (≈40% of the object) and the higher chroma; inside is the quieter state, about 15% of the view, opposite in temperature; one added colour at most, always inside; base darker than or equal to the mass.
Space and line: one mass; opening 1/7–2/3 of its width; offset ≈0.1 R, or toward an upper outer third; wall 0.05–0.15 of the diameter; no line anywhere on the mass except the 3–6 spanning ones; curved mass over a rectilinear base.
Type requirement: soft-shouldered letters with open counters, one weight per view, set as a compact block with a quiet closed outline on the solid part of the mass or on the base; never in the opening's rim zone and never across the spanning lines.
Motion and interaction: the opening dilates in place from the hollow size to the window size (400–600ms, ease-out); pointer, scroll or tilt turns the view a few degrees so more of the inside shows; nothing slides or bounces. The lip is the target: ≥44px wide, with a focus ring that follows its curve. Reduced motion: cross-fade to the open state.
Fails when: the view needs several peers (a grid of pierced cards is a tray of rings, and the single event is gone); the content is wider than 2/3 of any convex container (tables, long text); more than two levels of disclosure; a dense toolbar must sit on the mass.
Signature (do not carry together): carved wood or stone with a pale painted hollow · taut strings across the opening · an ovoid on a plinth.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Courier tracking screen, phone 390 × 844, near-black with an acid-lime accent, urgent and technical (the works are warm, slow and hand-made).
1. *Mass.* One order card: a superellipse 336 × 420px, convex, with no tabs or notches, in graphite (L 22). It stands on a rectangular action bar 390 × 96px (L 16), the only square-cornered element.
2. *Opening.* One window 216px wide (0.64 of the mass) holding the live map. The solid part keeps 52% of the card's area.
3. *Offset.* The window's centre sits 18px right of and 14px above the card's centre (about 0.1 R). The wall is 78px on the left (0.19 of the height, 0.23 of the width: over range, trimmed to 50px = 0.15) and 18px at the upper right. The "expand map" control sits on that thin lip as a 48px target.
4. *Inside.* The map surface is L 56, 34 L away from the card, with chroma at a fifth of the graphite's faint blue cast. No stroke at the lip.
5. *Accent.* Lime appears only inside the window: the courier dot (with a bearing arrow and a text ETA), about 1.2% of the screen. Outside the window nothing is lime, not even the primary button, which is a value step.
6. *Spanning lines.* Four thin straight lines cross the window from the pickup lip to the drop-off lip: the remaining route legs, each labelled with minutes at its end on solid card.
7. *Nest.* Tapping the courier opens one inner hollow 54px wide (1/4 of the window) with their photo. No third level.
- *Motion.* Before dispatch the window is a 48px hollow (1/7). On "out for delivery" it dilates in place to 216px over 500ms. Reduced motion cross-fades.
- *Type.* Order name and status in one weight, set as a compact block on the thick left wall.

Result: a dark, sharp-accented tracker that does not read as a sculpture. What survived is one convex whole, one off-centre cut, the inside as a quieter state of the same surface, and the accent only inside.

## Space & composition
- **One mass per view.** A single closed form fills ≈60–70% of the frame width, on a plain ground.
- **Opening high and outward.** The pierce sits off-centre, in the upper outer region (≈0.6–0.75 across, ≈0.25–0.45 down).
- **Thick and thin within one wall.** Wall thickness varies continuously around the opening (≈0.05–0.15 of the diameter); the thin lip marks where the inside is entered.
- **Nesting ratio.** A hollow inside a hollow steps down by ≈1/4 per level (2/3 → 1/6 of the diameter).
- **Rectilinear ground.** The organic mass stands on a strict rectangle; curve over orthogonal.

## Color
*The hex table below is a reference sample for strict studies and homage only. For design, use the colour relations in "Process (derived)".*

Two relations do all the work. (1) The outside is the material's own colour, mid-to-dark and warm (in Pelagos ≈40% of the visible object area); the inside is 30–40 L away and opposite in temperature; in Pelagos it is a lighter, cooler near-neutral (≈16% of the view plus the background seen through), so the hollow glows. (2) Where the material is one colour throughout, depth is carried by value from light alone, in a narrow band (Oval Sculpture: L≈66–90). There is never more than one added colour, and it is always inside. Hepworth wrote that colour in the concavities took her into depths of water and caves.

## Form, line & typography
Form is a closed convex curve with soft transitions and no corners except at the base. The only straight lines are tensioned strings across an opening. Type requirement: rounded, humanist, smooth-shouldered letters with open counters (the counters read as hollows), one weight for a whole view, set in a block whose outline is a quiet closed shape; never set text inside the "hollow" region or over a string pattern. For example, a humanist sans with soft curves, such as Nunito Sans or Source Sans 3.

## Rhythm, motion & time
Motion is slow, rotational and revealing: as the viewer moves (scroll, pointer or tilt) the hollow turns a few degrees and its lighter inside comes into view. Openings dilate rather than slide: a panel opens by its window widening from ≈1/7 to ≈2/3 of its width over 400–600 ms (ease-out). Nothing bounces. Reduced motion: switch to the open state with a cross-fade.

## UI translations
1. **Pierce the card, don't stack on it.** Give a primary card one off-centre window (≈1/7–2/3 of its width) at its upper outer third that reveals live content behind or inside, instead of adding a badge on top.
2. **Inside stepped and opposite.** Revealed or nested content sits on a surface 30–40 L away from its container, opposite in temperature and lower in chroma (lighter and cooler, as in Pelagos, is one option); the change in value, not a border, marks the inside.
3. **One added colour, inside only.** The container is neutral or material-coloured; the only accent colour appears within an opening, paired with a label or icon.
4. **Nest by quarters.** A disclosure inside a disclosure steps down ≈1/4 in size per level, and never goes more than two levels deep.
5. **Strings for relationships.** Show relationships between the two lips of an opening (start and end, before and after) as 3–6 thin straight lines spanning it.
6. **Convex silhouettes.** Containers are closed convex shapes (large radii, superellipses); no tabs, notches or protrusions break the outline.
7. **Curved mass on a rectilinear base.** Seat soft organic panels on a strict rectangular footer or toolbar.
8. **Light-only depth for monochrome themes.** In a one-material theme, show hollows by a soft inner shadow 10–20 L darker on one side only.
9. **Dilate to open.** Expanding content opens by its window widening in place (400–600 ms, ease-out), not by sliding in; reduced motion cross-fades.
10. **Hit areas on the lip.** The opening's edge is the target: at least 44 px wide, with a visible focus ring that follows the curve.

## What this artist would reject
- Flat rectangles stacked with badges and overlays.
- Holes centred and symmetrical, like a donut.
- Several unrelated accent colours on the outside.
- Hard borders where a value change would do.
- Jerky, sliding transitions that break the continuous surface.

## Signature prompt seed
Make each primary surface one smooth, closed, convex mass and open it once, off-centre and high toward the outside. Through the opening, show the live content on a surface 30–40 L away from the container and opposite in temperature, the only place an accent colour appears. Let the outer surface continue unbroken into the inside, thick on one side and thin at the lip. Span wide openings with a few taut straight lines that show relationships. Seat the soft forms on a strict rectangular base. Openings dilate slowly in place.
