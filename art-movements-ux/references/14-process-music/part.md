# Arvo Pärt — Process Music

*Evidence notes only. Nobody listened for this file. Figures come from recordings measured with scripts, from sheets (waveform plus log-pitch spectrogram) read by eye, from scans of scores and sketches, and from published analyses. Claims about instruments or mood carry their source. The earlier agy listening attempts all failed (quota or interrupted) and are not used.*

## Essence
Pärt binds two voices so that neither is free. One walks up and down a plain scale by step (the M-voice) and the other may sound only the three notes of one triad (the T-voice), chosen for each melody note by a fixed rule. A counting rule (add one note per phrase, double every duration, rotate by one) then decides length, range and end of the whole piece, and one low tone, bell, drone or silence marks the turns. The change is very small each time, predictable in kind, and usually arrives in mirrored pairs; the few places where the rule is broken, and the silence at each edge, carry the weight.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **One note more, then one note less.** Für Alina 1→8→2(3); Fratres 4, 6, 8 chords; Spiegel phrases 4–11 bars in pairs; Cantus A, A G, A G F; Ludus sections 25.8 → 86.5 s; Silentium 9.8 → 66 s. The step is constant (one note, one bar, about 10 s), so growth is linear, not faster.
- **Growth comes in equal mirrored pairs.** Spiegel (up phrase, then down phrase, same length), Silentium intervals 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3 (measured in two recordings), Fratres halves (fall, then rise), Summa (SA–SATB–TB, then reversed), Ludus canons. A mirror never costs extra time.
- **Opposed counts.** Ludus: sections lengthen by about 11 s each while silences shrink from 8 to 1.8 s. Für Alina: gaps grow with phrases. Length and emptiness are both numbers in the rule.
- **The home tone.** Every phrase of Spiegel ends on A (last A 578 s); every Silentium cycle passes D, announced by the bell; every Fratres chord row begins and ends at its start pitch; every Cantus line restarts at A. The melody is always a trip away from and back to one note.
- **A bell marks each arrival.** Low F octave at 15.0, 33.6, 52.7 s (Spiegel); piano roll and low D at the Silentium turns; refuges in Fratres (nine); bell in Cantus (48-beat cycle); bass octave once in Für Alina. One sound, one function, always the same, at increasing distances.
- **Two voices, one rule.** The T-voice uses only B D F♯ (Für Alina), A C E (Cantus, Fratres), E G B (Summa), D F A (Silentium, Te Deum), and is set by the M-voice note: nearest triad note below, or above, or alternating. The T-voice never exists without its M-voice (Krawczyk). Each instrument or hand keeps one role for the whole piece.
- **Same line, scaled.** Cantus 1 : 2 : 4 : 8 : 16 with octaves; Silentium 1 : 2 : 4; Arbos three sizes on one page. Scale in time and register moves together, but the direction may flip: fastest on top (Cantus), fastest at the bottom (Silentium).
- **Loop and frame that do not divide.** Summa's 15-note loop in a 16-note frame; Fratres's circle of 7 moved two steps at a time. Both return exactly only after many cycles, or never.
- **A surface change on an unchanged base.** Fratres keeps one theme, drone and refuge for 11 minutes while only the solo's figuration changes: density 0.6 to 7 onsets per second, −54 to −20 dBFS.
- **The exception is single and late.** Für Alina's C♯ in bar 11 of 15 (measured, followed by the longest gap), its 3-note last bar; Cantus's basses shortened; Summa's last two bars. The rule holds everywhere else, so the exception can be pointed at.
- **The end is a subtraction.** Cantus stops on a held chord and uncovers a bell; Silentium loses layers from the bottom and leaves out its last D; Te Deum decays to fragments over 150 s; Spiegel ends on the accompaniment figure alone (608–612 s).
- **Test rules, not notes.** Brauneiss: if a result fails, Pärt alters the whole rule set; the page 101 Passio sketch tries three versions, marks them X, (X), (X) and writes "recept" (recipe).

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Arvo Pärt.** *Bind two layers by one rule: a lead that walks by single steps away from one home value and back, and a companion that may take only three fixed values and is set by the lead; lengthen each trip by one unit in mirrored pairs, mark every return with one unchanging sign set apart by empty space, count the gaps in the same rule, break the rule once and late, and end by taking away.*
Process:
1. Fix the home and the ladder. One home value (a column, a level, a state) and one ordered ladder of equal steps on both sides of it. Every sequence in the view ends on home, and home never moves. If home changes, every later step is redone.
2. Bind two layers. The lead may stand on any rung but moves only to an adjacent one. The companion may take only three fixed values spread across the ladder, and for each lead value ONE rule picks it: the nearest of the three below, the nearest above, or the two in alternation. The rule is chosen once for the view. A companion is never shown without its lead, and each layer keeps one channel (a position, a second line, a bar) from start to end.
3. Reserve a band with a hole in front of it. The marker of step 5 gets a band of its own at one edge, parted from the content by an empty band 0.6–1 times as wide as the content band at its widest. Nothing else is ever put in the hole.
4. Grow by one, in mirrored pairs. A trip is a run of units that ends on home. Trips come in twos of equal length, one reaching home from below and one from above, and each pair is one unit longer than the last (1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3). Run 3–8 sizes. The step is constant, so growth is linear and never speeds up, and a mirror costs exactly what its original cost. Stop at the longest, or walk back down by the same steps.
5. Mark each arrival. One sign, identical every time, appears once whenever the lead is on home, and nowhere else and for no other reason; its spacing therefore widens as the trips grow. A separator between modules obeys the same law: constant size (0.2–0.3 of a module) and constant level while the modules vary.
6. Count the emptiness. The gap after each trip is a number in the rule, in one direction chosen once: it grows with the trip (to about 1.75 times across a fourfold growth of the trip), or it shrinks by one unit each time while the trips lengthen (8 down to 1). Gaps take 10–50% of the run. A layer with nothing to show keeps its slot, drawn empty, and a gap is never filled.
7. Vary one surface only. If the view needs variety, a single layer may change its density from section to section (up to about 12 : 1) while home, the three companion values, the marker and the separator stay exactly as they were.
8. Break the rule once, late. At 60–87% of the run, most often near three quarters, one companion value falls outside its three, or one count is off by a small whole number. The gap that follows is the longest in the view, about 1.4 times the next longest, and a small sign sits beside the exception. There is no second one.
9. End by subtraction. Remove layers one at a time from one side until the thinnest layer or the marker stands alone, leave out the last unit the rule predicts, and keep an empty interval after it of about 3% of the run.
Scaled mode (in place of step 4): show the same line 3–5 times at rates 1 : 2 : 4 (up to 16), each copy one band further along and entering after a delay that doubles each time; rate and band move together, in one direction chosen once, and no copy ever changes its rate.
Rotation mode (in place of step 4): put a loop of n − 1 items in a frame of n slots (15 in 16), or move the start by a stride that does not divide the ring (2 on 7), so that each row begins one position later and the first state returns only after n rows (or 7). Stack the rows, frame the part that has moved and put a small cross on the item that has not.
Why it works:
- Grouping by likeness: whatever the lead does, one of three known values stands beside it, so every state of the view belongs to one family and the lead's movement is the only news. Free the companion and there are two lines to follow; remove it and the lead has nothing to be measured against; give it five values and it stops being a constant.
- Scale reference: every trip ends on the same point, so its length is read as distance from home without counting. Let trips end anywhere and growth reads as drift.
- Adaptation to the previous state: the first trip of a pair teaches a size and the second confirms it from the other side, so the one added unit of the next pair is found with no emphasis. Unpaired growth has to be counted; growth that speeds up promises a peak that the rule never delivers.
- Salience by oddness: a marker that never varies carries one fact, where. Vary its form and it becomes a third layer; use it for anything but arrival and the widening distance between two markers no longer measures the growth.
- Figure and ground: an empty band as wide as the content turns the marker into a different kind of thing by position alone, with no second colour. Close the hole and the marker is one more unit.
- A gap is where the last group is taken in. Tied to the group's size, it gives long groups more room; equalised, the long groups crowd; filled, there is no boundary left to see.
- An exception is odd only against a rule that is already known, which takes most of the run. Centre it and the rule is not yet proven; multiply it and it is a second rule; the longest gap after it gives the eye time to check that it was meant.
- Removing layers from a full state uncovers what was under them all along, so the last thing seen is the constant. An ending that adds reads as a climax, and nothing before it was built to carry one.
Colour relations: the ground lies at one end of the value scale with low chroma and takes everything that is not marked; marks are one colour and stay under 5% of the surface. A second neutral close to the ground's value may hold one panel of about a third. Hue stands for a role (a layer or a function), never for an item, each hue under 5%, and only in working views; a finished view has one mark colour and at most one hue, under 1%. The marker differs by band and position, not by colour, and the exception carries a sign, not a tint.
Space and line: one home; two layers plus a marker; three companion values; adjacent steps only; trips in equal mirrored pairs, +1 unit per pair, 3–8 sizes; marker band parted by a hole 0.6–1 of the content band; separator 0.2–0.3 of a module; gaps 10–50% of the run; one density-varying layer, up to 12 : 1; one exception at 60–87% followed by a gap 1.4 times the next longest; last predicted unit left out, then about 3% empty. Rows are as long as their content and are never stretched to match; an unused slot keeps its rule.
Type requirement: two values only, short and long, shown as two forms of one size (a filled and an open form), not as two sizes; the face needs both forms on one width, so that exchanging them moves no neighbour. One weight. A line keeps its measure while its units keep their natural width, so later lines hold more. Dividers fall after a word or a group, drawn lighter than the text, never at a fixed interval. One instruction per view, set large and alone; title and subtitle share one size and differ by value.
Motion and interaction: nothing runs unprompted and nothing loops; trips, pairs and gaps are in the layout. A change moves the lead one rung in 240ms and the companion follows in half that, 120ms: long then short, 2 : 1, the only rhythm. An arrival sets the marker in one step and holds it, with no pulse and no repeat. Counts are exact and timing is the user's, so no timer advances a sequence; where a message must leave by itself, its dwell follows step 6, about 3 s plus 0.4 s per unit it holds. In scaled mode the copies enter 60, 120, 240 and 480ms apart. At the end layers leave one at a time, 240ms each, the last state is cut in 100ms or less, and the view stays empty. Reduced motion: every change is instant and in the same order; nothing is lost, because pairs, marker, gaps and exception are positions, not movements.
Fails when: fewer than 3 sizes or 6 trips fit, so no pair can be compared with the next; the values have no order or no natural home (unranked categories); the companion needs more than three values (many statuses); the view must be dense, since gaps under a tenth of the run stop reading as counted; several things are urgent at once, since there is one exception and the marker may not be borrowed for emphasis; the task must get faster with practice, because trips and gaps only lengthen; the brief asks for a peak or a hero.
Signature (do not carry together): a slow stepwise line shadowed by the three notes of one chord · a low bell stroke followed by a long ringing silence · stemless dots on cream manuscript paper with empty staves left in.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Eight-week strength plan on a gym-floor tablet, landscape 1024 × 768: black ground, the brand's acid lime, heavy condensed capitals, loud where the works are quiet. No sound, no notation, no pale paper.
1. *Home and ladder.* Home is the athlete's working load; the ladder is one plate step (2.5 kg) above and below it. Home is one column, x 512–592, and every week ends in it.
2. *Two layers.* The lead is the load of each session, one cell 80 × 40px, moving one plate step from cell to cell. The companion is the rest between sets, which may only be 60, 120 or 180 s, always the nearest of the three below the session's rung; it is a second line in the same cell and never appears alone.
3. *Hole.* Content band x 32–592 (seven cells). Empty band x 592–968 (376px, 0.67 of the content band). Marker column x 968–992.
4. *Pairs.* One row per week, right-aligned to the home column: 4, 4, 5, 5, 6, 6, 7, 7 sessions. The first week of each pair climbs to the working load from below and the second comes down to it from above.
5. *Marker.* One 24px square in the marker column on the line of each week that has reached the working load. It has one form and one value, and marks nothing else.
6. *Emptiness.* The gap under a week is 4px per session: 16, 16, 20, 20, 24, then 40 (step 8), 28. Gaps are 164 of the 484px the plan takes (34%). Short weeks leave their left cells empty, with the cell rule still drawn.
7. *One surface.* Each cell may list 1 to 7 exercises in small type as the weeks go on; cell size, rest values and marker do not change.
8. *Exception.* Week 6 of 8 holds one test session whose rest reads "as needed", outside the three values. A small ring stands beside it and the gap under that week is 40px, 1.4 times the next longest. No lime, no larger type.
9. *End.* Week 8 shows loads only, with the rest line gone; its home cell is an empty outline and has no marker. Below it 196px stay empty.
- *Colour.* Numerals and hairlines in one pale neutral, about 4% of the screen. Lime is the plan's title only, under 1%.
- *Type.* One weight of the brand's condensed face: done sessions filled, coming sessions in outline, both 20px. One instruction line, set large, in the 88px header.
- *Motion.* Logging a session fills its cell in 240ms and the rest line settles 120ms later. A week that reaches home gets its square in one step. Nothing advances by timer.
- *Reduced motion.* The same, instant.

Result: a black and lime training board with a ragged left edge and a column of identical squares far to the right. What survived is one home column, a companion with three values, pairs that grow by one, an unchanging marker across a hole, gaps that are counted, one late exception and an ending with something left out. Nobody would caption it with a composer's name or call it a music visualiser.

## Space & composition (texture, register, layering, the frame of the piece)
- Register is split with a hole in it: Für Alina's bass octave sits well below both hands with nothing between; Spiegel's triads, bells above and bells below; Te Deum's drone near 147–221 Hz under voices from 147 Hz upward, with a high breath band near 3–4 kHz.
- Layers are few and separately counted: 2 (Für Alina), 3 (Spiegel: solo, triads, bells), 4–5 (Fratres), 9 string lines + bell (Cantus), 3 + piano (Silentium), 2 or 4 voices at a time (Summa), 2–6 (Magnificat).
- Frame: silence is part of it. Ludus opens with 12 s of file silence, one chord and 8 s of rest; Cantus opens with bell and rest, ends in 10 s of nothing; Silentium's printed end is four bars of rest; Te Deum takes 24 s to rise above −60 dBFS.
- Density per block: 0.2–1.8 onsets/s (Für Alina, Cantus in strings is 0), 1–4 (Spiegel), 0.2–7.6 (Fratres). The sparsest textures use the fewest onsets but the longest decays.
- Wholes: a pyramid (Für Alina), a cone opening downward (Cantus), wedge around an axis (Silentium, Spiegel, Magnificat around C), a row of unequal bars with equal separators (Fratres), a staircase (Ludus).

## Form, line & typography (scores, notation, sleeves)
- Notation is as bare as the music: stemless dots and open heads, two durations, no metre (Für Alina); long values written as chains of tied notes (Cantus basses: eight whole notes tied, per Krawczyk); dashed barlines after words (Magnificat); squares for the double-length notes of Arbos. A bar is a phrase or a word, so bars have unequal widths.
- Proportion on the page equals structure: Für Alina's systems hold 2+3, 4+5, 6+7 notes; Te Deum's plan rows are lengths of sections; Arbos puts the same long–short cell on the page at three note sizes (half and quarter, whole and half, square double-whole and whole), slowest lowest, over a tremolo line with accents at the group starts.
- Empty staves stay on the page: one blank staff between systems (Für Alina), rests drawn in every silent staff (Magnificat, Tabula rasa's opening). Tabula rasa's first page places the music in the right 30% and leaves the rest as blank staves with a large cursive title.
- Colour is for reasoning, black or brown is for the copy: diaries use brown, blue, red, orange and green; the fair copies one ink.
- Frames and arrows: boxes for what rotates, crosses for what stays fixed, arrows up and down for direction, an X for a rejected rule, circles for a total. The pitch map (Patrick, on the Centre's page) draws the soprano and tenor (the two T-voices) as diamonds on a black grid, meeting and crossing at the centre row, with an overlap in green-brown: a symmetrical weave.
- Handwriting: large, even, widely spaced titles; dynamics set large and few (one p on the first page of Für Alina; pp and p only on Spiegel's).
- Sleeve: ECM 1275, centred sans capitals with worn edges, artist (grey) over title (pale blue) at one size in the top fifth, then a grey grained square about 65% of the sleeve's width and height, starting about 19% down, with works and performers in white at one size (capitals for works). A tiny label name sits under it. No picture.

- Change is slow relative to the pulse: Spiegel needs about 19 s for its first complete phrase and 52 s for its last; Cantus's bass needs about 20 s for two notes.
- Silence is a timed value: 8 half notes (7.6 s) down to one; 3.6–8.8 s; a hard stop at 286.2 s with 10 s after it.
- Loudness has one shape per piece: single arch that peaks late (Cantus; Te Deum at 87%; Fratres at about 60%), or a flat line (Silentium, −31 to −36 dBFS for nine minutes), or a stair (Ludus, −28 to −17).
- Tempo is fixed inside a piece; slowing is written as longer values (Cantus) or left to the last bars (Spiegel: 4.7 to 5.0 s a bar).
- Rhythmic variety is two values: long–short (2 : 1) is the commonest cell (Cantus, Silentium, Arbos); else a single repeated value with one long at the end (Für Alina, Fratres).
- Attack timing inside a rule is human: Für Alina's inter-note gaps vary by 2 : 1 while its counts never vary.

## UI translations
1. **Two layers:** Give every free value a companion that may take only three fixed values, picked by one rule (nearest below, nearest above, or alternating), and never show the companion alone. Für Alina uses three pitches for the whole second hand; Summa's rule set rebuilds over 93% of the notes.
2. **Home:** End every sequence, row or flow on the same value or column. All sixteen phrases of Spiegel im Spiegel end on one note, the last at 578 s.
3. **Growth:** Lengthen a series by one unit per pair and show each size twice, mirrored (4, 4, 5, 5 … bars in Spiegel; intervals of 1, 1, 2, 2 … 7, 7 units in Silentium, the same in two recordings). Do not accelerate.
4. **Marker:** Mark each return with one unchanging sign and use it for nothing else (the low octave at 15.0, 33.6 and 52.7 s in Spiegel). The widening distance between signs is the progress display.
5. **Hole:** Put the marker in a band of its own and leave between it and the content an empty band 0.6–1 of the content's extent (Für Alina: one and a half to two and a half empty octaves under about two and a half sounding).
6. **Gaps:** Size the space after a group from the group: grow it with the group (3.6 s after two notes, 6.3 s after eight) or shrink it by one unit per section while the sections lengthen (Ludus: 8 half-note rests down to 1, sections 25.8 to 86.5 s). An unused row or slot stays visible and empty.
7. **Separator:** Between modules of varying length use one separator of constant size and level, about a quarter of a module (Fratres: 13–16 s against 48–72 s, always near the same level).
8. **One variable surface:** Let a single layer change density from section to section (Fratres: 0.6 to 7 onsets per second) and hold every other layer still.
9. **Exception:** Break the rule in one place, about three quarters through (bar 11 of 15), give it the longest gap in the view (8.8 s against 6.3 s) and a small sign; no colour, no size change.
10. **Ending:** Close a flow by removing layers until the thinnest remains, leave out the last expected unit, and keep an empty interval (Cantus: 10 s after a hard stop; Silentium: the final note omitted, four bars of rest).
11. **Scaled copies:** Show one series at 1 : 2 : 4 in separate bands, slowest furthest from the fastest, entering at doubling delays (Cantus: 1.4, 2.7, 5.1, 10.4 s).
12. **Rotation:** For a rota or a featured slot, run n − 1 items through n slots so each cycle starts one later (Summa: 15 in 16, sixteen sections); stack the cycles and frame what moved.
13. **Colour:** One mark colour under 5% of the surface on a low-chroma ground; a hue only for a role, under 5%, and on a finished view at most one hue under 1% (autograph ink 4.2–4.5%; sleeve hue 0.7%).
14. **Type:** Two forms of one size for two values (filled and open); rows as wide as their content; dividers after a word or group; one instruction per view, set large.
15. **Motion:** Step the lead in 240ms and let the companion follow in 120ms (the 2 : 1 long–short cell). Nothing loops and no timer advances a sequence; a self-dismissing message stays about 3 s plus 0.4 s per item.
16. **Accessibility:** Under reduced motion every change is instant and nothing is lost, since the pattern is in positions. The marker and the exception each carry a text label, and the empty band is not a place for focusable controls.

## What this composer would reject
Grounded in the observed practice and his own words, not extrapolated to any other field:
- Writing that chooses each note freely; a note with no rule behind it. Brauneiss reports that every note should result from the system and be equally loved.
- Growth that speeds up, a build that arrives at a spectacular peak. All measured growth is linear, equal in steps, and often ends in less sound.
- Ornament or filler where a voice has a rest: rests keep their staff and their count.
- Several colours or themes competing: one triad per piece, one scale, one bell role.
- Ending on a full close that uses everything: the endings remove.
- Personal display: for Spiegel, the Centre reports Pärt asking composer and player alike to put the ego aside.
- Novelty between sections without a common frame; Fratres keeps the drone, the theme and the refuge while the surface alone varies.

## Signature prompt seed
Design the screen around one home column that every row ends on. Give each value a companion that may take only three fixed values, chosen by one rule, and never show it alone. Let rows grow by one unit in mirrored pairs of equal length. Mark each return home with one small unchanging sign in a band of its own, across an empty band at least 0.6 of the content's width. Size the gap after each row from the row and keep unused slots visible and empty. Use one mark colour under 5% of a quiet low-chroma ground and at most one hue under 1%. Break the rule once, about three quarters through, followed by the longest gap. End by removing layers and leaving the last expected unit out. Nothing loops; changes land as a long step and a short one, 2 : 1.
