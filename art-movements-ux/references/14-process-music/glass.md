# Philip Glass — Process Music

*How this was read: nobody listened. Every timing comes from measurement of recordings (onset, loudness, brightness, and self-similarity of the pitch spectrum, which gives the cycle length), from waveform and spectrogram sheets, and from published scores, transcriptions and analyses. Instrument names and moods are taken from sources and tagged. "Pulse" means the fastest even note. Cycle lengths in pulses are measured seconds divided by measured pulse; they came out within 0.2 of a whole number in almost every window of the early pieces. Several recordings are unofficial uploads of commercial discs; the performance date is stated only where a source gives it. The earlier Gemini listening runs all failed and are not used.*

## Essence
Glass fixes one even, unbroken pulse and a tiny stock of notes, repeats a figure until it is known, then changes only its length: a note or a small group is added to or taken from the figure, and the new figure is repeated in turn. Everything else (loudness, register, tone colour, tempo) is held nearly level, so the length of the figure is the event. From 1971 the additions are made to fit a fixed cycle, and from 1976 a short looping chord progression carries them; after 1980 the loop itself stays fixed and the change moves to section order and loudness.

## Detailed observations

**Two Pages.** One continuous stream of even notes; no rest and no sustained note is visible on either sheet. The first figure is five rising notes, x x x x x, G C D E♭ F (transcription; on the sheet the lowest partial at about 196 Hz returns every 0.85 s). Pulse 0.170 s, 5 pulses, 0.85 s a cycle. Each step holds about 28–30 s, so the repeat count falls from about 33 for the 5-note figure to about 12 for the 14-note one: time per step is kept, not the number of repeats. ## Process and relations (observed)
- **One pulse, never broken.** In Two Pages, Fifths, Similar Motion the whole ensemble plays one even subdivision with no rest from the first attack to the last. Pulse 0.13–0.18 s in the three; nothing is notated shorter or longer than that unit.
- **The figure is fixed while it repeats.** Within a step the cycle length is constant to within about 0.2 pulse in every measured window (Fifths 10, 12, 15 … 58; Similar Motion 15 steps). Change arrives whole, at a cycle boundary; no drift between parts was measured (Train does run 4 or 5 against 3 until they realign, per Gann).
- **Change is a step in length, not in level.** Loudness within a step sequence moves by under 2 dB (Two Pages −33, Fifths −30, Twelve Parts −22, Knee Play 1 −32.5, Koyaanisqatsi −25).
- **Four ways of changing the length, each used by itself for a stretch.** Append the same material shortened (Two Pages 5, 9, 12, 14); add one note (6, 7, 8 … 13; Trial 7–12); insert or repeat a group by a constant step (16, 22, 28, 34, +6; 14, 26, 38, 50, +12); double the tag (Similar Motion 12, 16, 24, 40, 72, 136). Rubin's and Gann's charts read the same four on paper.
- **Grow then return by the same path, or grow and strip.** Two Pages: 5, 9, 12, 14, 12, 9, 5. Twelve Parts: 5, 6, 7, 8, 6, 12, 16, 10, 6, 4 beats, run twice. Trial: 7 … 12 … 31 … 12 … 7, then up again to 12. Similar Motion: 8 → 41 → 12 → 136. Music in Fifths is the exception: it only grows, 8 to 210.
- **Seconds per step are more constant than repeats per step.** Two Pages: 28–30 s a step while the cycle triples. Similar Motion: 20–30 s for most steps, about 47–55 s for the frozen ones where a voice enters. Twelve Parts: 140 s on the plateau of 8.
- **The plateau is where something else can change.** Voices enter only while the pattern is still (Similar Motion 250–297 s and the 12-pulse plateaus); the Knee Play 1 loop is fixed from 49 s and the voices build over it.
- **Tempo is slightly elastic, on one scale only.** Pulse tightens 8–33% over minutes (Two Pages 0.172 → 0.128 s with one reset), never within a step; a new section sets it back.
- **Sameness of surface, changes of block by cut.** Parts join with no transition: Twelve Parts at 1,075 s; Spaceship at 70.7, 271.3 and 512 s (17 dB, about −5 dB, +5 dB); Similar Motion's bass entry. The cut is placed between two cycles.
- **One loop, independent lengths per chord (1976).** Knee Play 1: 4+6+8; Spaceship: F then D♭ then A stretch one after another while E stays at 0.48 s; Bed: four columns of sums (Gann). Additive process moves from melody to harmony. Glass describes the same move for Another Look at Harmony: a five-chord cadence put through an additive process, each chord returning with 3, 4, 5 or 6 beats (Contact, paraphrased).
- **Cyclic fitting (1971–74).** Glass on Part 5 of Twelve Parts: "All these rhythmic groupings have to fit the cycle"; they expand and contract until the start is reached again (Contact). Twelve Parts' measured lengths are all whole beats and its cycles 5, 6, 7, 8, 6, 12, 16, 10, 6, 4 close the loop.
- **Fixed ground, entries on the round (1982).** Koyaanisqatsi: 14.69 s held for 200 s; new layers begin at 7.3 s spacings on the bass changes. Opening: 34.8 s blocks; layer speeds 3 : 2 : 1 never move.
- **Block alternation with silence (1982–88).** Opening: 12 blocks each ending in a 6–10 dB dip. Metamorphosis One: an 89 s block repeated three times with a ~1 s gap of −45 to −53 dB between loud pairs and soft eighths. Loudness becomes a two-state property of a block, not a gradient.
- **Notation of counts.** Number sums and "×n" stand for music in Glass's hand: 8(4+4)×2 … (Knee Play 3), boxed ×12 (Building). Numbers and solfège also became the chorus text because they were the memory aid (Wikipedia, citing Glass).
- **The process is not a strict algorithm in 1976.** Gann shows three different routes from the same A♭ to F in Train, a 37-row Building plan with no predictable next step, and Dance 1 as 17+16+17 parallel phrases; he quotes Tom Johnson calling the additions whimsical. In our measurements the late-1960s pieces were predictable in pulses, the 1976 ones only in loops.
- **The generative pattern seen:** keep one fast even pulse; write a short figure from a few notes; repeat it until it is heard as fixed; at a cycle boundary change only its length by one named rule, and hold for roughly the same number of seconds; when a rule is exhausted, return or cut to a new block; let layers enter only while the figure is still; hold loudness and register flat so the length is the only thing that moves.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Philip Glass.** *Fix one pitch and a small stock, repeat a short figure until it is known, then change only its length: one named rule at a time, whole units at one end, at a boundary, with size, weight and tint held level; let anything else enter only while the length is still, and join blocks by a cut.*
Process:
1. Fix one pitch and one stock. A single smallest unit (a cell of one width, a row of one height, or one tick of time) serves the whole view, and every size and every moment of change is a whole multiple of it. No half units, and no gap inside a run. The stock is 3–5 kinds of unit and nothing is added to it later. If the pitch changes, every later step is redone.
2. Write the base figure: one group of 4–8 units from the stock. Show it unchanged at least 5 times (as repeated rows, or held over 5 ticks) before any variant appears, so that it is known and can serve as the measure.
3. Lock everything except count. Unit size, weight, value, tint and spacing are the same in every repeat and every variant of a block; any variation in them stays under about a tenth. No new unit is marked, accented or announced.
4. Choose ONE length rule for a run and name it: add one unit (8–20% of the figure); append a copy of the figure less its last unit; insert the same group of k units each time; or double a tag (+4, +8, +16). Run it for 4–8 stages, to between 2 and 4.5 times the base. New units always join at the same end and old units keep their column. Inside a run the rule is exact; which rule comes next is a free choice.
5. End the run in one of three ways: walk back by the same stages; strip from the old end so that what remains is the newest part; or stop at the longest stage.
6. Give every stage the same room, not the same number of repeats: an equal span of the view, or an equal dwell. A short figure is seen many times and a long one a few times (33 down to 5).
7. Change a second thing only on a plateau. A new layer, a new band or a new kind of unit enters only while the count is still; it starts on a cycle boundary or its half, takes a band of its own that overlaps no other (the lowest band is kept for the ground), and the plateau lasts about twice a normal stage.
8. Join blocks by a cut. A block sits flat at one of two levels (full or quiet) and the next block starts whole at the boundary, with no ramp or blend. A short empty interval, about 1% of the block, may stand at the join and nowhere else.
9. State the count. Beside each stage write its length as a sum and a multiplier ("5+3+3", "×12"), and stack the stages so that identical units share a column: additions show as marks spreading at one end, removals as blank at the other.
Loop mode (in place of steps 4–5): a ring of 3–5 members with unequal whole-unit lengths in a simple ratio (2 : 3 : 4). Reach it by shortening in two or three stages, or stretch one member at a time to about double while at least one member never moves. Once set, the ring stays fixed and step 7 applies.
Why it works:
- Adaptation: five or more identical repeats set what the eye expects, so one added unit is found by oddness with no emphasis at all. Accent the new unit and the accent is read in place of the length; show the variant before the figure is known and there is nothing to measure it against.
- Grouping by likeness: with size, weight and tint level, every repeat belongs to one texture and count is the only difference left. Let level move with length and the change reads as a swell, which hides where each stage begins.
- Alignment as scale reference: units that keep their column let the eye subtract one stage from the next, so growth is read as extent at one end. Reflow the row and every unit has moved, and the difference has to be counted.
- One change at a time can be attributed. A layer that enters while the count is still is read as an arrival; entering during a length change, neither is read. The plateau is long because the eye needs the old figure confirmed under the new layer.
- Equal room per stage keeps the form even: with equal repeats the long stages would take 3 to 25 times the room of the short ones and the end would swallow the beginning.
- A cut keeps level a category with two values. A ramp makes it a third variable and promises a climax that the process never delivers.
Colour relations: tint is a constant, not a variable: one mark colour on one ground inside a block, marks about a tenth of the surface. Layers are told apart by band (position and value range), never by a hue per item, and bands do not overlap; the lowest and darkest band belongs to the ground layer. A block has one of two levels, differing by a clear step, and a shift of tint happens only at a block boundary, where it means "new block". No colour marks what is new.
Space and line: one pitch; base figure 4–8 units; 5 or more repeats before a change; 4–8 stages a run; growth 2–4.5 times; one rule a run; additions at one end; equal room per stage; plateau about 2 times a stage; ring ratios 2 : 3 : 4; hard start and hard end with no fade; an empty interval only at a block join, about 1% of the block; density level inside a block and stepped between blocks.
Type requirement: every unit is the same symbol: one size, one weight, no emphasis style. The face needs figures of equal width and a sum sign that sets cleanly, since counts are text ("5+4+3", "×12"). Grouping is shown by a bracket or a rule over the group, not by weight. Labels sit beside the stage they count and are stepped in on a fixed indent.
Motion and interaction: nothing runs unprompted; the repeats are in the layout. A change lands whole in one step of 100ms or less, with no easing and no highlight, and only at a boundary: live updates are held and applied on a fixed tick (chosen once per product), one unit at one end, the rest keeping their columns. After a change the state holds for one dwell before the next is applied, and for two dwells when a layer has entered. Blocks swap in 100ms or less with no crossfade. Reduced motion: the same, with changes instant; nothing is lost, because nothing depended on movement.
Fails when: fewer than 5 repeats of the figure fit; the units are unequal (prose, media, mixed cards), so count cannot be the variable; one item must win the first glance or a change is urgent, since nothing new may be marked; items differ in kind, not in quantity; a figure grows beyond one line or one view (over about 30 units) and wraps, which breaks the columns.
Signature (do not carry together): a fast unbroken stream of identical marks that runs by itself · numbers and counting syllables set as the display text · ruled staves with beamed groups stepping out to one side.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Membership tiers for a co-working space, desktop 1280 × 800, in the brand's forest green on off-white with a rounded, friendly sans. No sound, no stream of marks, no staff lines.
1. *Pitch and stock.* One row unit of 40px and one column of 188px. The stock is three kinds of cell: included, included with a number, empty. A 280px label column on the left, five tier columns, 30px margins.
2. *Base figure.* The Day tier is 5 included cells. The same five rows sit at the top of all five columns, so the base is seen five times side by side under a 120px header.
3. *Locked.* Every cell is 188 × 40px in the same mid green at the same weight. No column is tinted, raised, widened or badged "most popular".
4. *One rule.* Insert a group of 3: the tiers are 5, 8, 11, 14 and 17 rows (3.4 times the base; 17 × 40 = 680px under the header). New rows always join at the bottom, so a benefit sits on the same row in every column.
5. *End.* Stop at the longest; the cells under the shorter tiers stay empty, not crossed out.
6. *Equal room.* All five columns are 188px wide although the last holds more than three times the first.
7. *Plateau.* The monthly or yearly switch changes the prices only, while the columns hold still. Add-ons, which change a column's length, are offered after the period is chosen, never in the same step.
8. *Cut.* Choosing a tier replaces the table with the checkout block at the second level (ground and mark values exchanged), whole, in under 100ms, with no slide or fade.
9. *Count stated.* Under each tier name: "5", "5+3", "5+3+3", "5+3+3+3", "5+3+3+3+3", in figures of equal width.
- *Motion.* Adding an add-on puts one row at the bottom of the chosen column in a single step of 100ms or less and the label becomes "5+3+3+1". Nothing flashes.
- *Reduced motion.* Identical.

Result: a plain green comparison table with no highlighted plan. What survived is one pitch, a known base, length as the only difference, additions at one end on shared rows, the count written as a sum, and a cut to the next block. Nobody would caption it with a composer's name.

## Space & composition (texture, register, layering, the frame of the piece)
- **Layer counts.** 1 (Two Pages), 2 parallel (Fifths), 2 to 4 (Similar Motion, voices entering at about 120 s and 272 s by the band measurements, and at unit 24, about 610 s, by Rubin), one beat layer plus a held field (Twelve Parts 1), bass + organ + chorus + speech (Knee Play 1), 1 violin line plus bass plus chorus reduced to 1 (Trial), 3 rhythmic layers (Opening), ground + chord + voice (Koyaanisqatsi), 1 (Metamorphosis).
- **Register bands.** Pattern pieces sit in one narrow band and change nothing: Two Pages 196–350 Hz fundamentals, Fifths 90–250 Hz, Trial violin 220–440 Hz. Widening is an event with a date (Similar Motion 271.9 s: the 60–130 Hz band goes from −23 to −7 dB of the total). The low floor is reserved for the bass (Knee Play 1 fundamentals A1–G1–C1, Koyaanisqatsi about 36–73 Hz).
- **The edges.** Early pieces start from silence to full in 0.1 s and end dead (Fifths 1,397.5 s, Two Pages about 1,072 s). No fade-in of the process; the only soft edge is Knee Play 1's first held chord.
- **Proportions.** Spaceship 70 : 201 : 241 : 273 s. Opening 12 × 34.8 s. Metamorphosis 3 × 89 s plus a shortened fourth of about 70 s. Twelve Parts part 1: two passes of about 520 s and 550 s.
- **Density.** 6 to 7 onsets a second sustained for 18–23 minutes in the early works (density in Fifths rises only from 6.2 to 7.0); 3.5 to 4.5 in Twelve Parts, 4 to 5 in Opening and Knee Play 1; Metamorphosis 1 to 4. It is flat inside a block and jumps between blocks.
- **Silence.** No rest was found inside any piece before 1988. Metamorphosis' 1 s rests are the only measured rests in the set, at the block joins.

## Form, line & typography (scores, notation, sleeves)
- **Notation as procedure.** Knee Play 3: one slurred row of chords, then numbered formulas 8(4+4)×2, 7(4+3)×2, 6(3+3)×2, 5(3+2)×2. Subtraction of 1 each time, in a fixed frame. Same thing in the Building page: pattern number in a box, ×12, and a verbal cue for entries ("during 5th repeat").
- **Grouping by beam and sum.** Two Pages transcription: brackets and a time signature written as 5/8+4/8+3/8; Music in Fifths 2+3+4 up, 2+3+4 down, no barline; every note the same symbol (eighth).
- **Alignment as explanation.** Gann's charts stack variants so identical notes share a column; additions read as ink moving right, removals as blank at left. Bed: each chord a column of growing sums. Dance: a diagram of 50 phrases in three columns (17+16+17).
- **Density on the page.** Almost no dynamics, no slurs, no rests; ink is about a tenth of the printed page (Building 11%).
- **Sleeves.** Glassworks: thin, wide-set sans capitals "PHILIP GLASS" in prismatic edges, over a photo of cut glass; Solo Piano: condensed white sans over a dark photograph, two lines in opposite corners; Koyaanisqatsi poster: a heavy condensed geometric red word, body text in old-style serif centred in even lines (all read off images). Three unlike faces: the composer's identity is in the process, not the lettering.

## UI translations
1. **Layout:** Choose one pitch (for example a 40px row or a 24px cell) and size everything in whole units of it; a figure is a group of 4–8 units, shown at least 5 times before any variant.
2. **Layout:** Stack variants of one row so the same item sits in the same column (as in Gann's charts). Additions extend the right end and removals leave blank at the left; never reflow or re-centre a row when it grows.
3. **Layout:** Give each stage of a sequence the same band height or column width, whatever its length. Two Pages holds each figure 28–30 s while the figure triples.
4. **State change:** A list, tier or record changes by whole units at one end, one rule at a time: +1, +k (Two Pages uses +6 and +12), or doubling (Similar Motion 12, 16, 24, 40, 72, 136). Do not animate the new unit in with a highlight.
5. **State change:** Never change two things at once. Filters, overlays or a new data series arrive only while the structure is still, and the still state lasts about twice as long as an ordinary step (Similar Motion 47–55 s against 20–30 s).
6. **Labels:** Write counts as sums and multipliers next to the thing counted: "5+4+3", "8 (4+4) × 2", "×12". A cue is a sentence tied to a count ("enter during the 5th repeat").
7. **Colour:** Hold one mark colour and one ground through a block, with marks at about a tenth of the surface (score pages 11–14% ink). Keep each layer in its own value band and reserve the lowest band for the ground layer.
8. **Sections:** Give each section one of two levels, flat inside (measured ranges of 3–5 dB over 17–23 minutes), and change section by a cut within 100ms. A gap of about 1% of the section's length may mark the join (Metamorphosis One: about 1 s in 89 s).
9. **Loops:** For a ring of 3–5 recurring items (tabs, a daily cycle, a rotation), give the members unequal whole-unit spans in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4 and keep the ring fixed once set (Knee Play 1: 4+6+8 beats from 49 s to the end).
10. **Layers:** Under a fixed ground, bring new layers in on the boundary of the cycle or at its half (Koyaanisqatsi: a 14.69 s round, entries 7.3 s apart), each in a band of its own; three layers may run at fixed speeds of 3 : 2 : 1 if none of them ever changes speed.
11. **Motion:** Apply live updates on a fixed tick, as one step with no easing. Do not use crescendo-like builds (growing size, rising opacity, accelerating stagger) to signal progress.
12. **Accessibility:** Nothing loops unprompted, so reduced motion needs no separate design. Because a new unit carries no highlight, every change is also stated in the count label and in a polite live region ("11 items, 3 added").

## What this composer would reject
- A build or swell. Loudness is flat for 17 minutes (Two Pages ±1 dB); steps are not crescendos.
- Rests inside a process, ornament, or an accent to mark the new step. No attack at a step boundary stands out on the sheets.
- Gradual drift of one part against another: parts change together, whole cycles at a time. The exception is on paper and bounded: in Train the voices' phrases of 4 or 5 beats run against a 3-beat saxophone ostinato only until they line up again (Gann).
- Two things changing at once. Length changes while register and timbre stay; voices enter only when length is still (Similar Motion).
- A hidden step: the score names every stage as a sum, and repeats are there to make each stage heard. In Contact he faults his own 1967 pieces for structure that was less clear, and says additive process came from simplifying all his procedures into one idea (paraphrased).
- Loose or approximated repetition, a loop running by machine. Glass: "We've never used a loop or a sequencer" (muzines 1984).
- Softening the joins. New blocks are cut in, not crossfaded.
- Full harmonic development: one chord row (3, 4 or 5 chords) is the whole harmonic stock of Einstein (Gann's "Elements" chart).

## Signature prompt seed
Design the screen on one small pitch, with a short figure of 4–8 identical units repeated in rows until it is familiar. Change only the length of the figure, one named rule at a time (add one, add a fixed group, double a tag), with new units joining at the right and old units keeping their columns. Hold size, weight, value and tint level, in one ink at about a tenth of the surface. Write each stage's count beside it as a sum ("5+4+3", "×12"). Let a new layer enter only while the figure is still, in a band of its own. Join sections by a hard cut between two flat levels, with no fades, swells or accents. Nothing animates unprompted; changes land in one step.
