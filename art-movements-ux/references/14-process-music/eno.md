# Brian Eno — Process Music

How to read this file: nobody listened. Timings come from the sheets, from `measure_time.py`, and from a semitone-band analysis of the whole files (10 frames per second, autocorrelation and onset-gap tests per pitch band, window-by-window comparison of onset patterns, "similarity" 0–1). Character words (voice, piano, synthesizer) carry their source. `measure_time.py` onset counts are weak on soft, beating, sustained sounds: its "pulse candidates" of 0.16–0.5 s on these works are vibrato, tremolo and partial beating, not a beat, and are not used here except for Komarek.

## Essence
Eno sets a small number of plain sounds going round on cycles of different length, lets them overlap with no conductor, and chooses the cycles so that the same combination hardly ever returns. The parts are fixed and short; the time they take to exhaust each other is long. He then behaves as planner and first listener, changing filters, levels or which layers are open rather than writing events. The measurements add one thing his reputation leaves out: in several works a long stretch of the result is itself copied whole, so the surface is loose while the frame is an exact loop.

## Detailed observations

**Discreet Music.** Three periods are in the sound. (1) Every band repeats at 5.65–5.70 s (similarity 0.74 for the whole texture; 0.61 at two, 0.50 at three, 0.39 at four). This is the tape delay. A note re-enters at that spacing and loses about 1.7 dB each time: D#3 after 132 s peaks at 45.8, 44.5, 42.8, 41.2, 39.4, 37.5, 35.8, 34.6, 32.7, 31.1, 29.2, 27.3 dB, twelve passes in 62 s, a loss of 18.5 dB. (2) A higher phrase (C#5, D#5, A#5) returns every 63.6–63.7 s (similarity 0.95 in C#5); its C#5 entries step up at about 28, 90, 153, 220, 282, 345, 407 s. (3) A lower phrase (D#3, D#4, C4) returns every 68.85–68.9 s (0.84); D#3 entries at about 62, 130, 198, 266, 333, 407, 475 s. Search summaries of the Reverb Machine analysis give 63 and 68 s; the measurement agrees. So each phrase arrives just as the last one has sunk about 18–20 dB, and one phrase lags the other by 5.2 s more at every turn. From the two periods the pair meets again after about 843 s (63.65 × 68.85 ÷ 5.2; calculated, not seen), so a 30-minute side holds roughly two meetings. The piece comes up from nothing: first above −60 dBFS at 10.7 s; first-minute mean −49 dB; −36 dB block at 60 s; full by about 95 s. Minutes 2–11 sit at −26 to −29 dB (one −32). After that it sinks unevenly to −37 to −39 in the last minutes, about 10 dB in 20 minutes, with no event marking the change. It falls below −60 dBFS at 1810 s (published length 30:35). The texture's centre moves with the equaliser: slowly — minute means of the spectral centre wander between about 260 and 570 Hz, low around minutes 12, 15–16 and 23–24, high around minutes 4, 21 and 28. On the 100–160 s sheet the two lines sit on opposite sides: the left channel carries bursts at 104.8 and 110.5 s (5.7 s apart) while the right is quiet, the right answers at 108.4 and 114.0 s, and the 2-second left-to-right balance swings from +12 dB to −6 dB and back. Notes swell and overlap; there is no gap in the texture after the first 40 s. Pitch classes by share: G# 28%, D# 27%, F 17%, C 10%, A# 8%: a five-note set. Energy lies D#3–G#5, centre F4.

**Discreet Music sleeve.** The LP back is a square in three bands. The top 35% is four narrow columns of small sans-serif text, headings bold. The next 20% is empty. The diagram fills 60–88% of the height and the full width. The heading reads as an "operational diagram", with one line of key: the black line is the signal path. Two line kinds only: thick grey halftone outlines for things that stand still (synthesizer, equaliser, echo unit, two tape recorders with reels) and one continuous black line with arrowheads for what moves. The boxes shrink as the signal goes right (about 250 × 200, 258 × 75, 136 × 75 px of 1500) and the two recorders are a matching pair (214 × 203). The black path runs straight right, climbs over, drops into the recorder, runs along the tape, then splits: one branch goes back up and left as the return, the other ends in a spiral of about 3 turns on the take-up reel, the master tape. Labels are eight or nine lower-case words set inside or beside their part. No time is drawn at all: the diagram shows the machine, not the piece.

## Process and relations (observed)
- **Plain cells, unequal periods.** 2/1: seven single notes on 16.13–31.81 s loops; Discreet Music: two phrases on 63.65 and 68.85 s; Bloom: chains at 7–22 s over drones at 30.35 and 36 s; Airports score lanes at 278–595 px. The longest period in a set is about twice the shortest (1.97 in 2/1; 2.14 in its drawing), never ten times.
- **The differences between periods are small and not multiples.** Discreet Music 5.2 s in 64; 2/1 neighbours differ by 0.4–3.4 s (19.7 and 20.06, 16.13 and 17.8). Small gaps give slow sliding: 1098 s for one pair in 2/1, about 843 s in Discreet Music.
- **Simple inside, complex between.** No cell in 2/1 has more than one note; the variety (0 to 7 voices at once) is all overlap. 1/1 is the opposite half of the rule: the cell is complex (90.4 s) and does not vary; only a 2–4% layer does.
- **Two kinds of material, drawn two ways.** Fixed black and free grey on the Airports sleeve; grey machine and black signal on the Discreet Music sleeve; struck notes against on/off layers in Thursday Afternoon; touched chains against drone in Bloom. One kind holds, the other travels.
- **Repetition with decay.** An event comes back quieter at a fixed spacing until it is gone: 1.7 dB per 5.66 s in Discreet Music, 1–3 dB per 8–20 s in Bloom, shrinking ticks at 2.9 s in Reflection. New entries arrive when the last has fallen about 18–20 dB (Discreet Music).
- **The block is copied, but too long to notice.** Neroli 1158.1 s × 3; Thursday Afternoon 620 s stretch repeated 2016.9 s later; 1/1's 90.4 s × 10.9; Reflection 743.1 s partial layer. Copy lengths seen run from 1.5 to 19 minutes.
- **No steady pulse, yet a hidden slot.** Reflection's 2.88 s lattice, Komarek's 2.05 ± 0.2 s chain, the 5.66 s delay. The slot spaces events; it is not marked by a beat.
- **Entries are slow, exits slower, and cuts are rare.** Discreet Music rises for 95 s; Ascent fades 15 s in; 2/1 swells 1.2 s; Reflection tones ring 20–60 s. Cuts appear only at a change of state: Thursday Afternoon's square gates, Bloom's 2481 s break.
- **Silence is part of the count.** 2/1: 20% of the time nobody sounds, with 4–5 s holes; Lysis: 21 rests of 3 s or more, up to 14 s; 1/1: a 9-second hole in every loop; Rothko Doric: whole 5 s blocks empty.
- **Level over an hour, unevenness over seconds.** Neroli 2-minute means within 3 dB for 57 minutes; Reflection within 6 dB; Thursday Afternoon within about 11; against second-to-second spreads of 10–28 dB.
- **Change is filter and layer, not new notes.** Discreet Music's centre drifts 260–570 Hz by equaliser (the sleeve says this is the composer's only intervention); Thursday Afternoon gates a layer; 1/1 adds tones into the gap. The pitch set stays (five main pitch classes cover 87–96% in Discreet Music, 2/1, 1/1, Reflection and Lysis).
- **Maker becomes listener.** Discreet Music sleeve: planner and programmer, then audience; JamBase 2017: choose materials and rules, let algorithms vary, then listen for days and adjust; Edge: control and surrender as one skill. The run is kept as it falls, errors and gaps included.

## Process (derived)
*From observation to process (see SKILL.md §1). This is what transfers; the rest of the file is evidence.*

**Brian Eno.** *Set a few plain parts going on cycles of unequal, near lengths over an exactly held frame, leave most of each cycle empty, let every event return weaker by equal steps until it is gone, and change the whole only by opening, shutting or filtering a layer; publish the rules, run them, and tune the rules, never a result.*
Process:
1. Fix the stock and the window. Choose 5–7 kinds of plain part and let five of them cover about nine tenths of everything shown; each part is one thing (one item, one mark, one line) and all sit in one narrow band of size and value. Nothing is added to the stock later. The view is a window on something that could run on: windows are the same size whatever the length of the content, the arrangement is cut by both edges, and there is no opening or closing figure. If the stock changes, every later step is redone.
2. Divide the stock into two kinds and draw them two ways. The held kind (frame, navigation, text, controls) is exact and identical at every return. The travelling kind recurs and shifts. Give each kind one treatment (tint against line, or thick against thin) and never swap them. The travelling kind is thin marks on 2–5% of the surface, or pale tint on up to a quarter of it.
3. Give every travelling part a lane and a cycle of its own, in 2–7 lanes. A cycle is counted in whatever the product already counts: distance along a scroll, rows, items, visits, days. The longest cycle is at most about twice the shortest, neighbours differ by 2–20%, and no cycle is a whole multiple of another, so that at least one pair of lanes does not line up again inside the span a person sees.
4. Leave most of each cycle empty. A part fills one fifth to one third of its cycle, so the rest is at least twice the part. No rules are drawn between lanes: where parts coincide they merge, and where none falls the ground shows and stays unfilled. Check the count over a long span: nothing present about a fifth of the time, two lanes or fewer about four fifths, every lane together almost never. Totals per screen or per day stay level within about a fifth, while short spans run from empty to crowded.
5. Start everything on a hidden slot. Every start falls on a whole multiple of one small unit, with a drift of up to a tenth of the unit; only some slots are used, and the slot itself is never drawn or marked.
6. Let an event come back weaker until it is gone. A new arrival, a touch or a change returns at one fixed spacing and loses an equal step of strength each time, reaching nothing after 10–14 returns. Events made together share a spacing; the next batch draws another from a range of about 1 : 3. Admit the next event in a lane when the last has lost about two thirds of its strength, so that fading balances arriving and density stays level.
7. Let the frame be an exact copy. Compose one block at least 8 turns of the longest cycle in length, fix it by a seed, and repeat it whole. Inside the block nothing lines up; the block itself returns unchanged, so the same seed always gives the same view.
8. Change state by gate and filter only. Open or shut a whole layer, or move one filter across the same stock so that a different band of it comes forward. No new kind of part ever marks a change. Make one such change at a time, at intervals 10 or more times the finest spacing of events.
9. Keep cuts for changes of state. Inside a state a part arrives slowly and leaves more slowly (the exit 2–4 times the entry) and nothing is cut. A gate from step 8 is square: the layer is there or not, whole, in one step.
10. Publish the rules and tune them by running. Show how the arrangement is made (lanes, cycles, what is held, what travels) as a one-line key or a small diagram of the apparatus beside it. Run the rules over a span long enough for the two closest cycles to meet, look at the whole run, then change a cycle, a filter or a layer. Never correct a single result by hand.
Why it works:
- Adaptation to the parts, not to the whole: a plain part on a fixed cycle is learned in two or three returns, and near, unequal cycles keep the combination from being learned at all, so the view stays familiar and never finished. Equalise the cycles and the lanes lock into one grid that is read once and then ignored. Stretch the ratio to ten and the fast lane becomes a texture under the slow one, no longer a peer. Make one a multiple of another and their meeting recurs as a visible stripe.
- Grouping by likeness: the variety is read as overlap only while each part can be recognised alone. Put complexity inside the parts as well as between them and the result is noise. The other way round also holds: an elaborate part may be used if it never varies and only a few per cent of the surface does.
- Figure and ground: with rest at least twice the part, a coincidence of two lanes is a small event found by oddness and an empty span is a pause. Fill the rests and overlaps cannot be told from the parts, and the level becomes one flat tone.
- Constancy as information: an exact repeat is discounted at a glance, so the eye goes to the few per cent that differs with no emphasis on it. A held kind that is only roughly repeated cannot be subtracted, and the travelling kind is lost in it.
- Value as age: equal steps of fading let strength be read as time since the event, and order as rank of strength. Without fading, an item leaves by a cut nobody sees and new ones pile on a full field; with unequal steps the ranking breaks.
- Salience of onset: an abrupt edge takes the eye. Slow edges inside a state leave attention free, and keeping the cut for a change of state makes the cut itself the message. Mark every slot and it becomes a beat that asks to be followed.
- Common spacing groups events made together without a drawn grid, and a slot that is used only now and then orders them without being seen.
Colour relations: the arrangement is close to one hue. Ground takes 70–80% of the surface; one kind is a mid tint on about a tenth and the other is the darkest value on 2–3%, as thin marks (which kind is tint and which is line is free, but fixed once chosen). No hue per lane: lanes are told apart by spacing and length, then by position. Where two tinted parts meet the overlap is simply the next darker tint and is the darkest tint allowed. Fading runs in equal value steps toward the ground. A brand hue stays a flat field outside the arrangement and carries no state. No colour marks what is new.
Space and line: 5–7 kinds of part, five covering about nine tenths; 2–7 lanes; cycles within 2 : 1 and neighbours 2–20% apart; a part one fifth to one third of its cycle; empty about a fifth of the span and two lanes or fewer about four fifths; long-span totals level within a fifth; slot drift up to a tenth; 10–14 fading steps; next admitted at two thirds faded; copy block 8 or more turns of the longest cycle; no rules between lanes; one line weight with square or quarter-round corners; equal windows cut by both edges; rests left open.
Type requirement: text belongs to the held kind: it never fades, drifts or takes part in a cycle. Labels are small and lower-case, set inside or beside the part they name with no box round them, flush left, in two weights at most and in narrow columns with empty space kept beside them. Each window carries its number at one end and its length at the other, so the face needs figures of equal width. The key to the arrangement is one line of plain words.
Motion and interaction: by default nothing runs on a clock; cycles advance on what the person does or on the calendar (scroll, page, visit, day), and the unequal spacing is already in the layout. A part that arrives while the view is open fades in over 600–1200ms and leaves over 2–4 times that; a trace loses one step per refresh or visit and is removed after 10–14. A gate is one step of 100ms or less with no easing. Live arrivals are held to a slot of 2–6s and enter one batch at a time. An ambient mode is allowed only on an unattended or idle surface: each lane has a period of 30s or more, the periods obey step 3, at most one part is changing at any moment, and it stops on the first input. Reduced motion: fades become single steps, the ambient mode is off, and cycles still advance per visit, so the arrangement loses nothing.
Fails when: something must be found in the same place every time and it has been put in the travelling kind (navigation, controls and totals are always held); one item must win the first glance or a change is urgent, since nothing new is marked and arrivals are slow; fewer than three returns of each cycle fall inside what a person sees, so no part is ever learned; the content cannot give up a fifth of its span to rest (dense tables, forms); values must be compared exactly, since overlap and fading blur them; the state must be reproducible and no seed is kept.
Signature (do not carry together): soft glowing shapes that drift and fade by themselves on a timer · pale bars in unruled lanes sliding out of step, shown as the picture · a card that gives a cryptic instruction drawn at random.

**Transfer test** (an illustration that the process survives new colours and content; never reuse its hues, layout or wording as a design answer):

Home page of a public library's member site, desktop 1280 × 800, in the brand's brick red on warm white with a slab serif. No sound, no drifting shapes, no lanes of bars, nothing on a timer.
1. *Stock and window.* Six kinds of part: search field, account line, text link, shelf card (cover, title, one line), notice line, section rule. The page is one 800px window with no hero and no footer flourish; the shelf band is cut by the right edge.
2. *Two kinds.* Held: search, opening hours, loans and holds, navigation; identical on every visit, drawn as dark text on the ground. Travelling: the shelf cards and notice lines, drawn as a pale tinted panel with no outline. Tint covers about 18% of the page on a full day.
3. *Lanes and cycles.* Four shelf lanes (staff picks, events, new arrivals, from the archive), each a ring that moves one place per visiting day: 5, 6, 7 and 9 places. 9 : 5 is 1.8 and none divides another; the four rings realign after 630 visiting days.
4. *Rest.* Two places in each ring hold a card and the rest are blank (three of nine in the longest), so a lane shows a card on about a third of days. On about one day in five the band is empty and shows only its ground; on nearly nine days in ten it holds two cards or fewer; all four appear about one day in a hundred. Empty lanes are not filled with a placeholder.
5. *Slot.* Cards start on a 96px column unit with up to 8px of drift taken from the seed; the columns are not drawn.
6. *Fading trace.* "Returned" and "renewed" lines under the account step down in value by one of 12 equal steps per visit and are removed after the twelfth. A second line for the same loan is admitted once the first is 8 steps down.
7. *Copy.* The ring contents and drifts are fixed by a seed for 72 visiting days (8 turns of the longest ring), then the block repeats; a member and the help desk see the same page for the same day.
8. *Gate and filter.* "Children's library" shuts the events and archive lanes and opens a reading-age lane; "this branch only" is a filter over the same cards. No banner or new kind of card announces either.
9. *Edges.* A hold that becomes ready while the page is open fades in over 800ms; a dismissed notice fades over 2s. The gates switch in under 100ms.
10. *Published and tuned.* A line under the band reads "four shelves, each on its own rota; some days are quiet". The rota is run over the full 630 days before release, and what gets adjusted is a ring length, never a single day.
- *Reduced motion.* The two fades become single steps. Nothing else moved.

Result: a plain red-and-white library page whose shelf differs a little every day and is sometimes bare. What survived is plain parts on near, unequal cycles over an exact frame, rest counted in, traces that fade by equal steps, and change by gate. Nobody would caption it with a composer's name or as a music visualiser.

## Space & composition
- **Frame.** The frame is a window on something that could run on: 1/1 stops in the decay of its eleventh turn (about 84 s into the loop) with no ending figure; 2/1 stops mid-process (no pair has closed); Reflection and Discreet Music are faded in and out; the sleeve boxes are equal windows whatever the length (8.25 or 16.35) and the lines run off both sides.
- **Register.** Each work keeps a narrow band: 2/1 fundamentals in a tenth (F3–G#4); Discreet Music D#3–G#5; 1/1 energy C#2–D5; Neroli E1–A#3; Lysis C4–F5; Reflection G1–C5. Within it the low part is held or rare (Reflection's G1 every 52–190 s; Neroli's E1–A1 bed), the middle carries most of the notes, the top is brief. 8 partials at once is the usual thickness (Ascent 9, Thursday Afternoon 10, Lysis 4).
- **Layers.** Two to seven: 2/1 seven loops; Discreet Music two phrases plus echo; Bloom drone plus chains; Reflection low strikes, held tones, ticks. Layers are told apart by spacing and length, then by register.
- **Stereo.** Discreet Music puts the two lines left and right (swing +12 to −6 dB); 2/1 and 1/1 sit near the middle (1.8 and 2.6 dB spread); Reflection ticks alternate sides.
- **Density.** Detected onsets 1–2 a second in 1/1 and Thursday Afternoon, 2 in 2/1 (counting partials); 4 in Reflection; Bloom 85–115 note starts a minute after the opening.
- **Emptiness.** Nothing fills the rests with activity: the tail of the last event is the only sound (1/1 at 15–24 s; Reflection 520–575 s).

## Form, line & typography
- **Notation signs (Airports back).** Ring, dotted ring, filled dot, black square, stepped line, straight line with a stop, quarter arc, grey bar. Line weight is constant (about 3 px of 1494) and corners are right-angled or quarter circles. Height within a box reads as register, length as time; no key is printed. Grey marks are thicker and unhatched; black marks are thin.
- **Constancy is the information.** The black cluster in 1/1 is pixel-regular (424 ± 1 px period); a glance shows what does not change, and the eye then finds the grey that does. In 2/1 the bars in a lane are the same length and only their phase against other lanes differs. The stack of lanes drifts right by a different amount, so blocks of merged grey never repeat.
- **Diagram (Discreet Music).** Grey halftone boxes with thick outlines (about 14 px of 1500), black path 6 px with arrowheads, one spiral. Words set as small lower-case labels, no boxes round them. The path goes round corners with a radius of about 10 px. Reading order is left to right, then a loop back.
- **Type.** The sleeves use a plain grotesque (Fonts In Use gives Helvetica for Airports): small, flush left, two weights, generous empty space; the text is set as narrow columns on the LP back. Neroli: an outlined roman capital title on a flat ground with a thin white curve and dashed cells along the base. Thursday Afternoon: small widely spaced capitals above and below a painted square.
- **Front covers.** Airports is a map fragment (rivers, contours, one large letter T); Discreet Music a screen photograph at the right half, black at the left, title the width of the picture (about 38% of the front). Nothing on them shows the music.
- **Proportions.** Airports boxes 7.8 : 1 (a timeline strip). Discreet Music diagram about 3.6 : 1. Cards 7.1 × 9.8 cm (1 : 1.38).

- **Event length against cycle length.** 2/1: 4–5 s in 16–32 s. 1/1: a loop of 90 s holds six bursts and a 9 s hole. Bloom: note 1–6 s, chain 110–220 s. Reflection: strike, 20–60 s ring, 52–190 s until the next low strike. Lysis: 2–4 s and 10–45 s tones mixed.

- **How change is paced.** Discreet Music: one event of change per 5.7 s at the finest, one filter drift per several minutes at the coarsest. Thursday Afternoon: layer switch every 60–140 s in the gated part. Reflection: a burst every 60–120 s. 2/1: a new combination roughly every 3 s (167 entries in 495 s), a rest of 1 s or more about every 24 s.
- **Rate feeling** (from numbers only): events sparser than one per 2 s in Komarek and Neroli, about one per 0.7 s inside 1/1's bursts, 5–10 per minute per pitch in Thursday Afternoon.
- **Chance and rule.** Eno's published example of a rule for Reflection: raise one note in a hundred by five semitones. Box text for Generative Music 1: about 150 parameters. Reflection app (Chilvers): harmony brighter in the morning, returning to the home key by evening, notes thinner and slower in the small hours; the album upload cannot show this.
- **Long-form timing spoken of by Eno.** Installations with cycles of 60 or 100 weeks (Muzines interview); loops of "every 23 seconds, or thereabouts" and 21½, 17 s in an E&MM interview of 1985 that the source warns may be half joking.
- **Attention.** Music should work at levels from foreground to background (Airports liner, 1978); the sleeve of Discreet Music advises a low level, near the limit of hearing. Measured: Discreet Music varies 14 dB (1-second 10th–95th percentile) and Neroli 15 dB, against 28 dB in 2/1, so the two that ask to be quiet are the more even.

## UI translations
1. **Repetition:** Give recurring things cycles that are near and unequal, with no cycle a multiple of another and the longest at most about twice the shortest (2/1: seven loops of 16.13–31.81 s, ratio 1.97). Rotating content rings of 5, 6, 7 and 9 items realign after 630 turns; rings of 4, 8 and 8 realign after 8.
2. **Repetition:** Count cycles in visits, days, rows or scroll distance, not in seconds on a live screen. The unequal spacing is then in the layout and in the calendar, and nothing has to move.
3. **Spacing:** Let a recurring part fill one fifth to one third of its interval and leave the rest open (2/1: 4–5 s of sound in loops of 16–32 s; 20% of the piece has no voice at all). Do not put a placeholder, a tip or a skeleton in the gap.
4. **Density:** Hold totals level per screen or per day and let them be uneven inside it (Neroli: 2-minute means within 3 dB for 57 minutes, 1-second levels spread over 15 dB). Budget for two concurrent items or fewer about four fifths of the time (2/1: 79%).
5. **Layering:** Draw what holds and what travels in two treatments and no more (Airports back cover: thin black for the fixed figure at 3.1% of the box, thicker grey for the free layer at 2.0%). Keep the held layer pixel-exact from visit to visit so that a 2–4% difference is found without a highlight.
6. **Layering:** Omit dividers between lanes; let neighbouring tinted items touch and merge, and tell lanes apart by interval and length first, position second (2/1 score: eight lanes with no lines between them).
7. **State change:** Mark recency by a trace that loses one equal value step per refresh and is gone after 10–14 (Discreet Music: twelve passes at 1.7 dB each; Bloom: 12–14 repeats over 25–30 dB). Admit the next trace in the same place when the last is about two thirds gone, so the count on screen stays level (Bloom: 85–115 note starts a minute, held steady).
8. **State change:** Switch a mode by opening or shutting a whole layer, or by moving one filter over the same items; do not bring in a new component to announce it (Thursday Afternoon: a layer on for 115, 49, 48, 38, 53, 66 and 82 s with square edges; Discreet Music: only the equaliser moves).
9. **Motion:** Inside a state, fade arrivals in over 600–1200ms and departures over 2–4 times as long; reserve the single-step cut of 100ms or less for a change of state (Bloom: nothing is cut inside a world, one clean break between worlds).
10. **Motion:** Hold live arrivals to one hidden slot of 2–6s with up to a tenth of drift and use only some slots (Reflection: 2.88 s lattice used now and then; Komarek: 2.05 ± 0.2 s). Never show the slot as a pulse, a tick or a progress beat.
11. **Generative variation:** Generate once from a seed, make the block at least 8 turns of the longest cycle, and repeat it whole (Neroli: one 1158.1 s block three times, holding 8.5 turns of its 136.3 s cycle; 1/1: one 90.4 s loop 10.9 times). Store the seed with the state so support and tests see the same view.
12. **Disclosure:** Put the rules beside the result as a one-line key or a small diagram: fixed things in one treatment, the path in the other, short lower-case labels with no boxes (Discreet Music sleeve: grey apparatus, one black signal line, eight or nine words). Tune by running a long span and changing a cycle or a filter, never one output.
13. **Accessibility:** Any ambient mode is off by default, runs only on an idle or unattended surface with periods of 30s or more, changes one part at a time, stops on input and is absent under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Because no arrival is marked, announce each one in a polite live region and state the count in text.

## What this composer would reject
- Drama by arrival: no work measured has a climax or a closing cadence; levels are flat over 30–60 minutes and endings are fades or stops.
- A steady stated beat: no reliable pulse in eight of nine recordings; Komarek's slot wobbles by ±0.2 s.
- Many notes at once as richness: 2/1 has two voices or fewer 79% of the time.
- Performer display or last-minute dabbling: on the Discreet Music sleeve he calls it a discipline to keep from interfering.
- Fixed clock-locked layers that line up often: periods in 2/1 avoid short common multiples, and the piece is shorter than several of its cycles.
- Filling rests: rests of 4–14 s stay open.
- Music that insists on attention or cheers up a place by force (Airports liner; canned music named as the contrast).
- Hiding how it works: the score is the apparatus, printed on the sleeve.
- Pre-decided results: he prefers to set rules and be surprised (Edge; In Motion lecture).

## Signature prompt seed
Build the screen from five to seven plain parts in two kinds: a held kind that is exact on every visit (frame, text, controls) and a travelling kind in a second treatment. Give each travelling part its own lane and a cycle counted in visits, rows or scroll distance, with cycles near and unequal (within 2 : 1, none a multiple of another). Let a part fill a third of its cycle at most and leave the rest empty, with no dividers between lanes. Show recency as a trace that loses one equal value step per refresh and is gone after about twelve. Change modes only by opening, shutting or filtering a layer, in one step; inside a mode, arrivals fade in slowly and leave more slowly. Fix the arrangement by a seed, print a one-line key to how it is made, and run nothing on a timer.
